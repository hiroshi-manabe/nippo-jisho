[c1-l001] Saicai. Nixino vmi. *Mar do poente.*
[c1-l001 note] Saikai is glossed by nishi no umi, the western sea. The initial entry also explains the preceding page's Sai- catchword without expanding it there.
[c1-l002] Saicaidǒ. *Caminho, ou eſtrada do Miaco pe-*
[c1-l002 note] Saikaidoo names the road from Miaco toward the west. The headword's long o has a downward caron; the Portuguese pera is divided at the edge.
[c1-l003 >] *ra as partes do poente.*
[c1-l003 note] This completes the route's direction, toward the western regions, and the divided pera from the preceding row.
[c1-l004] Saicaixi. l, Saicaxi. *Aruore aßi chamada*
[c1-l004 note] Saicaixi and Saicaxi are alternative names for a tree. The upright l introduces the alternative; its useful fruit is described next.
[c1-l005 >] *que dà hũa fruita boa pera mezinha.*
[c1-l005 note] The tree bears fruit considered useful as medicine. Printed dà and hũa retain their grave and tilde; fruita and mezinha are not modernized.
[c1-l006] Saican. *Induſtria, diligencia.*
[c1-l006 note] Saikan denotes resourcefulness and diligence. The scan restores the missing i in Saican and reads Induſtria, not Indaſtria.
[c1-l007] Saicanna. *Peßoa diligente, & induſtrioſa.*
[c1-l007 note] Saikanna describes a diligent, resourceful person, the adjectival counterpart of the preceding noun.
[c1-l008] Saicanni. *Aduer.*
[c1-l008 note] Saikanni is explicitly marked as an adverb, inheriting the sense of acting diligently or resourcefully from the preceding entries.
[c1-l009] Saicaxi. *Vide* Saicaixi.
[c1-l009 note] This is a cross-reference from the alternative tree name Saicaxi to Saicaixi, not a new botanical description. The target remains upright.
[c1-l010] Saichi. *Induſtria, & ſaber.*
[c1-l010 note] Saichi denotes ingenuity and knowledge. The Portuguese gloss separates resourcefulness from learning while keeping both senses together.
[c1-l011] Saichǔ. Monaca. *Meo, força, & feruor dal-*
[c1-l011 note] Saichuu is the midst or height of something. Printed Monaca is retained as the Japanese gloss; it is not silently normalized to a more expected modern form.
[c1-l012 >] *gũa couſa. ¶* Caxxenno ſaichǔni. *No-*
[c1-l012 note] Kassen no saichuu ni gives the first example, in the midst of battle. The printed division No- continues into meo on the next row.
[c1-l013 >] *meo, & feruor da batalha. ¶* Gacumǒno-
[c1-l013 note] The battle example is translated as its midst and fervour; gakumon no then begins the corresponding example about study. Its terminal hyphen is visible.
[c1-l014 >] ſaichǔni. *Nomeo, & força do eſtudo. ¶* Fu
[c1-l014 note] The study example means being in the midst or intensity of study. Fu begins fuyu, winter, below; no hyphen is added where none is printed.
[c1-l015 >] yuno ſaichǔ. *Coração do inuerno.*
[c1-l015 note] Fuyu no saichuu is the heart of winter. This completes the divided Japanese word and the sequence of examples for saichuu.
[c1-l016] Saicô. Futatabi vocoſu. *Reedificar, ou*
[c1-l016 note] Saikoo, explained by futatabi okosu, means building or raising again. The circumflex over o is retained rather than assimilated to the carons nearby.
[c1-l017 >] *tornar aleuãtar algũa couſa que eſtaua ja caida,*
[c1-l017 note] The object is something already fallen that is raised again. The abbreviation tilde in aleuãtar and the printed algũa remain unexpanded.
[c1-l018 >] *ou desbaratada. Vt,* Iyeuo ſaicôſuru. *Tor-*
[c1-l018 note] The object may also have been ruined. Iye wo saikoo suru illustrates rebuilding a house; desbaratada keeps the supplied short s.
[c1-l019 >] *nar a edificar, ou aleuantar caſa.*
[c1-l019 note] The example is translated as building or raising a house again, completing Tornar from the preceding physical row.
[c1-l020] Saicocu. Nixino cuni. *Reinos do poente.* i,
[c1-l020 note] Saikoku is glossed as nishi no kuni, the western countries or realms. The final i is upright in the scan; it introduces the following specification of the region rather than forming part of the italic definition.
[c1-l021 >] *Eſtes noue reinos deſta ilha do Ximo.* i, Qiǔ-
[c1-l021 note] The explanation identifies nine realms of the island called Ximo, then begins Qiuu-shuu. Ximo occurs within Portuguese prose, unlike the Japanese synonym that follows.
[c1-l022 >] xǔ. l, Chicuxi. || {mark} *(* || {word}*genhoſo de mãos.*
[c1-l022 note] Xuu completes Qiuu-shuu, followed by the alternative Chikushi. At far right, the separately placed (genhoſo de mãos. completes en- in the Saicu entry below, not this geographical entry. Its omitted de mãos. and exceptional placement are restored while preserving physical order.
[c1-l023] Saicu. Comacani tacumu. *Official, ou en-*
[c1-l023 note] Saiku is explained by komakani takumu, fine craftsmanship. The Portuguese means an artisan or someone dexterous with the hands; en- continues in the displaced far-right fragment on the preceding row.
[c1-l024] Saicu gatana. *Faca de que vſa o official. l,*
[c1-l024 note] Saiku gatana is the knife used by a craftsman. The scan reads Saicu, not Saica, and official, not the draft's separated and malformed o fficiat.
[c1-l025 >] *engenhoſo de maõs.*
[c1-l025 note] This alternative description means someone dexterous with the hands, completing the knife entry's account of its user. The supplied tilde position in maõs is retained.
[c1-l026] Saicunin. *Official mecanico, ou engenhoſo de mãos.*
[c1-l026 note] Saikunin denotes a manual craftsman or skilled artisan. The scan supports double f in Official and final o in engenhoſo rather than the draft's feminine ending.
[c1-l027] Saidai. Mottomo vôqinari. *Couſa muito grã-*
[c1-l027 note] Saidai, glossed by mottomo ookinari, means very great or large. The abbreviation tilde in grã- is retained across the Portuguese line break.
[c1-l028 >] *de.* S.
[c1-l028 note] This finishes grande, very large. The upright S. marks written usage rather than belonging to the italic definition.
[c1-l029] Saidaiji. *Certos grãos vermelhos de confeiçoens*
[c1-l029 note] Saidaiji names certain red medicinal pellets or prepared grains. The scan reads confeiçoens with f, distinguishing preparations from the draft's misleading long-s reading.
[c1-l030 >] *que ſeruem pera variasdoenças.*
[c1-l030 note] The preparations are said to be used for various illnesses. The close-set variasdoenças is retained as in the supplied transcription, without adding a modern word break.
[c1-l031] Saidan. Futatabi danzuru. *Conferencia, ou*
[c1-l031 note] Saidan is explained as discussing again, futatabi danzuru. The Portuguese gives consultation or conference before adding the repetition sense below.
[c1-l032 >] *repetição de algũa couſa.*
[c1-l032 note] This completes the alternative meaning, repetition of something. The printed tilde in algũa remains on u.
[c1-l033] Saido. Sucui taſuquru. *O ſaluar. Vt,* Fi-
[c1-l033 note] Saido means saving, explained by sukui tasukuru. Fi- begins hito, the people saved in the following example.
[c1-l034 >] touo ſaido ſuru. *Saluar aos homens.*
[c1-l034 note] Hito wo saido suru means saving people. The divided word is completed without replacing the historical Japanese forms with modern equivalents.
[c1-l035] Saido riyacu. *Saluação. Vt,* Saido riyacuno
[c1-l035 note] Saido riyaku denotes salvation. The following example begins a phrase about the benefit of salvation or redemption.
[c1-l036 >] gouon. *Beneficio da redẽpção, ou ſaluação.*
[c1-l036 note] Go-on completes the Japanese phrase for the benefit or grace of salvation. The Portuguese redẽpção retains its abbreviation tilde rather than expanding the missing nasal letter.
[c1-l037] Saifai. Futatabi vogamu. *O adorar duas vezes.*
[c1-l037 note] Saihai is explained as worshipping twice, futatabi ogamu. The abraded joined arches are read as m in vogamu rather than the draft's in sequence; the worship gloss supports that chosen reading.
[c1-l038] Saifen. Futatabi cayeſu. *Tornar amandar*
[c1-l038 note] Saihen, futatabi kayesu, means sending something again or returning it. The Portuguese amandar remains close-set as printed in the draft.
[c1-l039 >] *a couſa outra vez. ¶* Sacazzuqi faya ſai-
[c1-l039 note] The definition finishes sending the thing again. The cup-passing example then begins, with saifen divided across the line.
[c1-l040 >] fen touotte gozaru. *Ia o Sacazuqui correo a*
[c1-l040 note] The cup has already gone round a second time. Sacazuqui is a Japanese noun embedded in the Portuguese translation; the example's Sacazzuqi above has a different printed spelling.
[c1-l041 >] *ſegunda vez.*
[c1-l041 note] This completes the statement that the cup has circulated for the second time, the social example illustrating saifen.
[c1-l042] Saifi. Saino caua. *Pele de bada.*
[c1-l042 note] Saihi is a hide, expressed by sai no kawa and Portuguese pele de bada. The dictionary retains the historical fi spelling in the compound headword.
[c1-l043] Saifit. Comacana fude. *O eſcreuer algũa*
[c1-l043 note] Saihit is fine or detailed writing, glossed by komakana fude. The bare final t is retained as a historical checked ending rather than expanded to tçu.
[c1-l044 >] *couſa miudamente. Vt,* Saifitni atauazu.
[c1-l044 note] The example saihit ni atawazu is translated below as having no need to write in detail. The scan restores t and n in Saifitni, where the draft misread mi.
[c1-l045 >] *Não ha pera que eſcreuer miuda mente.* S.
[c1-l045 note] The dictionary explains the formula as there being no need to write minutely. The separated miuda mente is retained, and the final usage label S. is upright.
[c1-l046] Saifǒ. Nixino cata. *Parte do poente.*
[c1-l046 note] Saihoo is the western direction, nishi no kata. Its downward caron is a graphic distinction from the circumflex in Saicô above.
[c1-l047] Saifǒ gocuracu. *Paraiſo de Amida que di-*
[c1-l047 note] Saihoo gokuraku names Amida's paradise in the west. The scan restores i and the downward caron in Saifǒ; Amida is embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l048 >] *zem eſtar pera o poente.*
[c1-l048 note] The paradise is said to lie toward the west. The scan contains o between pera and poente, omitted from the draft; di- is completed here as dizem.

[c2-l001] Saifǒ jǒdo. *Idem.*
[c2-l001 note] Saihoo joodo is given as equivalent to the western paradise described at the foot of column 1. The scan restores the missing accented o in Saifǒ; both printed o marks are downward carons.
[c2-l002] Saifot. Futatabi vocoru. *Renouarſe adoẽça,*
[c2-l002 note] Saihot means a recurrence, explained by futatabi okoru. The Portuguese begins with an illness returning; the final t and the nasal abbreviation in adoẽça are preserved.
[c2-l003 >] *ferida, poſtema, ou chaga de pois deſtar bem.*
[c2-l003 note] A wound, abscess, or sore can likewise return after improvement. The separated de pois and contracted deſtar retain the supplied historical wording.
[c2-l004 >] *Vt,* Yamai, qizu nado ſaifot ſuru.
[c2-l004 note] Yamai, kizu nado saihot suru illustrates illness, wounds, and similar troubles recurring. The bare t before suru is not expanded to a modern syllabic spelling.
[c2-l005] Saifǔ. l, Xeifǔ. Nixino caje. *Vento O eſte.*
[c2-l005 note] Saifuu or seifuu is the west wind, nishi no kaze. The scan supplies a downward caron on the first u as well as the second; the unusual Portuguese spacing O eſte is retained.
[c2-l006] Saigue. Mottomo xita. *Couſa muito baixa.*
[c2-l006 note] Saige is explained as mottomo shita, extremely low. This can describe low rank or quality as well as physical position.
[c2-l007 >] *Vt,* Saigueno fito. *Homem muito baixo.*
[c2-l007 note] Saige no hito applies the term to a very low or base person, clarifying the personal or social use of the adjective.
[c2-l008] Saiguen. Qiua, caguiri. *Borda, ou limite.*
[c2-l008 note] Saigen denotes an edge or limit, with kiwa and kagiri supplied as Japanese equivalents. It is distinct from the preceding word for lowness.
[c2-l009 >] *¶* Saiguenmo nai coto. *Couſa ſem termo,*
[c2-l009 note] Saigen mo nai koto is something without a limit. The scan has an initial paragraph mark omitted from the draft; it is restored before the example.
[c2-l010 >] *ſem medida, ou immenſa.*
[c2-l010 note] The example is further described as immeasurable or immense, completing the negative construction saigen mo nai.
[c2-l011] Saiguet. Toxitçuqi. *Annos, & meſes.*
[c2-l011 note] Saiget, glossed as toshitsuki, means years and months. The historical checked final t is retained in the headword.
[c2-l012 >] *Vt,* Saiguetuo feru. *Paßarenſe annos, &*
[c2-l012 note] Saiget wo heru means years and months passing. The final t of the noun remains before the attached object particle uo, rather than being converted to tçu.
[c2-l013 >] *meſes.*
[c2-l013 note] This short continuation completes years and months in the time-passing example; it is not a separate entry.
[c2-l014] Saiguin. Futatabi guinzuru. *Repetir com*
[c2-l014 note] Saigin is explained by futatabi ginzuru, repeating or chanting again. The Portuguese com continues as com-sigo in the physical lineation.
[c2-l015 >] *ſigo algũa couſa como verſos, &c.em voz bai-*
[c2-l015 note] The action is reciting verses or similar material to oneself in a low voice. The scan has the abbreviation &c.em, not the draft's &crem; baixa is divided at the edge.
[c2-l016 >] *xa, & entoada.* S.
[c2-l016 note] The voice is low and intoned or chanted. Final S. is upright and marks written usage, outside the italic explanation.
[c2-l017] Saiguiri, ru, itta. *Anticiparſe. ¶* Sai-
[c2-l017 note] Saigiri gives verb forms for anticipating or taking the initiative. Sai- begins the following example's saigitte.
[c2-l018 >] guitte gojǒni azzucaru. *Receber carta dã-*
[c2-l018 note] Saigitte gojoo ni azzukaru describes receiving an honoured letter first. The Portuguese dã- begins the historical spelling of beforehand on the following row.
[c2-l019 >] *temão de peßoa alta. i, Antes de eſcreuer pri-*
[c2-l019 note] The letter comes from a person of high standing before the recipient has written first. The physical split pri- belongs to primeiro below.
[c2-l020 >] *meiro. Meliùs Sayeguiru. Vide infrà.*
[c2-l020 note] The dictionary prefers Sayeguiru and directs the reader to its later entry. The word is embedded in the italic editorial apparatus. Enlargement confirms the small grave marks in Meliùs and infrà, which are retained.
[c2-l021] Saigio. *Molher caſada.*
[c2-l021 note] Saijo is defined as a married woman. The printed molher is retained without modernizing its vowel.
[c2-l022] Saigo. i, Xinuru toqi. *Hora da morte. Vt,*
[c2-l022 note] Saigo is explained by shinuru toki, the moment of dying. The headword's final-time sense is illustrated in the following row.
[c2-l023 >] Saigoni voyobu. *Chegar à derradeira hora.*
[c2-l023 note] Saigo ni oyobu means reaching one's final hour. The Portuguese à carries its printed grave accent, and initial v in voyobu is retained.
[c2-l024] Sai-i. *Vt,* Sai-i daijchino coto. *Couſa*
[c2-l024 note] Sai-i is illustrated by sai-i daiichi no koto, something of outstanding eminence. The printed internal hyphen belongs to the Japanese form, not a physical line break.
[c2-l025 >] *muito eminente, & excelente.*
[c2-l025 note] This completes the definition as very eminent and excellent. The scan reads excelente with l, not the draft's excetente.
[c2-l026] Saijen. i, Ijen. *Antes. Vt,* Saijenyo-
[c2-l026 note] Saizen is given as equivalent to izen, before or previously. Saizen yori begins the example across the physical break.
[c2-l027 >] rimǒſugotocu. *Aßi como digo dantes.*
[c2-l027 note] Saizen yori moosu gotoku means as stated previously. The words remain joined as printed, rather than being segmented into modern grammatical units.
[c2-l028] Saiji. i, Toqi. *Horas do comer, ou jantar*
[c2-l028 note] Saiji, equated with toki, names the time when Buddhist monks eat or take their meal. The specification of the monks follows below.
[c2-l029 >] *dos Bonzos.* S. *¶* Saijini fanuo qiſſu.
[c2-l029 note] The definition concerns monks' meals, with upright S. marking written usage. Saiji ni han wo kissu illustrates taking food at that designated time; both long-s forms in qiſſu are retained.
[c2-l030 >] *Comerem os Bonzos, ou jantarem a ſua hora,*
[c2-l030 note] The Japanese example is translated as the monks eating or dining at their own appointed hour. The approximate time is given in the next row.
[c2-l031 >] *que ſera perto daſdez.*
[c2-l031 note] That hour is said to be near ten. The close-set daſdez, including its long s, is retained rather than silently normalized to separated modern words.
[c2-l032] Saijin. l, ſaijinna. Saicacuno fito. *Homẽ*
[c2-l032 note] Saijin or saijinna is explained as a person of saikaku, ingenuity or resourcefulness. The alternative's initial long s and the nasal abbreviation in Homẽ are preserved.
[c2-l033 >] *de induſtria, & ſaber. Item,* Saijinna fi-
[c2-l033 note] The person has ingenuity and knowledge. A second application of saijinna hito begins, describing fluent speech below.
[c2-l034 >] to. *Homem que fala bem, & expeditamẽte.*
[c2-l034 note] Saijinna hito can also mean a person who speaks well and readily. The tilde in expeditamẽte is retained without expanding it.
[c2-l035] Saijǒ. *Couſa excellentißima, ou ſuprema.*
[c2-l035 note] Saijoo means supremely excellent or highest. The source's long-o caron and superlative Portuguese wording are preserved.
[c2-l036 >] *¶* Saijǒno von chiye. *Suprema ſabedo-*
[c2-l036 note] Saijoo no on chiye illustrates supreme wisdom. Printed von chiye remains separated as in the scan, and sabedoria is divided at the right edge.
[c2-l037 >] *ria, ou ſaber.*
[c2-l037 note] This completes sabedoria and gives saber as an alternative Portuguese expression for the supreme knowledge in the example.
[c2-l038] Saimat. Toxino ſuye. *Fim do anno.*
[c2-l038 note] Saimat means year's end, glossed by toshi no sue. The historical final t and the long s in ſuye are not modernized.
[c2-l039] Saimi. *Certa laya de pano de linho canemo crù*
[c2-l039 note] Saimi names a kind of raw hemp or linen cloth. The Portuguese canemo and the grave in crù are retained as printed rather than replaced by modern spelling.
[c2-l040 >] *que ſerue pera Catabiras.*
[c2-l040 note] The cloth is used for katabira garments. Catabiras is a Japanese garment name embedded in Portuguese; enlargement supports retaining the local italic setting rather than imposing the more usual roman type for Japanese.
[c2-l041] Saimin. *Nome que ſe poem aos rapados, ou*
[c2-l041 note] Saimin denotes a name given to tonsured people or monks. The defining feature of the name is supplied in the next row.
[c2-l042 >] *Bonzos que ſe acaba em ſai. Vt,* Saimin-
[c2-l042 note] The name is said to end in sai. That suffix is embedded in Portuguese prose; Saimin- then begins the quoted question, with its printed line-end hyphen retained.
[c2-l043 >] ua nanto mǒſuca. *Como he o nome*
[c2-l043 note] Saimin wa nanto moosu ka asks what the tonsured person's name is. Initial ua is retained as the dictionary's spelling of the particle, and the Portuguese question continues.
[c2-l044 >] *de rapado?*
[c2-l044 note] This completes the question about the name of a tonsured person. The question mark belongs to the printed translation, not to reviewer uncertainty.
[c2-l045] Saimit. Comacani qibixij. *Couſa miuda, &*
[c2-l045 note] Saimit is explained as komakani kibishii, minute and strict. The bare final t is important for understanding the form reused before ni below.
[c2-l046 >] *riguroſa. Vt,* Saimitni qiǔmeuo togu-
[c2-l046 note] Saimit ni kyuume wo toguru describes carrying out a strict, exact examination. The damaged t-n sequence in Saimitni is read against the scan and the preceding headword, rather than the draft's mi.
[c2-l047 >] ru. *Iulgar com rigor, & exacção.* S.
[c2-l047 note] The dictionary translates the example as judging with rigor and exactness. Ru completes toguru; final S. is an upright written-usage label.
