[c1-l001 >] Megaqete yuqu. *Ir com os olhos poſtos,*
[c1-l001 note] Continues Megaqete from f312: megakete yuku is going with one’s eyes directed toward something. This is an indented example, not a new headword.
[c1-l002 >] *ou fitos nalgũa couſa: não ſe diz,* Mega-
[c1-l002 note] The eyes are fixed on something; fitos agrees with olhos and corrects provisional fitas. The colon introduces a restriction on the verb forms, beginning Mega- at the right edge.
[c1-l003 >] qe, uru.
[c1-l003 note] Qe, uru completes the expressly rejected form Megaqe, uru. The dictionary is warning against extending the accepted megakete to these principal forms.
[c1-l004] Megare. *O afaſtar os olhos. ¶* Megare
[c1-l004 note] Megare is turning the eyes away. A paragraph sign then introduces the example megare sezu across the physical line break.
[c1-l005 >] xezu. i, Meuo fanaſazu. *Sem tirar, ou*
[c1-l005 note] Megare sezu is paraphrased by me wo hanasazu, without taking one’s eyes away. The synonym after i, is Japanese explanatory material, not a Portuguese-embedded typeface candidate.
[c1-l006 >] *apartar os olhos.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes the Portuguese translation: not withdrawing or averting the eyes. This closes the negative example of megare.
[c1-l007] Megataqi. *Inimigo adultero que vſa, ou toma*
[c1-l007 note] Megataki denotes the adulterous rival who takes or has relations with another man’s wife. The pejorative inimigo belongs to the source’s definition.
[c1-l008 >] *amolher alhea. ¶* Megataqiuo vtçu. *Ma-*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the reference to another man’s wife and begins megataki wo utsu. The Portuguese Ma- continues as matar on the next row.
[c1-l009 >] *tar ao inimigo quelhe anda com ſua molher.*
[c1-l009 note] Tar completes matar in the source’s example of killing the adulterous rival. This is a lexical illustration from the historical dictionary, not an instruction added by the reviewer.
[c1-l010] Megue, guru, eta. *Que vem de* Megui,
[c1-l010 note] Mege is identified as deriving from Megui, gu. That cited verb lies within the Portuguese Que vem de construction and is therefore a whole-term typeface-review candidate.
[c1-l011 >] gu. *Quebrarſe, & des fazer ſe algũa couſa.*
[c1-l011 note] Gu completes the cited verb. The gloss is intransitive: something breaks or comes apart, contrasting with the transitive verb entered next.
[c1-l012] Megui, u, eida. *Quebrar, & des fazer.*
[c1-l012 note] Megi, megu, meida is the transitive breaking or undoing verb. The printed eida principal form is retained, not recast as a modern conjugation.
[c1-l013 >] *¶* Sarauo vchimegu. *Quebrar algum ba-*
[c1-l013 note] Sara wo uchimegu illustrates breaking a dish or bowl. The Portuguese noun is divided ba-cio across the two physical rows.
[c1-l014 >] *cio.*
[c1-l014 note] Cio completes bacio, the vessel broken in the preceding example. The short continuation retains its own line and crop.
[c1-l015] Mego. l, mecago. *Cesto, ou canaſtrinh com*
[c1-l015 note] Mego and mecago name a basket for fruit. The scan restores the period after Mego, the e in Cesto, and com; canaſtrinh visibly ends at h and is not silently expanded to canastrinho.
[c1-l016 >] *que metem fruita, &c.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes the basket definition with the fruit put into it. The source’s fruita spelling is retained.
[c1-l017] Meguma. *Vça animal.*
[c1-l017 note] Meguma is a female bear. Vça is the printed historical spelling in the Portuguese animal gloss and is not changed to modern ursa.
[c1-l018] Megumi, ǔ, uda. *Brotarem as aruores.*
[c1-l018 note] This megumi means trees budding. The first abbreviated principal form visibly bears a caron, while uda is unmarked; that occurrence-level distribution is retained.
[c1-l019] Megumi, u, ǔda. *Soſtentar, ou ter prouiden-*
[c1-l019 note] The homonymous megumi means sustaining, caring for, or benefiting someone. Enlargement shows the caron over the damaged u in ǔda, corroborated by the supplied NINJAL headword record; provisional ida is corrected.
[c1-l020 >] *cia, ou ajudar com beneficios. Vt,* Core
[c1-l020 note] Completes prouidencia and adds helping through benefits. Core begins the example explaining the reason for a ruler’s or benefactor’s action.
[c1-l021 >] tamiuo megumi tamǒniyotte nari. *Cam.*
[c1-l021 note] Kore tami wo megumi tamoo ni yotte nari says this is because he sustained or favored the people. The clearly printed italic Cam. is retained as an unexpanded short label, not rewritten as conjectured Portuguese wording.
[c1-l022 >] *Iſto porque ſoſtentou o pouo.*
[c1-l022 note] The gloss states “this because he sustained the people.” The scan’s uppercase I in Iſto and its abbreviated historical syntax are retained.
[c1-l023] Megumi. *O brotarem as aruores.*
[c1-l023 note] This noun megumi is the budding of trees. The scan reads aruores, correcting provisional araores; it pairs with the budding verb above.
[c1-l024] Megumi. *Prouidencia, ou ſoſtentação, ou*
[c1-l024 note] The second noun megumi is providence, support, or beneficence. It pairs with the sustaining verb rather than with plant growth.
[c1-l025 >] *beneficio.*
[c1-l025 note] Beneficio completes the list of meanings of the second megumi. It is a short Portuguese continuation, not a separate entry.
[c1-l026] Meguraxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer rodear, ou andar*
[c1-l026 note] Megurashi, megurasu, meguraita means making something go round. The aita principal form reflects the historical s-row inflection and is not replaced by a modern -ashita form.
[c1-l027 >] *às voltas. Vt,* Anjo tenuo meguraxi
[c1-l027 note] Completes the circling gloss and introduces an angel making the heavens revolve. The scan supports the grave in às and Anjo rather than provisional Aujo, despite abrasion of the n.
[c1-l028 >] tamǒ. *O Anjo faz andar o ceo em roda.*
[c1-l028 note] Tamoo completes the honorific Japanese predicate. The Portuguese says the angel makes the sky turn; the visible f corrects provisional ſaz. This is the source’s illustrative cosmology.
[c1-l029 >] *¶ Per met.* Facaricotouo meguraſu. *Vr-*
[c1-l029 note] A metaphorical extension is hakarikoto wo megurasu, devising or turning over a plan. Vr- begins the Portuguese verb urdir, to contrive.
[c1-l030 >] *dir, ou parafuſar algũa couſa. ¶* Fara-
[c1-l030 note] Dir completes urdir, followed by parafuſar in the sense of thinking or scheming. Fara- begins the next Japanese expression across the line break.
[c1-l031 >] uatauo meguraſu. *Agaſtarſe muito, ou*
[c1-l031 note] Harawata wo megurasu is glossed as becoming greatly upset or distressed. The visceral Japanese expression is not translated literally as a medical action.
[c1-l032 >] *entriſtecerſe.* S. *¶* Chiyeuo meguraſu.
[c1-l032 note] Completes the distress gloss and closes it with the upright written-register S. Chiye wo megurasu then begins the example of exercising or turning over one’s understanding.
[c1-l033 >] *Para fuſar, ou diſcorrer com o entendimento.*
[c1-l033 note] The Portuguese explains chiye wo megurasu as reasoning or turning a matter over with the understanding. The printed separation Para fuſar is preserved.
[c1-l034] Meguri. *Rodeo.*
[c1-l034 note] Meguri is a circuit or going around, glossed Rodeo. The noun is separately entered before the verb’s principal forms.
[c1-l035] Meguri, u, utta. *Rodear.*
[c1-l035 note] Meguri, meguru, megutta means going around. The doubled t in utta is visible and retained.
[c1-l036] Meguri ai, ǒ, yǒta. *Rodeando encõtrarſe.*
[c1-l036 note] Meguriai concerns meeting in the course of going around. The printed carons in ǒ and yǒta and the space before ai are retained.
[c1-l037] Meguriaxi. *Reuolução, ou volta. Vt,* Tẽ-
[c1-l037 note] Meguriashi denotes a revolution or turn. The example begins with roman Tẽ-, including a clearly printed tilde over e; the provisional mixed italic Te- missed both the mark and the type boundary.
[c1-l038 >] no meguriaxi. *Mouimento circular do ceo.*
[c1-l038 note] No completes Tẽno, referring to the heavens’ circular motion. The Japanese form is read as a compound beginning meguri-, not forced into the kana hint’s contracted meguryashi.
[c1-l039] Meguſuri. *Mezinha dos olhos.*
[c1-l039 note] Megusuri is eye medicine. The long s in the Japanese headword is retained rather than confused with f.
[c1-l040] Meguſuxi. *Medico que cura doença dos olhos.*
[c1-l040 note] Megusushi is a practitioner treating eye disease. The final xi distinguishes the physician term from megusuri, the medicine in the preceding entry.
[c1-l041] Meguuaxe. l, meguuaye. i, Memaje. *Olhar*
[c1-l041 note] Meguwase and meguwaye are equated with Memaje, signaling with the eyes. The synonyms occur in the Japanese headword apparatus and are not marked as Portuguese-embedded terms.
[c1-l042 >] *pera alguem peſtenejando com os olhos como*
[c1-l042 note] The person looks toward someone while blinking. The scan supports peſtenejando, without the extra m in provisional peſtemnejando.
[c1-l043 >] *quem dà ſinal. Vt,* Meguuaxe, l, megu-
[c1-l043 note] The blinking is like giving a signal. The missing long s in ſinal and the grave in dà are restored; Vt introduces the two alternative example forms.
[c1-l044 >] uayeuo ſuru.
[c1-l044 note] Uayeuo completes meguuayeuo, followed by suru. This short Japanese continuation belongs to the eye-signaling example, not to the inscription entry below.
[c1-l045] Mei. *Sinal, ou letras abertas em ferro, ou em*
[c1-l045 note] Mei here means a mark or letters cut into iron or stone. The Portuguese material examples continue on the next row.
[c1-l046 >] *pedra, &c. ¶* Catanano mei. *Nome*
[c1-l046 note] After stone and similar materials, katana no mei introduces the maker’s inscription on a sword. Nome begins the explanation of whose name is engraved.
[c1-l047 >] *do official que fez a Catana com as letras a-*
[c1-l047 note] The name is that of the craftsman who made the sword. Enlargement restores official from provisional ofital; a- begins abertas, continued at the top of column 2.

[c2-l006 >] *Mandado do ſenhor. Vt,* Meiuo vomonzu-
[c2-l006 note] Mei in this sense is the lord’s order. Mei wo omonzuru begins the example of respecting that command; the printed initial vo- is retained.
[c2-l007 >] ru. *Ter reſpeito ao mandado do ſenhor.*
[c2-l007 note] Ru completes omonzuru, followed by the Portuguese respect-for-the-command translation. The entire Ter belongs to italic Portuguese, not a roman T attached to the Japanese.
[c2-l008 >] *¶* Meini xitagǒ. *Obedecer ao mandado*
[c2-l008 note] Mei ni shitagoo is obeying a command. The caron in xitagǒ is visible and retained, and the Portuguese object continues below.
[c2-l009 >] *do ſenhor. ¶* Meini junzuru. *Idem.* S.
[c2-l009 note] Completes the lord’s command and adds mei ni junzuru with the same meaning. The final upright S. marks written usage and is kept outside Portuguese italics.
[c2-l010 >] *¶* Meiuo ſomuqu. *Quebrantar o manda-*
[c2-l010 note] Mei wo somuku is breaking or disobeying the command. The Portuguese noun is divided manda-do at the right edge.
[c2-l011 >] *do do ſenhor.*
[c2-l011 note] Do completes mandado, followed by “of the lord.” This short continuation closes the human-command sense before the heavenly-command entry.
[c2-l012] Mei. i, Tenmei. *Mandado doceo, ou ordena-*
[c2-l012 note] Mei, equated with tenmei, denotes heaven’s command or divine ordinance. The separator after i is a comma in the scan; doceo remains printed together.
[c2-l013 >] *ção diuina.*
[c2-l013 note] Ção completes ordenação, divine ordinance. The two-word continuation belongs to the heavenly-command entry.
[c2-l014] Mei. l, Fǒmei. *Folha do Cha. ¶* Meiuo
[c2-l014 note] Mei and foomei refer here to a tea leaf. The scan has Folha rather than provisional Tolha; the caron in Fǒmei is retained.
[c2-l015 >] niru. *Cozer o Cha.* S.
[c2-l015 note] Mei wo niru means cooking or boiling the tea. The final S. is upright, a written-register label rather than part of the italic gloss.
[c2-l016] Mei. *Sobrinha.*
[c2-l016 note] The homonymous mei means a niece. This short, independent headword is distinct from the tea and command senses above.
[c2-l017] Mei an. Yami curaxi. *Eſcuridade, ou trèuas.*
[c2-l017 note] Meian is paraphrased as yami kurashi and glossed as darkness. The grave in trèuas and the source’s space in Mei an are retained.
[c2-l018] Meiba. *Caualo bom, ou nomeado.*
[c2-l018 note] Meiba is a good or renowned horse. Nomeado here expresses reputation, not merely the act of giving a horse a name.
[c2-l019] Meibo, l, jimorai. *Doença dos olhos, como ter-*
[c2-l019 note] Meibo, alternatively jimorai, names an eye complaint exemplified by a stye. The Portuguese ter- continues as terçol on the following row.
[c2-l020 >] *çol que nace junto das pestanas dos olhos. Vt,*
[c2-l020 note] Çol completes terçol, growing near the eyelashes. The source’s nace spelling and aligned short-s pestanas are retained.
[c2-l021 >] Meiboga deqita. *Naceo me hum terçol*
[c2-l021 note] Meibo ga dekita says that a stye has appeared. The Portuguese switches to a first-person affected-person expression, “a stye developed on me.”
[c2-l022 >] *nos olhos.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes the location “in the eyes.” The short row closes the meibo example and is not merged with the next headword.
[c2-l023] Meibocu. i, Na aru qi. *Aruore excellente,*
[c2-l023 note] Meiboku, paraphrased as na aru ki, is an excellent or celebrated tree. The scan’s qi spelling represents ki and is not modernized.
[c2-l024 >] *ou nomeada.*

[c2-l025] Meibot. Curaxi xizzumu. *Grande eſcuri-*

[c2-l026 >] *dade em que hum esta metido, & ſubmerſo.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes the description of great darkness in which someone is immersed. Esta remains unaccented as transcribed; the scan does not require an added modern accent.
[c2-l027] Meibut. Sugureta mono. *Boa, ou excellen-*
[c2-l027 note] Meibut, paraphrased as sugureta mono, means a good or excellent thing. The final t distinguishes the printed form from modern expanded meibutsu.
[c2-l028 >] *te couſa.*
[c2-l028 note] Te completes excellente, followed by couſa. This small continuation finishes the definition, not a new headword.
[c2-l029] Meica. *Bons verſos, ou cantigas.*
[c2-l029 note] Meica denotes fine verse or songs. Both Portuguese alternatives are retained without narrowing the source’s literary scope.
[c2-l030] Meicai. Aqiracana imaxime. i, *Prohibição,*
[c2-l030 note] Meicai is paraphrased as akirakana imashime, a clear or upright precept. The scan places a roman i, before italic Prohibição, correcting both punctuation and typeface boundary.
[c2-l031 >] *ou mandamento puro, & recto.* S.
[c2-l031 note] Completes the prohibition or pure, upright commandment gloss. The terminal upright S. is the dictionary’s written-register label.
[c2-l032] Meican, l, meǒcan. Aqiracani caganmiru.
[c2-l032 note] Meican and meǒcan introduce the clear-mirror entry. The printed Japanese paraphrase Aqiracani caganmiru is retained despite its unusual verbal-looking shape; it is not silently replaced by cagami.

[c2-l033 note] The example meican kumori nashi states that a clear mirror has no clouding. The scan ends the Japanese with a period, then begins the Portuguese with uppercase italic O, not a roman lowercase o.
[c2-l034 >] *eſpelho claro não tem eſcuridade, ou neuoa.* S.
[c2-l034 note] Completes the clear mirror’s freedom from darkness or mist. The final S. is upright written-register notation; neuoa retains the source’s spelling.
[c2-l035] Meichi. i, Aqiracana chiye. *Saber claro, &*
[c2-l035 note] Meichi is paraphrased as akirakana chiye and glossed as clear, excellent understanding. The Japanese explanatory phrase remains separate from the italic Portuguese.
[c2-l036 >] *excellente.*

[c2-l037] Meichô, l, meǒchô. *Amenhãa.*

[c2-l038] Meichô. Naqu tori. *Paßaro que canta.* S.
[c2-l038 note] This meichô is a singing bird, paraphrased as naku tori. The final upright S. belongs to the dictionary’s usage apparatus.
[c2-l039] Meichô. *Excellente paſſaro.*
[c2-l039 note] The next homonymous meichô is an excellent bird. Its separate entry and the printed paſſaro with two long s characters are preserved.
[c2-l040] Meicǒ. *Excellente calambà.*

[c2-l041] Meicon. *Raiz da vida que ſão os ſentidos.*
[c2-l041 note] Meicon is defined as the root of life, identified with the senses. This is the historical source’s formulation, not a modern physiological claim.
[c2-l042] Meicu. *Excellente ſentença, ou ramo de certos*
[c2-l042 note] Meicu is an excellent saying or portion of a particular kind of verse. The source’s ramo metaphor is retained rather than silently replaced by a technical poetic term.
[c2-l043 >] *verſos. Vt,* Meicuuo faqu. *Falar boas*
[c2-l043 note] Meiku wo haku illustrates uttering good sayings or verses. The example follows Vt and is ordinary Japanese example text, not a Portuguese-embedded typeface candidate.
[c2-l044 >] *ſentenças, ou bõs verſos. ¶* Meicuuo fi-
[c2-l044 note] Completes the good-sayings or good-verses translation. The paragraph sign begins meiku wo hiku, divided fi-qu across the line break.
[c2-l045 >] qu. *Alegar boas ſentenças.*
[c2-l045 note] Qu completes hiku, here citing or adducing good sayings. The line closes the second example under meicu.
[c2-l046] Meicun. *Senhor recto. ¶* Matçurigotono
[c2-l046 note] Meicun means an upright ruler. Matsurigoto no begins the following example concerning the ruler’s governance.
[c2-l047 >] tadaxiqiua, meicunno tocu nari. Xix.
[c2-l047 note] Tadashiki wa, meikun no toku nari states that upright governance is the virtuous quality of a good ruler. The upright Xix. is retained as a source label and marked for whole-term typeface review without speculative expansion.
