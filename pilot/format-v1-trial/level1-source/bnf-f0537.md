[c1-l002 note] Os cabelos o caualo completes the description of shedding or changing the coat. Uma wo tsukuroo introduces tending or treating the horse, explained immediately below.
[c1-l003 >] *Curar ao caualo ſangrandoo, &c. ¶* Vma-
[c1-l003 note] The treatment includes bleeding the horse. The scan supports caualo rather than OCR cauulo; Vma- begins the next example and remains divided at the printed line end.
[c1-l004 >] uo fayamuru. *Dar de eſporas ao caualo, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Uma wo hayamuru means making the horse go faster. The gloss specifies applying the spurs, followed by a second paraphrase on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] *fazelo ir de preßa. ¶* Vmauo tatçuru. *Por*
[c1-l005 note] Fazelo ir de preßa completes the acceleration gloss. Uma wo tatsuru then introduces putting the horse up or stabling it; Por belongs to that new explanation.
[c1-l006 >] *o caualo na estrebaria. ¶* Vmauo fadaqu-
[c1-l006 note] The stable completes the preceding example. Uma wo hadaquru begins the following grooming expression; its final syllable continues below.

[c1-l007 note] Ru completes hadaquru, and coxirayuru is an alternative expression introduced by Latin l. Almofaçar identifies the action as curry-combing or grooming the horse.
[c1-l008 >] *¶* Vmaga agaru. *Porſe o caualo em ge-*
[c1-l008 note] Uma ga agaru describes a horse rearing. The Portuguese phrase porse o caualo em gemeas is divided ge-/meas across the physical rows.
[c1-l009 >] *meas. ¶* Vmaga tçuqe ſumǒ. *Repug-*
[c1-l009 note] Meas completes the rearing description. Uma ga tsuke sumoo introduces a horse resisting its rider mounting; the gloss begins repugnar across the line break.
[c1-l010 >] *nar o caualo quando quer ſubir o caualeiro che-*
[c1-l010 note] The horse resists when the rider tries to mount. Che- begins chegandose, describing how it crowds the rider in the next row.
[c1-l011 >] *gandoſe muito, & afastando ao que quer ca-*
[c1-l011 note] Gandose completes chegandose: the horse comes too close and drives away the person trying to mount. The scan supports o in gandoſe and the f of afastando; the short-s st spelling is retained rather than normalizing this difficult ligature.
[c1-l012 >] *ualgar. ¶* Tçuqevma. *Caualo que tem*
[c1-l012 note] Ualgar completes caualgar. Tsuqeuma names a horse with this obstructive habit, and its Portuguese explanation continues below.
[c1-l013 >] *eſta manha. ¶* Vmaga noquru. *Afaſ*
[c1-l013 note] After finishing the description of that habit, uma ga noquru means the horse moves away. Afaſ continues as tarſe on the next row; no terminal hyphen is supplied without visible ink.
[c1-l014 >] *tarſe o caualo pera não deixar ſubir o caualeiro.*
[c1-l014 note] The horse withdraws so that the rider cannot get on. This completes afastarse and explains the preceding noquru example.
[c1-l015 >] *¶* Noqevma. *Caualo que tem esta manha*
[c1-l015 note] Noqevma names a horse with the just-described habit of backing away from a would-be rider. The scan supplies the omitted paragraph sign and an upright v in the compound; the unpunctuated line ending is retained.
[c1-l016 >] *¶* Vmaga ſoramu. *Aleuantar o caualo a*
[c1-l016 note] Uma ga soramu introduces raising and tossing the head, with the intended consequence for the rider explained in the next two rows.
[c1-l017 >] *cabeça, & abanala pera derrubar o que vai em*
[c1-l017 note] The horse raises and shakes its head to throw the person riding it. The phrase em cima continues on the following physical row.
[c1-l018 >] *cima. ¶* Vmaga vchicomu. l, vchicô
[c1-l018 note] Cima completes the description of the mounted rider. Uma ga uchikomu, alternatively uchikonde yuku across the next row, describes another movement used to unseat him.
[c1-l019 >] de yuqu. *Ir o caualo com a cabeça entras*
[c1-l019 note] De yuqu completes uchikonde yuku. The Portuguese says the horse goes with its head between its legs; the scan supports Ir and the article a before cabeça rather than OCR Er and an omitted article.
[c1-l020 >] *pernas pera derrubar o que vai em cima.*
[c1-l020 note] The horse puts its head between its legs to throw the rider. This closes the long series of horse-handling examples before the new calendrical sense of uma.
[c1-l021] Vma. *Hum dos animaes por onde os Iapões cõ*
[c1-l021 note] Uma is now the Horse of the calendrical animal cycle. Onde is worn but its rounded initial and the following counting explanation support it over OCR cnde; cõ continues into tão below without expanding the abbreviation.
[c1-l022 >] *tão os annos, & as horas. ¶* Vmano cocu.
[c1-l022 note] The animals are used to count years and hours. Uma no koku is the Horse hour; the print has horas, not OCR boras, and the next row gives its approximate limits.
[c1-l023 >] *Das 11. atè ao meo dia, & mais.*
[c1-l023 note] The Horse hour runs from eleven to noon and beyond in the printed explanation. The scan reads 11., ao meo dia, and & mais; the OCR E/r fragment was a failed recognition of the number.
[c1-l024] Vmabixacu. *Coco de tomar agoa pera lauar o*
[c1-l024 note] Umabishaku is a coconut-shell dipper used to take water for washing a horse. The definition supplies its function without requiring a specific vessel shape beyond coco.
[c1-l025 >] *caualo.*
[c1-l025 note] Caualo completes the purpose of the dipper: washing the horse. This short line belongs to the preceding entry.
[c1-l026] Vmabune. *Mangedoura do caualo.*
[c1-l026 note] Umabune is the horse's manger. Enlargement supports the ending une rather than OCR uite, while the b is heavily abraded. The supplied NINJAL Entry Words Data of Nippojisho (Hideyuki Ohshima and Taichi Aida, version 202510, CC BY 4.0; https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/textdb_dataset/en/nipp/index.html), consulted after the independent reading, corroborates Vmabune; this remains a qualified scan reading, not a copied diplomatic authority.
[c1-l027] Vmacata. *Almocreue.*
[c1-l027 note] Umakata is the person who handles or transports goods with horses, matched by almocreue. The Portuguese is an occupational noun, not an adjective describing a horse.
[c1-l028] Vmadaqei. *Ser o caualo brioſo, ou que logo*
[c1-l028 note] Umadaqei describes a spirited horse. Logo, visible at the end of the line, introduces its immediate reaction on seeing other horses; OCR hoeo was not a coherent gloss.
[c1-l029 >] *ſe encreſpa, ou rincha vendo a outros. ¶* V-
[c1-l029 note] The horse bristles or neighs on seeing others. The damaged but legible encreſpa restores the Portuguese verb; V- begins the Japanese example on the next indented row.
[c1-l030 >] madaqei vmagia. *He caualo brioſo, &c.*
[c1-l030 note] V- plus madaqei forms the same adjective as the headword, followed by uma ja: it is a spirited horse. The scan supports madaqei vmagia rather than OCR madiqeivmagia.
[c1-l031] Vmafada. *Esteira großa, & branda que poẽ*
[c1-l031 note] Umafada names a thick, soft mat placed beneath a saddle. Esteira is damaged in the middle but the surviving letters and the following placement support the mat sense rather than OCR Esioro.
[c1-l032 >] *de baixo da ſela do caualo.*
[c1-l032 note] De baixo da sela do caualo completes the saddle-pad definition. The visible word division de baixo is retained.
[c1-l033] Vmafiqi. *Almocreue, ou moço que leua o*
[c1-l033 note] Umahiki is a horse-leader or attendant, compared with an almocreue. The visible initial V and the occupational gloss correct OCR Nmafiqi and Al nocreue; the sentence continues with the means of leading.
[c1-l034 >] *caualo pollo cabreſto, ou pollas rideas.*
[c1-l034 note] The attendant leads the horse by its halter or reins. Caualo replaces the OCR vowel error cauulo; the worn spelling rideas is retained.
[c1-l035] Vmagaqe. *O ir pella posta, ou correndo a ca-*
[c1-l035 note] Umagaqe denotes riding post or going at speed on horseback. The scan has the initial V omitted by OCR and the Portuguese phrase O ir pella posta, ou correndo.
[c1-l036 >] *ualo.* Vmagaqete yuqu. *Idem. Mas*
[c1-l036 note] Ualo completes caualo. Umagaqete yuku is the corresponding verbal expression; Idem repeats the meaning, and Mas begins a usage qualification.
[c1-l037 >] *não he muito vſado.*
[c1-l037 note] The expression is said not to be very commonly used. The negative is printed não, not the OCR nũo, and the historical form vſado is retained.
[c1-l038] Vmaguinu. *Manta do caualo.*
[c1-l038 note] Umaguinu is the horse's blanket. The concise gloss manta do caualo provides the whole meaning of this entry.
[c1-l039] Vmaguxi. *Pentem com que penteão a coma*
[c1-l039 note] Umaguxi is a comb used to groom the mane. The Portuguese coma refers to the horse's mane; it is not a person's hair in this context.
[c1-l040 >] *do caualo.*
[c1-l040 note] Do caualo completes the mane-comb definition, specifying the animal. This short continuation is distinct from the adjective umai below.
[c1-l041] Vmai. *Couſa gostoſa, ou ſabroſa.*
[c1-l041 note] Umai means pleasant-tasting or savoury, matching gostoſa and ſabroſa. The scan contains Vmai without the spurious n introduced by OCR.
[c1-l042 >] Vmaſi .
[c1-l042 note] Umaſi is the corresponding adjective in its -shi form, sharing the preceding definition of something tasty. It occupies its own indented line and has no separately repeated Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l043 >] Vmǒ.
[c1-l043 note] Vmǒ is the contracted adverbial form of the tasty adjective, grouped with umai and umashi. The visible caron belongs to o; the OCR extra na is absent.
[c1-l044] Vmaſacari. *Melius,* Vmazacari. *Lamaçel: ou*
[c1-l044 note] Vmaſacari is followed by Melius and the preferred form Vmazacari, then a definition of a mudhole. The scan supports c and the complete Melius. The tall glyph is retained as ſ: it lacks the right crossbar of nearby f types. The supplied NINJAL index instead gives Vmajacari and reports an apparent f interpreted as an inverted j; that editorial interpretation is not silently substituted for this copy's ambiguous glyph.
[c1-l045 >] *atoleiro por onde cuſtumão paßar os caualos dei*
[c1-l045 note] The mudhole is a place habitually crossed by horses. Cuſtumão has a visible t absent from the OCR; dei continues into xãdo below without adding an unprinted line-end hyphen.
[c1-l046 >] *xãdo huas couas, ou poças em que todos metẽ*
[c1-l046 note] Dei followed by xãdo explains that the horses leave hollows or puddles into which they put their feet, continued at the top of column 2. The scan supports xãdo and poças; the abraded huas is retained without adding an uncertain nasal mark.

[c2-l001 >] *os pès ſacudindo a lama, ou agoa.*

[c2-l004] Vma mauarixu. *Criados honrados dalgum ſe-*

[c2-l006] Vmamuxa. *Soldados de caualo, ou que vão*
[c2-l006 note] Umamusha are mounted soldiers. Soldados has a damaged d but is a single Portuguese word; the OCR split Sol lados is unsupported.
[c2-l007 >] *a caualo.*
[c2-l007 note] A caualo completes the alternative explanation: soldiers who go on horseback. It remains a short continuation of umamusha.
[c2-l008] Vmanori. *Caualeiro, ou o que vai a caualo,*
[c2-l008 note] Umanori means a rider, someone on horseback. The Portuguese continues with a further evaluative sense on the next row.
[c2-l009 >] *ou bom caualeiro.*
[c2-l009 note] Bom caualeiro adds the sense of a skilled rider. The definition thus covers both a rider generally and a good horseman.
[c2-l010] Vmanorinoxu. *Os que vão a caualo, ou gen-*
[c2-l010 note] Umanori no shu refers collectively to people on horseback or mounted troops. The Portuguese gente is divided gen-/te at the line break.
[c2-l011 >] *te de caualo.*
[c2-l011 note] Te completes gente de caualo, the mounted group. This closes the collective entry before the dust raised by horses is introduced.
[c2-l012] Vmaqemuri. *Poeira que fazem muitos caua-*
[c2-l012 note] Umagemuri is dust raised by many horses. The Portuguese caualos continues onto the next physical row.
[c2-l013 >] *los que vão juntos. ¶* Vmaqemuriuo ta-
[c2-l013 note] The horses are travelling together. Umagemuri wo tatete tatakoo begins the example of fighting while raising clouds of horse-dust, with tatete divided at ta-.
[c2-l014 >] tete tatacǒ. *Pelejar com grande eſtrondo,*
[c2-l014 note] Tete completes tatete before tatacǒ, fighting. The scan supports final e rather than OCR teto; the Portuguese emphasizes the noise and commotion of battle.
[c2-l015 >] *& poeira.*
[c2-l015 note] Poeira completes the battle gloss: great noise and dust. The visible second vowel is e, correcting OCR pocira.
[c2-l016] Vmare. *Nacimento.*
[c2-l016 note] Umare is the noun for birth. The worn Portuguese Nacimento fits the noun and the adjacent verb nacer; its c is faint, but there is no basis for retaining OCR Narimento.
[c2-l017] Vmare, uru, eta. *Nacer.*
[c2-l017 note] Umare, umaruru, umareta gives the verb for being born, paired with Portuguese nacer. The historical principal forms remain as printed rather than being converted to modern umareru.
[c2-l018] Vmareai. *Combinação dalgũas peßoas na natu-*
[c2-l018 note] Umareai names agreement or compatibility between people in their inborn nature. The visible e in the headword replaces OCR c, and the gloss continues natureza below.
[c2-l019 >] *reza, ou condição.*
[c2-l019 note] Reza completes natureza, followed by condição: the compatibility is of nature or disposition. The scan supports condição without the spurious extra l in OCR condlição.
[c2-l020] Vmareai, ǒ, ǒta. *Nacer em conjunção dal-*
[c2-l020 note] The verb umareai means being born under a particular conjunction or at a particular time. Its abbreviated principal parts ǒ and ǒta are retained, including the carons.
[c2-l021 >] *gum planeta, ou em algum tempo notauel. Vt,*
[c2-l021 note] The definition refers to a planet's conjunction or another notable time. Vt introduces a religious example of fortunate timing at birth.
[c2-l022 >] Govoqiteno firomaru jibunni vmarevǒ.
[c2-l022 note] Go-oqite no hiromaru jibun ni umareau means happening to be born when the divine law is spreading, as the Portuguese explains. The unusual printed vmarevǒ is retained; it should not be regularized from the headword's abbreviated ǒ. The stroke between e and ǒ is partly abraded, so the retained v remains uncertain.
[c2-l023 >] *Acertar de nacer em tẽpo que a ley de Deos ſe*
[c2-l023 note] The translation describes being born during the dissemination of the law of God. Tẽpo preserves the abbreviation tilde, while ley and Deos retain the source spelling.
[c2-l024 >] *diuulga.*
[c2-l024 note] Diuulga completes the statement that God's law is becoming known. It is a continuation of the example, not a new headword.
[c2-l025] Vmarecauari, ru, atta. *Tornar a nacer outra*
[c2-l025 note] Umarecauari means being born again or differently. The principal form ru is fully visible, correcting the OCR truncated r; the definition continues below.
[c2-l026 >] *vez differente do que era, ou nacer em outra*
[c2-l026 note] Rebirth is described as becoming different from what one was or being born in another form. The phrase em outra introduces figura on the following row.
[c2-l027 >] *figura, ou ſuſtancia. ¶ Item, No* X*i-*
[c2-l027 note] Figura and ſuſtancia complete the first rebirth sense. The entry then introduces a regional use in Ximo, with the place name divided Xi-/mo across the physical rows; its capital X is visible. The lowercase i and mo are italic, while the less secure upright-looking X retains the baseline font treatment.
[c2-l028 >] *mo, nacer ſomenos, ou inferior nos eſpiritos,*
[c2-l028 note] Mo completes Ximo. In that region the word can mean being born inferior in spirit or abilities; the comparison with the father continues below. The second pass identified the visible right crossbar in inferior, correcting OCR inſerior.
[c2-l029 >] *& partes ao pay. No Cami ſe diz* Vmare
[c2-l029 note] The child is inferior to the father in qualities. The Cami usage is then given as umare votoru across two lines; Vmare contains no extra n despite the OCR Vmnare.
[c2-l030 >] votoru.
[c2-l030 note] Votoru completes umare votoru, the contrasting regional expression for being born inferior. It is the quoted expression governed by No Cami se diz above.
[c2-l031] Vmaredachi. *Nacido de pouco, ou logo em*
[c2-l031 note] Umaredachi means newly born or immediately upon birth. The two Portuguese paraphrases continue with nacendo on the next row.
[c2-l032 >] *nacendo. Vt,* Vmaredachini yagate fito-
[c2-l032 note] Nacendo completes logo em nacendo. Umaredachini yagate fito-ni begins an example of handing the newborn to another person to be raised.
[c2-l033 >] ni yaxinauaxeta. *Logo depois de nacer*
[c2-l033 note] Ni yashinawaseta completes the causative example: having someone else raise the newborn. The Portuguese begins the explicit temporal paraphrase, immediately after birth.
[c2-l034 >] *o dei a criar a outro. ¶* Vmaredachino
[c2-l034 note] The translation says I gave the child to another to raise. Umaredachino then starts a second expression, modifying ko on the following line.
[c2-l035 >] co. *Criança nacida de pouco.*

[c2-l036] Vmaregauari. *O nacer outra vez neſte mun-*
[c2-l036 note] Umaregawari is rebirth into this world. The scan includes neſte before the divided mundo, correcting the OCR omission of n in eſte.

[c2-l037 note] The reborn being has a different form or substance. The gloss is reporting a belief, with ſegundo introducing its attribution below. The final a of ſuſtancia is worn; its small right foot favors a over OCR o, but this remains a qualified a/o reading.

[c2-l038 note] The compilers attribute this belief to gentios and illustrate it with Shaka, printed Xaca. The visible i restores gentios; the statement is the source's account of rebirth rather than an editorial assertion of its truth.
[c2-l039 >] *tornaße agora a nacer transformado em al-*

[c2-l040 >] *gum Bonzo.*
[c2-l040 note] Gum completes algum Bonzo: the rebirth would be as a Buddhist monk. Bonzo is the Portuguese term in the explanation, not a separately quoted Japanese headword.
[c2-l041] Vmaregayeri. *O degenerar, ou não ſair à*
[c2-l041 note] Umaregayeri describes degeneration or failure to take after the parents. The OCR extra n after não is stray ink; the line-final à has a visible accent and introduces caſta below.
[c2-l042 >] *caſta dos pays. Vt,* Vmaregayerini natta.
[c2-l042 note] The child does not match the parents' stock or qualities. Umaregayerini natta is the example; its repeated gayeri has e, not the OCR gayori, and the terminal mark is read as a period.
[c2-l043 >] *Ficar algum filho de homem, ou de animal in-*

[c2-l044 >] *ferior aos pays.*
[c2-l044 note] Ferior aos pays completes inferior to the parents. This closes the negative contrast before the next entry describes a child surpassing them.
[c2-l045] Vmaremaxi, ſu, ita. *Nacer auentajado, ou*
[c2-l045 note] Umaremaxi is being born with an advantage or superior qualities. Its -ita principal form is retained as historical morphology, and the gloss continues the comparison below.
[c2-l046 >] *milhor q̃ outro como o filho milhor que o pay,*
[c2-l046 note] The example is a son better than his father. Milhor and the abbreviated q̃ are preserved as printed, and the comma continues into &c. below.
[c2-l047 >] *&c.*
[c2-l047 note] The previously omitted &c. finishes the comparison of a child with the father. The signature and catchword sharing this baseline are recorded separately as page furniture, so neither is inserted into the running definition.
