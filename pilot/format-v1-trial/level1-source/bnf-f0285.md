[c1-l001 >] *arſe facilmente à virtude, ou ſer leuado facil-*
[c1-l001 note] Continues a verb from the preceding page: readily turning to virtue, or being led by it. The opening arſe is partly obscured by heavy ink; it is retained as a divided continuation, not expanded into a conjectural whole verb.
[c1-l002 >] *mente da virtude.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes facil-mente and the description of being easily led by virtue. This short continuation belongs to the preceding entry.
[c1-l003] Ienyǔ. Yoqi tomo. *Bõs companheiros, ou boa*
[c1-l003 note] Zenyuu is explained by yoki tomo, good companions; the Portuguese offers good companions or good company. The caron over u and abbreviation tilde in Bõs are distinct marks.
[c1-l004 >] *companhia.*
[c1-l004 note] Companhia completes boa companhia, the collective alternative to individual good companions.
[c1-l005] Ienzai. Yoqi tacara. *Boas riquezas, ou boa,*
[c1-l005 note] Zenzai is here good treasure or wealth, as yoki takara and boas riquezas both indicate. It is distinct from the exclamation in the following entry.
[c1-l006 >] *& rica couſa.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes the alternative gloss as a good and valuable thing; rica qualifies couſa, rather than beginning a new definition.
[c1-l007] Ienzai. Ycqi cana. *O boa couſa. S.*
[c1-l007 note] This zenzai is an approving exclamation, yoki kana, translated as an exclamation over something good. The scan and aligned OCR read Ycqi, although the sense calls for yoki. This apparent c may be a damaged o; the anomalous printed-looking form is retained rather than silently repaired. S. is the upright written-register label.
[c1-l008] Ienzô. i, Ienxǔno ſô. *Religioſo, ou Bon-*
[c1-l008 note] Zenzoo is a monk of the Zen school, explicitly identified by Zen-shuu no soo. Bon- begins Portuguese Bonzo on the next row; the printed vowel marks are not standardized to one shape.
[c1-l009 >] *zo da ſeita dos Ienxùs.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes Bonzo and specifies the Zen sect. Ienxùs is the sect-name embedded in the Portuguese explanation, with a Portuguese plural ending; enlargement supports a grave over u here, unlike the caron in the preceding roman example.
[c1-l010] Ieſsǒ. Xitano catachi. *Lingua. S.*
[c1-l010 note] Zessoo is explained literally as the tongue's form and glossed simply as tongue. The printed mixed long and short s pair in Ieſsǒ is retained; S. is an upright register label.
[c1-l011] Iet. Xita. *Lingua.*
[c1-l011 note] Zet is the Sino-Japanese tongue element, matched to shita and Portuguese lingua. Its final t is preserved rather than expanded to a modern open syllable.
[c1-l012] Ietcot. *Certo lugar dos pees onde ſe dão botões*
[c1-l012 note] Zekkot denotes a particular site at the feet where cautery is applied. The Portuguese visibly has pees, not the OCR's pes; the definition describes the historical treatment rather than giving advice.
[c1-l013 >] *de fogo.*
[c1-l013 note] De fogo completes botões de fogo, the cautery expression explaining the site named in the preceding row.
[c1-l014] Ietgacu. *Deixar, ou interromper o eſtudo.*
[c1-l014 note] Zetgaku means abandoning or interrupting study. This first explanation is followed by a more specific sense concerning completed learning.
[c1-l015 >] *¶ Item, Deixar de eſtudar depois de ſe con-*
[c1-l015 note] The second sense is ceasing to study after becoming accomplished; con- continues as ſumar below. The paragraph mark introduces another sense of the same headword.
[c1-l016 >] *ſumar no eſtudo, ou letras.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes con-ſumar: attaining accomplishment in study or letters before stopping. The scan supports m in ſumar and t in letras, correcting the OCR's ſunar and leiras.
[c1-l017] Ietguen. Cotobauo tayaſu. i, Gongo dǒdã.
[c1-l017 note] Zetgen is explained as cutting off words and as gongo doodan, the exhaustion of verbal expression. The initial of Gongo is G, not OCR C; the final nasal remains abbreviated by the tilde.
[c1-l018 >] *O não poder explicar com palauras. S.*
[c1-l018 note] The gloss specifies inability to explain in words, establishing the intended sense of the Japanese expressions above. S. remains outside the italic prose.
[c1-l019] Ietmet. Taye foroburu. *Deſtruição total. S*.
[c1-l019 note] Zetmet, with tae horoburu, means complete extinction or destruction. Enlargement supports foroburu, not OCR fotoburu. The damaged Portuguese word is read as Deſtruição in agreement with total and the Japanese; the upright S. marks written usage.
[c1-l020] Ietmiô. *Couſa marauilhoſa, & que não ſe po-*
[c1-l020 note] Zetmyoo here denotes something wonderful beyond verbal explanation. Its circumflex distinguishes the printed headword from the caroned form in the next entry.
[c1-l021 >] *de explicar com palauras.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes po-de and the claim that the wonderful thing cannot be expressed in words.
[c1-l022] Ietmiǒ. *Principio de deſtruição. Vt,* Co-
[c1-l022 note] The distinct caroned zetmyoo is glossed as the beginning of destruction. Co- begins the demonstrative cono in the example below, not a Portuguese word.
[c1-l023 >] no vazauaiua cano fitono tameni jetmiǒ-
[c1-l023 note] With Co- above, the example says that this calamity is a beginning of ruin for that person. The caron in jetmiǒ- agrees with this entry, not the previous wonderful-thing sense.
[c1-l024 >] gia. *Eſte trabalho he principio da perdição*
[c1-l024 note] Già completes the Japanese assertion; the Portuguese calls the trouble the beginning of the person's ruin. The small i and the accent over a support già rather than OCR gùa.
[c1-l025 >] *daquelle homem.*
[c1-l025 note] Specifies whose ruin is meant: that man's. This completes the example from the preceding three physical lines.
[c1-l026] Ietnai. *Pronunciação. Vt,* Ietnaino varui
[c1-l026 note] Zetnai is pronunciation; the example begins with someone whose pronunciation is poor. The headword has n, matching Ietnaino, and the Portuguese noun has its final o. The subsequently consulted supplied NINJAL index likewise identifies Ietnai.
[c1-l027 >] fito. l, yoi fitogia. H*ehomem de mà, ou*
[c1-l027 note] Hito completes the poor-pronunciation alternative; yoi hito ja supplies the good-pronunciation alternative. The tightly printed Hehomem is retained rather than silently respaced.
[c1-l028 >] *boa pronunciação.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes the Portuguese contrast between bad and good pronunciation; boa coordinates with mà on the preceding row.
[c1-l029] Iet ri. Taye fanaruru. i, Gongono tayetaru
[c1-l029 note] Zetri is explained through separation or cessation, specifically the exhaustion of words. The visible internal space in Iet ri is retained without treating ri as an independent entry.
[c1-l030 >] coto. *Falta de palauras, como pera explicar*
[c1-l030 note] Coto closes the Japanese explanatory phrase. The Portuguese defines a lack of words, for example when trying to explain something.
[c1-l031 >] *algũa couſa.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the explanatory example with some thing that cannot be put into words. The tilde is retained over u in algũa.
[c1-l032] Iet rin. i, ſugureta coto. *Couſa excellente.*
[c1-l032 note] Zetrin means something outstanding, matching sugureta koto and excellent thing. The headword's visible internal spacing is preserved.
[c1-l033] Iet riǒ. i, Cocoronimo voyobanu. *Cou-*
[c1-l033 note] Zetryoo denotes something beyond mental reach, as kokoro ni mo oyobanu states. The damaged capital is I, not OCR B; the spaced headword and caron are preserved.
[c1-l034 >] *ſa que não ſe alcança com o entendimento. Vt,*
[c1-l034 note] Completes Cou-ſa and explains that the understanding cannot reach it. Vt introduces the combined verbal-and-mental example below.
[c1-l035 >] Ietguen jetrionari. *Não ſe pode explicar*
[c1-l035 note] The example combines zetgen with zetryoo: neither expressible in words nor mentally comprehensible. The unaccented jetrionari is retained as printed rather than regularized to the headword's mark.
[c1-l036 >] *com palauras, nẽ alcançar com o entendimẽto.*
[c1-l036 note] Completes the paired limitations: not explainable in words and not graspable by the understanding. Both nẽ and entendimẽto preserve abbreviation tildes.
[c1-l037] Iettǒ. Taye tauoruru. i, Cuzzure fateta co
[c1-l037 note] Zettoo is glossed as complete collapse, with kuzure hateta koto divided across the row boundary. There is no supplied hyphen after co: the right edge shows the divided syllable without one.
[c1-l038 >] to. *Couſa totalmente destruida.*
[c1-l038 note] To completes coto, and the Portuguese describes a thing completely destroyed. The existing short-s st transcription is retained pending the project's separate ligature audit.
[c1-l039] Iettô. i, Xitano ſaqi. *Ponta da lingua. Vt,*
[c1-l039 note] The circumflexed zettoo is the tip of the tongue, explicitly shita no saki. Its mark and meaning distinguish it from the preceding destruction entry.
[c1-l040 >] Iettôni noberarenu cotonari. *He couſa q̃*
[c1-l040 note] The example describes something that cannot be uttered with the tongue. Portuguese q̃ is an abbreviated que and is not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l041 >] *não ſe pode explicar com palauras. S.*
[c1-l041 note] Completes the translation as something that cannot be explained in words. The written-register S. is upright, not part of the italic sentence.
[c1-l042] Iet von. Xita coye. *Voz dos que falão.*
[c1-l042 note] Zet-on concerns the voice of speakers, explained by shita and koe. The printed space in Iet von is retained rather than joining the headword editorially.
[c1-l043] Ietxi, ſuru. Tayuru. l, tatçu. *Cortar, ou*
[c1-l043 note] Zetshi, zetsuru means cutting off or interrupting, compared with tayuru and tatsu. The abbreviated principal form ſuru is not a separate Portuguese word.
[c1-l044 >] *interromper. Vt,* Xoxinuo jetſuru. *Ha*
[c1-l044 note] Completes the interruption gloss and introduces shoshin wo zetsuru, breaking off correspondence. Ha begins the Portuguese explanation of a long lapse in writing.
[c1-l045 >] *muito tempo que não vos viſito por carta.*
[c1-l045 note] Explains the example pragmatically: it has been a long time since writing to the addressee. Viſito por carta means a visit by letter, not a physical visit.
[c1-l046 >] *¶* Gongouo jetſuru. *Ceßar de falar, ou*
[c1-l046 note] The visible paragraph mark introduces gongo wo zetsuru, ceasing speech. It was omitted by the OCR; the alternative explanation continues on the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *não poder falar, & de clarar algũa couſa.*
[c1-l047 note] The second explanation is inability to speak or explain something. The printed internal gap in de clarar is retained rather than silently modernizing the spacing.

[c2-l012 initial=2] II. Tera. *Varela, ou moſteiro. Mas naõſe vſa*
[c2-l012 note] Ji here means a temple or monastery. The large initial I and the following capital I together form printed II.; the OCR omitted the second character. The statement about use only in compounds continues below.
[c2-l013 >] *ſem algũa compoſiçaõ. Vt* Iichǔ, jinai,
[c2-l013 note] The temple element occurs in compounds, illustrated by jichuu and jinai. The tilde over the final o of compoſiçaõ is retained, independently of algũa's u-tilde.
[c2-l014 >] jiriǒ.
[c2-l014 note] Jiryoo, temple land or holdings, is the third compound illustrating the temple element. It is a short continuation, not a separate entry.
[c2-l015] Ii. Toqi. *Hora: não ſe vſa ſem algũa compo-*
[c2-l015 note] Ji here is time, corresponding to toki and hora. The initial is capital I followed by lowercase i; the restriction to compounds continues below.
[c2-l016 >] *ſição. Vt,* Iiji coccocuni. *Cada hora, ou*
[c2-l016 note] Completes compo-ſição and illustrates jiji kokkoku ni, at every hour or moment. The Portuguese gives each hour and a broader alternative below.
[c2-l017 >] *em todo tempo.*

[c2-l018] Ii. i, Iitai. *Repugnancia. Vt,* Guioiuo
[c2-l018 note] Ji here is refusal or resistance, identified with jitai. The example begins gyoi wo, a superior's will or command; the damaged headword initial is I, not OCR F.
[c2-l019 >] jiſuru. *Repugnar ao que lhe mandão.*
[c2-l019 note] Jisuru completes the example of resisting what one is ordered to do. The bilingual context confirms refusal rather than the adjacent time or letter senses of ji.
[c2-l020] Ii. *Letra. Vt,* Iiuaqega miyenu. *Não*
[c2-l020 note] Ji here is a written character. Jiwake ga miyenu means the writing cannot be distinguished; the Portuguese has Letra, not OCR Leira.
[c2-l021 >] *ſe pode ler eſta letra.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes the translation: this character or handwriting cannot be read. The negative Não belongs to the preceding physical line.
[c2-l022] Ii. Chigo. *Minino. Vſaſe em compoſição de*
[c2-l022 note] Ji denotes a child, with chigo as the Japanese equivalent and minino in Portuguese. The following qualification concerns use in compounds.
[c2-l023 >] *outras ſyllabas, ou vocabulos. Vt,* Iixi.
[c2-l023 note] Explains combination with other syllables or words, illustrated by jishi. The visible y in ſyllabas replaces OCR o; Iixi begins with capital I, not lowercase l.
[c2-l024 >] *Idem.* Yeiji. i, Midorico. *Idem.*
[c2-l024 note] The first Idem refers back to child; yeiji, explained as midoriko, supplies another infant term. Both Idem labels belong to the italic explanatory apparatus.
[c2-l025] Ii. Tçuuamono. *Soldados, ou gente de guer-*
[c2-l025 note] Ji here denotes warriors or soldiers, matched by tsuwamono. Guer- begins the Portuguese word for war, completed on the next row.
[c2-l026 >] *ra. Vt,* Yumiǒno jiuo mexiguſu. *Le-*
[c2-l026 note] Completes guerra and illustrates taking valiant soldiers along. The printed Yumiǒ spelling and caron are retained; Le- begins leuar in the translation.
[c2-l027 >] *uar conſigo ſoldados valentes.*
[c2-l027 note] Completes Le-uar and specifies bringing brave soldiers with oneself. The initial in ſoldados is long s, not the OCR's f.
[c2-l028] Ii. Coto. *Couſa. Vt,* Ano fito jiga nara-
[c2-l028 note] Ji is a matter or thing, matched to koto. The example ano hito ji ga naranu is explained idiomatically as lack of means rather than translated word for word.
[c2-l029 >] nu. *Aquelle homem he pobre, ou não tem*
[c2-l029 note] Nu completes naranu; the Portuguese says that man is poor or lacks something, with the object supplied below.
[c2-l030 >] *poße.*

[c2-l031] Iiai. Mizzucara aiſuru. *A mimar, & mo-*
[c2-l031 note] Jiai is here love or care for oneself, explicitly mizukara aisuru. The Portuguese more generally says to cherish and show affection; that difference is not corrected editorially.
[c2-l032 >] *ſtrar amor.*

[c2-l033] Iiai. Itçucuximi aiſuru. *A mimar com grã-*
[c2-l033 note] This jiai is tender, compassionate affection, distinguished by itsukushimi aisuru. The Portuguese describes cherishing with great affection and love; grã- continues below.
[c2-l034 >] *de affeito, & amor.*

[c2-l035] Iian. Tera, iuori. *Ermida.*

[c2-l036] Iibacu. Mizzucara xibaru. *Atarſe, ou a-*

[c2-l038 >] *mortificação.*

[c2-l039] Iibai. Mizzucara vru. *O venderſe a ſi meſ-*
[c2-l039 note] Jibai means selling oneself, matching mizukara uru and venderſe. The worn middle consonant of vru is read as r rather than OCR i; initial v represents the vowel in this Japanese verb.
[c2-l040 >] *mo. Vt,* Iibai jitocu. *Venderſe aßi meſ-*
[c2-l040 note] Completes meſ-mo and illustrates jibai jitoku: selling oneself and personally obtaining the proceeds. The second meſ- continues below.
[c2-l041 >] *mo, & aproueitarſe do preço.*
[c2-l041 note] Completes the example as benefiting oneself from the price. The scan supports aproueitarſe, correcting OCR aproueitatſe.
[c2-l042] Iibo. Itçucuximu faua. i, Iifino faua. *Mãy*
[c2-l042 note] Jibo is a loving or compassionate mother, explained by itsukushimu hawa and jihi no hawa. The historical faua is retained, not modernized to haha; Mãy begins the Portuguese noun phrase.
[c2-l043 >] *piadoſa, ou amoroſa.*
[c2-l043 note] Describes the mother as compassionate or loving, completing Mãy above. The reading piadoſa follows the damaged but compatible letters, not a new lexical item.
[c2-l044] Iibocu. Mimi, me. *Orelhas, & olho. Vt,*
[c2-l044 note] Jiboku denotes ears and eyes, explicitly mimi and me. The example continues on the next page, whose initial Fito is anticipated by the separate catchword below; it is not added to this body line.
