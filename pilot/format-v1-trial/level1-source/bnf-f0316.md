[c1-l001] Mendan. i, Gigini cataru. *O falar preſen-*
[c1-l001 note] Mendan denotes speaking face to face. The paraphrase visibly reads Gigini cataru; its g spelling is retained rather than changed to suit an expected modern reading. Preſen- continues below.
[c1-l002 >] *cialmente, ou vendoſe. Vt,* Mendanuo to-
[c1-l002 note] Cialmente completes preſencialmente, reinforced by vendoſe, seeing one another. Mendanuo to- begins the example whose verb ends on the next row.
[c1-l003 >] guru. *Vendoſe falar entre ſi.*
[c1-l003 note] Guru completes toguru in Mendanuo toguru. The translation describes speaking together in person, not merely seeing someone at a distance.
[c1-l004] Menden. *Idem.*
[c1-l004 note] The first Menden has Mendan’s meaning by Idem. It remains distinct from the following homograph about land revenue.
[c1-l005] Menden. *Varzeas que ſe dão ſem pagar todo*
[c1-l005 note] The second Menden denotes fields granted without the full yield or revenue payment. Varzeas identifies the land; todo governs the continuation below.
[c1-l006 >] *o rendimento.*
[c1-l006 note] O rendimento completes the payment not owed in full. The statement concerns a remitted land obligation, not a lack of crops.
[c1-l007] Mendori. *Meliùs,* Medori. *Aue femea.*
[c1-l007 note] Mendori is a female bird; italic Meliùs recommends Medori as preferable. The two Japanese forms are alternatives rather than one compound.
[c1-l008] Mendǒna. *Couſa que não he pera ver, mal fei-*
[c1-l008 note] Mendǒna is something not fit to look at or badly made. This stated sense is retained instead of the familiar modern “troublesome”; fei- continues below.
[c1-l009 >] *ta, &c.*
[c1-l009 note] Ta completes mal feita, badly made, followed by &c. This brief row remains the continuation of Mendǒna.
[c1-l010] Mendǒni. *Adu.*
[c1-l010 note] Mendǒni is explicitly labelled an adverb and depends on the preceding adjective. Adu. belongs to the italic grammatical apparatus.
[c1-l011] Meneco. *Gata.*
[c1-l011 note] Meneco is a female cat, matched by Gata. Initial me- is meaningful and is not corrected to the unqualified Neco.
[c1-l012] Mengio. Yuruxi nozoqu. *Perdão ou priuilegio.*
[c1-l012 note] Mengio is pardon or privilege, paraphrased Yuruxi nozoqu. The wording covers exemption or release as well as forgiveness; the gi spelling remains unchanged.
[c1-l013 >] *Vt,* Mengio ſuru. *Perdoar, ou dar licen-*
[c1-l013 note] Mengio ſuru means granting pardon, permission, or a privilege. The Portuguese licen- continues as ça below.
[c1-l014 >] *ça, & priuilegio.*
[c1-l014 note] Ça completes licença. Priuilegio has i rather than the OCR’s extra l and agrees with the visible form in the headword gloss.
[c1-l015] Mengiǔ. *Canga de ſeda da China.*
[c1-l015 note] A clear caron over final u gives Mengiǔ, not unmarked Mengiu. The gloss identifies a Chinese silk fabric, with Canga used as a textile designation here.
[c1-l016] Menjen. Vomoteno maye. *Diante, ou em*
[c1-l016 note] Menjen means before someone or in their presence, paraphrased Vomoteno maye. The Portuguese preſença continues below.
[c1-l017 >] *preſença.* S.
[c1-l017 note] Preſença completes the locution, followed by upright S. for written usage. The label is separate from the italic gloss.
[c1-l018] Menji, zuru, ita. *Perdoar. ¶* Giǔqua
[c1-l018 note] Menji, zuru, ita supplies the principal forms of a verb meaning pardon. Giǔqua begins an example concerning a grave offence.
[c1-l019 >] ua vonruni menzu. Feiq. *Lib. 1. Per-*
[c1-l019 note] The grave offence is remitted by commutation to distant exile. Feiq. is an upright citation label; Lib. 1. and Per- belong to the italic reference and translation.
[c1-l020 >] *dou o grande crime commutando lho em deſter*
[c1-l020 note] Dou completes Perdou. The Portuguese explains commutation of the great crime to exile; deſter continues below without a securely visible hyphen.
[c1-l021 >] *ro. ¶* Sonatani menjite yuruſu. *Por a-*
[c1-l021 note] Ro completes deſterro. Sonatani menjite yuruſu means forgiving for the addressee’s sake; a- begins Portuguese amor below.
[c1-l022 >] *mor de vos perdoo.*
[c1-l022 note] Mor completes amor, giving “for your sake I forgive.” This closes the second example, not the exile narrative.
[c1-l023] Menjǒ. *Em preſença. Vt,* Menjǒuo mot-
[c1-l023 note] This Menjǒ means being in someone’s presence. Menjǒuo mot- starts a polite formula for speaking in person, with motte divided across rows.
[c1-l024 >] te mǒſubequſoro. *Empreſença falarei.*
[c1-l024 note] Te completes motte, and mǒſubequſoro is translated “I shall speak in person.” Enlargement shows that the trace over final o belongs to the preceding row; the unaccented o and joined Empreſença are retained.
[c1-l025] Menjǒ. Yuruxijǒ. *Carta de perdão.*
[c1-l025 note] The second Menjǒ is a letter of pardon, paraphrased Yuruxijǒ. It is a separate homophone from the presence expression.
[c1-l026] Menmen. *Cada hum.*
[c1-l026 note] Menmen means each person individually, matched by Cada hum. The repetition is distributive, not duplicated OCR.
[c1-l027] Menmiô. Vomote cauo. *Roſto. Vt,* Mẽ-
[c1-l027 note] Menmiô means face, paraphrased Vomote cauo. The example starts with roman Mẽ-, whose nasal abbreviation does not inherit the Portuguese italics.
[c1-l028 >] miô ichidan itçucuxixi. *Tem o roſto mui-*
[c1-l028 note] Miô completes abbreviated Menmiô; the example describes an exceptionally beautiful face. The printed itçucuxixi, including its unusual final xi, is retained rather than silently regularized.
[c1-l029 >] *to bello.*
[c1-l029 note] To completes muito, and bello finishes “very beautiful.” The row closes the facial-appearance example.
[c1-l030] Menmit. *Forte, & riguroſamente. Vt,*
[c1-l030 note] Menmit concerns strong or rigorous treatment. Final t remains a historical checked-coda spelling; Vt introduces its application below.
[c1-l031 >] Menmitni ſataſuru. *Iulgar, ou tratar as de*
[c1-l031 note] Menmitni ſataſuru describes judging or handling litigation rigorously. Demandas is divided after de without a secure hyphen supplied from its continuation.
[c1-l032 >] *mandas riguroſamente, & com justiça.*
[c1-l032 note] Mandas completes demandas; the explanation adds rigour and justice. Provisional justiça typography is not globally recast as long-s/st.
[c1-l033] Menmocu. *Roſto. ¶* Menmocuno yoi
[c1-l033 note] Menmocu first denotes face or appearance. Menmocuno yoi is completed by fito below; this is not the earlier Menbocu entry.
[c1-l034 >] fito. *Homem de bom parecer. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l034 note] Fito completes “a person of good appearance.” Item introduces a second sense, a professed principle or practice, below.
[c1-l035 >] *Profißão, ou inſtituto dalgũa couſa. Vt,* Va-
[c1-l035 note] The second sense is a profession or defining practice. Va- begins Vaga, “our,” in the example about a religious school.
[c1-l036 >] ga xǔno menmocuua jiccai nari. *O que*
[c1-l036 note] Vaga xǔno menmocuua jiccai nari identifies the school’s practice with ten precepts. Portuguese O que begins the translation; this need not mean the Christian Decalogue.
[c1-l037 >] *profeßamos em noßa ley he 10. mãdamentos.*
[c1-l037 note] The translation states that the religion professes ten commandments or precepts. The scan prints 10., not split to; the whole Portuguese statement is italic.
[c1-l038] Menǒ. *Certa pedra precioſa preta que luze co*
[c1-l038 note] Menǒ is described as a black, shining precious stone. The comparison continues after co without a visible terminal hyphen; no modern mineral identification is substituted.
[c1-l039 >] *mo azeuiche, ou por ventura he o meſmo aze-*
[c1-l039 note] Mo completes como: the stone shines like jet or perhaps is jet itself. Meſmo, not moſmo, is visible, and the author’s explicit uncertainty is retained.
[c1-l040 >] *uiche.*
[c1-l040 note] Uiche completes azeuiche, jet. This ends the tentative mineral comparison before the eye and nurse entries.
[c1-l041] Menotama. *Bugalho do olho.*
[c1-l041 note] Menotama is the eyeball, matched by Bugalho do olho. Its historical form is not replaced with a modern colloquial equivalent.
[c1-l042] Menoto. *Ayo, ou ama.*
[c1-l042 note] Menoto denotes a nurse or child’s attendant, with both Ayo and ama given. The alternatives are not narrowed to one sex or duty.
[c1-l043] Menpacu. Vomote xiroi. *Couſa clara, &*
[c1-l043 note] Menpacu is something clear and apparent to sight, paraphrased Vomote xiroi. The gloss does not supply the later everyday “interesting” sense.
[c1-l044 >] *manifeſta à viſta.*
[c1-l044 note] Manifeſta à viſta completes the description as manifest to sight. The two adjectives belong to the same definition.
[c1-l045] Menpai. Vomoteuo vogamu. *Ver com re-*
[c1-l045 note] Menpai, paraphrased Vomoteuo vogamu, means seeing someone with reverence. Re- begins reuerencia on the next row.
[c1-l046 >] *uerencia a peßoa nobre.*
[c1-l046 note] Uerencia completes reuerencia; the object is a noble person. Historical u/v and peßoa are retained.
[c1-l047] Menrui. *Comer feito de farinha de trigo, co-*
[c1-l047 note] Menrui denotes food made from wheat flour. Comer functions as a noun here; co- introduces the example below.
[c1-l048 >] *mo letria, &c.*
[c1-l048 note] Mo completes como, with letria as an example of flour-based food. This is genuine body text above the rule, not a catchword.

[c2-l007 >] *alguem.* S.

[c2-l008] Menyu. *Liſonja.*

[c2-l009] Menzǒ. *Camara, ou caſa em que os Bonzos*
[c2-l009 note] Menzǒ is a chamber or house for Buddhist monks. The next row specifies sleeping and keeping possessions there; the caron remains.

[c2-l011] Meô, l, meôna. Tayena. *Couſa maraui-*

[c2-l012 >] *lhoſa.*

[c2-l013] Meô. Neco. *Gato.*
[c2-l013 note] This Meô is a cat, equated with Neco and Gato. It is a distinct sense despite identical visible accenting.
[c2-l014] Meôcacu. *Dignidade de Fotoque.* Bup.
[c2-l014 note] Meôcacu is the dignity or rank of a Buddha. Fotoque is a whole Japanese-derived term inside the Portuguese explanation; final Bup. is wholly upright.
[c2-l015] Meôchi. Tayenaru chiye. *Excellente, &*
[c2-l015 note] Meôchi is excellent or marvellous wisdom, paraphrased Tayenaru chiye. The coordinated Portuguese description continues below.
[c2-l016 >] *marauilhoſo ſaber.* S.
[c2-l016 note] Marauilhoſo ſaber completes the wisdom gloss. Final S. is the upright written-usage label, not italic explanation.
[c2-l017] Meǒchǒ. *Rol de varios nomes de homẽs.*
[c2-l017 note] Meǒchǒ is a register of people’s names. The Portuguese has homẽs, not bomẽs, and both headword carons are retained.
[c2-l018] Meǒchô. *Amenhaã polla manhaã.*
[c2-l018 note] Meǒchô means tomorrow morning. Both tildes belong over the second a in Amenhaã and manhaã. Final ô remains circumflexed, unlike the preceding entry’s ǒ.
[c2-l019] Meôcu. Tayenaru cu. *Excellente ſentença,*
[c2-l019 note] Meôcu is an excellent phrase or saying, paraphrased Tayenaru cu. The Portuguese alternative follows below.
[c2-l020 >] *ou dito.*
[c2-l020 note] Ou dito completes the gloss as an excellent saying. This short row is not a new headword.
[c2-l021] Meôdo. Tayenaru tçuchi. i, Qidocuna to-
[c2-l021 note] Meôdo is a marvellous land or place, paraphrased Tayenaru tçuchi. The second paraphrase ends to-, not co-, forming Qidocuna tocoro below and matching Portuguese lugar.
[c2-l022 >] coro. *Excellente lugar, como aquelle onde*

[c2-l024] Meôfô. Tayenaru nori. *Excellente, ou ma-*

[c2-l031 >] ganno ſô. *Bonzo de entendimento claro pe-*

[c2-l033] Meôjet. *Couſa marauilhoſa q̃ não ſe pode facil-*
