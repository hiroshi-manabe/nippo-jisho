[c1-l001] Meitet. i, Meixǒ. *Letrado afamado. Vt,*
[c1-l001 note] Meitet denotes an eminent learned person, equated with Meixǒ and glossed as a celebrated scholar. Final t is the historical checked-syllable spelling, not an omitted tçu; Vt is italic.
[c1-l002 >] Furuqiuo tazzune, ataraxiqiuo xiruua
[c1-l002 note] The example contrasts investigating old things with knowing new ones. Furuqiuo and ataraxiqiuo contain the attached object particle; the statement continues with the scholar’s preference below.

[c1-l003 note] Meitetno conomu tocoro nari completes the statement that this is what a learned person delights in. Taif. is the upright source label; italic Lib. introduces the next row’s book number.
[c1-l004 >] *28. Eſcudrinhar as couſas antiguas, & ſa*
[c1-l004 note] The scan reads book 28, not the OCR mixture a8. The Portuguese begins “to investigate old things and know”; saber is divided after ſa without a visible terminal hyphen.

[c1-l005 note] Ber completes saber. The remainder says that knowing new things is something scholars desire, matching the Japanese contrast between old and new rather than adding another entry.
