## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Fatçufatçu, l, fatçufatçuna coto. *Couſa*
[c1-l001 note] Hatsuhatsu or hatsuhatsu na koto denotes something only just adequate. The reduplication is not the first-fruits sense of hatsu found in nearby entries.
[c1-l002 >] *que eſcaſamente quadra, vem juſta, ou que*
[c1-l002 note] The Portuguese describes something that barely fits or comes out just right. Quadra concerns suitability here, not a square shape.
[c1-l003 >] *por poucas não preſtara, ou não ſeruira pe-*
[c1-l003 note] It nearly would not be useful or serve any purpose. The negative construction explains the narrow margin of adequacy rather than declaring the object wholly useless.
[c1-l004 >] *ra nada.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes pera nada from the preceding row. This short fragment belongs to the adequacy definition, not the following hatsugan cross-reference.
[c1-l005] Fatçugan. *Vide ſuprà,* Fatçu.
[c1-l005 note] Hatsugan refers back to hatsu and its example of the first wild birds taken. The reference instruction is italic while Fatçu is Roman in this occurrence.
[c1-l006] Fatçumono. *Primicias de qualquer couſa. Vide*
[c1-l006 note] Hatsumono is the first produce of anything. Vide continues into the Japanese cross-reference on the next physical row.
[c1-l007 >] *fatçu.*
[c1-l007 note] This is the referenced hatsu, not a new headword. The word is visibly italic here, so its typography is retained independently of the Roman occurrence two rows above.
[c1-l008] Fatçune. *A prima voz do paſſaro na prima*
[c1-l008 note] Hatsune means a bird's first song of the season. Prima modifies voice, while the second prima continues into primavera across the row boundary.
[c1-l009 >] *vera como do roxinol, ou gallinha do mato,*
[c1-l009 note] Completes primavera and gives birds as examples. Roxinol is the Portuguese nightingale designation; it should not be used to force a modern zoological identification of every Japanese bird in the entry.
[c1-l010 >] *ou do Fototoguiſu no verão. ¶* Fatçune-
[c1-l010 note] Hototogisu is the Japanese cuckoo name embedded in italic Portuguese; the scan supports its italic treatment. The new example starts hatsune wo dasu, producing the first seasonal song.
[c1-l011 >] uo daſu. *Cantarem a primeira vez os paſſaros*
[c1-l011 note] Wo dasu completes hatsune wo dasu from the preceding row. The Portuguese plural birds supplies the subject of singing for the first time.
[c1-l012 >] *na prima vera, ou verão.*
[c1-l012 note] The first song is located in spring or summer. The printed two-word prima vera is preserved rather than standardized.
[c1-l013] Fatçuqi. i, Fachiguachi. *Oitaua lũa. S.*
[c1-l013 note] Hatsuki is the eighth lunar month, glossed by the printed Fachiguachi. Although modern hachigatsu is familiar, the visible final chi is retained; the Portuguese eighth moon confirms the calendrical meaning.
[c1-l014] Fatçutorigari, l, fatçutogari. *O ſair a pri-*
[c1-l014 note] Hatsutorigari or hatsutogari is the first outing for falconry. The shorter alternative is explicitly printed, not an accidental omission of ri in this transcription.
[c1-l015 >] *meira vez à caça do falcão.* P.
[c1-l015 note] Completes the first hunting excursion with a falcon. The register label P. and its period are Roman, distinct from the preceding italic explanation.
[c1-l016] Fatçuuo. *Primicias de qualquer couſa, fruita*
[c1-l016 note] Hatsuo is defined broadly as first produce or first fruit. It should not be replaced by the familiar fish word katsuo merely because the sound sequence looks similar.
[c1-l017 >] *primera, &c.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes fruita primera. The printed primera lacks the additional i of modern primeira and is preserved as a local spelling.
[c1-l018] Fatdaixù. *Certo liuro de verſos, ou utas*
[c1-l018 note] The title is associated with Hachidaishū, the eight major poetry collections. Utas is Japanese uta with a Portuguese plural ending, and its final s is short in the scan. The headword's worn accent is retained as transcribed rather than inferred from the modern title.
[c1-l019 >] *de Iapão.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes the identification of the verses as Japanese. The previous row's utas is therefore poetry terminology inside the Portuguese description, not an independent dictionary entry.
[c1-l020] Fatdô. *Apreſſarſe, ou menearſe com preſſa.*
[c1-l020 note] The compound denotes hurried activity or movement. The Portuguese reflexive verbs make this an action or state, not a place name resembling hattō.
[c1-l021 >] *Vt,* Fatdô ſuru.
[c1-l021 note] Fatdô suru supplies the verbal construction for that hurried activity. Suru is ordinary Japanese here and is not a grammatical abbreviation.
[c1-l022] Fate, uru, eta. *Acabarſe algũa couſa. Vt,*
[c1-l022 note] Hateru means coming to an end. The abbreviated historical inflection should not be expanded by simply concatenating every printed ending without accounting for stem replacement.
[c1-l023 >] Nôga fateta. *Acabouſe o auto. Item,*
[c1-l023 note] Nō ga hateta says the dramatic performance ended. Auto denotes the play here; the item marker introduces another sense of hateru on the following row.
[c1-l024 >] *Morrer.*
[c1-l024 note] To die is the further sense of hateru. This one-word definition belongs to the verb above, not the noun hate that follows.
[c1-l025] Fate. *Cabo, fim.*
[c1-l025 note] Hate is the end or limit, now a noun. Cabo and fim are synonymous in this context, not a rope or geographical cape.
[c1-l026] Fatecuchi. *Principio, ou cauſa da deſtrui-*
[c1-l026 note] Hatekuchi is the beginning or cause of destruction. Kuchi can mark an opening or point of origin; the compound is not interpreted literally as an anatomical mouth.
[c1-l027 >] *ção de algũa couſa.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes destruição de alguma coisa, the destruction of something. The cedilla and nasal ending belong to the split Portuguese noun.
[c1-l028] Fatefa. *Remate, ou cabo de algũa couſa.*
[c1-l028 note] Hateha denotes the conclusion or final end of something. The transmitted fa belongs to the native word and is not a Portuguese abbreviation.
[c1-l029] Fatei. Nami, ſoco. *De baixo das ondas. Vt,*
[c1-l029 note] Hatei is wave-bottom, glossed component by component as nami and soko. The scan supports long s in ſoco, and Portuguese beneath the waves excludes hoko or foco here.
[c1-l030 >] Fateini xizzumu. *Fundirſe de baixo das*
[c1-l030 note] Hatei ni shizumu means sinking below the waves. Fundir-se has the sinking sense here, not necessarily melting a material.
[c1-l031 >] *ondas. S.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the waves phrase; S. is a register label. It is not an additional Japanese word to feed to the kana converter.
[c1-l032] Fatexi. *Fim, ou termo.*
[c1-l032 note] Hateshi is an end or limit. The pairing fim/termo gives the nominal sense rather than the verb hatasu discussed on the previous page.
[c1-l033] Fatgicu. *Meliùs, fachigicu. Oito liuros, ou*
[c1-l033 note] The dictionary prefers the fuller hachijiku form to the compressed headword, referring to eight scrolls. Gi represents ji here; the preferred Japanese form is visibly italic within the explanatory sentence.
[c1-l034 >] *partes do* Foqueqiǒ.
[c1-l034 note] The eight divisions belong to the Lotus Sutra, Hokekyō. The scan shows the title in Roman with a final marked o; its worn caron is consistent with the title's established spelling, not an unmarked modernizing replacement.
[c1-l035] Fatguen cotobauo vocoſu. *Falar, ou co-*
[c1-l035 note] Hatsugen is explained as kotoba wo okosu, beginning to speak. The printed absence of a separating period after the headword is retained rather than supplied from NINJAL's normalized entry boundary.
[c1-l036 >] *meçar a falar. ¶ Itẽ, Palauras altiuas, ou de*
[c1-l036 note] Completes começar and adds lofty or boastful speech as another sense. The item distinction matters: not every act of beginning to speak is boasting.
[c1-l037 >] *fanfarrice. Vt,* Fatguenuo fanaſu. *Di-*
[c1-l037 note] Hatsugen wo hanasu illustrates uttering such boastful words. Fanfarrice supplies the pejorative sense and the final Di- continues into dizer.
[c1-l038 >] *zer ſemelhantes palauras.*
[c1-l038 note] Completes dizer semelhantes palavras, saying words of that kind. This closes the boasting example, not the forthcoming intelligence definition.
[c1-l039] Fatmei. i, Chiyeno aqiracana coto. *En-*
[c1-l039 note] Hatsumei is clarity of intelligence, chie no akiraka na koto. The older sense is intellectual brilliance, not simply the modern noun invention.
[c1-l040 >] *tendimento claro, & agudo como de Anjo, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Completes entendimento and compares a clear, sharp mind to an angel's. The comparison reflects the Portuguese explanatory frame, not an additional Japanese religious term.
[c1-l041 >] *homem muito raro em letras, & ſaber. Vt,*
[c1-l041 note] Alternatively the comparison is an exceptionally learned person. Raro means outstanding or uncommon in learning, not someone who seldom reads.
[c1-l042 >] Fatmeina fito. *Homem de grandes letras,*
[c1-l042 note] Hatsumei na hito is such a brilliant or learned person. The attributive na makes the construction grammatical and is not part of the noun hito.
[c1-l043 >] *ſaber, &c.*
[c1-l043 note] Knowledge completes the preceding person's accomplishments. This short row remains part of the hatsumei example.
[c1-l044] Fatmô. Camigue. *Cabellos da cabeça.*
[c1-l044 note] Hatsumō is glossed by kamige, hair of the head. This nominal hair sense must not be replaced by the modern growth-of-hair interpretation solely from familiar kanji.
[c1-l045] Fatǒ. Nami, nami. *Ondas. ¶* Fatǒ-
[c1-l045 note] Hatō denotes waves, with nami repeated as the component glosses. The new example starts the same noun followed on the next row by wo tatamu.
[c1-l046 >] uo tatamu. *Multiplicaremſe as ondas huas*
[c1-l046 note] Hatō wo tatamu describes waves piling over one another. Tatamu has the layering sense rather than literally folding an object by hand.
[c1-l047 >] *ſobre as outras, ou encapellarẽſe.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes the piling of waves one upon another; encapelar-se likewise describes rising or heaping seas. The printed nasal abbreviation is retained.

[c2-l001] Fatǒ. Nami, xima. *Ondas, & ilhas. Vt,*
[c2-l001 note] This hatō is glossed as waves and islands, nami and shima, distinct from the preceding column's repeated wave gloss. The Portuguese explicitly includes islands.
[c2-l002 >] Fatǒuo xinogu. *Paſſar mares, & ilhas*
[c2-l002 note] Hatō wo shinogu means traversing seas and islands with difficulty. Shinogu supplies the overcoming or enduring sense rather than a neutral sightseeing journey.
[c2-l003 >] *com trabalho.*
[c2-l003 note] With hardship completes the travel example. Trabalho denotes difficulty here, not employment or a separate work-related entry.
[c2-l004] Fato. *Pomba, ou pombo.*
[c2-l004 note] Hato is pigeon or dove. The Portuguese feminine and masculine forms are alternatives for the bird, not two Japanese headwords.
[c2-l005] Fato. *Cais, ou lugar acommodado pera deſ-*
[c2-l005 note] The homonymous hato is a wharf or suitable landing place. This geographical sense is distinguished from the bird by cais and the following disembarking verb.
[c2-l006 >] *embarcar.* X.
[c2-l006 note] Completes desembarcar. The Roman X. is the regional usage label, not a Japanese abbreviation; its period shares the Roman treatment.
[c2-l007] Fatomune. *Vt,* Abumino fatomune. *Pei*
[c2-l007 note] Abumi no hatomune is the protruding front or breast of a Japanese stirrup. Pei continues into peito; the compound's pigeon-breast imagery explains the shape.
[c2-l008 >] *to do estribo de Iapão. ¶ Itẽ,* Fatomune.
[c2-l008 note] Completes the stirrup definition, then repeats hatomune for a human chest shape. The item marker keeps these two related senses apart.
[c2-l009 >] *Peito alto, & ſaido a fora de homem.*
[c2-l009 note] A raised, outward-projecting chest of a person is the second sense. Saido a fora describes protrusion, not a person physically leaving a place.
[c2-l010] Fatpǒ. *Vide* Fappǒ.
[c2-l010 note] The compressed spelling redirects to Fappǒ, happō. The printed t before p is kept rather than replaced by the preferred spelling inside the headword itself.
[c2-l011] Fatqen, *l, potiùs,* Facqẽ. *Religioſos, ou Bõzos da*
[c2-l011 note] The dictionary prefers Facqẽ to Fatqen for religious colleagues of the same origin or affiliation. Latin potius is italic instructional text, not Japanese; the nasal abbreviation in the preferred form is visibly printed.
[c2-l012 >] *meſma terra, ſeita, ou eſcola.*
[c2-l012 note] The colleagues share the same country, sect, or school. This is an affiliation definition, not merely all Buddhist clergy indiscriminately.
[c2-l013] Fatquǒ. l, *potius,* Facquǒ. i, Fappǒ. *Oito*
[c2-l013 note] Hakkō is equated with happō, the eight directions or quarters. Potius again introduces the preferred spelling and is italic; the Japanese alternatives remain Roman.
[c2-l014 >] *partes do mundo.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes the eight parts of the world, meaning the directional quarters. The short row belongs to the preceding numerical definition.
[c2-l015] Fattai. *Pòs de farinha de arroz, ou de ceuada*
[c2-l015 note] Hattai is powdered rice or barley flour. The following torrada qualifies the roasted grain preparation, not a verb describing consumption.
[c2-l016 >] *torrada.*
[c2-l016 note] Roasted completes the preparation description. The single word is part of the food entry and should not be attached to the following mushroom definition.
[c2-l017] Fattaqe. *Hũa eſpecie de cugumelos que ſe co-*
[c2-l017 note] Hattake is a mushroom eaten in Japan. The spelling cugumelos is retained; no precise species is imposed from the name alone.
[c2-l018 >] *mem em Iapão.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes comem, are eaten, and identifies Japan as the place of use. This is a continuation, not an imperative to eat mushrooms.
[c2-l019] Fattato. *Adu. Repentinamente. Vt,*
[c2-l019 note] Hatta to is an adverb meaning suddenly. Adu. labels the part of speech and is italic with the Portuguese definition.
[c2-l020 >] Fattato aqireta. *Eſpantouſe derepente.*
[c2-l020 note] Hatta to akireta describes sudden astonishment. Historical akireru here means being struck with amazement, rather than only the modern sense of exasperation.
[c2-l021] Fattçuqe. *Cruz ẽ q̃ crucificão ao modo de Iapão.*
[c2-l021 note] Hattsuke names the cross or execution frame used for Japanese-style crucifixion. The dictionary defines the apparatus here, not just the act of punishment.
[c2-l022] Fatten. Yatçuno ten. *Oito ceos de que ſe tra-*
[c2-l022 note] Hatten is explicitly explained as yatsu no ten, eight heavens. The Portuguese continues into the Buddhist context on the next row.
[c2-l023 >] *ta no* Buppô. *¶ Item, Hũs pontinhos,*
[c2-l023 note] Buppō is the Buddhist teaching within which these heavens are discussed; the scan has Roman Buppô inside the Portuguese. Item then begins a different sense involving small marks.
[c2-l024 >] *ou riſcas que ſe poem no principio dalgũa eſcri-*
[c2-l024 note] These are dots or strokes placed at the beginning of a writing. The split escritura is Portuguese, not a Japanese technical term.
[c2-l025 >] *tura como ſinaes que o diabo teme.*
[c2-l025 note] The marks are described as signs feared by the devil. This is the missionaries' explanatory framing of protective signs, not a neutral identification of a particular deity.
[c2-l026] Fatto. i, Nori. *Leis, ordenações, &c.*
[c2-l026 note] Hatto, glossed nori, means laws and ordinances. The scan's initial L and Portuguese legal sense both support Leis, not the former Deis.
[c2-l027] Fattǒ. *Montinho de pedras, como tumulo que*
[c2-l027 note] This hattō is a small mound of stones serving as a tomb monument. Its long vowel distinguishes it from the preceding legal hatto.
[c2-l028 >] *fazem ſobre as couas dos defuntos.*
[c2-l028 note] The stone mound is made over the graves of the dead. Covas are burial pits in this context, not caves inhabited by living people.
[c2-l029] Fattǒ. *Lugar alto como pulpito.*
[c2-l029 note] Another hattō denotes a raised place like a pulpit. The o-mark is extremely faint in the scan; the existing caron is retained provisionally, and NINJAL also flags the original's unclear open/closed marking for this entry.
[c2-l030] Fattocu. *Certo Dôbucu ſingello que ſe veste*
[c2-l030 note] Hattoku is an unlined dōbuku outer garment. The embedded Japanese garment name is italic here, with a visible circumflex rather than a Portuguese nasal tilde.
[c2-l031 >] *por cima do outro veſtido.*
[c2-l031 note] It is worn over another garment. Singello in the previous row refers to a single or unlined layer, not necessarily a poor-quality garment.
[c2-l032] Faua. *Mãy.*
[c2-l032 note] Faua is the historical hawa form for mother. It is not mechanically modernized to haha merely because that is the familiar modern form.
[c2-l033] Fafago. *Idem. Falando com honra, &*
[c2-l033 note] Hahago means the same, mother, when speaking respectfully. The explicit fa-fa spelling contrasts with Faua above and should not be leveled to it.
[c2-l034 >] *reſpeito.*
[c2-l034 note] Respect completes the honorific qualification. This is usage information for hahago, not an independent translation of a new Japanese item.
[c2-l035] Fauaguimi. *Senhora mãy.*
[c2-l035 note] Hawagimi is an honorific mother expression, reflected in senhora mãe. Guimi represents gimi with hard g before i, unlike gi alone.
[c2-l036] Fauaqe. *Vt,* Fauaqeno caje. P. *Vento*
[c2-l036 note] Hawake no kaze denotes a wind passing over vegetation; the expression should not be connected with the nearby mother words merely by spelling. The usage label P. is Roman in the scan.
[c2-l037 >] *que vai paſſando por cima de muitas eruas.*
[c2-l037 note] The wind passes over many plants or grasses. Ervas supplies the foliage context for hawake, rather than an abstract emotional meaning.
[c2-l038] Fauaqi, u, aita. *Varrer.*
[c2-l038 note] Hawaku means sweeping. The historical wa sequence is retained and should not be normalized to modern haku during diplomatic transcription.
[c2-l039] Fauaqimono. *Sinal como hũa erua, ou flor q̃*
[c2-l039 note] Hawakimono is a plant- or flower-like emblem. It is not a broom despite its proximity to the sweeping verb.
[c2-l040 >] *ſe pinta no vestido.*
[c2-l040 note] The emblem is painted on clothing. This completes the visual-design sense and explains why a plant or flower is mentioned.
[c2-l041] Faua voya. *Mãy.*
[c2-l041 note] Hawa oya again means mother, with oya identifying the parental relation. Initial vo represents o here; it should not be read as a European v consonant.
[c2-l042] Fauchiua. *Abano redõdo de q̃ vſão nos autos*
[c2-l042 note] Ha-uchiwa is a round feather fan used in dramatic performances. Autos means plays in this context, not vehicles or documents.
[c2-l043 >] *feito de penas de Aguia, ou falcão.*
[c2-l043 note] The fan is made from eagle or falcon feathers. Penas is feathers, not penalties, and agrees with the fan's material description.
[c2-l044] Fauǒ. *Viſorey.*
[c2-l044 note] Haō is rendered as viceroy by the dictionary. This historical equivalence is preserved rather than replaced with a modern political definition of hegemon.
[c2-l045] Fauocoxi. *Eſcoproſinho a feição de martello*
[c2-l045 note] Haokoshi is a small chisel shaped like a hammer. Escoprosinho is a diminutive tool name, not an adjective describing a person's appearance.
[c2-l046 >] *com que abrem os dentes da lima.*
[c2-l046 note] The tool cuts or raises the teeth of a file. Dentes is a tool-surface metaphor, and lima is the file rather than a citrus fruit.
[c2-l047] Fauocu. Yabureta iye. *Caſa arruinada.*
[c2-l047 note] Haoku is a ruined house, glossed yabureta ie. This Sino-Japanese compound is unrelated to the preceding tool despite sharing the initial hao sequence.
