[c1-l001] Nariſagari, u, atta. *Tornarſe miſerauel, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Narisagaru first means falling into misery or reduced circumstances. The long ſ is part of sagari, not an f to be read as an h-row consonant.
[c1-l002 >] *deſcair do eſtado, &c. ¶ Item, Estar a*
[c1-l002 note] Completes decline in status, then introduces the physical sense of hanging fruit. The change of sense is explicitly marked by Item.
[c1-l003 >] *fruita dependurada da aruore. Vt,* Budǒga
[c1-l003 note] The second gloss describes fruit hanging from a tree. Budǒga begins the grape example; the caron over o is retained.
[c1-l004 >] nariſagaru. *Eſtarem as vuas dependuradas na*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the Japanese statement about grapes hanging down. The Portuguese translation continues with the vine on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] *parreira. No Cami ſe diz,* Naricodarete aru.
[c1-l005 note] Completes 'on the vine' and gives Naricodarete aru as the corresponding expression in Cami usage. Cami occurs within Portuguese explanatory prose; the Japanese expression is retained in its visible roman type.
[c1-l006] Nariuai. *Fruito da terra, ou ſementeira, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] Nariwai is glossed as the land's yield or cultivation. The definition emphasizes agricultural produce rather than imposing the full range of modern livelihood senses.
[c1-l007 >] *Vt,* Cotoxino nariuaiga yoi. *A nouida-*
[c1-l007 note] The example says that this year's crop is good. Portuguese novidade here concerns the new harvest and is divided after nouida-.
[c1-l008 >] *de, ou fruito da terra deste anno he bom.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the translation as this year's harvest or produce being good. The source's anno and the short-s convention in deste remain unchanged.
[c1-l009 >] *¶* Nariuai xezumba naniuoca cuuan? co-
[c1-l009 note] The rhetorical example asks what one will eat without farming. The printed xezumba is retained beside xezunba below: enlarged reinspection shows five vertical strokes after z, corresponding to u plus m, although one shoulder of m is weak. The final co- begins cogai, silkworm rearing.
[c1-l010 >] gai xezunba naniuoca qin? *Se não ſe cul-*
[c1-l010 note] Completes the parallel question about what one will wear without silkworm rearing. Qin is the prospective form in the question; the Portuguese cultivation verb begins at the right edge.
[c1-l011 >] *tiuar a terra, & colher fruito, que comeremos,*
[c1-l011 note] Completes cultivar and translates the food question: without tilling the land and gathering produce, what will we eat? The punctuation is retained as printed.
[c1-l012 >] *& ſe não ſe fizer borra, que vestiremos?*
[c1-l012 note] Completes the clothing question with the source's textile term borra. In the Japanese parallel it corresponds to silkworm rearing, not unrelated refuse or sediment.
[c1-l013] Narivatari, u, atta. *Paßar ſoando, ou com*
[c1-l013 note] Nariwataru means sound carrying across a space or resounding. The Portuguese initially describes passing with sound or a great noise.
[c1-l014 >] *grande estrondo. ¶* Teppôno fibiqini ta-
[c1-l014 note] Completes the loud-noise gloss and begins an example about a firearm's report echoing through a valley. Ta- continues tani on the next row.
[c1-l015 >] niga narivatatta. *Com estrondo da eſpingar*
[c1-l015 note] Completes the valley's resounding with the gunshot. The Portuguese eſpingarda is physically divided without a visible line-end hyphen.
[c1-l016 >] *da ficou o valle retumbando.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes the explanation that the valley was left reverberating. The historical doubled l in valle remains unchanged.
[c1-l017] Narixidai. *Segundo for poßiuel, ou conforme*
[c1-l017 note] Narishidai is defined as according to what is possible. This is the preferred form cited under Narifôdai on the preceding page.
[c1-l018 >] *ao que poder ſer.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes 'according to what can be.' The source's poder is retained without replacing it with a modern expected inflection.
[c1-l019] Nariyuqi, u, uita. *Irſe fazendo. Vt,* Vǒ-
[c1-l019 note] Nariyuku describes a condition progressively developing. The next example begins a word for great lamentation; the partly impressed mark over o is retained provisionally as the supplied caron.
[c1-l020 >] nagueqi figotoni fucaqu nariyuqiqere-
[c1-l020 note] The lamentation deepens day by day. The scan and the following cada dia support figotoni, higoto ni, not OCR ſigotoni; the conditional verb continues below.
[c1-l021 >] ba, &c. Taif. *Lib. 18. Indo ſua*
[c1-l021 note] Completes the conditional phrase and cites the Taiheiki, book 18. The Portuguese begins 'as his/her sadness ...'; the source has ſua, not the draft's truncated ſu-.
[c1-l022 >] *triſteza fazendoſe mayor cada dia.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes the sadness becoming greater every day. This supplies the direct bilingual check for figotoni in the Japanese example.
[c1-l023] Narucami. i, Icazzuchi, *Trouoẽs.*
[c1-l023 note] Narukami is equated with ikazuchi, thunder. The enlarged scan shows a descending comma after Icazzuchi, not the draft's period. The synonym follows i, and is not a term embedded in Portuguese explanatory prose for font toggling.
[c1-l024] Naruco. *Hũa laya de taramela pera eſpantar*
[c1-l024 note] Naruko is a clattering device used to frighten birds. The Portuguese taramela describes the mechanism; its purpose continues below.
[c1-l025 >] *os paßaros. ¶* Narucouo caquru. *Por*
[c1-l025 note] Completes the bird-scaring purpose. Naruko wo kakuru illustrates setting up the device, with its Portuguese translation divided after Por.
[c1-l026 >] *esta taramela.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes 'set up this clattering device.' This is the translation of the preceding example, not an additional headword.
[c1-l027] Naſa. *O não auer. Vſaſe em compoſição. Vt,*
[c1-l027 note] Nasa denotes absence or nonexistence and is said to occur in compounds. The next example demonstrates the nominal -nasa formation.
[c1-l028 >] Amarino yarucatanaſani, &c. *Por não*
[c1-l028 note] The compound yarukatanasa concerns having no remedy or outlet, intensified by amari no. The Portuguese causal explanation begins here and continues below.
[c1-l029 >] *auer nenhum remedio, &c.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes 'because there is no remedy.' The gloss supports the absence interpretation of -nasa rather than treating the Japanese ending as an unrelated noun.
[c1-l030] Naſaqe. *Piedade, ou amor. ¶* Fitoni na-
[c1-l030 note] Nasake is compassion or affection. The example begins showing compassion to someone, with the noun itself divided after na-.
[c1-l031 >] ſaqeuo caquru. *Fazer bem a alguem, ou*
[c1-l031 note] Completes nasake wo kakuru, explained as doing someone good. The alternative gloss of showing affection follows below.
[c1-l032 >] *mostrar lhe amor.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes 'show affection to someone.' The printed separation of lhe is preserved, without introducing a modern hyphen.
[c1-l033] Naſaqenagueni. *Adu. Cruelmente, ou*
[c1-l033 note] Nasakenage ni is presented as an adverb meaning cruelly. The adverbial label and Portuguese explanation are italic; the alternative follows below.
[c1-l034 >] *ſem piedade.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes 'without pity,' matching the absence of nasake. The short continuation remains a distinct physical row.
[c1-l035] Naſaqenai. *Couſa cruel, ou ſem piedade.*
[c1-l035 note] Nasakenai here means cruel or pitiless. The gloss's historical sense is retained rather than replaced with a modern colloquial sense such as pathetic.
[c1-l036 >] Naſaqenaſa.
[c1-l036 note] The indented nasakenasa is the corresponding abstract noun, lack of pity. The source gives no separate Portuguese gloss on this row, so none is supplied in the transcription.
[c1-l037 >] Naſaqenǒ. *Adu.*
[c1-l037 note] The indented nasakenô is marked as the adverbial form of the preceding adjective. The downward-pointing caron is retained; no Portuguese gloss beyond Adu. is printed.
[c1-l038] Naſubi. *Biringela.*
[c1-l038 note] Nasubi is eggplant. The historical Portuguese spelling Biringela is preserved, without substituting a modern initial vowel.
[c1-l039] Naſubiba. *Dẽtes pretos mais curtos, & gastados,*
[c1-l039 note] Nasubiba is defined as short, worn, black teeth. The tilde abbreviation in Dẽtes is retained and the comparison with children's teeth continues below.
[c1-l040 >] *como os de mininos que mamão. No Cami ſe*
[c1-l040 note] Compares these with the teeth of nursing children, then introduces a Cami-region equivalent. Mininos and mamão are the source's spellings, not modernized forms.
[c1-l041 >] *diz,* Chiguchita fa.
[c1-l041 note] Completes the regional naming statement with the printed Chiguchita fa. The unusual expression is retained from the visible type rather than forced into a conjectural modern Japanese form; fa denotes teeth in this gloss.
[c1-l042] Naſubizane. *Granſinhos, ou ſemente que està*
[c1-l042 note] Nasubizane means the small grains or seeds inside an eggplant. The Portuguese diminutive Granſinhos and the grave in està remain as printed.
[c1-l043 >] *dentro da biringela. ¶* Naſubizaneno
[c1-l043 note] Completes the seed's location, then begins a comparison 'like eggplant seeds.' The Japanese genitive continues with yǒni below.
[c1-l044 >] yǒni chaga voruru. *Cair o cha moido da*
[c1-l044 note] The example compares ground tea falling from a mill with eggplant seeds. The Portuguese loanword cha is visibly lowercase here, unlike the draft's Cha; it is not a roman Japanese example fragment.
[c1-l045 >] *mò à maneira desta ſementeſinha de biringela.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes the image of tea falling from the millstone in seed-like particles. Both graves in mò à and the historical diminutive ſementeſinha are retained.
[c1-l046] Nata. *Podão, ou fouce roçadoura.*
[c1-l046 note] Nata is explained as a billhook or clearing sickle. The transcription preserves the source's functional tool comparison rather than substituting a modern product name.
[c1-l047] Natamame. *Certa laya de feijoẽs.*
[c1-l047 note] Natamame is identified as a kind of bean. The source gives no species-level description, and the nasal-vowel spelling feijoẽs is retained.

[c2-l001] Natçu. *Verão, ou estio. ¶* Natçuni na-

[c2-l004 >] *ou estio.*

[c2-l005] Natçucaxij. *O auer ſaudades entre abſentes.*
[c2-l005 note] Natsukashii is explained through longing between people who are apart. The gloss emphasizes affectionate yearning rather than only modern nostalgia for the past.
[c2-l006 >] Natçucaxiſa.
[c2-l006 note] The indented natsukashisa is the corresponding abstract noun. No additional Portuguese wording is printed, and none is inserted into the canonical line.
[c2-l007 >] Natçucaxǔ. *Vt,* Natçucaxǔ vomô.
[c2-l007 note] The adverbial natsukashû is illustrated with omou, feeling longing. The carons over u and circumflex over the final o are kept distinct.
[c2-l008 >] *Ter ſaudades.*
[c2-l008 note] Translates the example as experiencing longing or missing someone. This completes the adjective's derivative forms before Natçugo begins.
[c2-l009] Natçugo. *Bichos da ſeda que ſe produzem no*
[c2-l009 note] Natsugo first means summer-bred silkworms. The scan reads ſeda as one word, correcting the draft's ſa da; the seasonal qualification follows below.
[c2-l010 >] *estio pera então começarem a comer a folha, &*
[c2-l010 note] The silkworms begin eating leaves in summer. The conjunction connects this feeding with silk production on the next row.
[c2-l011 >] *fazer a ſeda. ¶ Item, Filho de cão, ga-*
[c2-l011 note] Completes making silk, then extends the term to young animals born in summer. The divided ga- begins gato, not a Japanese word.
[c2-l012 >] *to, &c. que nace no verão, ou eſtio. ¶* Fa-
[c2-l012 note] Completes the summer-born animal sense and begins the contrasting Japanese harugo for spring-born creatures. Fa- follows the source's h-row romanization.
[c2-l013 >] rugo. *He o bicho de ſeda que ſe gèra na pri-*
[c2-l013 note] Completes harugo and defines spring-bred silkworms. The Portuguese primavera is divided after pri-, independently of the Japanese division above.
[c2-l014 >] *mauera, ou filho de animal, ou paſſaro que na-*
[c2-l014 note] Extends harugo to offspring of animals or birds born in spring. The final na- begins Portuguese nace, not a Japanese seasonal term.
[c2-l015 >] *ce na primauera.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes 'born in spring.' This finishes the comparison between summer-born and spring-born creatures.
[c2-l016] Natçuguicu. *Boninas do verão.*
[c2-l016 note] Natsugiku is glossed as summer flowers of the bonina kind. The transcription retains the source's general floral comparison without supplying a modern species.
[c2-l017] Natçumame. *Certa laya de feijoẽs, ou grãos*
[c2-l017 note] Natsumame is a kind of bean or small pulse. Its size and summer season are specified on the following row.
[c2-l018 >] *mais pequenos que vem no eſtio.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes the description as smaller pulses appearing in summer. The source's unaccented vem is preserved.
[c2-l019] Natçume. *Certas maçãas pequenas, como da-*
[c2-l019 note] Natsume is described as a small apple-like fruit. The comparison continues after the printed da-; the source's spelling is not replaced with a modern botanical name.
[c2-l020 >] *nàfega.*

[c2-l021] Natçume. *Hum vaſo pequenino em que metẽ*
[c2-l021 note] This natsume is a small vessel, distinct from the fruit entry. The relative clause explains what it holds and then describes its finish and shape.

[c2-l022 note] The vessel holds powdered tea and is lacquered and shaped like the fruit. The scan restores x in Vruxado and q in daque-, correcting Vruado and dapue-; Vruxado modifies the vessel, not the tea.
[c2-l023 >] *llas maçãas.*
[c2-l023 note] Completes 'those small apple-like fruits,' explaining the vessel's name by its shape. The doubled l across daque-/llas is preserved as printed.
[c2-l024] Natçuno muxi. *Bicho do verão que voa ao der-*

[c2-l025 >] *redor da candea como borboleta.*

[c2-l026] Natçuqe, uru, eta. *Domeſticar, ou aman-*

[c2-l028 >] *Vt,* Fitouo natçuquru. *Domesticar, &*

[c2-l031 >] nu. *Este homem não ſe domeſtica como per-*

[c2-l032 >] *digoto, ou filho de lobo.*
