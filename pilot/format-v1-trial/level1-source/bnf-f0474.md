[c1-l001] Taqitçuzzuqe, quru, eta. *Queimar em fiei-*
[c1-l001 note] Takitsuzuke means continuing to kindle or burn; the gloss begins with burning in a row. The visible terminal mark divides fieira across the next line.
[c1-l002 >] *ra, ou continuar com fogos por algũ eſpaço*
[c1-l002 note] Completes fieira and explains maintaining fires over a certain extent or interval. The abbreviation in algũ remains unexpanded; this is a continuation, not another entry.
[c1-l003] Tara. *Hum peixe de rio muito estimado que cõ-*
[c1-l003 note] Tara is described here as a prized river fish. The following life-cycle claim belongs to this historical definition and should not be replaced by modern zoological information.
[c1-l004 >] *mummente vai morrer ao mar.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes cõmummente: the fish is said usually to go to the sea to die. Preserve that statement even though the familiar modern referent of tara invites a different description.
[c1-l005] Tara. l, tarano qi. *Hũa aruore q̃ tem eſpinhos.*
[c1-l005 note] The second tara entry names a thorny tree, also tarano qi. The abbreviation q̃ is visibly separated from aruore; l introduces the alternative name.
[c1-l006] Tarachime. *P*. i, Faua. *Mãy.*
[c1-l006 note] Tarachime is the poetic word for mother. The damaged initial is T, not OCR r; preserve the printed synonym Faua without changing its internal u to the more expected f.
[c1-l007] Tarachine. *P*. i, Voya. *Pay, & mãy.*
[c1-l007 note] Tarachine is glossed as father and mother, with Voya supplying the Japanese explanation. The poetic label P is distinct from the synonym and the Portuguese definition.
[c1-l008] Tarachiuo. i, Chichi. *Pay.*
[c1-l008 note] Tarachiuo denotes a father, explained by Chichi and Pay. The historical final uo is not a typographical substitute for a modernized spelling.
[c1-l009] Tarai. *Gamela de pao, ou ſelha.*
[c1-l009 note] Tarai is a wooden basin or tub. The vessel word is ſelha: its initial lacks the rightward crossbar visible in nearby italic f, so the second pass corrects OCR felha rather than retaining a different letter on lexical grounds alone.
[c1-l010] Tarai, ǒ, ǒta. *Ser perfeito em algũa couſa,*
[c1-l010 note] Tarai is the verb of being sufficient or complete, here rendered being perfect in something. The principal parts carry carons; OCR Tafai confuses the clear r with f.
[c1-l011 >] *ou estar a couſa perfeita, & ſem falta.*
[c1-l011 note] The gloss continues with a thing being complete and lacking nothing. This explanatory sense connects the verb to the negative forms below.
[c1-l012 >] *¶* Yorozzuno cotoni tarǒta fitogia. *Ho*
[c1-l012 note] Yorozzuno cotoni tarǒta fitogia describes a person accomplished in everything. The final Ho begins Homem on the following row without a supplied hyphen.
[c1-l013 >] *mem perfeito em tudo. ¶* Iinbutno tarǒ-
[c1-l013 note] Mem completes Homem; the next example begins Iinbutno tarǒta, describing completeness or excellence of personal appearance. Keep the checked final t in Iinbut rather than expanding it.
[c1-l014 >] ta fito gia. *He peßoa de bom parecer, &*
[c1-l014 note] Completes tarǒta fito gia and starts its translation: a person of good appearance. The gloss is aesthetic here, not a claim about moral character.
[c1-l015 >] *de perfeita fermoſura. O negatiuo deste verbo.*
[c1-l015 note] Perfect beauty completes the second example. O negatiuo deste verbo introduces the negative forms that follow; historical fermoſura remains unchanged.
[c1-l016 >] *Vt,* Tarauanu, tarauazu. *Significa faltar.*
[c1-l016 note] Tarawanu and tarawazu are negatives meaning to be lacking. The scan supports faltar, not OCR ſaltar, which would wrongly change the meaning to jumping.
[c1-l017] Tarameqi, u. l, darameqi, u. *Fazer ſom*
[c1-l017 note] Tarameqi and darameqi are alternatives for producing a sound, with the dictionary supplying their verb endings. The water-drop comparison on the next line specifies the sound.
[c1-l018 >] *como quãdo caem goteiras dagoa, ou choue, &c.*
[c1-l018 note] The sound is likened to dripping water or rain. Preserve the contraction dagoa and the historical choue rather than adding a modern vowel or apostrophe.
[c1-l019] Taraju. *Palmeira braua em cujas folhas eſcreuẽ*
[c1-l019 note] Taraju is described as a wild palm whose leaves serve for writing. The visible headword starts with T, correcting Faraju; the final abbreviation in eſcreuẽ is retained.
[c1-l020 >] *os da India.*
[c1-l020 note] Os da India supplies the people who write on the palm leaves. This short line completes the preceding relative clause and has no independent headword.
[c1-l021] Tararito. *Modo, ou ſõ de goteiar, ou cair agoa.*
[c1-l021 note] Tararito expresses the manner or sound of dripping water. The initial is T and the mark over the o of ſõ is a tilde, not the OCR grave accent.
[c1-l022] Taratarato. *Adu. Modo de gotejar. Vt,*
[c1-l022 note] Taratarato is an adverbial representation of dripping. Adu. and Vt belong to the italic grammatical apparatus; the Japanese example begins on the following line.
[c1-l023 >] Mizzuga taratarato votçuru. *Cai a agoa*
[c1-l023 note] Mizzuga taratarato votçuru means water falls dripping. The scan supports votçuru, not votçuriu, and the full agoa at the edge rather than a divided ago-.
[c1-l024 >] *dalto ſoando.*
[c1-l024 note] Dalto ſoando completes the Portuguese example: falling from above with a sound. The short line modifies the water's fall, not a new sense of the next verb.
[c1-l025] Taraxi, ſu, aita. *Enganar, ou embair.*
[c1-l025 note] This taraxi means to deceive or beguile. The initial gloss reads Enganar, not Enzanar; its meaning distinguishes this verb from the homographic pouring verb below.
[c1-l026] Taraxi. l, taraxite. *Enganador, ou embaidor.*
[c1-l026 note] Taraxi and taraxite are names for a deceiver or beguiler. The alternative marker belongs to the dictionary apparatus, and the final punctuation is visible beside embaidor.
[c1-l027] Taraxi, ſu, aita. *Derramar. Vt,* Nami-
[c1-l027 note] The separate taraxi entry means to pour or shed. Nami- begins the first example's namida, tears, and the printed division must remain on this physical row.
[c1-l028 >] dauo taraſu. *Chorar, ou derramar lagri-*
[c1-l028 note] Dauo completes namidauo taraſu, shedding tears or crying. The Portuguese lagri- likewise continues into mas below; neither divided word is a new headword.
[c1-l029 >] *mas. ¶* Yodareuo taraſu. *Babarſe.*
[c1-l029 note] After completing lagrimas, Yodareuo taraſu supplies the saliva example, glossed Babarſe. This illustrates dribbling or drooling rather than weeping.
[c1-l030 >] *¶* Chiuo taraſu. *Derramar ſangue.*
[c1-l030 note] Chiuo taraſu means shedding blood. The Portuguese ſangue identifies chi here as blood, not another Japanese homophone.
[c1-l031] Tarayo. *Folhas de ola em que eſcreuem os da*
[c1-l031 note] Tarayo names ola leaves used as writing material. The gloss refers back to the palm-leaf context of Taraju, without requiring a modern botanical identification.
[c1-l032 >] *India.*
[c1-l032 note] India completes os da India, the people using those leaves for writing. The line belongs entirely to Tarayo's definition.
[c1-l033] Tare. *Quem. Vt,* Tarega maittaca. l,
[c1-l033 note] Tare asks who; Tarega maittaca asks who came. The visibly comma-punctuated l introduces the alternative question on the next line.
[c1-l034 >] tareca maitta. *Quem veo? ¶* Tare na-
[c1-l034 note] Tareca maitta is the alternate question, translated Quem veo? The final printed question mark replaces OCR ẽ; Tare na- begins the indefinite expression below.
[c1-l035 >] ritomo. *Quem quer que ſeja.*
[c1-l035 note] Ritomo completes Tare naritomo, whoever it may be. Keep the physical word division and the source's older form rather than replacing it with modern dare.
[c1-l036] Tare, ruru, eta. *Deitar de cima pera baixo.*
[c1-l036 note] Tare, taruru, tareta describes letting something down from above. The following examples develop the literal downward action and its figurative extensions.
[c1-l037 >] *¶* Xôbenuo taruru. *Ourinar por ſi.*
[c1-l037 note] Xôbenuo taruru is the urination example. The o-mark is an upward-pointing circumflex on reinspection; por ſi, not par ſi, completes the Portuguese expression.
[c1-l038 >] *¶* Daibenuo taruru. *Fazer camara por*
[c1-l038 note] Daibenuo taruru gives the parallel defecation example. Fazer camara is the historical Portuguese expression, with por ſi completed on the next row.
[c1-l039 >] *ſi. ¶* Yodareuo taruru. *Babarſe. ¶* Su-
[c1-l039 note] After completing the previous phrase, Yodareuo taruru means to drool. Su- starts sudare, the blinds in the next example; it is not part of Babarſe.
[c1-l040 >] dareuo taruru. *Abaixar as eſteiras*
[c1-l040 note] Dareuo completes Sudareuo taruru, lowering blinds. The Portuguese calls them eſteiras, mats, and the next line specifies their use at windows.
[c1-l041 >] *das janelas, &c. ¶* Tçuriuo taru-
[c1-l041 note] Das janelas completes the window-blind gloss. Tçuriuo taru- starts another application of the same verb, now to lowering fishing tackle.
[c1-l042 >] ru. i, Tçuribaride iuouo tçuru. *Peſcar cõ*
[c1-l042 note] The explanatory Japanese Tçuribaride iuouo tçuru identifies fishing with a hook. Iuo is the fish being caught; do not replace the source's historical fish word with a modern synonym.
[c1-l043 >] *anzol. ¶* Iifiuo taruru. *Vſar de miſe-*
[c1-l043 note] Anzol completes the fishing gloss. Iifiuo taruru then means to extend mercy, introducing a figurative use; the initial Ii has consonantal value, not two separate vowels here.
[c1-l044 >] *ricordia. ¶* Xitauo taruru. *Botar a lin-*
[c1-l044 note] Ricordia completes miſericordia. Xitauo taruru introduces putting the tongue out and down; the translation's lin- continues into the printed gue below, an anomalous ending retained from the scan.
[c1-l045 >] *gue fora derrubandoa pera baixo. ¶* Cami-
[c1-l045 note] Gue completes the printed lin-/gue. Second-pass enlargement shows an e with an open counter, not the expected a of lingua; preserve the anomaly. Cami- then begins the hair example after the description of letting the tongue hang downward.
[c1-l046 >] uo taruru. *Rapar a cabeça dos mininos co-*
[c1-l046 note] Uo completes Camiuo taruru, glossed as shaving children's heads. Retain the dictionary's explanation rather than substituting the modern literal sense of letting hair hang.
[c1-l047 >] *mo custumão em Iapão do primeiro ate os tres*
[c1-l047 note] The shaving practice is described as customary in Japan from the first through the third year. Annos at the top of column 2 completes this sentence across the column boundary.

[c2-l009] Taremuxiro. *Eſteiras dependuradas por guar-*

[c2-l010 >] *da da porta, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] Da completes guarda: the hanging mats serve as protection or screening at the door. The line is visibly indented beneath the headword.
[c2-l011] Tarenaritomo. *Quem quer que ſeja.*
[c2-l011 note] Tarenaritomo means whoever it may be. This independent entry repeats the expression already illustrated under Tare in column 1.
[c2-l012] Ta enuno. *Guardaporta de linho canemo.*
[c2-l012 note] The gloss describes a hemp-cloth door hanging, identifying the entry as Tarenuno in the supplied NINJAL index. The r-position is severely abraded; retain the visible Ta enuno with a gap rather than silently supplying a secure letter. Guardaporta is continuous.
[c2-l013] Tari, ru, atta. *Correr como ſangue, ſuor, &c.*
[c2-l013 note] This tari is flowing or running, illustrated by blood and sweat. Correr and the closing &c. are visible; OCR Corter and &t. are not Portuguese variants to retain.
[c2-l014] Tari, ru, ata. *Verb. defect. Estar a couſa*
[c2-l014 note] The next tari is the defective verb of sufficiency or completeness. Preserve the visibly single t in ata despite the preceding atta and the NINJAL normalization; defect. has f, and no hyphen follows couſa.
[c2-l015 >] *perfeita, & juſta. Vt,* Cazuni tatta. *Estar*
[c2-l015 note] Cazuni tatta illustrates a number being complete or exact. The translation joins perfeita and juſta, and the printed tatta is not modernized to another past form.
[c2-l016 >] *o numero perfeito. O ſeu negatiuo,* Taranu.
[c2-l016 note] The number gloss ends, and Taranu is explicitly introduced as the negative of this verb. Its roman type contrasts with the surrounding Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l017 >] *Significa faltar. ¶* Gojǔni taranu. *A-*
[c2-l017 note] Taranu means to be short of something. Gojǔni taranu supplies an example of not yet reaching fifty, with the Portuguese A- beginning Ainda on the next row.
[c2-l018 >] *inda não chega aos 50. ¶* Nijǔni futa-
[c2-l018 note] Completes Ainda não chega aos 50, not yet fifty. The next example begins being two short of twenty; the round final numeral is recorded as 0 rather than the OCR letter o.
[c2-l019 >] tçu taranu. *Faltão dous pera vinte. ¶* A-
[c2-l019 note] Tçu completes futatçu, two: two are lacking to make twenty. Faltão has t rather than OCR i; the final A- begins Amarazu on the following row.
[c2-l020 >] marazu tarazuxite totonoyeyo. *Apare-*
[c2-l020 note] Amarazu tarazuxite totonoyeyo asks for an arrangement with neither surplus nor deficiency. Preserve the historical imperative and the physical A-/marazu division rather than rewriting the syntax.
[c2-l021 >] *lhai de maneira que não ſobeje, nem falte. He*
[c2-l021 note] Lhai completes aparelhai, prepare or arrange. The Portuguese repeats the instruction that nothing should be excessive or missing, then begins a usage qualification.
[c2-l022 >] *ſentido particular neſte modo de falar.*
[c2-l022 note] The dictionary calls this a particular meaning in this manner of speaking. The visible words are modo and falar, replacing OCR mode and falor.
[c2-l023] Tariǒ. Yoſonoriǒ. *Rẽda, ou terras alheas.*
[c2-l023 note] Tariǒ concerns another person's revenues or lands, explained by Yoſonoriǒ. The Portuguese adjective reads alheas, agreeing with terras; the carons and abbreviated Rẽda are retained.
[c2-l024] Tariqi. Tano chicara. *Forças alheas, ou*
[c2-l024 note] Tariqi is another's strength or aid, explicitly Tano chicara. Enlargement reveals the weak dot and foot at the first i-position, supporting Tariqi rather than Tarqi; alheas corrects OCR albeas.
[c2-l025 >] *de outro.*
[c2-l025 note] De outro completes the explanation of strength belonging to another. It does not introduce a theological qualification beyond what the printed gloss itself states.
[c2-l026] Tariǔ. Bechino nagare, l, tçutaye. *Outra*
[c2-l026 note] Tariǔ denotes another school or transmitted tradition. Nagare has a descending g, not OCR p; Bechino and tçutaye give Japanese explanations rather than words embedded in Portuguese.
[c2-l027 >] *arte, doutrina, ou ſeita, &c.*
[c2-l027 note] The Portuguese lists another art, doctrine, or sect. Second-pass enlargement shows a narrow i between e and t in ſeita, correcting OCR ſeta; the definition's broad range should not be narrowed to religion alone.
[c2-l028] Taru. *Piparite, ou barça.*
[c2-l028 note] Taru is a cask or barrel, rendered Piparite or barça. The unusual printed diminutive Piparite is retained rather than regularized from a modern Portuguese dictionary.
[c2-l029] Tarume, uru, eta. *Afroxar, ou alargar. Vt,*
[c2-l029 note] Tarume is the transitive act of loosening or making slack. The principal parts lead to tarumuru, the form used in the rope example below.
[c2-l030 >] Tçunauo tarumuru. *Afroxar a corda.*
[c2-l030 note] Tçunauo tarumuru means to loosen the rope. The object marker uo and the Portuguese corda make the transitive construction clear.
[c2-l031 >] *¶ Per met.* Cocorouo tarumuru. i, Yu-
[c2-l031 note] The figurative example is relaxing one's attention or resolve, equated with Yudan ſuru. The visible capital Y replaces OCR I in the divided Yu-; Per met. is printed separately here.
[c2-l032 >] dan ſuru. *Deſcuidarſe, ou afroxar nalgũa*
[c2-l032 note] Dan completes Yudan ſuru, becoming careless or slack. The damaged final Portuguese word reads nalgũa, in some, and retains its visible tilde rather than the OCR noise nalhjia.
[c2-l033 >] *obra, &c.*
[c2-l033 note] Obra completes the explanation of slackening in some work or task. This short continuation belongs to the figurative sense of Tarume.
[c2-l034] Tarumi, u, ǔda. *Afroxar. ¶ Permet.*
[c2-l034 note] Tarumi is the corresponding intransitive loosening or slackening. Afroxar has f, and the abbreviated Permet. ends with t; the long-u principal part remains as printed.
[c2-l035 >] Cocoroga tarumu. *Deſcuidarſe, ou en-*

[c2-l036 >] *tibiarſe.*
[c2-l036 note] Tibiarſe completes entibiarſe, to become lukewarm or lose ardour. It is the second Portuguese paraphrase of the preceding figurative example.
[c2-l037] Tarumiga yuqu. *Ir afroxando couſa que eſta-*
[c2-l037 note] Tarumiga yuqu describes progressive slackening. The Portuguese infinitive is Ir, not OCR Ie; eſta- begins the description of a thing that had been taut.
[c2-l038 >] *ua teſa. ¶ Item, permet. Se diz de couſa*
[c2-l038 note] Va completes eſtaua teſa, had been taut. The paragraph then announces a figurative application, retaining the compact printed permet. rather than expanding its abbreviation.
[c2-l039 >] *que hia com feruor, & ſe eſfriou, ou afroxou.*
[c2-l039 note] The figurative object had been proceeding with ardour but cooled or slackened. The worn endings support eſfriou and afroxou, not eſfricu or afronou; the entry ends here, without a continuation hyphen.
[c2-l040] Taruqi. *Aguieiros.*
[c2-l040 note] Taruqi names rafters, rendered by the historical building term Aguieiros. The internal letter is i rather than the OCR j; no modernization of the Portuguese term is imposed.
[c2-l041] Taruqi bana. *Frontaria dos aguieiros que eſtão*
[c2-l041 note] Taruqi bana concerns the exposed front or ends of rafters. The gloss's final word is eſtão, not eſtũs, and its description continues below.
[c2-l042 >] *com as pontas juntas.*
[c2-l042 note] Com as pontas juntas describes the rafters with their ends together. This completes the architectural definition rather than beginning a separate construction term.
[c2-l043] Taruxen. *Caixas que ſeruem pera ſe darem de*
[c2-l043 note] Taruxen is defined as boxes used for gift-giving. The precise relationship to wine casks is supplied on the next row; the gloss does not itself call the boxes money.
[c2-l044 >] *preſente em lugar de barças de vinho.*
[c2-l044 note] The boxes are given in place of casks of wine. Preserve barças as the source's vessel term and avoid adding contents that the definition does not specify.
[c2-l045] Taſacu. *Obra feita por outro. ¶* Iiſacu.
[c2-l045 note] Taſacu means work done by another person. Iiſacu begins the contrasting example of one's own work; restore the line to the headword margin, not continuation indentation.
[c2-l046 >] *Obra q̃ hum fez cõ ſuas mãos. Vide ſuo loco.*
[c2-l046 note] Iiſacu is work someone made with his own hands, followed by a see-its-entry cross-reference. The abbreviations q̃ and cõ and the printed mãos remain unexpanded.
[c2-l047] Taſai. Tano tçuma. *Molher alhea.*
[c2-l047 note] Taſai means another person's wife, explicitly Tano tçuma. The abraded m is supported by its surviving strokes and the wife gloss; remove the OCR's spurious internal space and restore headword-margin placement.

[u2b-l001 note] The bottom-right word is the catchword Taſo., confirmed by the first entry of f475. Its final o and full stop are visible; it is page furniture rather than another body entry.
