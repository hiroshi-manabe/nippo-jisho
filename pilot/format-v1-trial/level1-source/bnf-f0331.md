[c1-l002 note] Hito completes the preceding Japanese phrase, translated as curious or inquisitive. The OCR borrowed Cada couſa, ou from the next row; that duplicate is removed here and remains fully represented in c1-l003.
[c1-l003] Monogoto. l, monogotoni. *Cada couſa, ou*
[c1-l003 note] Monogoto and monogotoni mean each thing and in each thing. This independent entry starts at the column margin.
[c1-l004 >] *em cada couſa.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the distributive explanation. The OCR's attached Metendoſe no belongs to c1-l005, where it is retained; no printed material is lost by removing that duplicate here.
[c1-l005] Monogoxini. i, Fedatete. *Metendoſe no*
[c1-l005 note] Monogoshi ni means with something intervening, paraphrased by hedatete. The Portuguese begins a description of an object placed between observer and target.
[c1-l006 >] *meo algũa couſa, como parede, grades, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] The intervening object may be a wall, bars, or similar barrier. The printed meo is preserved rather than modernized to meio.
[c1-l007 >] *Vt,* Monogoxini nanzouo qiqu. *Ouuir*
[c1-l007 note] Monogoshi ni nanzo wo kiku means hearing something through an intervening barrier. The damaged sequence is provisionally read nanzouo qiqu from its remaining rounded types and Japanese construction, replacing the OCR's unparseable nanzqumqiqu; the vowel after z remains a caveat.
[c1-l008 >] *algũa couſa metendoſe no meo parede, taboa,*
[c1-l008 note] Continues the translation of hearing something with a wall or board in between. The physical row remains separate although the sentence runs on.
[c1-l009 >] *&c. ¶* Monogoxini miru. *Ver por*
[c1-l009 note] Introduces seeing through or past an intervening object. The scan supports the g in Monogoxini, not the OCR's p.
[c1-l010 >] *antre algũa couſa que està no meo.*
[c1-l010 note] Completes the seeing example with something situated in between. The historical antre and grave in està are retained.
[c1-l011] Monogurui. *Doudo. ¶* Monoguruini na-
[c1-l011 note] Monogurui denotes a mad person or madness in this gloss. The example begins becoming mad and continues naru below.
[c1-l012 >] ru. *Endoudecer.*
[c1-l012 note] Completes monogurui ni naru, to become mad. Endoudecer is the printed Portuguese verb and is not modernized.
[c1-l013] Monoguruuaxij. *O eſtar, ou ſer, como*
[c1-l013 note] Monoguruuaxij describes being mad or behaving like a mad person. After the NINJAL lexical check prompted reinspection, enlargement clearly resolves two successive u-types after r; both are retained, rather than the first pass's mistaken simplification. The Portuguese continues below.
[c1-l014 >] *doudo.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes the comparison with a mad person. This short gloss continuation is indented rather than a new headword.
[c1-l015 >] Monoguruuaxiſa.
[c1-l015 note] This is the nominal derivative for madness or mad behaviour. Enlarged comparison with the adjective above confirms two u-types after r and a long ſ in the ending; the short derivative row stays indented.
[c1-l016 >] Monoguruuaxǔ.
[c1-l016 note] This is the adverbial derivative of the same adjective. The scan confirms the doubled u sequence after r and a downward-pointing caron over final u; neither is simplified.
[c1-l017] Monoiy. *O que falla, ou pratica bẽ. ¶* Mo-
[c1-l017 note] Monoii is someone who speaks or converses well. The last abbreviated word is bẽ, not OCR tẽ; Mo- begins the further example below.
[c1-l018 >] noiygotoga aru. *Auer, ou correr pratica*
[c1-l018 note] Monoiigoto ga aru means that talk or discussion is current. The Portuguese begins the explanation of such circulating talk.
[c1-l019 >] *como de aleuantamento, guerra, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] The talk may concern an uprising, war, and similar matters. This qualifies the preceding expression rather than narrowing every use of monoii to military discussion.
[c1-l020] Mono imi. *Agouro. ¶* Monoimino ji-
[c1-l020 note] Monoimi is explained through ominous or taboo observances. The printed space in the headword is retained; ji- begins jibun, a time or period.
[c1-l021 >] bun. *Tempo em que os gentios tem por a-*
[c1-l021 note] Jibun completes the Japanese phrase for a period of abstention. The Portuguese describes a time when the people called gentios by the dictionary regard an act as inauspicious.
[c1-l022 >] *gouro fazer algũa couſa, como ir diante do* Ca-
[c1-l022 note] Completes agouro and gives approaching a kami as an example of the avoided action. The embedded Japanese Ca- is kept in its locally upright type and continues on the next row.
[c1-l023 >] mi*, &c. por reſpeito da morte dalguem, ou*
[c1-l023 note] Mi completes Cami. The abstention is associated with someone's death or another circumstance; reſpeito corrects OCR veſpeito, without endorsing the dictionary's description as a universal rule.
[c1-l024 >] *por outra couſa.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the alternative explanation for the observance. This is continuous Portuguese prose attached to monoimi.
[c1-l025] Monomi. *Couſa pera ver, ou apraziuel à vi-*
[c1-l025 note] Monomi first denotes something worth seeing or pleasing to the eye. The Portuguese divides vista at the physical line boundary.
[c1-l026 >] *ſta. Vt,* Monomina coto. *Couſa pera ver.*
[c1-l026 note] Monomina koto exemplifies something to see. The final stop after ver is visible and restored; ſta completes the previous row's vista.
[c1-l027 >] *¶ Item, Ianelinha, ou fresta por onde ſe ve*
[c1-l027 note] A second meaning is a small window or slit through which one sees. The paragraph sign omitted by OCR is restored at the start of this physical row.
[c1-l028 >] *de dentro das andas, carro, &c.*
[c1-l028 note] Specifies looking from inside a litter, carriage, or similar conveyance. This completes the viewing-aperture sense of monomi.
[c1-l029] Monomǒ. *Modo de chamar à porta, como*
[c1-l029 note] Monomoo is a formula for calling at a door. The long-o caron is visible; the Portuguese comparison continues below.
[c1-l030 >] *quem diz oula.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the comparison with a call equivalent to hallo. The printed oula is retained rather than replaced by a modern Portuguese greeting.
[c1-l031] Monomonoxij. *Peſſoa, como ſoldado bem poſto,*
[c1-l031 note] Monomonoshii describes an imposing person, exemplified by a well-equipped soldier. The source's description concerns bearing and outward appearance.
[c1-l032 >] *& concertado, & que mostra no exterior, &*
[c1-l032 note] Continues the image of someone well arranged or equipped, whose outward appearance suggests capability. The final conjunction belongs to the next row's description of manner.
[c1-l033 >] *modo que farà façanhas. ¶* Monomono-
[c1-l033 note] Completes the impression that the person will perform notable feats. The cedilla in façanhas is visible; Monomono- starts the next example.
[c1-l034 >] xijtei. *Modo, ou aparencia de peßoa que*
[c1-l034 note] Monomonoshii tei describes an imposing manner or appearance. The gap before t is very narrow; joined xijtei is provisionally retained in agreement with the aligned draft, while its grammatical components are distinguished only in this note.
[c1-l035 >] *tem brio, & ſe arma bem, &c.*
[c1-l035 note] The person has spirit or mettle and arms himself well. This completes the outward-bearing example rather than defining a separate military rank.
[c1-l036] Monono fon. *Cartapacio eſcrito, ou liuro.*
[c1-l036 note] Mono no hon is a written notebook or book. The historical h-row spelling fon and Portuguese liuro are retained.
[c1-l037] Mononofu. *Soldados.*
[c1-l037 note] Mononofu denotes warriors or soldiers. The Portuguese gives the plural soldados directly.
[c1-l038] Mononogu. *Armas.*
[c1-l038 note] Mononogu here means arms or military equipment. It is a separate entry from the similar-looking expression about a frightened horse that follows.
[c1-l039] Mononogu vodoroqi. *O eſpantarſe o caualo*
[c1-l039 note] This expression describes a horse taking fright. Its printed Mononogu is retained even though the associated meaning differs from the preceding arms entry.
[c1-l040 >] *com algum ſom, ou estrondo q̃ ouue. ¶* Mo-
[c1-l040 note] The horse is startled by a sound or crash it hears. The abbreviation q̃ is preserved, and Mo- starts the example repeated on the next row.
[c1-l041 >] nonogu vodoroqi ſuru vma. *Caualo eſpã-*
[c1-l041 note] Completes the expression for a horse that startles at noise. The Portuguese adjective continues after the abbreviated nasal syllable.
[c1-l042 >] *tadiço do ſom, ou voz q̃ ouue.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the description of sensitivity to a sound or voice. The scan has ouue with two u types, correcting the draft's oue.
[c1-l043] Mononi voſouaruru. *Ficar denoite em ſonhos*
[c1-l043 note] Mono ni osowaruru describes being overcome by something in a dream. Denoite is the closely set Portuguese night-time expression, not OCR dencite; the account continues with fear and inability to speak.
[c1-l044 >] *com medo dalgũa couſa ſem poder falar, co-*
[c1-l044 note] The dreamer is afraid of something and cannot speak. Co- begins the comparison completed below; this describes the dictionary's experience-label, not a modern medical diagnosis.
[c1-l045 >] *mo quem eſtà com o peſadelo.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes the comparison with someone suffering a nightmare. The wording ties the preceding fear and speechlessness to the dictionary's pesadelo gloss.
[c1-l046] Mononoqe. *Demonio, rapoſa, ou couſa ſeme-*
[c1-l046 note] Mononoke is described as a demon, fox, or similar agency. The Japanese headword and Demonio correct the OCR's letter and spacing errors; the faint short terminal stroke is provisionally retained as a hyphen.
[c1-l047 >] *lhante que dizem estar no corpo de hum en-*
[c1-l047 note] The source explicitly says that such an agency is said to be in the body of a possessed person. The scan supports dizem, corpo, and hum; en- continues the final Portuguese word below.
[c1-l048 >] *demoninhado.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes endemoninhado. Enlargement shows the weak n before h; the historical nh spelling is retained rather than replaced with modern endemoniado. This last body row is distinct from the lower page rule.

[c2-l011] Monoſugoi. *Couſa ſolitaria, & que faz me-*

[c2-l012 >] *do, como hum mato grande, & ſolitario.*

[c2-l013] Monotachigatana. *Hum inſtrumento de fer-*
[c2-l013 note] Monotachigatana is an iron cutting implement. The definition continues with its broad blade and use in cutting garments.
[c2-l014 >] *ro, como faca larga com que cortão de vestir.*

[c2-l015] Monotçucuri. *Laurador que cultiua bem os*

[c2-l016 >] *campos, ou varzeas. Palaura vſada em al-*

[c2-l017 >] *gũas partes do Cami entre os lauradores.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes the regional restriction: the word is used among farmers in some parts of Cami. Cami is an embedded Japanese regional designation, but its locally italic printing is retained.
[c2-l018] Monovarai. *Couſa ridiculoſa, ou de que ſe po*
[c2-l018 note] Monowarai denotes something laughable or mockable. The divided po/de has no securely visible terminal hyphen here, so none is supplied.
[c2-l019 >] *de rir, & zombar. ¶* Monovaraini na-
[c2-l019 note] Completes poder rir e zombar, then begins becoming an object of ridicule. The faint i of Monovaraini is restored from the visible trace and the noun above, rather than retaining OCR Monovara ni.
[c2-l020 >] ru. *Dar occaſião de ſe rirem, & moteja-*

[c2-l022] Monovchi. *Vt,* Catanano monovchi. *Par-*
