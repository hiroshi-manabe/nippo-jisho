## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Faxitaca. *Hũa laya de falcões.*
[c1-l001 note] Hashitaka names a kind of hawk. The Portuguese gives only a broad falcon category, so a modern species identification is not supplied.
[c1-l002] Faxitamono. *Couſa imperfeita, ou falta pera*
[c1-l002 note] Hashitamono here means something incomplete or falling short. The scan reads Couſa, not Conta; the adjective imperfeita describes the thing, not necessarily an account.
[c1-l003 >] *algum numero, ou obra, &c.*
[c1-l003 note] The shortfall may concern a number or a piece of work. This continues the preceding definition and should not be read as a separate Japanese entry.
[c1-l004] Faxitamono, l, faxita. *Certa laya de molhe-*
[c1-l004 note] Hashitamono and hashita are alternative names for female servants in this entry, distinct from the numerical sense immediately above. The Portuguese molhe- continues below.
[c1-l005 >] *res baixas que ſeruem em caſa dos ſenhores.*
[c1-l005 note] The definition describes lower-ranking women serving in lords' households. Baixas is a social classification in the dictionary's wording, not a modern judgment added here.
[c1-l006] Faxitana. *Couſa imperfeita, ou que não he in-*
[c1-l006 note] Hashitana denotes something incomplete. The final in- belongs to inteira on the following line; it is not a separate abbreviated word.
[c1-l007 >] *teira, & perfeita no numero, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] This completes the description as not whole or complete in number. The qualification helps distinguish this entry from the harshness sense of hashitanai below.
[c1-l008] Faxitanai. *Couſa aſpera, & riſpida. Vt,*
[c1-l008 note] Hashitanai is glossed as harsh or rough, rather than simply the modern sense 'immodest.' The following literary example is needed to understand that range.
[c1-l009 >] Faxitanai cotogarani naru. Feiq. *Lib.*
[c1-l009 note] Hashitanai kotogara ni naru is followed by a Feiq. citation, not more Japanese. The gloss below interprets the expression in terms of harsh speech.
[c1-l010 >] *2. Falar aſperamente, ou moſtrar aſpereza*
[c1-l010 note] The numeral completes the citation to book 2; the remaining words explain speaking harshly or showing harshness. Historical aſperamente is retained without modern accentuation.
[c1-l011 >] *nas palauras.*
[c1-l011 note] Nas palauras completes 'show harshness in one's words.' This short Portuguese line belongs to the example above, not to the derivative below.
[c1-l012 >] Faxitanaſa.
[c1-l012 note] Hashitanasa is the nominal derivative in -sa, expressing harshness. Its isolated placement does not imply a missing Portuguese definition.
[c1-l013 >] Faxitanǒ.
[c1-l013 note] Hashitanǒ is the contracted adverbial form of hashitanai. The open long-vowel mark is preserved rather than replacing it with the modern -ku ending.
[c1-l014] Faxiyagaxi, ſu, aita. *Enxugar, ou deſecar*
[c1-l014 note] Hashiyagashi represents hashiyagasu, to dry something, here by heat. This is a drying sense of the hasyag- family, not the modern excited-behavior sense alone.
[c1-l015 >] *ao fogo.*
[c1-l015 note] Ao fogo specifies drying at the fire. The whole short line qualifies the verb above; no additional Japanese text is implied.
[c1-l016] Faxiyague, ru, eta. *Idem.*
[c1-l016 note] Hashiyage is the related drying form; Idem refers back to the preceding definition. The historical stem and supplied inflection are retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l017] Faxizzume. *Principio, ou entrada da ponte.*
[c1-l017 note] Hashizume is the beginning or approach of a bridge. The spelling zz gives the historical voiced tsu-series sound, consistent with the compound's second member.
[c1-l018] Faxǒ. Namino vye. *Em cima das õdas. S.*
[c1-l018 note] Hashǒ is explained by nami no ue, 'on the waves.' The scan abbreviates ondas as õdas; the Japanese long vowel belongs to the Sino-Japanese compound, not hashi 'bridge.'
[c1-l019] Faxxen. *Vt,* Doyǒ faxxen. *Certas con-*
[c1-l019 note] Hassen appears in the calendrical expression doyǒ hassen. The Portuguese begins con- here and continues junções below; Vt introduces the Japanese example.
[c1-l020 >] *junções, ou constellações de tempos q̃ concorrẽ*
[c1-l020 note] Junções completes conjunções, describing calendrical conjunctions or combinations. The initial descender is j, not long s, and the visible nasal mark is retained.
[c1-l021 >] *por vezes no anno, as quais os Iapões obſer-*
[c1-l021 note] The periods recur during the year and are observed by Japanese people. This is the dictionary's account of calendrical practice, not an endorsement of its predictive validity.
[c1-l022 >] *uão pera fazer algũa couſa, ou deixar de a*
[c1-l022 note] The sentence says these times affect whether an activity is undertaken or omitted. Algũa keeps the scan's nasal abbreviation rather than spelling out alguma.
[c1-l023 >] *fazer.*
[c1-l023 note] Fazer closes deixar de a fazer: to refrain from doing it. Its small physical row remains part of the full calendrical explanation.
[c1-l024] Faxxenmai. *Hum pequeno de pao que ſe quei-*
[c1-l024 note] Hassenmai names a small wooden piece burned ritually. The scan includes de before pao, despite the compressed phrase Hum pequeno de pao.
[c1-l025 >] *ma diante do Idolo cõ certas cerimonias.*
[c1-l025 note] The wooden piece is burned before an idol with ceremonies. Idolo is the missionary dictionary's terminology; the line does not establish a more precise ritual identification.
[c1-l026] Faxxeqi. *Lei, ou prohibição: a propria palaura*
[c1-l026 note] Hasseqi is glossed as a law or prohibition, then explicitly redirected to another form. The difference between e and i is intentional in the printed explanation.
[c1-l027 >] *he* Faxxiqi.
[c1-l027 note] The preferred form is Faxxiqi, hashiki. It is an embedded Japanese spelling in Portuguese prose, not a new definition or an error to harmonize with Faxxeqi.
[c1-l028] Faxxi, ſuru, ita. *Afaſtar, ou botar fora.*
[c1-l028 note] Here hassuru means to expel or exclude. The Portuguese afastar/botar fora supports this sense, distinct from 'emit' in the next homographic entry.
[c1-l029 >] *Vt,* Connichi yori chôruino ichimonuo
[c1-l029 note] Konnichi yori chōrui no ichimon wo supplies 'from today, from the family of birds.' The object belongs to the verb on the next physical line.
[c1-l030 >] faſſuruzoto. Fab. *Deſdoje vos exclui-*
[c1-l030 note] Hassuru zo to completes the quoted exclusion; Fab. is its source label. Deſdoje begins the Portuguese rendering 'from today we exclude you.'
[c1-l031 >] *mos da familia das aues.*
[c1-l031 note] This completes exclui-mos and identifies the family of birds, matching chōrui no ichimon. The cross-line syntax explains the otherwise abrupt initial fragment.
[c1-l032] Faxxi, ſuru, ita. *Mouer, ou excitar. Vt,*
[c1-l032 note] This second hassuru entry means to stir or bring forth. Its examples concern anger, words, and voice, rather than exclusion from a group.
[c1-l033 >] Icariuo faſſuru. *Agaſtarſe. ¶* Icariga
[c1-l033 note] Ikari wo hassuru means to give vent to anger. The next example switches from object-marker wo to subject-marker ga; the capital I is visible at the row's beginning.
[c1-l034 >] faſſuru. *Idem. ¶* Gongouo faſſuru.
[c1-l034 note] The first hassuru finishes 'anger arises'; gongo wo hassuru then introduces uttering words. The shared verb has two differently structured examples on this line.
[c1-l035 >] *Começar a falar. ¶* Coyeuo faſſuru.
[c1-l035 note] The Portuguese 'begin to speak' explains gongo wo hassuru. Koe wo hassuru introduces the separate example of emitting one's voice.
[c1-l036 >] *Deitar a voz.*
[c1-l036 note] Deitar a voz gives the voice-emission sense, completing the previous example. It is not the start of the following legal entry.
[c1-l037] Faxxiqi. Fatto, nori. *Ley, ou prohibição.*
[c1-l037 note] Hashiki is glossed by hatto and nori, both law-related words. This confirms the earlier cross-reference from hasseqi without requiring identical Portuguese spelling of lei/Ley.
[c1-l038] Faxxito. *Adu. Modo de dar a frecha, ou*
[c1-l038 note] Hashitto is an expressive adverb for an arrow striking or a blow landing. The word frecha is historical Portuguese and is not normalized to flecha.
[c1-l039 >] *de dar pancada, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] The scan reads pancada, a blow, not pancara. It completes the paired impact examples used to explain the Japanese expressive adverb.
[c1-l040] Faxxù. *As oito ſeitas que ha em Iapão: ſ.*
[c1-l040 note] Hasshū denotes eight Buddhist schools in Japan. The following list supplies the eight; the printed ù is retained although modern romanization would use ū.
[c1-l041 >] Faſſǒ, ſanron, cuxa, jǒjit, rixù, qe-
[c1-l041 note] The list begins Hossō, Sanron, Kusha, Jōjitsu, and Risshū, then qe- starts Kegon. The scan's rixù supports Risshū, not the earlier OCR nixù.
[c1-l042 >] gon, tendai, xingon.
[c1-l042 note] Gon completes qe-gon, followed by Tendai and Shingon. Together with the preceding row these are the eight schools, not four additional headwords.
[c1-l043] Faxxun. *Oitenta annos.*
[c1-l043 note] Hasshun means eighty years, matching oitenta annos. Its final n distinguishes it from hasshū 'eight schools' immediately above.
[c1-l044] Faya. *Adu. Ia.*
[c1-l044 note] Haya is an adverb 'already,' represented by Portuguese Ia. The i/j historical spelling is kept rather than silently changing it to modern já.
[c1-l045] Fayabaxiri. *Correo. ¶ Item, O ir, ou*
[c1-l045 note] Hayabashiri is a courier and also the act of going rapidly. Item opens a second sense within the same entry, continued on the next row.
[c1-l046 >] *correr de preſſa.*
[c1-l046 note] Correr de preſſa completes the rapid-motion sense of hayabashiri. It does not introduce another kind of courier.
[c1-l047] Fayabaya. *Adu. De preſſa.*
[c1-l047 note] Hayabaya is an adverb meaning quickly or early. The doubled Japanese element corresponds to the concise Portuguese de preſſa.

[c2-l001] Fayabayaxij. *Couſa apreſſada.*
[c2-l001 note] Hayabayashii describes something hurried. Final ij represents a long i sequence; it is not a consonantal j or an additional syllable.
[c2-l002 >] Fayabayaxiſa.
[c2-l002 note] Hayabayashisa is the -sa noun formed from the preceding adjective. Its physical line supplies a derivative, without a repeated Portuguese definition.
[c2-l003 >] Fayabayaxù.
[c2-l003 note] Hayabayashù is the contracted adverbial derivative. The grave-shaped mark over u is retained as printed rather than converted to a modern macron.
[c2-l004] Fayabumi. *Carta apreſſada.*
[c2-l004 note] Hayabumi is a hurried or urgent letter. Carta contrasts it with hayabusa, the bird name immediately below, despite their similar beginnings.
[c2-l005] Fayabuſa. *Certa laya de falcão.*
[c2-l005 note] Hayabusa names a kind of falcon. The definition is broad; no particular modern taxonomic species is inferred from it.
[c2-l006] Fayacuchi. *O que fala de preſſa.*
[c2-l006 note] Hayakuchi here designates someone who speaks quickly. The Portuguese is personal, O que fala, rather than only the abstract act of rapid speech.
[c2-l007] Fayacuchini. *Aduerb. Vt,* Fayacuchini
[c2-l007 note] Hayakuchi ni is the adverbial form, repeated at the start of its example. The ni combines with the speech expression on the following line.
[c2-l008 >] monouo yǔ. *Falar de preſſa.*
[c2-l008 note] Mono wo yǔ completes 'speak rapidly.' The mark over u has two visible arms here; the kana guide's meaning should not depend on expanding it into modern iu.
[c2-l009] Fayafaya. *Adu. De preſſa, de preſſa.*
[c2-l009 note] Hayahaya is translated by the doubled instruction 'quickly, quickly.' The repeated Portuguese wording parallels the emphatic Japanese reduplication.
[c2-l010] Fayafune. *Embarcação ligeira.*
[c2-l010 note] Hayafune means a fast vessel. Embarcação ligeira supports fune 'boat,' not a similarly shaped personal or place name.
[c2-l011] Fayagaqi. *Eſcriuão que eſcreue de preſſa.*
[c2-l011 note] Hayagaki denotes a scribe who writes rapidly in this definition. It is not only the act of rapid writing implied by the modern compound in isolation.
[c2-l012] Fayai. *Couſa apreſſada, ou ligeira.*
[c2-l012 note] Hayai is glossed as hurried or swift. The two Portuguese adjectives give the basis for both derivative forms printed below.
[c2-l013 >] Fayaſa. Fayǒ.
[c2-l013 note] Hayasa is the nominal derivative; hayǒ is the contracted adverbial form. Their side-by-side position preserves the dictionary's concise derivative notation.
[c2-l014] Fayama. *Mato pequeno, ou de poucas aruores.*
[c2-l014 note] Hayama is a small or sparsely wooded area. The next line explicitly supplies its opposite, so a reading connected to haste is inappropriate here.
[c2-l015 >] *O contrario he,* Xigueyama.
[c2-l015 note] Shigeyama is the densely wooded counterpart to hayama. Xigueyama is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese sentence 'the opposite is.'
[c2-l016] Fayamari, u, atta. *Fazerſe algũa couſa de preſ-*
[c2-l016 note] Hayamaru means for something to happen quickly or ahead of time. The final preſ- continues below; the definition is broader than a modern moral sense of acting rashly.
[c2-l017 >] *ſa, ou ãtes do tẽpo determinado, ou anticiparſe*
[c2-l017 note] The definition specifies before the appointed time or an event being advanced. The scan has the nasal abbreviation ãtes, not fully written antes.
[c2-l018 >] *o tẽpo dalgũa feſta, cõuite, &c.* X. *No Cami*
[c2-l018 note] A feast or invitation may take place earlier. No Cami introduces a regional equivalent; Cami belongs within the italic Portuguese explanation in this printing.
[c2-l019 >] *ſediz, Toricoſu.*
[c2-l019 note] Torikosu is the regional alternative, 'bring forward' in this scheduling context. Its italic setting does not make it a Portuguese word.
[c2-l020] Fayame, uru, eta. *Apreſſar, ou dar preſſa.*
[c2-l020 note] Hayame gives the transitive verb 'hasten.' The supplied uru belongs to the older hayamuru paradigm, not an error to replace with modern hayameru.
[c2-l021 >] *¶* Vmauo fayamuru. *Fazer andar de*
[c2-l021 note] Uma wo hayamuru means to make a horse go faster. The Portuguese causative fazer andar corresponds to the Japanese transitive construction.
[c2-l022 >] *preſſa o caualo.*
[c2-l022 note] The object o caualo closes the example's Portuguese gloss. This short row is essential to the horse example rather than an isolated definition.
[c2-l023] Fayamichi. *Correo. ¶ Item, O ir polla*
[c2-l023 note] Hayamichi is a courier and also travel by post. The second sense begins here and is completed by posta on the next row.
[c2-l024 >] *posta. Vt,* Fayamichiuo noru. *Idem.*
[c2-l024 note] Hayamichi wo noru is illustrated as traveling by post. Nōru need not be supplied with an accent: the printed short noru is appropriate.
[c2-l025] Fayaracaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer correr algum*
[c2-l025 note] Hayarakasu is the causative 'make something circulate or become current.' The object is a custom or art, not a literal runner.
[c2-l026 >] *cuſtume, arte, &c.*
[c2-l026 note] Cuſtume and arte complete the objects of fazer correr. This supports the spread-of-practice sense of the Japanese verb above.
[c2-l027] Fayari, ru, atta. *Correr algum cuſtume, ar-*
[c2-l027 note] Hayaru means for a custom or art to become current. It contrasts with the causative entry directly above and the impetuous-motion sense below.
[c2-l028 >] *te, &c.*
[c2-l028 note] Te completes ar-te. Despite its brevity, this row closes the cultural-practice definition before the next hayaru entry.
[c2-l029] Fayari, u, atta. *Correr o rio com grande im-*
[c2-l029 note] This hayaru entry describes a river rushing with force. The different sense is explicit in o rio, not inferred merely from the repeated headword.
[c2-l030 >] *peto. ¶ Item, Ser apreſſado, & arrebata-*
[c2-l030 note] Im-peto finishes the river definition, then Item introduces personal eagerness or impetuosity. Arrebata- continues on the next row.
[c2-l031 >] *do como pera arremeter aos inimigos fora de*
[c2-l031 note] The impetuous person may charge enemies too early. The military context supplies the sense of rash eagerness rather than fashionable popularity.
[c2-l032 >] *tẽpo, &c.*
[c2-l032 note] Tẽpo completes fora de tẽpo, 'at the wrong time.' Its nasal abbreviation is retained without expanding the missing m.
[c2-l033] Fayariai, ǒ. *Moſtrarem dous, ou mais que*
[c2-l033 note] Hayariai describes mutually showing spirit or eagerness. The supplied ǒ is the contracted ending for hayariau, as also represented in the headword index.
[c2-l034 >] *pelejão entre ſi, brio, & feruor.*
[c2-l034 note] The participants fight one another with spirit and fervor. Entre ſi supplies the reciprocal relationship expressed by Japanese -ai.
[c2-l035] Fayarimono, l, fayattamono. *Soldado a-*
[c2-l035 note] Hayarimono or hayattamono names an eager, spirited soldier. The Portuguese a- begins animoso below rather than constituting a complete article.
[c2-l036 >] *nimoſo, & forte, & que acomete dos pri-*
[c2-l036 note] The soldier is spirited, strong, and among the first to attack. The final pri- continues primeiros, which completes that comparison.
[c2-l037 >] *meiros.*
[c2-l037 note] Meiros completes primeiros: one of the first. It remains attached to the soldier definition despite occupying its own physical row.
[c2-l038] Fayate. *Refega de vento rijo. ¶* Fayate
[c2-l038 note] Hayate is a sudden strong gust. The example repeats hayate here, with its case particle and verb placed on the next row.
[c2-l039 >] ni vǒ. *Encontrar com eſta refega, ou pè*
[c2-l039 note] The scan reads ni vǒ, not mivǒ: hayate ni au means to encounter a gust. Portuguese encontrar confirms this interpretation; the printed contracted vǒ is retained.
[c2-l040 >] *de vento.*
[c2-l040 note] De vento completes pè de vento, another expression for a gust. This is still the hayate example's translation.
[c2-l041] Fayauaza. *Obra apreſſada, ou ligeireza, &*
[c2-l041 note] Hayawaza describes quick work or dexterity. The Portuguese coordinates ligeireza with the following row's manual skill.
[c2-l042 >] *deſtreza de mãos.*
[c2-l042 note] Deſtreza de mãos specifies dexterity of the hands. This is a continuation of the compound's explanation, not a second headword.
[c2-l043] Fayauchi. *Correo.*
[c2-l043 note] Hayauchi is glossed simply as a courier. It belongs to the rapid-message family around hayabumi, hayamichi, and hayauma.
[c2-l044] Fayavma. *Caualo ligeiro. ¶* Faya vmauo
[c2-l044 note] Hayauma is a swift horse; the example separates faya vma wo in print. That word spacing is retained rather than forced to match the headword.
[c2-l045 >] tatçuru, l, yaru. *Mandar pella posta.*
[c2-l045 note] Tatsuru or yaru completes dispatching a fast horse, rendered 'send by post.' The alternative-marker l is not part of either Japanese verb.
[c2-l046] Fayaxi. *Canauial, ou lugar de muitas aruores*
[c2-l046 note] Hayashi names a cane thicket or a place with many trees. Canauial is read as the historical Portuguese form for a cane stand.
[c2-l047 >] *juntas como boſque.*
[c2-l047 note] Juntas como boſque completes the definition: trees together like a wood. This is the end of the column's body, separate from the catchword.
