[c1-l001] Fiaccu. Fiacuno curuximi. *Cem tormen-*
[c1-l001 note] Hyakku is a hundred hardships, explained by hyaku no kurushimi; the initial and medial consonants are damaged but supported by the visible form and torment gloss.
[c1-l002 >] *tos, & perigos. Vt,* Fiaccu xennanuo
[c1-l002 note] Hyakku sennan wo begins enduring a hundred hardships and a thousand difficulties; xennanuo, not xentranuo, matches that phrase.
[c1-l003 >] xinogu. *Paſſar, & ſofrer muitos trabalhos.*
[c1-l003 note] Shinogu completes the endurance example; passar and sofrer translate getting through many hardships.
[c1-l004] Fiacqei. Fiacuno qei. *Muitas viſtas.*
[c1-l004 note] Hyakkei, literally a hundred views, is glossed as many vistas; qei represents kei, not a g-series syllable.
[c1-l005] Fiacqicu. *Cem caſtas de boninas varias do*
[c1-l005 note] Hyakkiku names many varieties of autumn flowers; preserve the dictionary's broad boninas rather than imposing a modern botanical definition.
[c1-l006 >] *Outono que hà em Iapão.*
[c1-l006 note] Outono and Iapão specify the flowers' season and location, completing the preceding entry.
[c1-l007] Fiacqua. Fiacuno fana. *Muitas flores.*
[c1-l007 note] Hyakkwa is explained as hyaku no hana, many flowers; qua preserves the historical labialized syllable.
[c1-l008] Fiacuchô. Fiacuno tori. i, *Muitos paſſaros.*
[c1-l008 note] Hyakuchō is many birds, directly unpacked by hyaku no tori; the accented vowel belongs to the compound ending.
[c1-l009] Fiacufeqi. Fiacuno cuxe. i, Xujuno cuxe.
[c1-l009 note] Hyakuheki is many habits or quirks; shuju no kuse provides the second Japanese paraphrase.
[c1-l010 >] *Muitos maos coſtumes, ou manhas.*
[c1-l010 note] Maus costumes or manhas gives these habits a negative sense; costumes restores the visible u without changing that meaning.
[c1-l011] Fiacufucu. Fiacuno ſaiuai. *Muitas proſpe-*
[c1-l011 note] Hyakufuku is many blessings, glossed by saiwai and prosperity; proſpe- continues below.
[c1-l012 >] *ridades, & riquezas. ¶* Fiacufucu mi-
[c1-l012 note] Prosperity and riches complete the noun gloss; mi- begins the body/person in the example.
[c1-l013 >] ni amaru. *Abundar em riquezas.*
[c1-l013 note] Mi ni amaru says blessings overflow one's person, rendered as abundance in riches.
[c1-l014] Fiacufucu xǒgon. Vt, Fiacufucu xǒgon
[c1-l014 note] Hyakufuku shōgon introduces rich adornment; its repetition in the example confirms the headword rather than OCR hyakufaku.
[c1-l015 >] no vtena. *Aſſento, ou tribunal ornado de*
[c1-l015 note] No utena makes the decorated seat or platform the object of the definition; ornado continues its description.
[c1-l016 >] *todas as riquezas.*
[c1-l016 note] Todas as riquezas closes the description of the adorned seat, not a new wealth entry.
[c1-l017] Fiacumi. Fiacuno agiuai. i, Iroirono agi-
[c1-l017 note] Hyakumi is many tastes, explicitly unpacked by ajiwai; the visible u rules out OCR hyakami.
[c1-l018 >] uai. *Muitos goſtos, ou ſabores. ¶* Fi-
[c1-l018 note] Aji-wai completes flavor; Fi- begins the hyakumi compound attached to food and drink below.
[c1-l019 >] acumino vonjiqi. *comeres, & beberes de*
[c1-l019 note] Hyakumi no onjiki describes food and drink of many flavors; the physical split is retained.
[c1-l020 >] *muitos, & varios goſtos.*
[c1-l020 note] Muitos e varios gostos completes the variety of flavors, not another sense of onjiki.
[c1-l021] Fiacunari. *Cabacinhas pequenas.*
[c1-l021 note] Hyakunari is a small gourd, according to cabacinhas pequenas; its name is not interpreted as a literal hundred fruits in Level 1.
[c1-l022] Fiacuracu. Fiacuno tanoximi. i, Xujuno
[c1-l022 note] Hyakuraku is many pleasures, with tanoshimi and shuju introducing its Japanese explanation.
[c1-l023 >] tanoximi. *Muitos goſtos, & paſſatempos.*
[c1-l023 note] Tanoshimi finishes the alternative paraphrase; gostos and passatempos emphasize enjoyment.
[c1-l024] Fiacuren. Fiacu qitǒ. i, Fiacutabi neru.
[c1-l024 note] Hyakuren is repeated refining or working, explained by hyakutabi neru; retain qitǒ as printed rather than forcing a modern synonym.
[c1-l025 >] *Cem vezes fundido. Vt,* Fiacurenno vǒ-
[c1-l025 note] A hundred meltings introduces hyakuren no ōgon; the mark on vǒ- is a long-vowel mark, not a nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l026 >] gon. *Ouro de cem quilates. S.*
[c1-l026 note] Ō-gon is gold. The gloss's hundred quilates is the dictionary's wording, not a modern assay claim; S is a usage label.
[c1-l027] Fiacuſǒ. i, Amatano cuſa. *Cem layas de er-*
[c1-l027 note] Hyakusō is many grasses or herbs; amata no kusa supplies the native paraphrase.
[c1-l028 >] *uas que ſe queimão pera mezinha em lugar*
[c1-l028 note] The herbs are burned for medicine; the next row explains a substitute rather than another plant species.
[c1-l029 >] *daſquais vſão às vezes da fullugem que eſtà*
[c1-l029 note] Soot may substitute for the burned herbs; fullugem is retained as historical spelling.
[c1-l030 >] *pegada no fundo da panella que eſtà ao fogo*
[c1-l030 note] The soot adheres to the bottom of a cooking pot over the fire, specifying the medicinal material.
[c1-l031 >] *a que chamão tambem fiacuſǒ.*
[c1-l031 note] That soot is also called hyakusō; the visible vowel mark is retained and the embedded Japanese term is available for typeface switching.
[c1-l032] Fiacuſocu. Fiacuno axi. i, mucade. *Cen-*
[c1-l032 note] Hyakusoku, a hundred feet, is equated with mukade, the centipede; Cen- continues the Portuguese animal name.
[c1-l033 >] *topea.*
[c1-l033 note] Topea completes Centopea; this short row belongs to the centipede definition.
[c1-l034] Fiacuuǒ. Fiacudai. *Muitas idades. Vt,*
[c1-l034 note] Hyakuō and hyakudai refer to many reigns or ages, not to birds despite similar syllables nearby.
[c1-l035 >] Fiacuuǒ fiacudai ſugui vouatta. *Paſſa-*
[c1-l035 note] Hyakuō hyakudai sugi owatta says many ages have passed; the repeated hyaku helps identify the missing i.
[c1-l036 >] *rão ſe muitas idades, & tempos.*
[c1-l036 note] Passarão se completes the passage of many ages and times; its spelling remains diplomatic.
[c1-l037] Fiacuxe. Fiacuno yo. *Cem idades de certo*
[c1-l037 note] Hyakuse is a hundred generations, explained by hyaku no yo; the following prose defines how the ages are counted.
[c1-l038 >] *numero de annos cada idade, que ſe contão,*
[c1-l038 note] Each age contains a stated number of years; que se contão introduces the Japanese counting examples.
[c1-l039 >] *ixxe, nixe*. *¶ Item, Couſa antiga.*
[c1-l039 note] Ixxe and nice are the printed counting examples for generations; the second is linguistically expected to represent ni-se, but its middle letter remains visually ambiguous. Retain nice rather than silently regularizing it to nixe. Item then introduces the “ancient thing” sense.
[c1-l040] Fiacuxen. Fiacutabi tatacǒ. *Cem bata-*
[c1-l040 note] Hyakusen is a hundred battles, with hyakutabi tatakō giving the verbal paraphrase.
[c1-l041 >] *lhas. Vt,* Fiacuxen fiacuxô funho ichi-
[c1-l041 note] Hyakusen hyakushō funyo ichi- starts the maxim contrasting many victories with one act of patience; the split ichi-nin is essential.
[c1-l042 >] nin. Xix. *Mais val ter hũa vez paciencia*
[c1-l042 note] Nin completes ichinin, one endurance; Xix is the source label before the Portuguese translation.
[c1-l043 >] *que vencer em batalha cem vezes.*
[c1-l043 note] The maxim values one act of patience above winning a hundred battles, confirming the preceding Sino-Japanese sequence.
[c1-l044] Fiacuxen. Fiacuno caua. *Muitos Rios.*
[c1-l044 note] A homophonous hyakusen means many rivers, explicitly hyaku no kawa; it is independent of the battle entry.
[c1-l045] Fiacuxet. *Muitos nòs, ou junturas.*
[c1-l045 note] Hyakusetsu appears as Fiacuxet, with final checked t; the gloss describes knots or joints, not battles.
[c1-l046] Fiacuxi. *Cem, ou muitas mortes.*
[c1-l046 note] Hyakushi here means a hundred or many deaths; retain it separately from the following homophone.
[c1-l047] Fiacuxi. *Muitos officios, cargos, ou digni-*
[c1-l047 note] The next hyakushi concerns offices, posts, and dignities; its different gloss establishes the separate entry.
[c1-l048 >] *dades.*
[c1-l048 note] Dades completes dignidades from the preceding row and is body text despite its short length.
