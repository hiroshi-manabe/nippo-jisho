[c1-l001] Funhoina. *Peſſoa pobre, & falta do neceßa-*
[c1-l001 note] Funhoina continues the preceding page's Funhoi family: a person lacking means or capacity. The close-up shows two tall s forms in Peſſoa, not a t; this differs from the compact ß in neceßa-.
[c1-l002 >] *rio, ou de poder, & habilidade, ou arte pera*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the range of lacks: power, skill, or craft. Restored habilidade from OCR babilidade; the initial h agrees with the repeated discussion on the preceding page.
[c1-l003 >] *fazer algũa couſa.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes 'to do something'. The statement concerns capability as well as material poverty, so a narrowly financial reading of Funhoina would be incomplete.
[c1-l004 >] Funhoini.
[c1-l004 note] Funhoini is the adverbial form of the preceding expression. It is separately printed without a new Portuguese gloss; no definition is invented.
[c1-l005 >] Funhoiſa.
[c1-l005 note] Funhoiſa gives the nominal degree/state form. Its final ſa is meaningful Japanese morphology, not an alternative Portuguese word.
[c1-l006] Funi. Futatçu narazu. *Não auer dous, ou*
[c1-l006 note] Funi (不二) is explained literally as Futatçu narazu, not being two. The dictionary immediately paraphrases this as unity rather than leaving the negation unexplained.
[c1-l007 >] *ſer hũa ſò couſa.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes the paraphrase 'be one single thing'. Both the numeral and restrictive ſò matter to the Japanese non-duality expression.
[c1-l008] Funicu. Cuſaritaru xiximura. *Carne po-*
[c1-l008 note] Funicu is rotten flesh, explained Cuſaritaru xiximura. The shishi element is flesh here, not necessarily a particular animal.
[c1-l009 >] *dre. S.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes podre, rotten. Italic S. is a usage label; it is not part of the Japanese noun or an additional sentence.
[c1-l010] Funin. *Dignidade que dà o Rey, ou outro ſe-*
[c1-l010 note] Funin denotes a dignity or appointment conferred by a king. This is the dictionary's specific sense, not simply the modern act of arriving at one's post.
[c1-l011 >] *nhor grande por algum ſeruiço. ¶* Funin
[c1-l011 note] Extends the granting authority to another great lord and specifies reward for service. The following Funin starts an example continued below.
[c1-l012 >] uo daſaruru. *Dar eſta dignidade, ou grao.*
[c1-l012 note] Funin uo daſaruru is granting the dignity or rank. The -raruru expression is honorific in this context, not necessarily a passive grant being received.
[c1-l013] Funjin. Miuo vaquru. *Corpo, ou ſustan-*
[c1-l013 note] Funjin, explained Miuo vaquru, concerns manifestation of a divided body (分身). Its religious interpretation is supplied by the following Fotoque clause.
[c1-l014 >] *cia do Fotoque que ẽ varias partes ſẽdo o meſ*
[c1-l014 note] The same Fotoque manifests in multiple places. The embedded Japanese name is visibly italic here and remains so; ẽ and ſẽdo are Portuguese nasal abbreviations.
[c1-l015 >] *mo ſe manifeſta de diuerſas maneiras.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes the definition: although the same being, it appears in diverse ways. This is the dictionary's explanatory account, not a separate theological claim added by the review.
[c1-l016] Funmiǒ. *Clareza.*
[c1-l016 note] Funmiǒ denotes clarity. Its nasal-plus-m spelling and caron are retained; do not normalize it to the familiar modern spelling of 分明.
[c1-l017] Funmiǒna. *Couſa clara, & manifesta.*
[c1-l017 note] Funmiǒna is the adjective, clear and manifest. Restored clara from OCR clura; the following manifesta confirms the intended Portuguese adjective.
[c1-l018] Funmiǒni. *Adu. Claramente.*
[c1-l018 note] Funmiǒni is the adverb, clearly. Adu. is italic grammatical metadata, not a Japanese reading unit.
[c1-l019] Funnobi, uru, ita. *Eſtender os pès como quã*
[c1-l019 note] Funnobi is stretching out the feet or legs, with inflections uru, ita as printed. The Portuguese comparison continues into a stretch or yawn-like bodily action.
[c1-l020 >] *do hum ſe eſpriguiça.*
[c1-l020 note] The printed eſpriguiça describes stretching oneself. It is not modernized to espreguiça merely because the latter is more familiar.
[c1-l021] Funnu. Icari, icaru. *Indignarſe. S.*
[c1-l021 note] Funnu is anger or indignation (憤怒), paraphrased Icari, icaru. S. is retained in italic as a usage label.
[c1-l022] Funô, l, munô. *Não ter artes, nem habi-*
[c1-l022 note] Funô and munô describe lacking arts or abilities. Both circumflexes are visible; their nasal consonants differ and should remain distinct.
[c1-l023 >] *lidades.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes habilidades from the preceding row. This short fragment is Portuguese, not an independent Japanese entry.
[c1-l024] Funôna. *Idem.*
[c1-l024 note] Funôna is the adjectival counterpart. Idem refers to the preceding lack-of-ability definition rather than supplying a new meaning.
[c1-l025] Funôni. *Adu.*
[c1-l025 note] Funôni is the adverbial counterpart. The dictionary gives only Adu.; the review should not fabricate a fuller printed gloss.
[c1-l026] Funori. *Certa laya de limos do mar. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l026 note] Funori names a kind of marine weed. The following Itẽ introduces a second related meaning, not another plant species.
[c1-l027 >] *Certo grude que ſe faz deſtes limos.*
[c1-l027 note] The second sense is glue or sizing made from that seaweed. This is consistent with funori's material use but the text itself supplies the connection.
[c1-l028] Funoyaqi. *Certos bolinhos que fazem do ro-*
[c1-l028 note] Funoyaqi is a preparation of small cakes. The ingredient term ro-lão is physically divided and should be read with the next row.
[c1-l029 >] *lão da farinha aſados em panela, ou tacho.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes the coarse flour/bran ingredient and cooking in a pot or pan. Preserve the printed single-long-s aſados instead of imposing modern assados.
[c1-l030] Funpǒ. Cǒbaxij, cǒbaxij. *Couſa cheiroſa,*
[c1-l030 note] Funpǒ concerns fragrance. Both Cǒbaxij and cǒbaxij show carons in enlargement; corrected the second OCR circumflex, retaining the repeated adjective rather than removing it as redundant.
[c1-l031 >] *ou o cheirar muito. Vt,* Reiqio xiqirini
[c1-l031 note] The example Reiqio xiqirini introduces an excellent fragrance spreading strongly. Reiqio is likely the Sino-Japanese fragrance expression; its faint or absent length mark is not supplied from expectation.
[c1-l032 >] funpǒ ſu. *O excelente cheiro recẽde muito. S.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes funpǒ ſu, emitting fragrance abundantly. Portuguese recẽde means giving off an odor; the nasal abbreviation and S. label are retained.
[c1-l033] Funpun. Maguire, maguiruru. *O trauarſe,*
[c1-l033 note] Funpun here means entangling or mingling, explained Maguire, maguiruru. This is distinct from the fragrance homonym below.
[c1-l034 >] *& miſturarſe algũa multidão de couſas entre ſi.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the definition as many things mixing among themselves. The multiplicity is essential to the sense, not a single object being misplaced.
[c1-l035 >] *Vt,* Funpuntaru yuqi. *Neue que cae mui*
[c1-l035 note] Funpuntaru yuqi describes thick, mingled falling snow. The adjectival -taru formation agrees with the attributive snow phrase.
[c1-l036 >] *ta miſturada hũa com outra.*
[c1-l036 note] Completes the snowy mixture explanation. It is a continuation of mui-ta, not a fresh statement about another substance.
[c1-l037] Funpun. Cǒbaxixi, cǒbaxixi. *Cheiro ſua-*
[c1-l037 note] A separate Funpun concerns fragrance. The unusual Cǒbaxixi, cǒbaxixi sequence is actually visible; retain it as printed rather than automatically changing both to cǒbaxij.
[c1-l038 >] *ue, ou eſpalharſe o cheiro. S.*
[c1-l038 note] Completes the sweet-fragrance/spreading-smell definition. This separates the aromatic homonym from the earlier mingling sense.
[c1-l039] Funqi. Funo vtçuuamono. *Vaſo, ou ſeruiço.*
[c1-l039 note] Funqi is a vessel for excrement, explained Funo vtçuuamono. Portuguese ſeruiço here denotes a chamber utensil, not abstract service.
[c1-l040] Funran. Maguire, midaruru. *Perturbação,*
[c1-l040 note] Funran means confusion or disorder, with Maguire and midaruru as Japanese paraphrases. The Portuguese defines disturbance rather than necessarily armed conflict.
[c1-l041 >] *ou contrariedade de qualidades, &c. S.*
[c1-l041 note] Restored contrariedade from OCR contrartedade. The phrase concerns opposed qualities; the ordinary Portuguese noun and the visible letters agree.
[c1-l042 >] *¶ Item, Mistura, & perturbação como de*
[c1-l042 note] Adds another sense of mixed, disturbed activity. Item and the paragraph marker signal the extension; the insects specified next make it concrete.
[c1-l043 >] *moſquitos, abelhas, &c.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes the examples with mosquitoes and bees. This supports swarming or confused mingling, not a metaphor arbitrarily supplied by the reviewer.
[c1-l044] Funſui. Funno mizzu. *Agoa do esterco.*
[c1-l044 note] Funſui is water associated with dung, explicitly Funno mizzu. It must not be read as the modern fountain homophone merely from kana.
[c1-l045] Funto. *Eſterco de homem, & animaes.*
[c1-l045 note] Funto denotes human or animal excrement. The Portuguese supplies both sources; the spelling animaes is preserved.
[c1-l046 >] *¶ Item, Esterco, & terra.*
[c1-l046 note] Adds dung and earth as a further sense. Restored the clearly printed ¶ before Item; this is not an inferred entry boundary.
[c1-l047] Funxit. Midare vxinǒ. *Perderſe algũa cou-*
[c1-l047 note] Funxit means loss amid confusion (紛失), paraphrased Midare vxinǒ. The definition continues across the column boundary, so its final cou- is not a completed clause.

[c2-l001 >] *ſa enuolta, ou de mistura com outras.*
[c2-l001 note] Completes the preceding column's Funxit: something lost while mixed among other things. This physical first row is a continuation, not a new entry.
[c2-l002] Fupputo. *Adu. Modo deſe fazer, ou a-*
[c2-l002 note] Fupputo describes a sudden manner of appearing or rising. Adu. is italic metadata; the printed deſe grouping is kept.
[c2-l003 >] *leuantar algũa couſa derepente como nacida,*
[c2-l003 note] Completes suddenly arising, with nacida introducing a swelling/eruption-like example. Do not automatically reinterpret it as a person's birth.
[c2-l004 >] *burbulha, &c. ¶* Fupputo monouo yǔ
[c2-l004 note] Burbulha is another sudden-emergence example. The Japanese Fupputo monouo yǔ begins a separate illustrative use about speaking.
[c2-l005 >] fitogia. *He homem que fala ſem conſidera*
[c2-l005 note] Fitogia completes 'a person who says things abruptly'. The Portuguese specifies lack of consideration, so this is more than merely fast speech.
[c2-l006 >] *ção, ou arrebatadamente.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes the alternative 'impetuously'. This explains the adverb's pragmatic sense without changing the Japanese spelling.
[c2-l007] Fuqe. *Lamaçal, ou atoleiro. ¶* Fuqeni fa-
[c2-l007 note] Fuqe is a muddy or boggy place. Fuqeni famaru starts the example of getting trapped in it; f in famaru is the period spelling for h.
[c2-l008 >] maru. *A tolar no lamaçal.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes famaru and the bogging-down gloss. The visibly separated A tolar is retained rather than automatically joined.
[c2-l009] Fuqe, uru, eta. *Fazerſe de muita idade, ou*
[c2-l009 note] Fuqe, uru, eta covers becoming old and the advancing night. The two modern senses of fukeru are explicitly brought together in this entry.
[c2-l010 >] *alta noite. Vt,* Miya võmacurauo cata-
[c2-l010 note] The literary example starts with Miya and võmacurauo, honorific on-makura. Enlargement shows the nasal abbreviation over o, not a caron denoting length; the Japanese construction supports the correction.
[c2-l011 >] muqeſaxe tamayeba, fito mina xizzuma
[c2-l011 note] Cata-muqeſaxe tamayeba is the honorific reclining action, followed by everyone growing quiet. The older causative/honorific sequence is grammatical and should not be simplified.
[c2-l012 >] rite, yo ſudeni fuqeni qeri. Taif. *Lib.*
[c2-l012 note] The night had already grown late: yo ſudeni fuqeni qeri. Roman Taif. identifies the Taiheiki citation; its final period is included in the label.
[c2-l013 >] *18*. *A Rainha encostandoſe, & aquietan-*
[c2-l013 note] The citation gives book 18 and translates Miya as Rainha. Preserve that printed Portuguese choice without treating it as independent proof of the figure's identity.
[c2-l014 >] *doſe todos: fezſe quaſi alta noite. ¶* Toxiga
[c2-l014 note] Completes the quieting of everyone and late-night gloss, then begins Toxiga fuqeta. The new example returns to human age.
[c2-l015 >] fuqeta. *He ja de muita idade.*
[c2-l015 note] Toxiga fuqeta is being advanced in years. This confirms the first meaning of Fuqe rather than a different pronunciation.
[c2-l016] Fǔqei. i, Tocorono qexiqi. *Feição do lu-*
[c2-l016 note] Fǔqei is scenery or the appearance of a place, paraphrased Tocorono qexiqi. The u caron is visible; qexiqi is keshiki in the dictionary's spelling.
[c2-l017 >] *gar, boa viſta, &c.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes the pleasant view sense. Boa viſta provides the favorable scenic interpretation rather than an unrestricted appearance alone.
[c2-l018] Fuqen. Caxicocarazu. *Não prudente. Não*
[c2-l018 note] Fuqen here is not being wise, explained Caxicocarazu. This is a different negative compound from the not-seeing Fuqen below.
[c2-l019 >] *ſe vſa per ſi ſe não cõ o affirmatiuo juntamen-*
[c2-l019 note] The dictionary says this form is not used alone but together with its affirmative counterpart. That restriction is a substantive usage observation, not editorial commentary.
[c2-l020 >] *te. Vt,* Qen, fuqẽtomoni. *Prudentes,*
[c2-l020 note] Qen, fuqẽtomoni means wise and unwise together. Restored the visible nasal abbreviation over e; without it the Japanese reading would wrongly lose the final n.
[c2-l021 >] *& não prudentes.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes the paired wise/unwise gloss. It confirms the conjunction of the two forms described in the usage restriction.
[c2-l022] Fuqen. Mizu. *Não ver. Vt,* Mimon fu-
[c2-l022 note] A second Fuqen is not seeing, explained Mizu (見ず). Here mizu is a negative verb, not the water noun, and its single z is retained.
[c2-l023 >] qen. *Couſa que nem vi, nem ouui, nẽ ſei.*
[c2-l023 note] Mimon fuqen means neither heard nor seen, with the Portuguese also adding lack of knowledge. The initial fu- continues from the preceding physical row.
[c2-l024] Fuqeô, l, fuqiô. *Polamanhaã cedo, ou an-*
[c2-l024 note] Fuqeô and fuqiô name an early-morning time. The scan really prints Polamanhaã here; it is not normalized to the more frequent menhaã form just from a frequency rule.
[c2-l025 >] *te menhaã.*
[c2-l025 note] Completes ante menhaã, before morning. The first vowel differs from manhaã immediately above; the visible variation is retained.
[c2-l026] Fuqeracaxi, ſu, aita. *Publicar, ou dar mo-*
[c2-l026 note] Fuqeracaxi means making a display of something, closely related to fukerakasu. The principal parts ſu, aita remain as printed.
[c2-l027 >] *ſtra dalgũa couſa que hum tem em estima, ou*
[c2-l027 note] Specifies the displayed thing as something one esteems. The Portuguese mo-ſtra continuation is kept at its physical boundary.
[c2-l028 >] *preza muito.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes 'values highly'. This supplies the motivation for display without assuming vanity is the only possible nuance.
[c2-l029] Fuqeri, ru, etta. *Gloriarſe, ou deleitarſe ẽ*
[c2-l029 note] Fuqeri denotes taking pride or delight in something. The close-up supports printed etta; it is not simplified to eta on grammatical expectation.
[c2-l030 >] *vicios, &c. Vt,* Acuni fuqeru. *Estar*
[c2-l030 note] Acuni fuqeru is delighting or immersing oneself in evil. The following Portuguese explicitly says maldade, so the reading is not inferred from a modern homophone.
[c2-l031 >] *com deleitação na maldade. ¶* Miuo fuqe-
[c2-l031 note] Completes the evil-indulgence gloss, then starts Miuo fuqeru, self-glorification. The object mi gives the second example a reflexive focus.
[c2-l032 >] ru. *Gloriarſe, & preſumir de ſi. ¶* Ta-
[c2-l032 note] Restored Gloriarſe from OCR Cloriarſe. The gloss of pride in oneself agrees with the preceding Miuo phrase; Ta- begins tacara.
[c2-l033 >] cara, l, yocuni fuqeru. *Deleitarſe, ou eſ*
[c2-l033 note] Tacara, l, yocuni fuqeru concerns wealth or desire. The text allows these alternatives without requiring that they be identical nouns.
[c2-l034 >] *tar metido nas riquezas, ou na cobiça. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l034 note] Completes being engrossed in riches or covetousness. Itẽ then starts the display-of-valuables sense developed below.
[c2-l035 >] Fuqeri, ru. *Dar mostra dalgũa couſa pre-*
[c2-l035 note] A further Fuqeri, ru means showing a prized object. The principal-part fragment is separately indented, but it continues this semantic discussion.
[c2-l036 >] *zada como de hum boyão, Catana, &c. Vt,*
[c2-l036 note] Gives examples of a jar and a Catana. The embedded Japanese weapon word is italic in the scan and remains so; Vt is also italic.
[c2-l037 >] Cudaſaretaru monouo fitobitoni fuqeri
[c2-l037 note] Cudaſaretaru monouo fitobitoni fuqeri means showing others what has graciously been given. The donor honorific belongs to Japanese social phrasing, not a change of object.
[c2-l038 >] mǒxi ſoro. *Aquillo que me deſtes aſoalhoo*
[c2-l038 note] Mǒxi ſoro completes the polite statement. Enlargement supports the Portuguese aſoalhoo, with two final o letters; retained that historical spelling rather than the OCR's single o.
[c2-l039 >] *& mostrò a todos. ¶* Chauanuo fuqeru.
[c2-l039 note] Completes showing the gift to everyone and introduces Chauanuo fuqeru. The mark in mostrò is retained without allowing modern tense expectations to decide its shape.
[c2-l040 >] *Dar moſtras dalgũa boa Chauan.*
[c2-l040 note] The example is showing a fine tea bowl. Chauan is another italic Japanese word embedded in Portuguese, and its printed gender construction is left unchanged.
[c2-l041] Fuqi. *Hũa erua que tem grandes folhas, &*
[c2-l041 note] Fuqi names the large-leaved edible plant, fuki. This is a plant entry, distinct from the bellows and verbs that follow.
[c2-l042 >] *ſe come.*
[c2-l042 note] Completes the statement that it is eaten. The short Portuguese continuation is assigned to the plant rather than the bellows entry.
[c2-l043] Fuqi, l, fuigo. *Folles de ferreiro, ouriues, &c.*
[c2-l043 note] Fuqi, also fuigo, denotes a smith's or goldsmith's bellows. The connection with blowing is lexical, but the noun must not be silently changed to the following verb.
[c2-l044] Fǔqi. *Certa doença contagioſa de febres rijas.*
[c2-l044 note] Fǔqi is described as a contagious illness with strong fevers. The review does not assign a modern diagnosis merely from this historical label.
[c2-l045] Fuqi, qu, uita. *Vt,* Caneuo fuqu. *Fun*
[c2-l045 note] Fuqi, qu, uita is illustrated by Caneuo fuqu, smelting metal. The Portuguese Fun continues fundir; the uita principal part is retained as printed.
[c2-l046 >] *dir metal.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes fundir metal. The action is metallurgical blowing/smelting rather than literally blowing a finished metal object.
[c2-l047] Fuqi, u, uita. *Vt,* Vuo, l, vrocouo fuqu.
[c2-l047 note] The next Fuqi entry concerns scaling fish: Vuo, l, vrocouo fuqu. The visible initial letter and headword support f, not OCR ſ; the Eſcamar catchword anticipates the gloss on the next page.
