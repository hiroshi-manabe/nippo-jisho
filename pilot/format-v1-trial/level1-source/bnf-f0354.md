## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Nanto xitemo. *Por mais que faça, ou por ne-*
[c1-l001 note] Nanto shitemo introduces doing something by whatever means or despite every effort. The Portuguese gives both the concessive sense and the negative construction continued below; ne- is visibly divided at the edge.
[c1-l002 >] *nhũa via. Vt,* Nanto xitemo naranu.
[c1-l002 note] The example means that it cannot be done by any means. The scan reads naranu, not the draft's natanu; nhũa completes the divided Portuguese negative expression.
[c1-l003 >] *Não pode ſer por nenhũa via.*
[c1-l003 note] This is the translation of the preceding negative example: it cannot be, by any means. The abbreviated nasal vowel in nenhũa is retained rather than expanded.
[c1-l004] Nanxi. Vonocogo. *Filho macho.*
[c1-l004 note] Nanshi is explained by onokogo, a male child. Initial V in Vonocogo belongs to the historical romanization and is not a modern consonantal v.
[c1-l005] Nanxi. Minamino yeda. *Ramos que estão*
[c1-l005 note] This different nanshi is explained as southern branches, minami no eda. The Portuguese relative clause continues below; it is not another definition of a male child.
[c1-l006 >] *pera a parte do Sul.* S.
[c1-l006 note] Completes the gloss as branches facing south. The final upright S. is the dictionary's written-register label, separate from the italic Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l007] Nanxin. i, Xinjigataxi. *Couſa difficil de*
[c1-l007 note] Nanshin is explained by shinjigatashi, difficult to believe. The i, introduces the Japanese equivalent, and the Portuguese infinitive continues on the following row.
[c1-l008 >] *ſe creer.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the gloss 'difficult to believe.' The doubled e in creer is the printed historical spelling, not a new Japanese item.
[c1-l009] Nanxin nangue. *Couſa difficultoſa de ſe crèr,*
[c1-l009 note] Nanshin nange denotes something hard to believe and understand. The scan separates Nanxin and nangue; the draft's extra i is not printed. The gloss continues with difficulty of explanation below.
[c1-l010 >] *& deſe declarar.* S.
[c1-l010 note] Adds that the matter is difficult to explain, then marks written usage with upright S. The close printed deſe is retained, without normalizing it to a modern reflexive construction.
[c1-l011] Nanxocu. *Peccado mao, ou nefando.*
[c1-l011 note] Nanshoku is described through the missionary compilers' condemnatory expression for male-male sexual activity. The moral wording belongs to the historical source; nefando is one printed word, not ne fando.
[c1-l012] Nanyen. Minamino ſono. *Iardim que està*
[c1-l012 note] Nanyen is explained as minami no sono, a garden to the south. The final Portuguese clause continues below, and the long ſ of ſono is not f.
[c1-l013 >] *pera à parte do Sul.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the garden's location on the southern side. The printed accent in à is retained without making this gloss match the similar branch definition above.
[c1-l014] Nanzan. *Parto perigoſo, ou trabalhoſo.*
[c1-l014 note] Nanzan here means a dangerous or difficult childbirth. This obstetric sense must be distinguished from the identically spelled southern-mountain entry below.
[c1-l015 >] *¶* Nanzanni voyobu. l, nanzande go-
[c1-l015 note] The two expressions describe encountering a difficult delivery or being in that condition. Latin l introduces the alternative, whose gozaru is divided across the physical rows.
[c1-l016 >] zaru. *Ter parto trabalhoſo.*
[c1-l016 note] Zaru completes gozaru; the Portuguese renders the two Japanese constructions as having a difficult childbirth. It is continuation text, not an independent headword.
[c1-l017] Nanzan. Minamino yama. *Montes da par-*
[c1-l017 note] This nanzan is minami no yama, mountains to the south. The explicit Japanese equivalent separates it from the childbirth homograph above.
[c1-l018 >] *te do Sul.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes parte do Sul, the location of the mountains. The physical division par-/te is preserved rather than joined editorially.
[c1-l019] Nanzo. *Algũa couſa. ¶ Itẽ, Que couſa?*
[c1-l019 note] Nanzo is given both indefinite 'something' and interrogative 'what thing?' senses. The final mark is a question mark, not the draft's nasal-vowel character.
[c1-l020] Nappô. *Certo, & determinado fructo, ou rẽ-*
[c1-l020 note] Nappô denotes a fixed yield or revenue. The scan supports fructo with the ct group rather than OCR frusto; rẽ- begins rendimento with an abbreviated nasal.
[c1-l021 >] *dimento, que recolhem os ſenhores de ſuas*
[c1-l021 note] Completes rendimento and explains that this is income collected by lords from their holdings. The source of that income is completed on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *terras.* S.
[c1-l022 note] The lords collect the specified yield from their lands. Upright S. marks the term's written usage and is outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l023] Naqi, u, aita. *Chorar. ¶ Item, Cantar*
[c1-l023 note] The principal forms of naku cover human weeping and, in the second gloss, the calls of birds. The final Cantar is continued by its complement below.
[c1-l024 >] *dos paßaros.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the second sense as birdsong or bird calls. The printed double-s form in paßaros is retained in the supplied transcription convention.
[c1-l025] Naqi. *Choro, ou pranto. Vt,* Nhôbǒ ta-
[c1-l025 note] The noun naki means weeping or lamentation. The example begins with nyôbô-tachi, women or ladies, divided after ta-; the different long-o marks are retained individually.
[c1-l026 >] chino xinobi goyeni naqi nado xerareta.
[c1-l026 note] Completes the example: the women wept in subdued voices. Xinobi goyeni is visibly spaced in this impression; xerareta belongs to the historical honorific construction, not a malformed modern verb.
[c1-l027 >] Feiq. *Lib. 3. As molheres com voz*
[c1-l027 note] The upright Feiq. cites the Heike text, book 3. The italic translation begins 'the women, in a low voice,' with the adjective on the next row.
[c1-l028 >] *baixa chorauão, &c. ¶* Xinobi naqi ſu-
[c1-l028 note] Completes the translation as quiet weeping, then begins a second example, shinobi-naki suru, to weep silently. The printed su- continues below.
[c1-l029 >] ru. *Chorar caladamente. ¶ Item, Can-*
[c1-l029 note] Ru completes suru, glossed as weeping quietly. The paragraph mark then introduces the noun's birdsong sense, whose Portuguese Canto is divided.
[c1-l030 >] *to dos paßaros.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes Canto dos paßaros, birdsong. This is the noun counterpart to the bird-call sense of Naqi, u, aita above.
[c1-l031] Naqiacaxi, ſu, aita. *Chorar toda a noite.*
[c1-l031 note] Nakiakasu means to spend the entire night weeping. The historical principal forms include the s-row i-onbin past in -aita; they are not modernized to -ashita.
[c1-l032 >] *¶* Nobarano tçuyuni ſodeuo cataxiqite
[c1-l032 note] The example describes laying one sleeve beneath oneself in the dew of an open field. Its verb of weeping all night follows on the next line; no terminal hyphen is supplied here.
[c1-l033 >] naqiacaxi, &c. Taif. *Lib. 33. En-*
[c1-l033 note] Nakiakashi completes the Japanese example, followed by the upright Taiheiki citation Taif., book 33. The Portuguese En- begins Encostado across the physical division.
[c1-l034 >] *coſtado ſobre a manga ao ſereno chorando toda*
[c1-l034 note] Completes Encostado and describes resting on the sleeve outdoors in the night dew while weeping. Toda modifies noite on the next row.
[c1-l035 >] *a noite.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes 'all night,' matching the duration expressed by nakiakashi. The short continuation is retained as its own physical line.
[c1-l036] Naqicanaximi, u, ǔda. *Chorando entriſte-*
[c1-l036 note] Nakikanashimu combines weeping and grief. The final principal-form vowel is poorly impressed, but its visible downward-pointing mark supports the retained ǔda; no unprinted n is supplied. The Portuguese verb continues below.
[c1-l037 >] *cerſe.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes entristecerse, becoming sorrowful while weeping. The physical division in the Portuguese reflexive verb is retained.
[c1-l038] Naqicogare, uru, eta. *Chorar com ſumma triſ-*
[c1-l038 note] Nakikogaru denotes intense, consuming grief expressed in weeping. The Portuguese describes extreme sadness and continues its final noun below.
[c1-l039 >] *teza. Vt,* Tamotouo cauoni voxiatete
[c1-l039 note] Completes tristeza, correcting the draft's taza from the damaged but visible e. The example begins by pressing one's sleeve against one's face; tamoto and kao match the translation below.
[c1-l040 >] coyeuo voximazu naqi cogareraruru.
[c1-l040 note] The person weeps without restraining the voice. The final honorific form belongs to the example; the visible spacing in naqi cogareraruru is preserved.
[c1-l041 >] Qir. *Pondo as mangas no roſto em voz al-*
[c1-l041 note] The upright label Qir. precedes the Portuguese translation: putting the sleeves to the face and crying aloud. The precise expansion of this short source label is not assumed; al- continues alta below.
[c1-l042 >] *ta choraua, & desfazia ſe em lagrimas.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the account of loud weeping and dissolving in tears. The historical spelling and the printed separation in desfazia ſe are retained.
[c1-l043] Naqicudoqi, u, oita. *Chorar amezquinhan-*
[c1-l043 note] Nakikudoku is weeping in complaint or lament. The Portuguese amezquinhan- is a continuous word, not the draft's amez quinhan-, and continues with its reflexive ending below.
[c1-l044 >] *doſe, ou lamentar.*
[c1-l044 note] Completes amezquinhando-se and gives lamentar as the alternative gloss. This remains continuation text for Nakikudoku.
[c1-l045] Naqicuraxi, ſu, aita. *Chorar tododia, ou*
[c1-l045 note] Nakikurasu is spending the day in weeping. The source prints tododia together; the alternative gloss of continual weeping follows below.
[c1-l046 >] *ſempre.*
[c1-l046 note] Adds 'always' or continually to the preceding all-day definition. The very short row is a full physical continuation and has its own crop.
[c1-l047] Naqifare, uru, eta. *Incharem os olhos de*
[c1-l047 note] Nakiharu describes the eyes becoming swollen through crying. The scan supports Naqifare with a, not the draft's Nuqifare; the causal phrase continues below.
[c1-l048 >] *chorar.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes the cause of the swollen eyes as crying. The first letters are weakly impressed, but the remaining strokes and the complete gloss support chorar.

[c2-l001] Naqifore, uru, eta. *Sair fora deſi cõ choro.*
[c2-l001 note] Nakihoru describes being beside oneself with weeping. The f belongs to the historical h-row spelling; the close Portuguese deſi and abbreviated cõ are retained.
[c2-l002] Naqifuxi, ſu, uita. *Estar chorando deitado*
[c2-l002 note] Nakifusu means lying down and weeping. The given -uita principal form is retained; the following line specifies a face-down position.
[c2-l003 >] *de bruços.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes the posture as lying prone. This short Portuguese continuation explains fusu in the headword above.
[c2-l004] Naqigauo. *Roſto choroſo, ou de quem quer*
[c2-l004 note] Nakigao denotes a tearful face or the face of someone about to cry. The old gauo spelling is preserved rather than normalized to modern gao.
[c2-l005 >] *chorar.*
[c2-l005 note] Completes 'the face of someone who wants to cry.' This belongs to Naqigauo, not to the following entry about the voice.
[c2-l006] Naqigoye. *Voz chorosa.*
[c2-l006 note] Nakigoe is a crying or tearful voice. The locally aligned short-s form chorosa is retained rather than made to match the preceding choroſo mechanically.
[c2-l007] Naqimodaye, uru, eta. *Chorando afligirſe,*
[c2-l007 note] Nakimodayuru is distress or agitation expressed through weeping. Enlargement resolves the joined fl in afligirſe, which the draft reduced to f; the explanation continues below.
[c2-l008 >] *ou moſtrar impaciencia.*
[c2-l008 note] Adds showing impatience or agitation as the alternative gloss. The historical impaciencia is preserved without a modern accent.
[c2-l009] Naqiſaqebi, u, qeôda. *Chorando gritar.*
[c2-l009 note] Nakisakebu means shouting while weeping. The abbreviated past qeôda is the printed principal form; it is not expanded or regularized in the transcription.
[c2-l010] Naqitçubuxi, ſu, uita. *Perder a viſtà pollo mui-*
[c2-l010 note] Nakitsubusu describes damaging one's eyes through excessive crying. The scan supplies ſu, not the draft's u. The visibly printed uita is retained despite the more expected -aita pattern in other s-row entries. The grave in viſtà is very faint; the aligned draft's accent is retained provisionally rather than removed without positive contrary evidence.
[c2-l011 >] *to chorar. Vt,* Manacouo naqitçubuſu.
[c2-l011 note] Completes muito chorar and supplies the example 'cry one's eyes out,' literally involving the eyes. The final verb confirms the headword's -subusu form.
[c2-l012 >] *Perder os olhos chorando, ou cegar de choro.*
[c2-l012 note] The Portuguese makes the claimed consequence explicit: losing the eyes or becoming blind through weeping. This is the historical dictionary's explanation, not a present-day medical assertion.
[c2-l013] Naqitçure, uru, eta. *Chorar em companhia*
[c2-l013 note] Nakitsureru is weeping together with others. The companion phrase continues onto the next physical row.
[c2-l014 >] *de outros, ou chorarem muitos.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes 'in the company of others' and offers the equivalent description of many people weeping. Both glosses concern shared crying.
[c2-l015] Naqivameqi, u, eita. *Chorando dar gritos.*
[c2-l015 note] Nakiwameku means crying out while weeping. The scan has initial N, not OCR X, and Chorando rather than Chocando; the separate Portuguese words dar gritos are visible.
[c2-l016] Naqivomeqi, u, eita. *Idem.*
[c2-l016 note] Nakiwomeku is given the same meaning as the preceding variant. Initial vo preserves the source's w/o-series notation rather than being silently assimilated to va.
[c2-l017] Naqixini, uru, xinda. *Morrer de chorar*
[c2-l017 note] Nakishinu means dying from weeping or dying while weeping. The source's principal forms are retained exactly, including uru.
[c2-l018 >] *muito, ou morrer chorando.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes the distinction between dying from excessive crying and dying in tears. This is the continuation of Naqixini.
[c2-l019] Naqixizzumi, u, unda. *Chorar com ſumma*
[c2-l019 note] Nakishizumu is sinking into grief while weeping. The scan supports Chorar, correcting OCR Cborar; the zzu spelling and nasal past unda are preserved.
[c2-l020 >] *triſteza.*
[c2-l020 note] Completes the gloss as weeping with extreme sadness. This printed tristeza also supplies a local comparison for the damaged divided word in column 1.
[c2-l021] Naqunaqu. *Chorando.*
[c2-l021 note] Nakunaku is glossed as 'weeping' or 'in tears.' Enlargement shows two weak stems of the final u rather than a dotted i. The supplied NINJAL headword 177d13 independently supports Naqunaqu after the scan reading was reinspected. [c2-l022] Nara. *Aruore aßi chamada.*
[c2-l022 note] Nara is identified simply as a tree bearing that name. The transcription does not replace the source's non-specific botanical gloss with a modern species name.
[c2-l023] Narabe, uru, eta. *Por por ordem, ou igualar.*
[c2-l023 note] Naraberu means to arrange in order or place on an equal footing. Igualar is wholly Portuguese italic text; the draft's alternating roman fragments were recognition artefacts.
[c2-l024 >] *¶* Feifeitoxitaru vmimo yuqino yamauo
[c2-l024 note] The example begins with a calm sea and compares it with mountains of snow. Its arrangement verb follows below, illustrating naraberu rather than starting a new entry.
[c2-l025 >] narabetaru gotocuni toqino mani natte.
[c2-l025 note] The sea quickly became like snow-covered mountains set side by side. The scan supports the doubled t in natte; the draft's nate lost a consonant.
[c2-l026 >] Voiſ. *O mar que estaua brando, & ſoſega*
[c2-l026 note] The upright source label precedes the translation of the formerly calm sea. The final long letter of Voiſ. is retained provisionally. Portuguese context and the scan support ſoſega rather than ſofega; no line-end hyphen is visible.
[c2-l027 >] *do em breue eſpaço ſe fez como ſerras de neue*
[c2-l027 note] Do completes sossegado across an unhyphenated physical division. The sea becomes, in a short time, like ranges of snowy mountains.
[c2-l028 >] *igualadas, & prainas. ¶* Sono fitoni ca-
[c2-l028 note] Completes the comparison with aligned, level ranges, then begins an example about having no equal. The divided ca- belongs to kata, shoulder, on the next row.
[c2-l029 >] tauo naraburu monoua nai. *Não ha quẽ*
[c2-l029 note] The Japanese says that no one can stand shoulder to shoulder with that person. The Portuguese begins the equivalent 'there is no one who,' with abbreviated quẽ.
[c2-l030 >] *ſe compare, ou iguale com eße homem.*
[c2-l030 note] Completes the translation: nobody compares with or equals that man. This is figurative equality, contrasting with the preceding physical arrangement example.
[c2-l031] Narabetate, tçuru, eta. *Por algũa couſa por*
[c2-l031 note] Narabetatsuru is arranging something in an orderly, well-set fashion. The -tçuru principal form belongs to the historical inflection and is not replaced by modern -teru.
[c2-l032 >] *ordem, & bem concertada.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes the orderly-arrangement gloss. Concertada is retained as printed, without replacing it with a modern spelling chosen from its meaning.
[c2-l033] Narabevoqi, u, oita. *Por por ordem.*
[c2-l033 note] Narabeoku means setting things out in order and leaving them arranged. The source prints voqi in the compound and abbreviates the remaining principal forms.
[c2-l034] Narabi. *Parelha. ¶* Narabimo nai coto.
[c2-l034 note] The noun narabi means a match or equal. Narabi mo nai koto introduces something without an equal, translated on the following row.
[c2-l035 >] *Couſa ſem igual.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*larſe.*
[c2-l035 note] The main text completes the example as something unequalled. At the far right, the omitted printed (larſe. is restored in its physical position: it completes igua- in the following Narabi gloss, not this sentence. It is not a catchword.
[c2-l036] Narabi, u, ôda. *Estar por ordem, ou igua-*
[c2-l036 note] The verb narabu means being in order or matching something. Its final igua- is completed by the displaced larſe. printed at the far right of the preceding row; the physical arrangement is preserved rather than silently reordered.
[c2-l037] Narabini. *Adu. Apos ißo, ou conſeguinte*
[c2-l037 note] Narabini is treated as an adverb meaning thereafter or consequently, before a second sense below. The scan supplies the i in conſeguinte, missing from the draft; its continuation has no printed hyphen.
[c2-l038 >] *mente. ¶ Item, Iunto.* Iyeno narabi
[c2-l038 note] Mente completes the Portuguese adverb. The additional meaning is 'beside' or 'next to,' illustrated by the house example ending on the next row.
[c2-l039 >] ni. *Iunto da caſa, ou logo a diante da caſa.*
[c2-l039 note] Ni completes ie no narabi ni. The Portuguese explains the location as next to the house or just in front of it, retaining the printed separation a diante.
[c2-l040] Naracu. *Inferno.*
[c2-l040 note] Naraku is glossed as hell. The short entry is complete in one physical line and does not absorb the following learning verb.
[c2-l041] Narai, ǒ, ǒta. *Aprender.*
[c2-l041 note] Narau means to learn. Both abbreviated principal forms have downward-pointing carons over o; they are not made to match the circumflex in Narabi above.
[c2-l042] Narai. *Cuſtume. Vt,* Yoni ſadame naiua
[c2-l042 note] This noun narai means custom or habitual behavior. Its example begins with the absence of constancy in the world and continues with men and women below.
[c2-l043 >] votoco vonnano naraigia. Feiq. *Lib.*
[c2-l043 note] Completes the statement about the customary behavior of men and women. The upright Feiq. cites the Heike text, while Lib. is italic apparatus continued by the book number below.
[c2-l044 >] *2. He ordinario do homem, & da molher*
[c2-l044 note] Book 2 precedes the translation: it is ordinary for husband and wife. The last word is molher with r, not the draft's molhet.
[c2-l045 >] *caſados não terem constancia. i, apartarem*
[c2-l045 note] The source comments that married people lack constancy, explaining this as separation. This is the historical example's generalization, not an editorial endorsement.
[c2-l046 >] *ſe facilmente.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes apartarem-se facilmente, separating readily. The reflexive continuation is preserved on its own physical row without inventing a line-end hyphen above.
[c2-l047] Narano qi. *Hũa certa aruore.*
[c2-l047 note] Nara no ki is identified as a particular tree. The separate Nara beneath this line is a catchword for the next page, not additional body text.
