[c1-l001] Toqiua. l, toqiuana. *Couſa q̃ não ſe muda,*
[c1-l001 note] Tokiwa and tokiwana describe something unchanging. The capital T and following oqiua are visible; the initial OCR fro- does not belong to the printed headword.
[c1-l002 >] *ou ſẽpre eſtà no meſmo ſer. ¶* Toqiua-
[c1-l002 note] The gloss continues with remaining always in the same condition. The repeated adjective starts at the right and is divided after Toqiua-.
[c1-l003 >] na fito. *Homẽ q̃ parece ſẽpre està no meſmo*
[c1-l003 note] Na hito completes tokiwana hito, a person who appears always to remain the same. The Portuguese sentence continues into the explanation of aging and consistency below.
[c1-l004 >] *ſer ſem enuelhecer, ou q̃ não faz mudança*
[c1-l004 note] The person remains unchanged without growing old, or does not change his intentions and habits. Preserve the printed enuelhecer spelling and the abbreviation q̃.
[c1-l005 >] *nos propoſitos, cuſtumes, &c.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes the second application of tokiwana hito: constancy in purposes and customs. Cuſtumes is the printed historical form, not silently modernized to costumes.
[c1-l006] Toqiua caqiua. *P. Modo de caſas, ou edi-*
[c1-l006 note] Tokiwa kakiwa is labeled poetic and describes a configuration of houses or buildings. The narrow printed separation between the two Japanese forms is retained; the Portuguese edi- continues as edificios on the following row.
[c1-l007 >] *ficios muito vnidos, & juntos.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes edificios: the buildings are closely joined together. This explains the preceding compound rather than beginning another headword.
[c1-l008] Toqiuagui. *Aruores que ſempre estão verdes*
[c1-l008 note] Tokiwagi denotes evergreen trees. The Portuguese ſempre has long s, not the OCR f; the following line explains the persistence of their leaves.
[c1-l009 >] *& nunca lhes cae a folha.*
[c1-l009 note] The trees never lose their foliage, completing the evergreen definition. The initial n of nunca is visible and corrects OCR munca; cae remains the historical spelling.
[c1-l010] Toqiyo. *Tempo, ou idade que corre. ¶* To-
[c1-l010 note] Tokiyo is the current time or age. A capital T is printed, not F; the repeated To- begins an example extending onto the next row.
[c1-l011 >] qiyoni xitagǒte. *Conforme ao tempo,*
[c1-l011 note] Tokiyo ni shitagōte means according to the time or age. The caron over o is retained in the historical contracted form xitagǒte.
[c1-l012 >] *ou idade.*
[c1-l012 note] Completes the gloss with age or era. The short continuation belongs to the example above and remains a separate physical line.
[c1-l013] Toqiyuruxi, ſu, ita. *Deſatar, & ſoltar.*
[c1-l013 note] Tokiyurushi, with the abbreviated inflected forms, means untying and releasing. The Portuguese verbs express loosening a bond, consistent with toki- and yurusu.
[c1-l014] Toqizaxi. *O determinar, ou limitar a hora*
[c1-l014 note] Tokizashi means fixing or specifying an hour. The initial capital is T; the Portuguese construction continues with the purpose for which the time is set.
[c1-l015 >] *pera algũa couſa. ¶* Toqizaxiuo xite mai-
[c1-l015 note] The appointed time is for some activity. Tokizashi wo shite mai- begins the example of going at a fixed time; mairu continues on the next line.
[c1-l016 >] ru. *Ir determinando a hora, ou tempo.*
[c1-l016 note] Ru completes mairu. The Portuguese paraphrase is going with the hour or time specified, rather than a second independent verb entry.
[c1-l017] Toqizzuqe. *O eſcreuer a hora como em que ſe*
[c1-l017 note] Tokizuke denotes writing down the time. The explanation begins a comparison with noting when a letter is dispatched or an event occurs.
[c1-l018 >] *manda a carta, ou aconteceo algũa couſa no-*
[c1-l018 note] Continues with sending a letter or the occurrence of a notable event. The printed no- is the first part of notauel, not the Japanese particle no.
[c1-l019 >] *tauel. Vt,* Toqizzuqeuo ſuru.
[c1-l019 note] Completes notauel and gives tokizuke wo suru, making such a time notation. Vt is the italic example introducer, while the Japanese phrase remains roman.
[c1-l020] Tǒqua. Momono fana. *Flor de peſegueiro.*
[c1-l020 note] Tōka is explained by momo no hana, peach blossom. The scan clearly has Flor, including the small l missed by OCR; the headword carries a caron.
[c1-l021] Tôqua. i, Tomoxibi. *Fogo de candea de*
[c1-l021 note] Tōka with the printed circumflex is equated with tomoshibi, lamplight. The oil-lamp explanation continues below and distinguishes it from the peach-blossom entry above.
[c1-l022 >] *azeite.*
[c1-l022 note] Azeite completes candea de azeite, an oil lamp. It is the final word of the preceding definition.
[c1-l023] Tora. *Tigre.*
[c1-l023 note] Tora is the ordinary word for a tiger, directly glossed by Portuguese tigre. The following separate entry gives its calendrical use.
[c1-l024] Tora. *Hum dos animaes, ou ſignos celestes por*
[c1-l024 note] Tora here names the Tiger among the zodiacal animals or signs. The scan has a capital T, not the lowercase OCR r, and the explanation continues below.
[c1-l025 >] *onde os Iapões contão as horas, ou annos.*
[c1-l025 note] The Japanese count hours and years using these animal signs. This is the indented continuation of the Tiger entry, not an unindented new entry.
[c1-l026 >] *¶* Torano cocu. *As 4. ou 5. horas*
[c1-l026 note] Tora no koku is the Tiger hour, glossed here as four or five hours after midnight. Preserve the dictionary's stated time rather than harmonizing it to another timekeeping system.
[c1-l027 >] *depois da mea noite.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes the time reference with after midnight. Mea noite is the printed phrase and is retained as two words.
[c1-l028] Toracaxi, ſu, ita. *Delir, ou derreter, como cera,*
[c1-l028 note] Torakashi, torakasu means dissolving or melting something. Wax supplies the first example, and further material and figurative uses follow.
[c1-l029 >] *chumbo, &c. ¶ Item,* Fitouo toracaſu.
[c1-l029 note] Lead completes the list of things melted. Hito wo torakasu then extends the verb to a person, with the following gloss explaining the figurative softening.
[c1-l030 >] i, Fitono cocorouo toracaſu. *Abrandar*
[c1-l030 note] Hito no kokoro wo torakasu elaborates softening someone's heart. The third letter of Fitono is abraded, but its lower curved foot agrees with the t in Fitouo directly above rather than the r in toracaſu; retain this damaged-glyph caveat. Cocorouo contains the object marker, and Abrandar begins its translation.
[c1-l031 >] *o coração dalguem que estaua duro, & tornalo*
[c1-l031 note] Abrandar governs the heart of someone who had been hard, making it pliable. Preserve the historical estaua and tornalo spellings and the aligned short-s form.
[c1-l032 >] *como cera derretida. ¶* Nicu, l, vuouo
[c1-l032 note] The heart becomes like melted wax. The next example begins with meat or fish; the abraded vowel after N is read as i in Nicu, supported by the surviving foot and the following carne translation.
[c1-l033 >] ni toracaſu. *Cozer carne, ou peixe de ma-*
[c1-l033 note] Ni torakasu describes cooking meat or fish until soft. Ma- begins maneira, the Portuguese phrase describing how thoroughly it is cooked.
[c1-l034 >] *neira que fique delido, & desfeito.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes maneira and explains that the food becomes dissolved and broken down. The printing's short s in desfeito is retained.
[c1-l035] Torague. *Cor, ou cabello de tigre. ¶* To-
[c1-l035 note] Torage names a tiger's colour or coat. The divided To- begins examples applying the coat pattern to domestic animals.
[c1-l036 >] rague inu, l, torague neco. *Cão, ou gato*
[c1-l036 note] Torage inu and torage neko are a tiger-patterned dog or cat. Latin l introduces the second alternative and is not Japanese text.
[c1-l037 >] *malhado como tigre.*
[c1-l037 note] The dog or cat is patterned like a tiger. This completes the animal-coat examples, without requiring a modern distinction between stripes and mottling.
[c1-l038] Tǒrai. Itari, qitaru. *O vir, ou chegarſe.*
[c1-l038 note] Tōrai is explained by itari, kitaru and by coming or arriving. The printed caron is visible and the entry concerns arrival rather than the following homonymous meanings.
[c1-l039 >] *Vt,* Iicocu tǒrai ſuru. *Chegarſe, ou vir*
[c1-l039 note] Jikoku tōrai suru means that the time arrives. Initial Ii has the consonantal reading ji; the worn lower-case t in tǒrai and separated ou vir correct OCR fǒrai and ourvir.
[c1-l040 >] *tempo, & hora de fazer algũa couſa.*
[c1-l040 note] The time and hour for doing something arrive. This is the continuation of chegarſe, ou vir above and closes the arrival entry.
[c1-l041] Tǒrai. *Nouas. Vt,* Tǒraiga aru, l, go-
[c1-l041 note] This tōrai means news. Tōrai ga aru or gozaru illustrates having or receiving news; the final go- continues on the next row.
[c1-l042 >] zaru. *Auer, ou virem nouas.* X.
[c1-l042 note] Zaru completes gozaru, and the Portuguese means that there is news or that news arrives. Nouas corrects the OCR nonas; the upright X. is a regional usage label.
[c1-l043] Tǒrai. i, raixe. *Mundo vindouro, ou ou-*
[c1-l043 note] A third tōrai is equated with raise, the world to come. The Portuguese Mundo has an abraded u but is supported by the surviving shape and the explanation, not OCR Mando.
[c1-l044 >] *tro mundo.*
[c1-l044 note] Tro completes outro, giving the alternative other world. The text continues the eschatological definition and is not a separate entry.
[c1-l045] Toramadara. *Couſa malhada, ou de cor de ti-*
[c1-l045 note] Toramadara describes something patterned or coloured like a tiger. The Portuguese ti- continues into tigre on the next row.
[c1-l046 >] *gre. ¶* Toramadar. no yenoco. *Cachor-*
[c1-l046 note] The example concerns a puppy with a tiger-like coat. The final part of expected Toramadara is badly abraded: only a dot-like remnant is secure after Toramadar, so Toramadar. no is retained without supplying the missing a. Cachor- continues as cachorrinho below.
[c1-l047 >] *rinho malhado como tigre.*
[c1-l047 note] Rinho completes cachorrinho, a little dog patterned like a tiger. Enlargement recovers malhado como tigre from the damaged OCR sequence; the physical line is retained.

[c2-l001] Toramaye, ru, eta. *Meliùs,* Torayuru.

[c2-l004] Toraqe, uru, eta. *Derreterſe, ou delirſe co-*
[c2-l004 note] Torake is the intransitive counterpart of melting or dissolving. The reflexive Portuguese infinitives agree with that sense; co- continues into como.

[c2-l005 note] Completes como and gives lead, wax, and thoroughly cooked fish as things that soften or dissolve. These examples parallel the transitive torakashi entry in column 1.

[c2-l007 >] *Abrandarſe, & mouerſe de todo pera algũa*
[c2-l007 note] The heart softens and is wholly moved toward something. This begins the Portuguese explanation of the wine example and continues onto the next row.
[c2-l008 >] *couſa o coração do homem com vinho.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes the statement that wine moves a person's heart toward something. The line belongs to the same figurative example, not a new definition.
[c2-l009] Toratçuqigue. *Cor de caualo malhado como ti-*
[c2-l009 note] Toratsukige names a horse colour or coat pattern likened to a tiger. The Portuguese ti- continues across the physical line boundary.
[c2-l010 >] *gre. Vt,* Toratçuqigueno vma.
[c2-l010 note] Completes tigre and gives toratsukige no uma, a horse with that coat. The example is standalone Japanese rather than a word embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c2-l011] Torauare, ruru, eta. *Ser preſo, ou catiuo*
[c2-l011 note] Toraware means being taken prisoner or captive. The dictionary gives the passive verb's principal forms and continues by specifying wartime capture.
[c2-l012 >] *na guerra, &c.*
[c2-l012 note] In war completes the captive definition. The short line is a physical continuation of toraware.
[c2-l013] Torauarebito. *Catiuo, ou tomado dos inimigos.*
[c2-l013 note] Torawarebito is a captive or someone taken by enemies. Catiuo has u rather than the OCR n in Catino.
[c2-l014] Toraxe, ſuru, eta. *Dar a peßoa inferior.*
[c2-l014 note] Torase means giving to a socially inferior person. The Portuguese marks the direction of the social relation rather than describing an intrinsically inferior gift.
[c2-l015] Toraye, ru, eta. *Afferrar, ou prender, ou*
[c2-l015 note] Toraye means seizing, taking prisoner, or laying hold of someone. The third Portuguese expression continues on the following row.
[c2-l016 >] *lançar mão dalguem.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*ou toma.*
[c2-l016 note] Lançar mão dalguem completes the seizure definition. The separate far-right ( and ou toma. are printed on this same physical row but complete the next entry's lugar por onde ſe afferra, ou toma; they are retained in place rather than moved into its text line.
[c2-l017] Torayedocoro. *Lugar por onde ſe afferra,*
[c2-l017 note] Toraedokoro is a place by which something can be grasped. The comma links it semantically to the displaced ou toma. printed at the far right of the preceding row.
[c2-l018] Tore, uru, eta. *Abrandar, ou ceſſar o ven-*
[c2-l018 note] Tore is a nautical verb for the wind abating or ceasing. Ven- begins vento, continued on the next row.
[c2-l019 >] *to. Palaura dos marinheiros. Vt,* Cajega
[c2-l019 note] Completes vento and explicitly identifies sailors' vocabulary. Kaze ga introduces the example whose verb appears below.
[c2-l020 >] toreta. *Ceßou o vento.*
[c2-l020 note] Kaze ga toreta means the wind stopped. Ceßou is the printed Portuguese past tense, with its double-s sort retained.
[c2-l021] Tore, uru, eta. *Paßiuo de* Tori, u. *Tomar-*
[c2-l021 note] A second tore is described as the passive of Tori, u, to take. The referenced Japanese form is roman throughout; the printed abbreviated u is retained without supplying an expected r.
[c2-l022 >] *ſe. Vt,* Vuoga vouoqu toreta. *Tomou-*
[c2-l022 note] Uo ga ōku toreta means that many fish were caught. The historical vouoqu spelling is retained; Tomou- is entirely italic and continues into the next row.
[c2-l023 >] *ſe muito peixe.*
[c2-l023 note] Completes tomou-se muito peixe, much fish was caught. The scan includes the i in peixe and shows the continuation at the normal indentation.
[c2-l024] Tori. *Paßaro.*

[c2-l025] Tǒri. Momo, ſumomo. *Peſegueiro, & a-*
[c2-l025 note] Tōri names peach and plum, explained by momo and sumomo. The mark above o is a downward caron, correcting the provisional circumflex; a- begins the second Portuguese tree name.
[c2-l026 >] *meixieira.* S.

[c2-l027] Tori. *Hum dos animaes por onde contão os Ia-*
[c2-l027 note] Tori here names the Bird or Rooster zodiacal sign by which the Japanese count time. The scan supplies the t in contão missed by OCR; Ia- continues into Iapões.

[c2-l029 note] The dictionary gives the Bird hour as six or seven hours after midday. Preserve its explicit reckoning and printed meo dia wording rather than substituting a modern conversion.
[c2-l030] Tori, ru, otta. *Tomar. ¶* Toriuo toru.
[c2-l030 note] Tori, toru, totta means taking or catching. Tori wo toru supplies the first example, catching birds, and is translated on the next line.

[c2-l031 note] Birds are caught with birdlime or a net. Tomar belongs wholly to the italic Portuguese gloss; its capital does not create a separate Japanese T.
[c2-l032 >] *¶* Cubiuo toru. *Tomar cabeça na guerra.*
[c2-l032 note] Kubi wo toru means taking a head in warfare. The Portuguese gloss explains this martial use of the general taking verb.
[c2-l033 >] *¶* Nauo toru. *Alcançar nome, ou fama.*
[c2-l033 note] Na wo toru means gaining a name or reputation. The meaning is acquiring fame rather than physically taking someone's name.
[c2-l034 >] *¶* Yadouo toru. *Tomar, ou alugar caſas.*
[c2-l034 note] Yado wo toru is taking lodgings or renting a house. The whole Portuguese Tomar is italic; the OCR split off its capital incorrectly.
[c2-l035 >] *¶* Xoriǒuo toru. *Alcançar renda.*

[c2-l036 >] *¶* Tano meuo toru. *Furtar a molher a-*

[c2-l037 >] *lhea. ¶* Teuo toricumu. *Tomar entre*

[c2-l043 >] uo totte voxiyuru. *Enſinar ao aprendiz*

[c2-l044 note] The teacher takes and guides the learner's hand. Aca- begins the next example referring to bilge water, not the colour red in this context.
[c2-l045 >] uo toru. *Dar à bomba. ¶* Miuo toru.
[c2-l045 note] Aka wo toru means working the pump to remove bilge water. Mi wo toru then introduces gathering fruit; the worn u of Miuo corrects the OCR t in Mito.
[c2-l046 >] *Tomar, ou recolher fruito dos campos, ou das*
[c2-l046 note] Mi wo toru means taking or gathering produce from fields or trees. The abraded opening ou is read from the surviving curved remnant and syntax; recolher corrects the OCR vowel, while the printed fruito spelling is retained.
[c2-l047 >] *aruores, &c. ¶ Itẽ, per met.* Miuo toru.
[c2-l047 note] Trees completes the literal gathering-fruit sense. Itẽ, per met. introduces a figurative use of mi wo toru that continues onto the following page, anticipated by the catchword Tirar.
