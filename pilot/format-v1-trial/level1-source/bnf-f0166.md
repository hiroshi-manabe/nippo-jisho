[c1-l001 >] *turbando ſe como diante de algũa peſſoa de reſ-*
[c1-l001 note] This continues f165's `Faimǒ`: becoming confused before a respected person. `reſ-` belongs to `reſpeito` below, not the next Japanese example.
[c1-l002 >] *peito. Vt,* Faimǒ tçucamatçutta.
[c1-l002 note] `Faimǒ tçucamatçutta` is a deferential statement of having become confused or forgetful. The polite verb helps explain the socially intimidating context.
[c1-l003] Fainobori, u, otta. *Trepar pegando ſe com*
[c1-l003 note] `Fainobori` combines crawling or clinging with ascending. Portuguese describes climbing while holding on rather than ordinary walking uphill.
[c1-l004 >] *as mãos, como quem ſobe a algũ lugar ingreme*
[c1-l004 note] The climber uses the hands to ascend a steep place. This supplies the physical manner implicit in the Japanese compound.
[c1-l005 >] *como fortaleza, &c. ¶ Item, Trepar, &*
[c1-l005 note] A fortress is one example of the steep place. `Item` switches from a person to a creeping plant.
[c1-l006 >] *ſubir da era por algũa aruore, ou parede, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] Ivy climbing a tree or wall is the botanical extension of `Fainobori`. Here Portuguese `era` means ivy.
[c1-l007] Faino zǒ. *Bofes.*
[c1-l007 note] `Faino zǒ`, 肺の臓, means lungs; `Bofes` confirms the organ. This repeats the anatomical expression encountered on f165.
[c1-l008] Faiqen. Vogami, miru. *Ler a carta com*
[c1-l008 note] `Faiqen` 拝見 is explained by reverently seeing; the Portuguese example concerns reading a letter, not eyesight alone.
[c1-l009 >] *reuerencia. ¶* Gojǒuo faiqen tçucama-
[c1-l009 note] `Gojǒuo faiqen tçucama-` begins a deferential acknowledgment of the recipient's letter. The polite verb is completed below.
[c1-l010 >] tçutta. *Vi, ou li a carta de voſſa mercè.*
[c1-l010 note] `Vi, ou li` means “I saw or read your letter.” The initial word is Portuguese `Vi`, not the Latin example marker `Vt`; the scan supports that contextual distinction.
[c1-l011] Faiqiacu. Sutaruru. *Deſfazer, ou deſtruir.*
[c1-l011 note] `Faiqiacu` concerns discarding or destroying, compatible with 廃却. `Sutaruru` and the Portuguese destructive verbs distinguish it from respectful 拝 compounds.
[c1-l012 >] *Vt,* Faiqiacu ſuru.
[c1-l012 note] `Faiqiacu ſuru` makes the noun into a verb. The example marker introduces only that construction, with no additional Portuguese definition on this line.
[c1-l013] Faiquai. i, Vôrai ſuru coto. *Correr terras,*
[c1-l013 note] `Vôrai ſuru coto` explains going about, compatible with 徘徊. `Correr terras` means traveling through lands, not necessarily running physically.
[c1-l014 >] *ou andar peregrinando por diuerſas partes.*
[c1-l014 note] Portuguese adds wandering or journeying through different places. This confirms the movement sense rather than an honorific reading of `Fai-`.
[c1-l015 >] ¶ Cuniguniuo faiquai itaſu. *Diſcorrer,*
[c1-l015 note] `Cuniguniuo faiquai itaſu` is traveling through various countries. Here `Diſcorrer` means ranging or passing through, not simply discussing a topic.
[c1-l016 >] *ou peregrinar por varios Reinos.*
[c1-l016 note] The object is several kingdoms or countries. This completes the travel example from the preceding line.
[c1-l017] Fairan. i, Vogami, miru. i, Faiqen. *Ler,*
[c1-l017 note] `Fairan` 拝覧 is equated with `Vogami, miru` and `Faiqen`. Both reading and seeing can realize respectful inspection.
[c1-l018 >] *ou ver com reuerencia.*
[c1-l018 note] “Or see with reverence” completes the gloss. The distinction from ordinary looking is social register, not a different visual action.
[c1-l019] Fairiǒ. Vogami riǒzuru. i, Tamauaru. *Re-*
[c1-l019 note] `Tamauaru` identifies humble receiving, supporting 拝領. `riǒzuru` should be understood within that compound rather than as an unrelated word.
[c1-l020 >] *ceber algũa couſa de peſſoa nobre. Vt,* Ca-
[c1-l020 note] The item is received from a noble person. `Ca-` begins the Japanese sword example, not a Portuguese word.
[c1-l021 >] tanauo fairiǒ ſuru. *Receber Catana dal-*
[c1-l021 note] `Ca- / tanauo fairiǒ ſuru` means receiving a sword respectfully. The second `Catana` is embedded in the Portuguese translation.
[c1-l022 >] *gum ſenhor, ou peſſoa nobre.*
[c1-l022 note] The donor is a lord or noble person. This completes the direction and social status of the gift in the preceding example.
[c1-l023] Fairi, u, itta. *Entrar.*
[c1-l023 note] `Fairi, u, itta` is the entering verb はいる. Its short Portuguese gloss distinguishes it from the adjacent compounds in 拝.
[c1-l024] Fairino coya. *P.* i*,* Xebai iuori. *Choupa-*
[c1-l024 note] `Xebai iuori` is a narrow dwelling or hut. The headword's precise historical formation should not be inferred merely from modern はいる; `P.` is an editorial label.
[c1-l025 >] *na eſtreita, & pequena.*
[c1-l025 note] The Portuguese specifies a hut that is narrow and small. It supplies the dwelling sense needed to interpret the previous Japanese phrase.
[c1-l026] Fairiu. *Ardil, ou inuenção.*
[c1-l026 note] `Fairiu` is glossed as a stratagem or contrivance. Earlier NINJAL comparison recorded the same spelling with a likely intended `Fairiǔ`; that length restoration remains annotation, not transcription.
[c1-l027] Faiſǔ. Vogami vaxiru. *Reuerenciar, ou*
[c1-l027 note] `Faisǔ` describes reverential or diligent attendance. The printed `vaxiru` is suspicious if one expects はしる in 拝趨; keep the discrepancy visible rather than treating the automatic kana as proof.
[c1-l028 >] *ſeruir com diligencia, & reſpeito.*
[c1-l028 note] Serving with diligence and respect completes the definition. This supports the honorific action but does not independently settle the unusual Japanese letter above.
[c1-l029] Faitaca. *Gauião que toma codornizes.*
[c1-l029 note] `Faitaca` ハイタカ is a hawk that catches quail. The prey named in Portuguese helps distinguish it from an unspecified bird.
[c1-l030] Faitçuqi, u, uita. *Trepar pegãdoſe como*
[c1-l030 note] `Faitçuqi` is clinging while climbing. The Portuguese comparison begins here and names ivy below.
[c1-l031 >] *era na aruore, &c.*
[c1-l031 note] Ivy adhering to a tree supplies the model for the preceding verb. The gloss concerns attachment as well as upward movement.
[c1-l032] Faitei. Vogami ſaſaguru. *Reſponder a*
[c1-l032 note] `Vogami ſaſaguru` explains respectful presentation. Portuguese associates `Faitei` with a deferential response, so the following epistolary usage matters.
[c1-l033 >] *peſſoa alta cõ reuerencia: palaura q̃ ſe põe nos*
[c1-l033 note] The addressee is high-ranking. The word is used in written addresses or superscriptions, not merely spoken as a reply.
[c1-l034 >] *ſobreſcritos das cartas, ou Vtas que vão em*
[c1-l034 note] Letters and `Vias` are mentioned as vehicles of the response. The latter's interpretation is not secure enough here to label it automatically as Japanese vocabulary.
[c1-l035 >] *repoſta a peſſoas honradas, &c.*
[c1-l035 note] These communications answer honored people. The line closes the description of the term's placement in correspondence.
[c1-l036] Faitǒ. Cubari, atçuru. *Repartição.*
[c1-l036 note] `Cubari, atçuru` means distributing or allotting, supporting 配当. This noun differs from the identically spelled respectful-reply entry below.
[c1-l037] Faitǒ. *Repoſta com reuerencia a peſſoa nobre.*
[c1-l037 note] This `Faitǒ` denotes a reverent answer to a noble person, compatible with 拝答. The homograph above instead concerns allocation.
[c1-l038] Fai u. i, Sacazzuqi. *Copo, ou taça. S.*
[c1-l038 note] `Sacazzuqi` gives サカヅキ, identifying the cup sense. `S.` is an editorial label; the unusual printed headword spacing is not resolved by its meaning.
[c1-l039] Faiuatari, u, atta. *Eſtenderſe, ou paſſar de*
[c1-l039 note] `Faiuatari` describes spreading or passing from one place to another. The compound combines creeping with crossing.
[c1-l040 >] *hũa parte a outra como era, ou erua de latada q̃*
[c1-l040 note] Ivy or a trellised plant provides the first example. The movement is between supports, completed below.
[c1-l041 >] *paſſa de hũ pao a outro. Itẽ, Eſtẽder ſe o bicho,*
[c1-l041 note] The plant passes from one stick to another. `Item` begins the same extension or bridging movement in an animal.
[c1-l042 >] *ou cobra, & paſſar de hũ ramo a outro, &c.*
[c1-l042 note] A creature or snake stretches from branch to branch. This completes the animal example of the preceding creeping-across verb.
[c1-l043] Faiuô. *Repoſta que ſe daa com reuerencia:*
[c1-l043 note] `Faiuô` is a reverential reply, distinct from crawling compounds. The following lines explain its written epistolary setting.
[c1-l044 >] *palaura que ſe poem nos ſobreſcritos das car-*
[c1-l044 note] The expression is placed in the superscription of letters. Read `car-` with the next line rather than treating it as Japanese.
[c1-l045 >] *tas que vão pera peſſoas honradas.*
[c1-l045 note] The letters go to honored people. This closes the usage restriction rather than adding another sense of the headword.
[c1-l046] Faiuocu. Sutareta iye. *Caſa caida, ou velha.*
[c1-l046 note] `Sutareta iye`, an abandoned house, explains 廃屋. Portuguese gives a fallen or old house, a compatible but not identical descriptive emphasis.
[c1-l047] Faixi, ſuru, ita. *Adorar.*
[c1-l047 note] This `Faixi, ſuru, ita` means worshipping, from 拝. The next column begins another verb of the same spelling with a different meaning.

[c2-l001] Faixi, ſuru, ita. *Lançar longe, ou dester-*
[c2-l001 note] This `Faixi` instead means casting away or exiling. Keep it separate from the worshipping verb at the preceding column's foot.
[c2-l002 >] *rar. Vt,* Qimiuo yentǒye faixitatema-
[c2-l002 note] `Qimiuo yentǒye` sends the lord to a distant island. The honorific verb continues across the physical line break.
[c2-l003 >] tçurite. *Deſterrãdo a o ſenhor pera hũa ilha*
[c2-l003 note] `faixitatema- / tçurite` completes the deferential exile construction. Portuguese confirms exiling the lord to an island; the final adjective follows below.
[c2-l004 >] *longe.*
[c2-l004 note] `longe` completes “a distant island.” This short line supplies distance, not another headword or geographical name.
[c2-l005] Faixin. *Offerecer algũa couſa ao ſenhor, ou*
[c2-l005 note] `Faixin` denotes offering something to a lord. The recipient rather than the object determines the honorific interpretation.
[c2-l006 >] *a peſſoa nobre.*
[c2-l006 note] A noble person is the alternative recipient. This finishes the offering definition without a separate Japanese example.
[c2-l007] Faixo. Sutçuru tocoro. i, Runinno toco-
[c2-l007 note] `Sutçuru tocoro` and `Runinno toco-` explain an exile destination. `Runin` is a banished person, not a modern masterless-samurai gloss imposed here.
[c2-l008 >] ro. *Lugar de desterro.*
[c2-l008 note] `toco- / ro` completes “place.” Portuguese `Lugar de deſterro` confirms a place of banishment.
[c2-l009] Faixǒ. Vogami, vquru. *Receber a alguem*
[c2-l009 note] `Vogami, vquru` describes respectfully receiving someone, in a deferential reception context. Avoid choosing kanji solely from the mechanical reading.
[c2-l010 >] *com reuerencia em ſua caſa, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] The reception occurs in one's house and is reverent. This is hosting a person, not receiving a gift as in 拝領.
[c2-l011] Faixo. i, faijo. *Oratorio, ou ermida que ſe*
[c2-l011 note] `Faixo, i, faijo` names a shrine or small oratory. The memorial context in the next lines distinguishes this from the exile homograph.
[c2-l012 >] *aleuanta pera algum defunto pondolhe ſeu no-*
[c2-l012 note] The oratory is erected for a deceased person. Their name is placed on an object specified below.
[c2-l013 >] *me eſcrito em hũa taboa, &c.* || {mark} *(* || {word} *ruina.*
[c2-l013 note] The name is written on a board. The displaced `( ruina.` physically shares this row but logically completes the next entry's Portuguese definition.
[c2-l014] Faiye. Sutare, yabururu. *i*, *Deſtruição, ou*
[c2-l014 note] `Sutare, yabururu` explains destruction or ruin, compatible with 敗壊. Read the Portuguese together with the displaced `ruina` above, without changing physical line order.
[c2-l015] Faiyet. Vogami qemiſuru. *Ver com reue-*
[c2-l015 note] `Faiyet` corresponds to 拝閲, reverential inspection; final `-t` is a historical checked ending. `qemiſuru` is the reading/examining gloss, not an accidental word ending.
[c2-l016 >] *rencia.*
[c2-l016 note] `reue- / rencia` completes “with reverence.” The line supplies the manner of inspection defined above.
[c2-l017] Faiyoxe. *O ajuntarem os parentes, & amigos*
[c2-l017 note] `Faiyoxe` begins the gathering of cremation ashes. Relatives and friends, rather than officials, are the actors named here.
[c2-l018 >] *do morto ao outro dia no campo as cinzas do*
[c2-l018 note] They gather the next day at the cremation place. The ashes belong to the deceased body described in the next line.
[c2-l019 >] *corpo queimado, & enterralas. Vt,* Faiyo
[c2-l019 note] The burned body's ashes are collected and buried. `Faiyo-` begins the corresponding Japanese verbal example.
[c2-l020 >] xe ſuru. ¶ Faiyoxeni mairu. *Ir ajun-*
[c2-l020 note] `Faiyo- / xe ſuru` means performing the ash-gathering; `Faiyoxeni mairu` means going to it. The Portuguese translation continues below.
[c2-l021 >] *tar, & enterrar eſtas cinzas.*
[c2-l021 note] “Collect and bury these ashes” completes the example. This confirms that the action is funerary, not ordinary sweeping.
[c2-l022] Faji. l, fajino qi. *Hũa aruore de que fazẽ*
[c2-l022 note] `Faji, l, fajino qi` gives a tree name and its expanded form. The Portuguese identifies a practical use rather than a botanical description.
[c2-l023 >] *os paoſinhos com que comem.*
[c2-l023 note] The wood supplies eating sticks, or chopsticks. This connects the tree entry with the similar account on f165 without merging their spellings.
[c2-l024] Fajicami. *Iingiuere.*
[c2-l024 note] `Fajicami` is はじかみ, here glossed as ginger. The faint Portuguese word deserves glyph-level caution; its botanical sense is clearer than each printed letter.
[c2-l025] Fajimari. *Principio.*
[c2-l025 note] `Fajimari` means a beginning. It is the noun, distinguished from the inflected verb in the next entry.
[c2-l026] Fajimari, u, atta. *Começarſe.*
[c2-l026 note] `Fajimari, u, atta` is the intransitive “begin.” The Portuguese reflexive form similarly presents something beginning, not someone initiating an object.
[c2-l027] Fajime. *Começo, ou principio.*
[c2-l027 note] `Fajime` is another nominal beginning or commencement. The following entry supplies its verbal use.
[c2-l028] Fajime, uru, eta. *Começar. Ajuntaſe às rai-*
[c2-l028 note] `Fajime, uru, eta` means beginning something. Portuguese explicitly says it combines with many verb stems, introducing examples below.
[c2-l029 >] *zes de muitos verbos. Vt,* Yomi fajimuru.
[c2-l029 note] `Yomifajimuru` is beginning to read, with 読み as the preceding stem. This illustrates the compounding rule rather than a separate headword.
[c2-l030 >] *Começar a ler.* Caqifajimuru. *Come-*
[c2-l030 note] The first gloss finishes “begin to read.” The fresh pass and a larger crop support `Caqifajimuru`, beginning to write: `Caqi-` is the writing stem, parallel to `Yomi-` above, not the earlier `Caqu-` reading.
[c2-l031 >] *çar a eſcreuer, &c.*
[c2-l031 note] `Come- / çar a eſcreuer` completes “begin to write.” This confirms the intended sense of the questionable Japanese compound above.
[c2-l032] Fajimete. *A primeira vez. Vt,* Fajime-
[c2-l032 note] `Fajimete` means “for the first time.” The example begins here and must be interpreted with the respectful meeting expression below.
[c2-l033 >] te võ meni cacaru. *Esta he a primeira vez*
[c2-l033 note] `Fajimete vǒ meni cacaru` means meeting someone for the first time. The Portuguese translation continues with the object of seeing.
[c2-l034 >] *que vos vejo.*
[c2-l034 note] `que vos vejo` completes “this is the first time I see you.” The direct Portuguese wording translates a deferential Japanese expression.
[c2-l035] Fajimeto xite. *Adu. Principalmente. Vt,*
[c2-l035 note] `Fajimeto xite` is explained as “principally,” not merely “at the beginning.” `Adu.` identifies adverbial use.
[c2-l036 >] Coreuo fajimeto xite. *Este principalmẽte*
[c2-l036 note] `Coreuo fajimeto xite` means taking this especially or foremost. The Portuguese “this principally” supports that selective emphasis.
[c2-l037] Fajiqi, u, ijta. *Dar papirote com os dedos.*
[c2-l037 note] `Fajiqi` is flicking with the fingers. The displayed paradigm's `ijta` is unusual beside expected `ita`; retain the printed sequence rather than inferring an inflectional correction.
[c2-l038 >] *¶ Item, Dar couces o animal, principalmen*
[c2-l038 note] An additional sense is an animal kicking. The restriction `principalmen` continues onto the next line.
[c2-l039 >] *te boi, ou vaca. ¶ Item, Largar algum*
[c2-l039 note] Oxen and cows are the principal animals meant. `Item` then begins releasing something held under tension.
[c2-l040 >] *pao, ou ramo da aruore que esta enteſado, ou*
[c2-l040 note] A piece of wood or branch has been bent or tensioned. Releasing it creates the springing motion underlying this sense of はじく.
[c2-l041 >] *vara como abuis, &c.*
[c2-l041 note] A rod is another example of the tensioned object. The comparison `abuis` is not securely identified here and should not be translated as a known Japanese term without evidence.
[c2-l042] Fajitomi. *Portas abertas do meo para cima co*
[c2-l042 note] `Fajitomi` denotes doors or shutters opening from the middle upward, compatible with 半蔀. The physical description continues with their grating-like form.
[c2-l043 >] *mo grades.*
[c2-l043 note] “Like gratings” completes the shutter definition. This describes construction rather than an additional opening action.
[c2-l044] Fama. *Praya.*
[c2-l044 note] `Fama` is 浜, a beach. The next homographs have specialized object senses rather than geography.
[c2-l045] Fama. *Pedras que ſe tomão ao contrario ven*
[c2-l045 note] This `Fama` denotes stones captured from an opponent. The game is identified in the following line.
[c2-l046 >] *cendo no jogo do* Go *de Iapão.*
[c2-l046 note] `Go` is the Japanese board game. The definition concerns captured stones, compatible with the go term ハマ, not pebbles gathered at a beach.
[c2-l047] Fama. *Hum circulo de palha, ou de junco que*
[c2-l047 note] Another `Fama` is a straw or rush ring. Its users and activity continue on f167, so the object should not be identified from its shape alone.
