[c1-l004] Guareô. Yeni caita reô. *Lagarto pintado. S.*
[c1-l004 note] The Japanese explanation means a dragon depicted in a picture. The Portuguese calls it a painted lagarto; the circumflexes in Guareô and reô and the upright written-language label S. are preserved.
[c1-l005] Guareô. Fuſu reô. *Lagarto q̃ eſtà deitado. S.*

[c1-l006] Guariacu. Cauara, tçubute. *Telhas, & pe-*

[c1-l007 >] *dras. Vt,* Meixuuo xirazareba cayette
[c1-l007 note] Completes pedras and begins an example about failing to recognize a precious jewel. The Japanese conditional continues on the next row; Vt is printed in the italic explanatory apparatus.
[c1-l008 >] guariacuto naſu. Xix. *Não conhecen-*
[c1-l008 note] The example treats the unrecognized jewel as rubble. Xix. is an upright source label, not part of the Japanese sentence; the damaged Portuguese word reads conhecen-, continued as conhecendo below.
[c1-l009 >] *do as pedras precioſas estimalas como telhas,*
[c1-l009 note] Completes conhecendo: not recognizing precious stones, one values them as tiles. The sentence supplies the literal explanation of the Japanese example rather than a new entry.
[c1-l010 >] *ou pedras. i, Quem não conhece as couſas não*
[c1-l010 note] After 'or stones', i introduces the broader lesson: someone who does not know things does not appreciate them. Its final verb is on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] *as estima.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes the proverbial interpretation: such a person does not esteem them. The short line closes Guariacu.
[c1-l012] Guaſſui. i, Tçuqino ſauari. *Regra das mo*
[c1-l012 note] Guaſſui is menstruation, paraphrased by Tçuqino ſauari. The Portuguese word molheres is physically divided after mo without a secure printed hyphen; none is supplied.
[c1-l013 >] *lheres. ¶* Guaſsuiga tomaru. *Deixar*
[c1-l013 note] Completes molheres and introduces the menstrual flow stopping. Enlargement shows a short second s, while the first s is abraded to a tall stem: Guaſsuiga is selected, with the headword Guaſſui supporting its identity but not overriding that local allograph. The damaged tomaru is compatible with the visible letters and cessation gloss; these worn strokes remain a nonblocking caveat.
[c1-l014 >] *a molher de fazer ſua regra ordinaria.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes Deixar: a woman ceases to have her usual menstrual period. This supports the interpretation of tomaru without adding a medical cause not stated in the entry.

[c1-l022] Guaxacu. Cauara ixi. *Telha, & pedra.*

[c1-l023] Guaxeqi. *Idem.*

[c1-l024] Guaxǒ. *Hũa certa taboa com hũa pintura de*
[c1-l024 note] Guaxǒ denotes a board bearing a picture. The caron distinguishes the printed headword, while the Portuguese description continues with the picture's material and placement.

[c1-l025 note] The paper picture is pasted to the board, which hangs in temples. Teras is the Japanese temple term used within the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l026] Guaxxocu. *Eclypſe da lũa. ¶* Coyoiua guax
[c1-l026 note] Guaxxocu means a lunar eclipse. Coyoiua starts 'tonight', and guax continues on the next row as the same eclipse word; there is no secure hyphen at this line end.

[c1-l027 note] Completes the example: there is a lunar eclipse tonight. The translation reads eclypſe, matching the preceding definition rather than the draft's eolypſe.
