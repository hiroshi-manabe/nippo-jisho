[c1-l001] Sonyeqi. i, Son, tocu. *Perda, & proueito.*
[c1-l001 note] Sonyeqi is loss and gain, explicitly unpacked as son and tocu. The small separator after i is a comma; the Portuguese gives the same paired contrast.
[c1-l002] Sonyô. Tattoqi catachi. *Figura de peßoa*
[c1-l002 note] Sonyô denotes the dignified appearance of a noble or sacred person; Tattoqi catachi gives the Japanese paraphrase. The definition continues on the next row, so peßoa is not an isolated gloss.
[c1-l003 >] *nobre, ou ſancta como de* Fotoque, *&c.*
[c1-l003 note] The person is noble or holy, with Fotoque as the example. Fotoque is upright Japanese embedded in the otherwise italic Portuguese explanation, not a new headword.
[c1-l004] Sonzaxi, ſu, aita. *Danar, ou tratar mal.*
[c1-l004 note] Sonzaxi gives the principal forms of a verb meaning to damage or mistreat. The -aita form is historical morphology and should not be modernized to an expected -shita spelling.
[c1-l005 >] *¶* Gotaiuo ſonzaſu. *Tratar, ou fazer*
[c1-l005 note] Gotaiuo ſonzaſu applies the verb to the body, meaning to injure or mistreat it. The object particle remains attached to Gotaiuo as printed.
[c1-l006 >] *mal ao corpo.*
[c1-l006 note] Mal ao corpo completes the preceding Portuguese example: doing harm to the body. This short continuation is a real physical body line.
[c1-l007] Sonzǒ. Tattoqi ſugata. *Figura, ou eſtatua*
[c1-l007 note] Sonzǒ is an image or statue of an honored figure; Tattoqi ſugata parallels the appearance gloss above. The final vowel carries a caron in this entry.
[c1-l008 >] *de* Fotoque, *&c.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the statue definition by naming Fotoque. The Japanese religious term is in upright type within the Portuguese phrase.
[c1-l009] Sonzon. *Deſcendentes. Vt,* Xixi ſonzõni
[c1-l009 note] Sonzon denotes descendants, and Xixi ſonzõni begins the expression extending through successive generations. The mark in ſonzõni is read as an abbreviation tilde, not the caron of the preceding Sonzǒ entry.
[c1-l010 >] itarumade. *Atè os derradeiros deſcẽdentes.*
[c1-l010 note] Itarumade completes the Japanese expression, extending to the last descendants. The Portuguese abbreviated deſcẽdentes retains its tilde rather than expanding the omitted nasal.
[c1-l011] Soppǒ. *Ley vniuerſal que ha em todas as*
[c1-l011 note] Soppǒ is explained as a universal law found everywhere. The scan has vniuerſal; the extra u in the provisional vnuiuerſal has no printed counterpart.
[c1-l012 >] *partes.*
[c1-l012 note] Partes completes em todas as partes, meaning in all places. Its separate short line is retained rather than merged with the definition above.
[c1-l013] Sǒqe. *Ceſto de canas, ou canaſtrinha.*
[c1-l013 note] Sǒqe is a cane basket or small hamper, according to both Portuguese nouns. The scan is the authority for the marked first vowel; no modern basket-name spelling is substituted.
[c1-l014] Sǒqei. *Nome de hũa certa iguaria.*
[c1-l014 note] Sǒqei is described only as the name of a particular prepared dish. The note does not infer ingredients that the printed definition does not supply.
[c1-l015] Soqen. Xiroi qinuno coromo. *Veſtido de*
[c1-l015 note] Soqen is paraphrased as a white silk garment. The Japanese reads Xiroi, not the provisional Xitoi; white in the continuing Portuguese confirms the word identity.
[c1-l016 >] *ſeda branca que trazem por cima algũs Bonzos*
[c1-l016 note] The garment is white silk worn over other clothing by some Buddhist monks. The qualification mais mancebos follows on the next physical row.
[c1-l017 >] *mais mancebos.* || {mark}*(* || {word}*de palha.* || S.
[c1-l017 note] Mais mancebos completes the description of younger monks. The right-hand (de palha. S. is displaced material completing Sǒqen on the following row, not part of the clothing definition; its physical placement and upright usage label are retained.
[c1-l018] Sǒqen. Cuſano noqiba. *Borda do tecto da caſa*
[c1-l018 note] Sǒqen, paraphrased as Cuſano noqiba, denotes the eaves of a thatched house. De palha and the written-register label are physically placed at the far right of the preceding row and must not be moved into this line.
[c1-l019] Soqi. i, Coinegǒ. *Pedir, ou deſejar. Vt,*
[c1-l019 note] Soqi is requesting or wishing, with Coinegǒ as its Japanese equivalent. The caron in Coinegǒ is preserved rather than replacing the historical form with modern spelling.
[c1-l020 >] Soqiſuru.
[c1-l020 note] Soqiſuru is the verbal example for making a request or expressing a wish. Its short standalone physical row belongs to the preceding entry.
[c1-l021] Sǒqi. l, sǒqina. *Couſa feita deſatentada, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Sǒqi and the alternative adjective sǒqina describe something done inattentively or clumsily. The lowercase alternative has a short s in the scan and is not globally changed to long ſ.
[c1-l022 >] *atabalhoadamente. ¶* Sôqina fito. *Ho-*
[c1-l022 note] Atabalhoadamente completes the careless-action gloss. Sôqina fito introduces a heedless person; the tiny vowel mark is abraded, so the aligned baseline circumflex is retained rather than harmonizing it to sǒqina above. Ho- begins Homem on the next row.
[c1-l023 >] *mem leue, arremeſado, ou deſatentado.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes Homem: a flighty, rash or inattentive person. The final adjective is continuous deſatentado, not the provisional split deſa tentado.
[c1-l024] Soqiacu. Ai fedatçu. *Deſcuido.*
[c1-l024 note] Soqiacu is neglect or failure of attention. Ai fedatçu supplies the Japanese paraphrase, and its f is retained as part of fedatçu rather than being confused with long ſ.
[c1-l025] Sôqiǒ. i, Tattomi vyamǒ. *Veneração, ou*
[c1-l025 note] Sôqiǒ denotes veneration or respect. Tattomi vyamǒ expresses honoring and revering; the final mark of vyamǒ is read as a caron from the scan.
[c1-l026 >] *reuerencia. Vt,* Fitouo ſôqiǒ ſuru. *Hon-*
[c1-l026 note] Fitouo ſôqiǒ ſuru means to honor a person. The Portuguese translation starts Hon- here and finishes on the next physical row.
[c1-l027 >] *rar, & venerar a alguem.*
[c1-l027 note] Rar completes Honrar, followed by venerating someone. The fragment is the continuation of the preceding example, not a separate entry.
[c1-l028] Sǒqua. Cuſabana. *Flores, ou fulas que nacẽ*
[c1-l028 note] Cuſabana identifies flowers of herbaceous plants; the scan supports n rather than the provisional u in the final component. The second Portuguese noun is read fulas, an unusual form retained without lexical modernization; its precise status remains uncertain.
[c1-l029 >] *deruas.*
[c1-l029 note] Deruas completes the explanation of flowers growing from herbs. The printed contraction is kept instead of expanding it to de ervas.
[c1-l030] Soquai. i, Fonmǒ. *Proprio deſejo, & legi-*
[c1-l030 note] Soquai means one's proper or legitimate desire, with Fonmǒ as its Japanese equivalent. The initial is S rather than OCR B, and legi- continues below.
[c1-l031 >] *timo. Vt,* Soquaiuo toguru. *Comprir*
[c1-l031 note] Timo completes legitimo. Soquaiuo toguru is to fulfill one's desire, translated by Comprir and the following row.
[c1-l032 >] *totalmente ſeu desejo.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes the example as fully satisfying one's own desire. The printed desejo spelling is retained independently of the long-s form used elsewhere on the page.
[c1-l033] Sôquan. l, ſôguan. *Hũa dignidade de*
[c1-l033 note] Sôquan and ſôguan are alternative pronunciations of a Buddhist clerical dignity. The scan preserves the q/g contrast and the circumflexes; the alternatives are not separate Portuguese words.
[c1-l034 >] *Bonzos.*
[c1-l034 note] Bonzos completes the definition of a dignity held by Buddhist monks. The brief continuation remains a separate physical line.
[c1-l035] Soquat. Yomigayeri, u. *O reſuſcitar.* S.
[c1-l035 note] Soquat is reviving or rising again, paraphrased by Yomigayeri. The terminal t belongs to the printed Sino-Japanese form; the written-register label S. is upright.
[c1-l036 >] *Vt,* Soquat ſuru.
[c1-l036 note] Soquat ſuru gives the corresponding verbal construction for reviving. It continues the preceding entry and does not alter the headword's final consonant.
[c1-l037] Sora. *Ar, ou ceo.*
[c1-l037 note] Sora is air or sky, the literal sense underlying many of the following compounds. Both Portuguese nouns are retained as printed.
[c1-l038] Sorabore. *O moſtrar fingidamente ter grande*
[c1-l038 note] Sorabore means pretending to have great love or affection. The Portuguese construction continues directly with amor on the next line.
[c1-l039 >] *amor, ou afeição. Vt,* Soraboreuo ſuru.
[c1-l039 note] The gloss completes the idea of feigned affection. The scan reads amor and repeats Soraboreuo, correcting the provisional antor and Sotaboreuo.
[c1-l040] Soraboye. *O ladrar do cão de noite à maneira*
[c1-l040 note] Soraboye is a dog's night barking in the manner of a howl. The headword starts at the normal entry margin, and O ladrar has a real word space.
[c1-l041 >] *de vyuo. No Cami ſe diz,* Nagaboye. *No*
[c1-l041 note] De vyuo completes the comparison to howling. The Portuguese then identifies Nagaboye as the term used in Cami, before introducing a second regional name below.
[c1-l042 >] *Ximo,* Tatçuboye.
[c1-l042 note] The Ximo regional equivalent is Tatçuboye. The initial of Ximo is visibly capitalized; the Japanese name remains upright within this continuation of the Portuguese comparison.
[c1-l043] Soradachi. *O eſtar em pee nalgum lugar ſoſpei-*
[c1-l043 note] Soradachi means standing idly in a suspicious place. Enlargement establishes two e-types in the historical spelling pee, restoring the second one omitted by the provisional text; ſoſpei- continues on the next row.
[c1-l044 >] *toſo ſem fazer nada. ¶* Soradachiuo ſu-
[c1-l044 note] The gloss completes standing without doing anything. Soradachiuo ſu- begins the illustrative verb phrase and keeps its printed terminal hyphen.
[c1-l045 >] ru. *Idem. No Ximo ſe diz,* Sozoroda-
[c1-l045 note] Ru completes ſu-ru. The Portuguese introduces the Ximo equivalent Sozorodachi, whose last syllable is physically continued on the next row.
[c1-l046 >] chi ſuru.
[c1-l046 note] Chi ſuru completes Sozoroda-chi ſuru from the preceding row. It belongs to the regional equivalent, not to a separate headword.
[c1-l047] Soradamari. *Fingimento. Vt,* Soradama-
[c1-l047 note] The worn headword reads Soradamari, meaning dissimulation, not the provisional Buracanran. Its example breaks as Soradama- at the column foot and resumes with ri xite iru at the top of column 2.
