[c1-l001] Tǒura. *Esta praya, ou lugar maritimo.*
[c1-l001 note] Tōura denotes this shore or maritime place. The initial caron and the gloss’s printed Esta are retained without regularizing either form.
[c1-l002] Tǒxa. *Eſte templo de Camis.*
[c1-l002 note] Tōsha denotes this kami shrine. Camis occurs inside the Portuguese explanation as the plural designation of the deities, rather than as another headword.
[c1-l003] Toxa. Faqi, cudaſu. *Vomitar, & fazer ca-*
[c1-l003 note] Tosha is vomiting and purging, explained by haki and kudasu. The comma after Vomitar and the terminal ca- are visible; camaras continues below.
[c1-l004 >] *maras, como quãdo hũ eſtà doente de mordexim.*
[c1-l004 note] Maras completes camaras, bowel evacuations. The comparison concerns a patient suffering from mordexim; the historical disease term is retained without imposing a modern diagnosis.
[c1-l005] Tôxacu. Tatacǒ ſuzume. *Pardal que eſtà*
[c1-l005 note] Tōshaku is a sparrow engaged in fighting, with tatakō suzume as its Japanese explanation. The circumflex on the headword differs visibly from the caron in tatacǒ.
[c1-l006 >] *baralhado com outro pelejando. ¶* Tôxacu
[c1-l006 note] The sparrow is entangled with another in combat. The repeated Tôxacu begins the proverb continued on the next physical row.
[c1-l007 >] fitoni vogizu. *Opardal que eſtà trauado*
[c1-l007 note] Hito ni ojizu completes the claim that the fighting sparrow does not fear people. The Portuguese starts its matching explanation; the close printed Opardal is retained.
[c1-l008 >] *com outro pelejando não temegente. Aplicaſe*
[c1-l008 note] The sparrow, caught up in fighting, does not fear people. Aplicaſe announces an application of the saying to human behaviour, continued below.
[c1-l009 >] *ta nbem a homens que quando eſtão brigando não*
[c1-l009 note] The saying also applies to men absorbed in a fight. The damaged opening is retained as ta nbem from the aligned draft: tambem is the clear intended word, but the missing stroke and unusual gap are not silently supplied.
[c1-l010 >] *tem conta com ſenhor, nem com ninguem.*
[c1-l010 note] The fighters pay no heed to their lord or anyone else. This completes the proverb’s human application, rather than introducing a separate maxim.
[c1-l011] Tǒxei. Ataruyo. l, imano jidai. *Eſta ida-*
[c1-l011 note] Tōsei denotes the present age, explained as the current world or present era. The attached Ataruyo spacing and the alternative marker l are preserved.
[c1-l012 >] *de, ou eſte mundo de agora. ¶ Item, Cou*
[c1-l012 note] De completes idade. The entry then adds the sense of something current now; Cou begins couſa without a securely visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l013 >] *ſa que corre agora de preſente.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes couſa: something in current use at the present time. The short continuation belongs to the preceding Tǒxei entry.
[c1-l014] Toxei. Youo vataru. *Paſſar a vida. Vt,* To-
[c1-l014 note] Tosei is making one’s way through life, yo wo wataru. The example begins with roman To- after italic Vt; the OCR had incorrectly carried italics into the Japanese fragment.
[c1-l015 >] xeiuo naguequ. *Negocear a vida com tra-*
[c1-l015 note] Xei completes Toxei in the example tosei wo nageku. The Portuguese describes earning or managing one’s livelihood with hardship, with trabalhos divided at the line break.
[c1-l016 >] *balhos, & fadigas. ¶* Toxeiuo itona-
[c1-l016 note] Balhos completes trabalhos, followed by fadigas. The next example, tosei wo itonamu, begins on this row and continues below.
[c1-l017 >] mu. *Negocear a vida, & ſuſtentação.*
[c1-l017 note] Mu completes itonamu. The Portuguese explains arranging one’s livelihood and sustenance; the divided Japanese verb remains in physical line order.
[c1-l018] Tôxen. Noboru yamabito. i, Xennin. *Ho-*
[c1-l018 note] Tōsen is explained by an ascending mountain person, identified as xennin. The scan supports initial T, not OCR F; the description of a Chinese philosopher begins with Ho-.
[c1-l019 >] *mem philosopho da China que viue nos mõtes,*
[c1-l019 note] Mem completes homem. The Chinese philosopher lives in the mountains; philosopho is one printed word, correcting the OCR’s artificial division philoso pho.
[c1-l020 >] *& dizem fazer marauilhas como aleuantarſe*
[c1-l020 note] These figures are said to perform wonders, including rising into the air. The visible i in marauilhas corrects the OCR omission without modernizing the historical u/v spelling.
[c1-l021 >] *nos ares, &c.*
[c1-l021 note] Nos ares completes the claim of rising into the air. This short row closes the description of the mountain immortal.
[c1-l022] Tôxen. Tatacai, tatacǒ. *Peleja, ou batalha.*
[c1-l022 note] This second Tôxen means combat or battle, explained by tatakai and tatakō. The broken capital is T, and the second explanatory form begins with lowercase t in the scan.
[c1-l023] Tǒxen. Carano fune. *Embarcação da China.*
[c1-l023 note] Tōsen here means a Chinese vessel, kara no fune. Its caron distinguishes it graphically from the two preceding circumflex-bearing entries.
[c1-l024] Tôxendǒ. *Estrada, ou caminho por montes*
[c1-l024 note] Tōsendō is a road through mountains. The printed final caron and the historical Portuguese Estrada are preserved; the route’s direction is specified below.
[c1-l025 >] *pera a parte do Leſte, em reſpeito de Miaco.*
[c1-l025 note] The road runs eastward relative to Miaco, the capital. Miaco is a Japanese place designation embedded in a Portuguese sentence, so it is recorded for typeface review.
[c1-l026] Tǒxet. Catanade coroſu. *O matar cõ Catana.*
[c1-l026 note] Tōset is explained as killing with a sword, katana de korosu. Catana is repeated within the Portuguese gloss; the final t of the headword is not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l027] Tǒxi. Tatacai xiſuru. *O Morrer pelejando,*
[c1-l027 note] The compound is explained as dying while fighting. The Portuguese morrer pelejando corresponds directly to tatakai shisuru rather than to the ordinary word for a year below.
[c1-l028 >] *ou à eſpada.*
[c1-l028 note] Ou à eſpada adds death by the sword. This short continuation belongs to the battle-death entry.
[c1-l029] Toxi. *Anno. Vt,* Toxiga tçumoru. *En-*
[c1-l029 note] Toshi means year. Toshi ga tsumoru, literally accumulating years, is glossed as growing old; the Portuguese infinitive is divided after En-.
[c1-l030 >] *uelhecer. ¶* Toxiga taquru. *Ir*
[c1-l030 note] Velhecer completes enuelhecer. Toshi ga takuru introduces advancing in age, with the Portuguese ir continued on the next line.
[c1-l031 >] *crecendo na idade. ¶* Toxiga yuqu. *En-*
[c1-l031 note] The first phrase completes growing in age. Toshi ga yuku supplies another expression for ageing, again followed by divided En-uelhecer.
[c1-l032 >] *uelhecer. ¶* Toxiuo coyuru. l, toru.
[c1-l032 note] After completing enuelhecer, the entry gives toshi wo koyuru or toru. These examples lead into passing from one year to another below.
[c1-l033 >] *Paßar de hum anno a outro. ¶* Toxino cu-
[c1-l033 note] The gloss describes crossing from one year to another. Toshi no kure begins at the end of the row and continues across the physical break.
[c1-l034 >] re. *Cabo do anno. ¶* Toxiuo curaſu.
[c1-l034 note] Re completes kure, the year’s end. A new example, toshi wo kurasu, then introduces living or passing one’s life.
[c1-l035 >] *Viuer, ou paßar a vida. ¶* Toxiuo vo-
[c1-l035 note] The Portuguese glosses living or passing one’s life. Toshi wo okuru begins with the printed vo- segment, which continues below.
[c1-l036 >] curu. *Paſſar os annos. ¶* Toxiga aquru.
[c1-l036 note] Curu completes okuru, passing the years. Toshi ga akuru then names the beginning of a new year, explained on the next row.
[c1-l037 >] *Começar o anno.*
[c1-l037 note] Começar o anno completes the example about the year beginning. Its brevity does not make it page furniture or an independent entry.
[c1-l038] Tǒxi. Taitǒno cami. *Papel da China.*
[c1-l038 note] Tōshi is Chinese paper, explained as paper from Taitō. Both visible carons are retained; this is distinct from the preceding unmarked toshi “year.”
[c1-l039] Toxibai. l, toxiubai. *Proporção da idade.*
[c1-l039 note] Toshibai, alternatively toshiubai, describes a person’s stage of life. Both printed forms remain distinct and the Portuguese emphasizes relative age.
[c1-l040 >] Toxibaiga yoi. *Ter ainda boa idade como*
[c1-l040 note] Toshibai ga yoi describes being of a good or suitable age. The numerical illustration of that age continues below.
[c1-l041 >] *de 30. tè 40. & tantos. ¶* Toxibaiga
[c1-l041 note] The example gives an age from thirty to forty and more. Enlargement shows a single grave stroke in tè, not the OCR tilde; Toxibaiga begins the next illustration.
[c1-l042 >] ſuguita. *Paſſouſe ja aboa idade, ou he ja*
[c1-l042 note] Sugita completes toshibai ga sugita, having passed the good age. The Portuguese says that the person is already old; the close aboa spacing is retained.
[c1-l043 >] *velho. ¶* Toxibaiga vacai fito. *Homẽ*
[c1-l043 note] The previous gloss ends with velho. Toshibai ga wakai hito introduces a person still young, whose Portuguese description is continued below.
[c1-l044 >] *ainda mancebo.*
[c1-l044 note] Ainda mancebo completes the young-person gloss. The short continuation is part of the same age example.
[c1-l045] Toxicaſa. *O mais velho, ou de mais idade.*
[c1-l045 note] Toshikasa denotes someone older or senior in age. The long s in the Japanese form is retained as printed.
[c1-l046 >] *¶* Toxicaſano fito. *Idem.*
[c1-l046 note] Toshikasa no hito is the illustrative phrase for an older person, with Idem referring to the preceding definition. The visible pilcrow and capital T restore material lost or misread by OCR.
[c1-l047] Toxicoro. *Idade, ou proporção de idade.*
[c1-l047 note] Toshikoro is an age or stage of life. The scan supports initial T and final Portuguese idade, correcting OCR r and idado.

[c2-l001] Toxicoxi. *O paßar de hum anno à outro, ou*
[c2-l001 note] Toshikoshi is passing from one year to another. The scan supports paßar, hum and anno, correcting the garbled OCR; the following lines explain two calendar occasions.
[c2-l002 >] *ſair do inuerno, & entrar na prima vera, o*
[c2-l002 note] The first description is leaving winter and entering spring. The final o belongs to o que acontece below, rather than the OCR’s additional alternative ou; prima vera remains printed as two words.
[c2-l003 >] *que acontece na derradeira noite do Xiuaſu,*
[c2-l003 note] The event occurs on the last night of Xiuaſu. The month name is Japanese embedded within Portuguese and is therefore a typeface-review term.
[c2-l004 >] *que he a duodecima lũa, & no cabo, ou ter-*
[c2-l004 note] Xiuaſu is identified as the twelfth lunar month. The gloss then turns to the end or limit of winter; termino is divided between ter- and mino.
[c2-l005 >] *mino do inuerno, que às vezes ſe desencontra*
[c2-l005 note] Mino completes termino. The text states that the winter endpoint sometimes does not coincide with the date explained in the next row.
[c2-l006 >] *do derradeiro dia do Xiuaſu: estes dous tem-*
[c2-l006 note] The comparison is with the last day of Xiuaſu. Tem- begins tempos on the next line; the baseline st spelling in estes is retained because the joined italic sorts do not justify overriding the provisional reading.
[c2-l007 >] *pos chamão Rixxun que ſão duas maneiras de*
[c2-l007 note] Pos completes tempos. The text calls these two occasions Rixxun and describes them as two ways of beginning the spring; the explanation is retained as the source states it.
[c2-l008 >] *começar o Faru.*
[c2-l008 note] Faru completes the explanation with the Japanese word for spring. It occurs inside the Portuguese sentence and is marked for optional typeface adjustment.
[c2-l009] Toxicoxi. *Cada anno.*
[c2-l009 note] The gloss is “each year.” NINJAL identifies the corresponding entry as Toxidoxi, but this impression’s damaged fifth letter has the open shape of c and no secure ascender; the visible Toxicoxi is retained as a nonblocking textual caveat.
[c2-l010] Toxifiſaxij. *Couſa de muitos annos.*
[c2-l010 note] Toshihisashii means lasting many years. The scan’s fi sequence and following long s support Toxifiſaxij, correcting the OCR’s malformed Toxihſaxij.
[c2-l011] Toxigoro. *O que ha muitos annos que eſtà em*
[c2-l011 note] Toshigoro describes someone who has been in a place for many years. The Portuguese relative clause continues below; this use differs from the age noun at the preceding column’s end.
[c2-l012 >] *hua caſa, ou lugar, ou o que ha muito que ſer-*
[c2-l012 note] The person has long lived in a house or place, or has long served. The scan reads lugar rather than lagar, and ſer- continues into the next row.
[c2-l013 >] *ue. Vt,* Toxigorono mono.
[c2-l013 note] Ve completes serve. Toshigoro no mono is the Japanese phrase illustrating a long-established resident or long-serving person.
[c2-l014] Toxigoto. *Cada anno.*
[c2-l014 note] Toshigoto means each year. The annual sense is supplied directly by Cada anno and stands as a separate entry.
[c2-l015] Toximairi. *O ir cada anno à varella, ou er-*
[c2-l015 note] Toshimairi denotes making an annual visit to a shrine or hermitage. The Portuguese ermida is divided after er-.
[c2-l016 >] *mida, &c.*
[c2-l016 note] Mida completes ermida, followed by the printed etcetera. This short line completes the annual shrine-visit definition.
[c2-l017] Tôxin. *Pauio, ou torcida: propriamente ſe*
[c2-l017 note] Tōshin is a lamp wick. The scan supports Pauio rather than OCR Panio; torcida gives a second Portuguese word for the wick.
[c2-l018 >] *diz do miolo do junco que ſerue de torcida.*
[c2-l018 note] The term is specifically applied to rush pith used as a wick. This material description confirms the lexical reading of Pauio on the previous row.
[c2-l019] Toxinami. i, Toxinorei. *Proporção, ou a-*
[c2-l019 note] Toshinami is explained by Toxinorei and glossed as the proportion or appearance of age. The synonym’s visible e corrects OCR o, while a- begins aparencia below.
[c2-l020 >] *parencia da idade. Vt,* Fatachi bacarino
[c2-l020 note] Parencia completes aparencia da idade. Hatachi bakari no begins the example of someone appearing about twenty years old.
[c2-l021 >] toxinami. *Aparencia de idade de 20. an-*
[c2-l021 note] Toshinami completes the age-appearance phrase. The Portuguese repeats the age of twenty, with annos divided after an-.
[c2-l022 >] *nos pouco mais, ou menos.*
[c2-l022 note] Nos completes annos; pouco mais, ou menos qualifies the estimate as approximately twenty. It is a continuation of the example rather than a separate sense.
[c2-l023] Tôxite. i, Fayǒ. *Depreßa*
[c2-l023 note] Tōshite is equated with hayō and glossed “quickly.” No final punctuation is supplied after Depreßa where the scan does not establish one.
[c2-l024] Toxitocu. *Cami que os gentios vão viſitar,*
[c2-l024 note] Toshitoku is a kami visited by the people described in the gloss. The initial capital is T, not OCR r; Cami is embedded in Portuguese.
[c2-l025 >] *ou adorar no principio do anno.*
[c2-l025 note] The visit or worship occurs at the beginning of the year. This completes the description of Toshitoku.
[c2-l026] Toxiuoi. *Velho.*
[c2-l026 note] Toshi oi means old or elderly. The Portuguese supplies the direct adjective velho without an additional example.
[c2-l027] Toxiyori, ru, otta. *Enuelhecer.*
[c2-l027 note] Toshiyori, toshiyoru, toshiyotta gives the principal forms of growing old. The printed otta preserves the inflectional pattern rather than a modernized expansion.
[c2-l028] Toxiyori. *Velho.*
[c2-l028 note] Toshiyori as a separate nominal entry denotes an old person. Its distinction from the preceding verb is supported by the independent headword and gloss.
[c2-l029] Toxiyuqi. *Idem.*
[c2-l029 note] Toshiyuki is assigned the same meaning by Idem, referring back to the old person in the previous entry.
[c2-l030] Toxizacari. *Flor da idade. ¶* Toxizaca-
[c2-l030 note] Toshizakari is the prime of life. The repeated example starts with Toxizaca- and continues across the line break.
[c2-l031 >] rini naru. *Ser, ou eſtar na flor da idade.*
[c2-l031 note] Rini naru completes toshizakari ni naru, being or reaching the prime of life. The Portuguese retains its paired ser and estar formulation.
[c2-l032] Toxo. *Lugar onde matão as vacas, ou outros*
[c2-l032 note] Tosho is a place where cattle or other animals are slaughtered. The visible r in outros corrects the OCR omission.
[c2-l033 >] *animaes como carneiros, ouelhas, &c.*
[c2-l033 note] The animals include rams and sheep. Ouelhas is one printed word, not the OCR’s artificial oue lhas division.
[c2-l034] Tǒxo. Cono tocoro. *Este lugar, ou esta po-*
[c2-l034 note] Tōsho means this place, kono tokoro. The Portuguese gives this place or settlement, with povoação divided after po-.
[c2-l035 >] *uoação.*
[c2-l035 note] Uoação completes povoação. The visible ç and continuous word fragment correct the OCR’s spaced uo ação.
[c2-l036] Tǒxocu. *Officio de ſuperior, ou ſuperinten-*
[c2-l036 note] Tōshoku denotes a current superior’s office or superintendency. The Portuguese superintendente is divided at the line break.
[c2-l037 >] *dente que agora corre, ou deſte tempo.*
[c2-l037 note] Dente completes superintendente. The temporal qualification explains that it is the office currently held or belonging to this time.
[c2-l038] Tǒxǔ. *Os deſta ſeita, ou religião.*
[c2-l038 note] Tōshū refers to those of this sect or religion. The two carons are retained independently, including the mark over u.
[c2-l039] Tǒxun. Ataru faru. *Eſta primauera, ou*
[c2-l039 note] Tōshun is this spring, explained by ataru haru. The printed f of faru is partly abraded but the word and gloss agree; the second seasonal gloss follows below.
[c2-l040 >] *anno nouo.*
[c2-l040 note] Anno nouo adds “new year” to the spring definition. This is a short continuation of Tǒxun.
[c2-l041] Tôxut xeibot. *O ſair do Oriente, & porſe no*
[c2-l041 note] Tōshut seibot describes rising in the east and setting in the west. The scan supports porſe rather than OCR perſe; the final t spellings are retained.
[c2-l042 >] *Occidente. S.*
[c2-l042 note] Occidente completes the westward setting phrase. The final S. is the upright written-language label and is separated from the italic Portuguese.
[c2-l043] Toxut. Faqi idaſu. *Vomitar. S.*
[c2-l043 note] Toshut is vomiting, explained by haki idasu. The final upright S. marks written usage and is not part of the italic gloss.
[c2-l044] Toxxut. *O botar fora com força, ou ẽpuxãdo.*
[c2-l044 note] Tosshut means throwing or thrusting something out forcefully. The abbreviated ẽpuxãdo is preserved without expansion.
[c2-l045] Toya. *Caſa onde guardão aues, ou paßaros.*
[c2-l045 note] Toya is a place for keeping birds. The broken initial is capital T, and the Portuguese aues and paßaros provide the enclosure’s occupants.
[c2-l046 >] *¶ Item, Capoeira de gallinhas.*
[c2-l046 note] The additional sense is a chicken coop. The printed pilcrow introduces a second gloss within the same Toya entry.
[c2-l047] Tôya. Fuyunoyo. *Noite de inuerno.*
[c2-l047 note] Tōya denotes a winter night, fuyu no yo. Its circumflex is distinct from the unmarked bird-house headword immediately above.
