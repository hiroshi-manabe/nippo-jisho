[c1-l001] Quaran. Vazauai midare. *Males, & trabalhos*
[c1-l001 note] Quaran is calamity and disorder, explained by Vazauai midare. The scan supports u in Vazauai, not OCR n, and the preceding page's catchword anticipates this entry.
[c1-l002 >] *com perturbação, & reuoltas.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the definition with disturbance and upheavals accompanying the troubles. The Portuguese reuoltas retains the printed u/v spelling.
[c1-l003] Quararito. *Adu Modo de abrir aporta de*
[c1-l003 note] Quararito describes opening or spreading something wide. Adu has no secure following stop in the scan; the Portuguese clause continues below.
[c1-l004 >] *par em par, ou de estender totalmente algũa*
[c1-l004 note] Opening a door wide and spreading something completely are parallel illustrations of the adverb. The printed joined aporta above and algũa here remain.
[c1-l005 >] *couſa, &c.*
[c1-l005 note] Couſa, &c. closes the spreading-something definition. The next physical row starts a distinct Quarei entry that the provisional OCR omitted.
[c1-l005a] Quarei. i, Qeccôni bibixiqi coto. *Couſa*
[c1-l005a note] The omitted Quarei entry is restored from the scan. Qeccôni bibixiqi coto explains splendour or fine appearance; Couſa begins the Portuguese gloss completed below.
[c1-l006 >] *mui fermoſa, luſtroſa, & grande.*
[c1-l006 note] Describes the thing as very beautiful, splendid, and grand. This continuation belongs to the restored Quarei row, not to Quararito.
[c1-l007] Quareina. *Idem. Vt,* Quareina detachi.
[c1-l007 note] Quareina is the adjectival form with the same sense. Quareina detachi illustrates a splendid appearance or outfit; italic Vt, replaces the OCR's false paragraph sign.
[c1-l008 >] *Vestido, ou atauio limpo, & luſtroſo. ¶* Qua-
[c1-l008 note] The example means clean, splendid clothing or attire. A genuine paragraph sign introduces another example beginning with the divided Qua-.
[c1-l009 >] reina furumai. *Conuite eſplendido.*
[c1-l009 note] Reina completes Quareina; furumai is a feast or entertainment, translated as a splendid banquet. It is a second example of the same adjective.
[c1-l010 >] Quareini.
[c1-l010 note] Quareini is listed separately as the adverbial form of the splendour adjective. No Portuguese words are printed on this indented row.
[c1-l011 >] Quareiſa.
[c1-l011 note] Quareiſa is the corresponding quality noun, with visible long ſ. Like Quareini, it is listed without a fresh Portuguese definition.
[c1-l012] Quareô. i, Togano quatai. *Pena que ſe im-*
[c1-l012 note] Quareô is a penalty for an offence, explicitly Togano quatai. The eô sequence is retained; im- begins the divided Portuguese verb.
[c1-l013 >] *poem por algũa culpa, ou delicto. Vt,* Qua-
[c1-l013 note] Poem completes impoem, a penalty imposed for a fault or offence. The example begins Qua-, the first part of Quareôuo.
[c1-l014 >] reôuo daſu. *Pagar atal pena.*
[c1-l014 note] Reôuo daſu completes the example of paying a penalty. Pagar atal pena retains the printed joining of a and tal.
[c1-l015] Quari. Fanano vchi. *Dentro da roſa, ou*
[c1-l015 note] Quari, explained by Fanano vchi, means inside a flower. The Portuguese offers rose or flower, with the final alternative below.
[c1-l016 >] *flor. S.*
[c1-l016 note] Flor completes the alternative to rose; the vowel is o, not OCR e. The upright S. labels written usage. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l017] Quari. Fino vchi. *Dentro no fogo. S.*
[c1-l017 note] This second Quari means inside the fire, explicitly Fino vchi. Its written-register S. is upright, and the entry is distinct from the flower sense. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l018] Quarin. *Hum certo pao da China hum pouco*
[c1-l018 note] Quarin names a Chinese wood described as somewhat red. The gloss does not by itself settle a modern botanical identification.
[c1-l019 >] *vermelho.*
[c1-l019 note] Vermelho completes the colour qualification of the Chinese wood. The short continuation is part of Quarin, not the next sound-symbolic entry.
[c1-l020] Quariquarito. *Adu. Modo de ſoar a cou-*
[c1-l020 note] Quariquarito describes the sound of chewing something hard. The example substance and remaining Portuguese clause follow on the next row.
[c1-l021 >] *ſa dura como caramelo quando ſe maſtiga.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes the chewing-sound comparison with something hard, called caramelo. The historical word is retained without imposing a modern confectionery interpretation.
[c1-l022] Quariǔ. Fana, yanagui. *Flor, & ſal-*
[c1-l022 note] Quariǔ is explained by Fana and yanagui, flower and willow. The caron belongs to the final u; the Portuguese salgueiro is divided after ſal-.
[c1-l023 >] *gueiro. S.*
[c1-l023 note] Gueiro completes salgueiro, willow. The upright S. marks this flower-and-willow expression as written usage. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l024] Quaſai. *Males cauſados do fogo, ou queima.*
[c1-l024 note] Quaſai denotes harm caused by fire or burning. The gloss specifies a fire disaster rather than the mere presence of fire.
[c1-l025] Quaſan. Fanano yama. *Monte de muitas flo*
[c1-l025 note] Quaſan, Fanano yama, is a mountain of flowers. The Portuguese flores is divided after flo without an added hyphen.
[c1-l026 >] *res, ou onde eſtà florecido o mato.*
[c1-l026 note] Res completes flores; the alternative describes a mountain where the vegetation is in blossom. The older eſtà and florecido remain.
[c1-l027] Quasǒ. *O queimar o corpo do defunto dando*
[c1-l027 note] Quasǒ denotes burning a deceased person's body as a form of burial. The printed short s and final caron are retained.
[c1-l028 >] *lhe desta maneira ſepultura, ou fazendolhe o*
[c1-l028 note] The explanation treats cremation as giving burial or performing the funeral. Fazendolhe remains joined as printed.
[c1-l029 >] *enterramento. ¶* Quasǒ ſuru. *Queimar*
[c1-l029 note] Enterramento completes the burial explanation. Quasǒ ſuru is the example for cremating; the extra i in OCR Qiuasǒ is absent from the scan.
[c1-l030 >] *corpo morto deſta maneira.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes Queimar with a dead body treated in this way. It closes the cremation example before the house-of-fire entry.
[c1-l031] Quatacu. Fino iye. *Caſa de fogo. i, Este*
[c1-l031 note] Quatacu, Fino iye, is literally a house of fire. The Portuguese explicitly identifies the metaphor with this world on the next row.
[c1-l032 >] *mundo.*
[c1-l032 note] Mundo completes Este mundo, the gloss for the metaphorical burning house. No additional religious explanation is inserted into the canonical text.
[c1-l033] Quatai. *Caſtigo, ou pena que ſe dà por algum*
[c1-l033 note] Quatai denotes a punishment or penalty imposed for an offence. The definition prepares the example of imposing such a penalty.
[c1-l034 >] *delicto. Vt,* Quataiuo caquru. *Impor*
[c1-l034 note] Quataiuo caquru means imposing a penalty. The visible final Portuguese word is Impor, not OCR Impos; its object follows below.
[c1-l035 >] *pena por algum crime. ¶ Item, Pecado,*

[c1-l036 >] *ou falta.*
[c1-l036 note] Ou falta closes the second sense, a fault. It is an indented continuation, not a new headword.
[c1-l037] Quatdat. *Grãdeza, ou magnificencia, &*
[c1-l037 note] Quatdat denotes largeness, magnificence, or generosity. The abbreviated Grãdeza keeps its printed tilde; the list continues with liberalidade.
[c1-l038 >] *liberalidade. ¶* Quatdatni fodocoſu.
[c1-l038 note] Liberalidade completes the generosity sense. Quatdatni fodocoſu illustrates bestowing something in a generous manner.
[c1-l039 >] *Dar liberal, & magnificamente. ¶* Chi-
[c1-l039 note] Dar liberal, & magnificamente translates the giving example. Chi- begins a new example about understanding or knowledge, not a Portuguese word.
[c1-l040 >] ye quatdat nari. *Tem grande, & claro*
[c1-l040 note] Ye completes Chiye, understanding. Quatdat nari describes it as broad and clear; the entire Portuguese Tem is italic, correcting the OCR's isolated roman T.
[c1-l041 >] *ſaber. ¶* Quatdatna chiye. *Saber cla-*
[c1-l041 note] Saber completes the preceding description of broad, clear understanding. Quatdatna chiye supplies the adjectival version of the same idea; claro is divided after cla-.
[c1-l042 >] *ro, & grande, & que ſe estende a todo genero*
[c1-l042 note] Ro completes claro. The explanation adds that this extensive understanding reaches every kind of thing, with the object completed below.
[c1-l043 >] *de couſa.*
[c1-l043 note] De couſa closes the universal-scope explanation of understanding. This short row finishes Quatdat before the next headword.
[c1-l044] Quatei. Fanano ſoco. *Meo, ou dẽtro da roſa.*
[c1-l044 note] Quatei, Fanano ſoco, denotes the middle or interior of a flower. Meo and dẽtro preserve the Portuguese forms actually printed.
[c1-l045] Quatei.. Fino ſoco. i, Fino vchi. *Dentro*

[c1-l046 >] *do fogo.*
[c1-l046 note] Do fogo completes Dentro do fogo. It is the final body continuation in the first column, above the rule.

[c2-l001] Quatet. Fino curogane. *Ferro abraſado.*
[c2-l001 note] Quatet is heated or incandescent iron, explained by Fino curogane. Abraſado is the printed Portuguese form; no modern spelling is substituted.

[c2-l002 note] Quatmiacu is identified as a particular pulse. The short gloss does not specify its diagnostic characteristics, so none are supplied in the transcription.
[c2-l003] Quatnin. Yomigayeru fito. *Homem reſuſ-*
[c2-l003 note] Quatnin is a person returned to life, explicitly Yomigayeru fito. The Portuguese reſuſcitado is divided after reſuſ-.
[c2-l004 >] *citado.*
[c2-l004 note] Citado completes reſuſcitado, resurrected. This short continuation belongs to the person entry, not the following expression of thanks.
[c2-l005] Quatǒ. i, Quabunna coto. *Palaura de a-*
[c2-l005 note] Quatǒ is an expression of gratitude or appreciation, equated with Quabunna coto. The headword includes t, omitted by OCR, and the Portuguese agradecimento begins after de.
[c2-l006 >] *gradecimento como tenhoo ẽ muito. ¶* Go-
[c2-l006 note] Gradecimento completes agradecimento. The explanatory formula expresses holding something in high esteem; Go- begins the Japanese letter-of-thanks example.
[c2-l007 >] jǒ quatǒno itari. *Muitas graças pella car-*
[c2-l007 note] Jǒ completes Gojǒ, the other person's letter. Quatǒno itari expresses extreme appreciation, translated with many thanks for the letter.
[c2-l008 >] *ta que me mandaſtes. S.*
[c2-l008 note] Ta completes carta, the letter sent by the addressee. The upright S. marks the written-language formula. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l009] Quatô. *Hũa maneira de touca, ou veo que ſe*
[c2-l009 note] Quatô, with circumflex rather than the preceding caron, denotes a cap or veil. The artificial OCR space after Q is removed.
[c2-l010 >] *amarra na cabeça por de baixo da barba.*
[c2-l010 note] The head covering is tied beneath the beard. De baixo remains two printed words rather than modernized debaixo.
[c2-l011] Quatocu. *Retribuição do outro mũdo.* Bup.
[c2-l011 note] Quatocu is recompense in the other world. The upright Bup. identifies a Buddhist term; its stop belongs to the same roman label.
[c2-l012] Quatto. *Adu. Modo de dar largamente,*
[c2-l012 note] Quatto describes giving generously or opening something wide. The initial sense is followed by a spreading-out sense on the next row.
[c2-l013 >] *ou de abrir, & eſtender algũa couſa totalmẽte.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes the adverb's opening and spreading sense: something is extended completely. Algũa and totalmẽte retain their abbreviation tildes.
[c2-l014] Quauǒ. Vazauai vazauai. *Males, & tra-*
[c2-l014 note] Quauǒ is explained by the repeated Vazauai, calamities or troubles. The Portuguese trabalhos is divided after tra-.
[c2-l015 >] *balhos.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes tra-/balhos in the preceding definition: the first Quauǒ entry concerns calamities and afflictions, not the flower named in the next entry.
[c2-l016] Quauǒ. i, Botan. *Hũa certa roſa grande,*
[c2-l016 note] A second Quauǒ is equated with Botan and described as a certain large rose-like flower; the separate entry and caron distinguish the printed form without conflating its sense with calamity.
[c2-l017 >] *& fermoſa.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes the flower description with “and beautiful”; the very short continuation belongs to the Botan entry.
[c2-l018] Quauocu. Fanano iye. *Caſa edificada onde*
[c2-l018 note] Fanano iye explains Quauocu as a house associated with flowers; the Portuguese begins a description of a house built in a particular setting.
[c2-l019 >] *ha muitas fulas, & roſas. S.*
[c2-l019 note] Completes the setting with many fulas and roses. The scan reads fulas, an unexpected form in this floral context; it is retained rather than silently replaced by flores. The terminal S. is upright. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l020] Quaxa. Fino curuma. *Carro de fogo. S.*
[c2-l020 note] Fino curuma corresponds directly to a chariot of fire. This is a separate Quaxa homograph; the terminal S. is upright. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l021] Quaxa. *Homem que ſe trata bem, & luzida-*
[c2-l021 note] The next Quaxa describes a man who maintains an elegant appearance; the adverb luzida-/mente continues onto the following line.
[c2-l022 >] *mente no veſtido, &c.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes the description of elegance in clothing and related matters, rather than the preceding fire-chariot sense.
[c2-l023] Quaxa. Fanaguruma. *Carro ricamente or-*

[c2-l024 >] *nado. S.*

[c2-l025] Quaxacu. Xacuuo caquru. *Agaſalharſe em*
[c2-l025 note] Xacuuo caquru explains Quaxacu through a lodging practice. The Portuguese begins the account of a monk taking accommodation; the following line supplies the subject Bonzo.
[c2-l026 >] *algũa caſa* Bonzo, *pondo dentro algum liuro,*

[c2-l027 >] *ou outra peça em ſinal q̃ toma aquella pouſada.*
[c2-l027 note] Completes the lodging practice: a book or another object signals that the monk is taking that lodging. The printed q̃ abbreviation is preserved.
[c2-l028] Quaxi. Fanano yeda. *Ramo de roſas, ou*
[c2-l028 note] Fanano yeda explains this Quaxi as a branch bearing roses or flowers; it is distinct from the fruit and verbal entries below.
[c2-l029 >] *flores.*
[c2-l029 note] Supplies flores as the alternative to roses, completing the preceding branch definition.
[c2-l030] Quaxi. *Fruita: propriamente ſe diz da frui-*
[c2-l030 note] The second Quaxi means fruit, especially fruit served at the end of a meal. Its final frui- continues on the next physical line.
[c2-l031 >] *ta de ſobre meſa.*
[c2-l031 note] Completes frui-/ta and specifies de ſobre meſa, dessert fruit; the historical spacing is retained.
[c2-l032] Quaxi, ſuru, ita. *Abrandar. ¶* Fito-

[c2-l034 >] *ção dalguem. S.*

[c2-l035] Quaxin. Fino fari. *Ponteiro de ferro como a-*
[c2-l035 note] Fino fari explains Quaxin as a fire-heated needle or iron point; the Portuguese agulha is divided a-/gulha.
[c2-l036 >] *gulha abraſado com fogo. Vt,* Quaxinuo

[c2-l037 >] tatçuru. *Meter eſte ferro abraſado no corpo.*

[c2-l038] Quaxit. *Falta, ou erro.*

[c2-l043] Quaxǒ. Fino xǒ. *Natureza do fogo.*

[c2-l047 note] Completes the description as dressing cleanly and splendidly; the upright S. closes the entry above the separate signature and catchword. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
