[c1-l001 >>] Feǒxi, l, fiǒxi qiqi. *Homem compaſſado,*
[c1-l001 note] Feǒxi / fiǒxi qiqi is concerned with hearing or keeping rhythm. The Portuguese begins by calling the person well measured, rather than defining a particular instrument.
[c1-l002 >] *& entoado no tanger, bailar, &c.*
[c1-l002 note] The coordinated verbs tanger and bailar specify instrumental performance and dancing. Entoado adds being in tune or suitably coordinated; it continues the preceding person's description.
[c1-l003] Feôzǒ, l, fiôzǒ. *Figura, ou moſtra de cou-*
[c1-l003 note] Feôzǒ / fiôzǒ denotes a representation or image. Figura and moſtra describe something that makes absent events present to view, rather than necessarily a physical statue.
[c1-l004 >] *ſas paſſadas, ou de couſas que aõ de vir.*
[c1-l004 note] Both past things and things yet to come can be represented. The printed aõ de vir is the future construction; no modern spelling or accent normalization is needed.
[c1-l005] Feqi. Cabe. *Parede. Vt,* Feqiuo fedate-
[c1-l005 note] Feqi is heki, here glossed cabe, 'wall'. Feqiuo fedatete begins a Japanese example of separation by a wall; Parede and Vt belong to the Portuguese explanation, not the Japanese phrase.
[c1-l006 >] te ita. *Eſtaua diuidido com parede no meo.*
[c1-l006 note] Fedatete ita means being separated, with the wall intervening. Portuguese no meo makes the intervening position explicit; the initial te completes the preceding physical line.
[c1-l007] Feqi. *Meliùs,* Qenbeqi. *Doença das eſpadoas.*
[c1-l007 note] This second Feqi is an ailment of the shoulders, not 'wall'. Meliùs recommends Qenbeqi as the preferred term; that distinction makes the OCR's Melnits / Cenbeqi suspect and the scan supports their correction.
[c1-l008 >] *¶* Feqiuo vtçu. *Dar pancadas naquella*
[c1-l008 note] Feqiuo vtçu describes striking or patting the affected shoulder area. The Portuguese continues into the next line, so pancadas should not be read as a separate dictionary sense.
[c1-l009 >] *parte das eſpadoas que doe, ou està leſa.*
[c1-l009 note] The target is the part of the shoulders that hurts or is injured. This confirms the bodily sense of Feqi rather than the homographic wall entry above.
[c1-l010] Feqigan. *Hum liuro da China de certas medi-*
[c1-l010 note] Feqigan is identified as a Chinese book of meditations. The wording supports treating it as a title; identifying a particular modern edition would go beyond what this line establishes.
[c1-l011 >] *taçoẽs.*
[c1-l011 note] Taçoẽs completes meditaçoẽs from the preceding line. It is a short continuation, not an independent entry or page furniture.
[c1-l012] Feqiguiocu. Tama, tama. *Pe dras precioſas.*
[c1-l012 note] Feqiguiocu corresponds to heki-gyoku, with tama, tama supplying a Japanese gloss for precious stones. The repeated tama is printed and is not removed as an accidental duplication.
[c1-l013] Feqira. Midorino tçuta. *Certa era verde,*
[c1-l013 note] Feqira is glossed midorino tçuta, green ivy. Portuguese era here names ivy; it is not the unrelated verb form 'was'.
[c1-l014 >] *& viçoſa.*
[c1-l014 note] Viçoſa adds luxuriant or vigorous growth to the ivy description. This brief continuation belongs with the previous line's botanical sense.
[c1-l015] Feqiracu. *Ceo, ou ar. S.*
[c1-l015 note] Feqiracu refers to the sky or air. The scan supports restoring the missing r in the headword and reading ar, rather than the OCR's meaningless dr.
[c1-l016] Feqirano ten. *Ceo claro, & fermoſo. S.*
[c1-l016 note] Feqirano ten is another expression for a clear, beautiful sky. Ten is the Sino-Japanese sky/heaven element, while the Portuguese supplies its descriptive qualities.
[c1-l017] Feqireqi. Canàximi. *Tristeza. Vt,* Fe-
[c1-l017 note] The headword is indexed as Feqireqi, while Canàximi plainly supplies kanashimi, sorrow. The example wraps after Fe-; its continuation needs special care because its spelling is not a familiar modern phrase.
[c1-l018 >] qireqi xǔxǒ ſu. *Ter grande dòr, & tri-*
[c1-l018 note] Feqireqi xǔxǒ ſu describes great pain and grieving. The continuation is qireqi, agreeing with the headword, and the enlarged u carries a caron; the OCR had lost r and misread the marked vowel as i. The Japanese interpretation remains tied to the dictionary's kanashimi gloss.
[c1-l019 >] *ſteza. S.*
[c1-l019 note] ſteza completes triſteza. Together with dòr, this confirms that the preceding example concerns grieving rather than the thunder sense that a similar-looking word might suggest.
[c1-l020] Feqiri. i, Feriqiri. *Repartimento. ¶* Fe-
[c1-l020 note] Feqiri and Feriqiri are equated as names for a partition or division. The next example begins after the paragraph sign and continues across the line break.
[c1-l021 >] qiriuo ſuru. *Fazer este repartimento na*
[c1-l021 note] Feqiriuo ſuru means making such a partition. The qiri continuation fits both the preceding headword and the scan, unlike the OCR's quri.
[c1-l022 >] *caſa, ou diuiſão.*
[c1-l022 note] The partition is made within a house. Diuiſão is an alternative Portuguese explanation of repartimento, not another Japanese word.
[c1-l023] Feqiri, u, itta. i, Feriqiriuo ſuru.
[c1-l023 note] The verb paradigm Feqiri, u, itta is equated with Feriqiriuo ſuru. The shared gloss explains why the noun and verbal entry appear together.
[c1-l024] Feqiriù banjin. *Couſa mui alta como rocha,*
[c1-l024 note] Feqiriù banjin evokes a sheer wall or cliff of immense height, conventionally 'wall-standing ten thousand fathoms'. NINJAL supports this headword identity; the scan, not that normalized index, governs its printed accent.
[c1-l025 >] *ou monteingreme.*
[c1-l025 note] Monte ingreme completes the comparison with a steep mountain. This is still the explanation of Feqiriù banjin, not the beginning of the next botanical entry.
[c1-l026] Feqiſǒ. Cabeno cuſa. *Eruaſinha que nace na*
[c1-l026 note] This Feqiſǒ is cabeno cuſa, grass growing on a wall. The wall element distinguishes it from the homophonous green-grass entry below.
[c1-l027 >] *parede de barro que logo ſe ſeca. Tomaſe por*
[c1-l027 note] The grass grows on an earthen wall and soon dries. Tomaſe por introduces a figurative application rather than another botanical characteristic.
[c1-l028 >] *couſa breue, & de pouca dura. S.*
[c1-l028 note] The figurative sense is something short-lived or of little duration. Dura here functions as duration or lastingness, not simply the modern adjective 'hard'.
[c1-l029] Feqisǒ. Midorino cuſa. *Eruas freſcas, &*
[c1-l029 note] The second Feqisǒ is midorino cuſa, fresh green grass. The initial is F, not the OCR's r; the printed short s is retained independently of the preceding headword's ſ.
[c1-l030 >] *verdes. S.*
[c1-l030 note] Verdes completes the description of fresh, green plants. It belongs to the second Feqisǒ entry, not the earlier grass that quickly dries.
[c1-l031] Feqitai. Cabeno coqe. *Muſgos da pare-*
[c1-l031 note] Feqitai is cabeno coqe, moss on a wall. Coqe reads koke, and the Portuguese Muſgos gives the same identification.
[c1-l032 >] *de. S.*
[c1-l032 note] De completes parede. This short line is essential to the preceding definition even though most of its width is blank.
[c1-l033] Feqitan. Midorino fuchi. *Pègo fundo que*
[c1-l033 note] Feqitan is midorino fuchi, a green-looking deep pool. Portuguese pègo names the deep water, not the unrelated verb 'I catch'.
[c1-l034 >] *parece verde por ſer muito fundo. S.*
[c1-l034 note] The pool looks green because it is very deep. That causal explanation belongs to the dictionary and should not be replaced with a modern physical explanation of water colour.
[c1-l035] Feqiten. *Ceo. Vt,* Feqitenno xeixucu.
[c1-l035 note] Feqiten denotes the sky; Feqitenno xeixucu means the stars or constellations of that sky. Ceo and Vt are Portuguese portions within the otherwise Japanese line.
[c1-l036 >] *Eſtrelas do ceo, &c. S.*
[c1-l036 note] Eſtrelas do ceo confirms the astronomical reading of xeixucu. The final register label is separate from the Japanese expression.
[c1-l037] Feqitǒ. Midorino momo. i, Xiroi momo.
[c1-l037 note] Feqitǒ is glossed midorino momo and then xiroi momo. The next Portuguese line specifies white blossoms, so xiroi describes the tree's floral variety rather than necessarily its fruit colour.
[c1-l038 >] *Peſegueiro de flores brancas. S.*
[c1-l038 note] The definition explicitly says a peach tree with white flowers. Peſegueiro is kept in its printed historical spelling.
[c1-l039] Feqixo. *Proibição eſcrita em papel pegado*
[c1-l039 note] Feqixo is a written prohibition posted on paper. The scan supports Proibição, not the OCR's extra b; its association with a wall is completed on the next line.
[c1-l040 >] *na parede, ou dependurado.*
[c1-l040 note] The notice can be pasted on the wall or hung up. These are alternative ways of displaying the same written prohibition.
[c1-l041] Feqixǒ. Cabeno vye. *Na ſuperficie da pa-*
[c1-l041 note] Feqixǒ is cabeno vye, the surface of a wall. This differs from both the posted notice above and the green pine below, despite their similar romanization.
[c1-l042 >] *rede. ¶* Cuchi feqixǒni caqu. *Não*
[c1-l042 note] Cuchi feqixǒni caqu is followed by Não, continuing a negative explanation. Reading the initial as N matters semantically: the dictionary says the matter cannot be expressed.
[c1-l043 >] *ſe pode dizer nem explicar. S.*
[c1-l043 note] The phrase is explained as something one cannot say or explain. I preserve the dictionary's idiomatic interpretation rather than forcing a literal reading of writing a mouth on a wall.
[c1-l044] Feqixô. Midorino matçu. *Pinheiro verde,*
[c1-l044 note] Feqixô is midorino matçu, a green pine. This is the pine sense rather than Feqixǒ, 'on the wall'; the scan's initial F and medial qi also repair substantial OCR corruption.
[c1-l045 >] *& freſco. S.*
[c1-l045 note] Freſco supplements verde in the pine description. The short continuation remains attached to the botanical entry.
[c1-l046] Fera. *Cana com que amaſſão, & affeiçoão gru-*
[c1-l046 note] Fera is hera, here a cane implement used to mix and shape paste. The Portuguese gru- continues as grude on the following line.
[c1-l047 >] *de. ¶* Ferauo tçucǒ. *Buſcar algũa*
[c1-l047 note] De completes grude, so the OCR's qe is untenable. Ferauo tçucǒ then begins an idiom about finding an excuse, continued in column 2. The worn c is read with the Japanese tsukau construction; this is a context-assisted reading, not a claim that the damaged letter is unambiguous.

[c2-l003 note] Ferauo faru is explained as committing adultery. This is a distinct idiom from the preceding excuse for leaving work; the final X is a usage label, not Japanese text.
[c2-l004] Feraxi, u, aita. *Diminuir, ou ir gaſtando.*

[c2-l006 >] touo feraſu. *Deſfazer em outro. Ajuntaſe*
[c2-l006 note] Fitouo feraſu is explained as disparaging another person. Ajuntaſe introduces the verb's productive use after other verbal stems.
[c2-l007 >] *às raizes de alguns verbos. Vt,* Monouo
[c2-l007 note] The Portuguese explicitly refers to roots of verbs; verhos was an OCR error for verbos. Monouo begins an example of this compound construction.
[c2-l008 >] cui feraſu. *Ir gaſtando o comer. ¶* Ta-
[c2-l008 note] Monouo cui feraſu means using up food by eating it. The next compound begins with Ta-, completed as tacara, riches, on the next line.
[c2-l009 >] carauo tçucai feraſu. *Gaſtar as riquezas.*
[c2-l009 note] Tacarauo tçucai feraſu means spending down wealth. The Portuguese must be Gaſtar, 'spend', and the scan supports that reading rather than Guſtar.
[c2-l010 >] *¶* Cuſuriuo xenji feraſu. *Cozendo me-*
[c2-l010 note] Cuſuriuo xenji feraſu describes reducing medicine by boiling. Cozendo introduces the process; me- continues as mezinhas, remedies.
[c2-l011 >] *zinhas fazer minguar a agua.*
[c2-l011 note] The water is made to diminish as the medicines boil. This completes the decoction example, not a general instruction for preparing every medicine.
[c2-l012] Feri. *Debrum, ou bainha do veſtido. ¶* Fe-
[c2-l012 note] Feri is heri, a border or hem on clothing. The scan's F and the hemming example below distinguish it from the OCR's Peri.
[c2-l013 >] riuo toru. *Botar debrum, ou a buainhar al-*
[c2-l013 note] Feriuo toru means adding a border or hemming something. Riuo completes the preceding Fe-; the OCR's ritio does not fit either the printed strokes or the Japanese object phrase.
[c2-l014 >] *gum veſtido, barrete, &c.* Tatamino feri-
[c2-l014 note] Clothing and a cap are examples of things that can be hemmed. Tatamino feri- introduces the border of a tatami mat.
[c2-l015 >] uo tçuquru. *Botar debrum no Tatami.*
[c2-l015 note] Tatamino feriuo tçuquru means making or fitting the mat border. Tatami inside the Portuguese explanation is an embedded Japanese noun whose typeface can be reviewed independently.
[c2-l016] Feri, u, etta. *Ir ſe diminuindo. Vt,* Miz-
[c2-l016 note] This Feri is the intransitive verb heru, to decrease. Etta is its contracted past form; it should not be confused with the earlier noun heri, 'border'.
[c2-l017 >] zuga fetta. *Diminuioſe, ou mingou a agua.*
[c2-l017 note] Mizzuga fetta means that the water decreased. The apparent i in zugaifetta was a segmentation error; the scan reads the particle ga followed by fetta.
[c2-l018] Fericudari. *Humildade.*
[c2-l018 note] Fericudari is the noun for humility, corresponding to herikudari. The separate verb immediately below confirms the noun/verb distinction.
[c2-l019] Fericudari, u, atta. *Humilharſe.*
[c2-l019 note] Fericudari, u, atta supplies the verbal paradigm for humbling oneself. The Portuguese reflexive ending in Humilharſe expresses the same relationship.
[c2-l020] Feriſaxi. *Meliùs,* vſuberi. *Certa laya de eſtei-*
[c2-l020 note] Feriſaxi is referred to the preferred form vſuberi. The definition begins a kind of mat and continues with its lining on the next line.
[c2-l021 >] *ras com forro.*

[c2-l023] Feſaqi. *Proa, ou eſporão do nauio.*
[c2-l023 note] Feſaqi is hesaki, a ship's prow or projecting bow. This is an ordinary Japanese lexical s, irrespective of whether the source uses a long or short glyph.
[c2-l024] Feſo. *Imbigo. ¶* Cama, l, quanſuno
[c2-l024 note] Feſo is heso, the navel. The example extends that shape metaphor to an iron vessel's projecting part; quanſu is a vessel name rather than a Portuguese word.
[c2-l025 >] feſo. *Remate que tem as panellas de ferro*
[c2-l025 note] The vessel's feſo is a finishing projection on iron pots. The location, at the bottom, is supplied by the next physical line.
[c2-l026 >] *no fundo. ¶* Iacǒno feſo, l, foſo. *Pa-*
[c2-l026 note] The entry then gives Iacǒno feſo / foſo, the musk pouch. The alternate vowel is explicitly recorded and should not be normalized to only heso.
[c2-l027 >] *po dalmiſcre.*
[c2-l027 note] Papo dalmiſcre completes the musk-pouch definition. The historical almiſcre spelling is supported by the scan; it is not a reason to modernize the text to almíscar.
[c2-l028] Feſono vo, l, foſono vo. *Tripa que fica no*
[c2-l028 note] Feſono vo / foſono vo denotes the umbilical cord. The printed long s, not t, makes the connection with the preceding navel entry explicit.
[c2-l029 >] *imbigo das crianças. ¶* Feſono vouo qi-
[c2-l029 note] The Portuguese locates the cord at the child's navel. Feſono vouo qi- begins a Japanese example with vo as the noun 'cord' and uo as its following object particle.
[c2-l030 >] ru, l, tçugu. *Cortar eſta tripa.*
[c2-l030 note] Qiru and tçugu are given as alternatives for cutting the cord here. The intervening l is an alternative marker, not part of a word lotçugu.
[c2-l031] Feſubi. *Fulugem que eſtà pegada na panella de*

[c2-l033] Feta. *Borda do mar, ou de outra couſa. ¶* Fe-
[c2-l033 note] Feta here means an edge or border, including the seashore. Its nautical example continues after Fe- on the next line.
[c2-l034 >] tauo noru. *Nauegar ao longo da terra.*
[c2-l034 note] Fetauo noru means sailing along the land or coast. The Portuguese explains a coastal course, not simply boarding a boat.

[c2-l037 >] tauo toru. *Tirar eſta folha, ou caſca dos*

[c2-l038 >] *figos.*

[c2-l039] Feta. *Deſengenhoſo, ou chambão.*
[c2-l039 note] This Feta is heta, unskilled or clumsy. It is a different lexical sense from edge and calyx, and the Portuguese Deſengenhoſo / chambão makes that contrast clear.
[c2-l040] Fetafetato, l, fetato. *Adu. Modo de ca-*
[c2-l040 note] Fetafetato / fetato is an adverbial expression for a broad, flat fall or impact. The repeated feta is meaningful; the OCR had misread both the initial F and the internal f.
[c2-l041 >] *ir, ou daralgũa couſa larga de chãpa. Vt,*
[c2-l041 note] Ir completes cair from the preceding ca-. The Portuguese describes a broad object striking flat; reading ar would create a spurious word across the physical break.

[c2-l043 >] *pancada deſta maneira.*
[c2-l043 note] Pancada deſta maneira completes the flat-impact explanation. It is the Portuguese continuation of the adverbial examples, not an additional Japanese entry.
[c2-l044] Fetano caua. *Peſſoa pouco deſtra, ou desma*
[c2-l044 note] Fetano caua describes someone inept or careless. This is the unskilled heta sense, with a further expression involving caua; a precise modern equivalent is less certain than the dictionary's own gloss.
[c2-l045 >] *zelada.*
[c2-l045 note] Zelada completes desmazelada, careless or slovenly. The physical split is preserved rather than merging the two printed lines.
[c2-l046] Fetçucuxi, ſu. *Eſtender o fio todo. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l046 note] Fetçucuxi combines passing or extending with tçucuxi, doing completely. Its first gloss concerns extending all the thread, while Itẽ introduces a second use.

[c2-l047 note] The second use is finishing the experience or endurance of hardships. The visible traba. continues across the page in trabalhos; I preserve the printed terminal mark rather than inventing a hyphen.
