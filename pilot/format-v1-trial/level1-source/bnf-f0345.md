[c1-l001] Naburi, u, utta. *Zombar, ou motejar.*
[c1-l001 note] Naburi means mocking or making fun of someone, matching both Portuguese verbs. The initial is a damaged capital N, not OCR x; the printed abbreviated principal parts u, utta are retained.
[c1-l002] Naburimono. *Peßoa de que ſe zomba, ou mo*
[c1-l002 note] Naburimono is a person made an object of ridicule. The final mo belongs to moteja on the following row; no terminal hyphen is visible here.
[c1-l003 >] *teja. ¶* Naburimononi naru. *Ser zõ-*
[c1-l003 note] Teja completes moteja. Naburimono ni naru means becoming an object of mockery; the translation starts the passive ser zombado, with the printed nasal abbreviation in zõ-.
[c1-l004 >] *bado, & eſcarnecido, &c.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the preceding passive translation: being mocked and scorned. This short continuation belongs to Naburimono rather than to the next entry.
[c1-l005] Naca. *Dentro, meo, entre, &c. ¶Itẽ, Amiza*
[c1-l005 note] Naca covers inside, middle, and between, then introduces the relational sense of friendship. Amiza continues as amizade below; the unhyphenated physical division is preserved.
[c1-l006 >] *de. ¶* Nacauo chigǒ. *Quebrar a amizade.*
[c1-l006 note] Naca wo chigau is breaking off friendly relations. The visible gloss reads Quebrar, not OCR Qusbrar; the Japanese final caron in chigǒ is retained without modernizing the contracted form.
[c1-l007 >] *¶* Nacauo nauoſu. *Fazer as amizades.*
[c1-l007 note] Naca wo naosu means repairing relations or making peace. The Portuguese contrasts this reconciliation with the rupture described immediately above.
[c1-l008] Nacaba. *Meo da couſa que ſe vai fazendo, &*
[c1-l008 note] Nacaba denotes the middle of an activity in progress, rather than only a spatial centre. The coordinated Portuguese description continues onto the following row.
[c1-l009 >] *continuando como meo da meſa, lição, &c.*
[c1-l009 note] The examples illustrate being midway through a meal or a lesson. Historical meo and the printed meſa are kept rather than replaced by modern vocabulary.
[c1-l010] Nacabone. *Eſpinha grande, que està no meo*
[c1-l010 note] Nacabone is the large central fishbone or backbone. The relative clause locates it in the middle, with its object supplied on the short next row.
[c1-l011 >] *do peixe.*
[c1-l011 note] Do peixe completes the description of the central bone of a fish. It is a complete physical continuation line, not an independent gloss.
[c1-l012] Nacadacana. *Couſa alta no meo. ¶* Naca-
[c1-l012 note] Nacadacana describes something raised in the middle. The repeated Naca- begins the compound example continued below, and its printed hyphen is visible.
[c1-l013 >] dacana cauo. *Entre as molheres ſe toma*
[c1-l013 note] Completes nacadacana cauo, a face with a raised central profile. The Portuguese introduces a particular use among women, explained more fully in the next two rows.
[c1-l014 >] *por roſto que tem o nariz aleuantado, & mais*
[c1-l014 note] The phrase is interpreted as a face with a high, projecting nose. The final mais modifies the following description, not a second headword.
[c1-l015 >] *ſaido pera fora.*
[c1-l015 note] Saido pera fora completes the image of a nose projecting outward. The historical phrase and its unaccented spelling are preserved.
[c1-l016] Nacadachi. *Terceiro. Caſamenteiro. Al-*
[c1-l016 note] Nacadachi is an intermediary, including a marriage broker and, in the divided final word, a procurer. Caſamenteiro is one printed word, not OCR Ca ſamenteiro.
[c1-l017 >] *couiteiro. ¶* Nacadachiuo ſuru. *Fa-*
[c1-l017 note] Couiteiro completes Alcouiteiro. Nacadachi wo suru means acting as such an intermediary; the Portuguese verb Fazer is divided after Fa-.
[c1-l018 >] *zer eſtes officios.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes Fazer estes officios, performing the intermediary roles just enumerated. Officios refers to those functions rather than to a separate occupation entry.
[c1-l019] Nacagaqi. *Sebe que ſe faz no meo pera di-*
[c1-l019 note] Nacagaqi is a dividing hedge or fence erected in the middle. The Portuguese purpose clause begins the divided noun divisão.
[c1-l020 >] *uiſão dalgũa couſa. Vt,* Nacagaqiuo ſuru.
[c1-l020 note] The hedge separates something into parts; nacagaqi wo suru illustrates making that partition. The tilde is over u in dalgũa, and Vt is printed in the italic apparatus.
[c1-l021 >] *Fazer esta ſebe.*
[c1-l021 note] The translation means making this hedge, directly explaining the preceding Japanese example. The provisional st classification in esta is retained rather than globally regularized.
[c1-l022] Nacagoro. *Meo tempo, tendo reſpeito ao prin-*
[c1-l022 note] Nacagoro denotes a middle period relative to a beginning and an end. The physical line divides principio after prin-.
[c1-l023 >] *cipio, ou fim quer ſeja de hum anno, ou mes.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes the contrast between beginning and end, whether of a year or a month. The scan supports fim, not OCR ſim; the Portuguese temporal comparison confirms the word identity.
[c1-l024] Nacagoto. *Couſas que ſe dizem de hum pera*
[c1-l024 note] Nacagoto concerns remarks about someone intended to damage relations. The purpose clause is unfinished here and continues with causing a break with another person.
[c1-l025 >] *o fazer quebrar com outro. ¶* Nacagoto-
[c1-l025 note] The remarks are intended to make one person fall out with another. The repeated headword begins an example and remains divided from its object particle by the physical line break.
[c1-l026 >] uo yǔ. *Dizer couſas que cauſão diſcordia.*
[c1-l026 note] Nacagoto wo yuu means saying things that cause discord. The translation supplies the hostile interpersonal sense rather than neutral conversation between people.
[c1-l027] Nacaguro. *Vt,* Nacagurono ſoya. *Fre-*
[c1-l027 note] Nacaguro describes arrows blackened at the middle; the example is nacaguro no soya. Fre- begins the Portuguese plural for arrows on the next row.
[c1-l028 >] *chas vruxadas de preto no meo.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes Frechas: arrows lacquered black in the middle. Vruxadas is retained as the dictionary's Portuguese description of lacquer treatment.
[c1-l029] Naca-i. *Molheres de ſeruiço que não ſão das*
[c1-l029 note] Naca-i denotes women in service who are not of the lowest rank. Enlargement shows an internal hyphen before i, omitted in the OCR; it is not a physical line-end division.
[c1-l030 >] *mais baixas.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the qualification that these servants are not the lowest-ranking women in service. The short row belongs to Naca-i.
[c1-l031] Nacaichijit. *Dia do meo que fica entre dous.*
[c1-l031 note] Nacaichijit denotes a full intervening day between two other days. The final t is the historical checked ending and is not expanded to a modern Japanese syllable.
[c1-l032 >] *Vt,* Naca ichijit tôriǔ itaita. *Detiueme*
[c1-l032 note] The example describes staying for one intervening day. The scan visibly separates Naca ichijit here, unlike the headword above. Tôriǔ refers to a stay or sojourn; itaita is retained as the historical past form rather than modernized.
[c1-l033 >] *hum dia inteiro, & parte do em que cheguei, &*
[c1-l033 note] The stay includes one whole day and part of the arrival day. The next row adds part of the departure day, clarifying exactly what the intervening day excludes.
[c1-l034 >] *parte do em que me parti.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the stay with part of the day of departure. The three physical rows together explain one complete day between arrival and departure.
[c1-l035] Naca ichinen. *Anno meo entre dous.*
[c1-l035 note] Naca ichinen similarly means an intervening year between two others. The visible space after Naca is preserved, unlike the joined preceding day expression.
[c1-l036] Nacaje. *Lugar do meo dalbarda do caualo que*
[c1-l036 note] Nacaje names the central place in a horse's pack saddle. The scan supports Lugar rather than OCR Lagar; the relative clause continues with its position between the side loads.
[c1-l037 >] *fica entre os dous coſtais.* B.
[c1-l037 note] Specifies the space between the two side loads or panniers. The final upright B. is the dictionary's low-register label, not part of the italic Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l038] Nacajima. *Ilha que eſtà no meo do rio, ou do*
[c1-l038 note] Nacajima is an island in the middle of a river or sea. The surviving i and m support jima, correcting OCR juna and the resulting misleading kana hint.
[c1-l039 >] *mar. ¶ Item, Ilha que fazem artificioſa-*
[c1-l039 note] Completes the sea-island sense and adds an artificial island. Artificioſa- continues with mente on the next row, retaining the visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l040 >] *mente no meo dalgum tanque.*
[c1-l040 note] The artificial island is made in the middle of a pond. This explains the second sense of Nacajima without assigning a particular garden tradition not named in the gloss.
[c1-l041] Nacamichi. *Caminho do meo.*
[c1-l041 note] Nacamichi means the middle road or path. The damaged medial type is m, supported by its surviving strokes and Caminho, not OCR n in Nacanichi.
[c1-l042] Nacanaca. *Adu. Si, em verdade, &c.*
[c1-l042 note] Nacanaca is here an affirmative adverb, yes or indeed. This explicitly recorded historical sense should not be replaced by the better-known modern negative-polarity construction.
[c1-l043] Nacanacani. *Adu. Aſsi como aßi milhor he,*
[c1-l043 note] Nacanacani introduces the sense that something would be better anyway or rather. The scan separates Aſsi como and has aßi without the OCR's extra s.
[c1-l044 >] *&c. ¶* Nacanacani xindaga maxigia.
[c1-l044 note] The example means that dying would be preferable. The Japanese xinda ga maxi gia is printed with the observed joined forms, not respaced into modern analytical units.
[c1-l045] *Aßi como aßi milhor he morrer.*
[c1-l045 note] The Portuguese explicitly translates the preceding example as it being better to die anyway. This flush-left continuation remains body text, not a new headword.
[c1-l046] Nacanacano coto. *Pois não ? ſi, &c.*
[c1-l046 note] Nacanacano coto supplies an emphatic affirmative response, rendered by Pois não? and yes. The initial is N, correcting OCR V and its erroneous derived reading.
[c1-l047] Nacanauori. *O fazer as amizades. Vt,* Na-
[c1-l047 note] Nacanauori denotes making peace or restoring friendship. The example begins with Na- and continues on the final physical row of this column.
[c1-l048 >] canauoriuo ſuru.
[c1-l048 note] Completes nacanauori wo suru, to reconcile or make peace. The scan places this continuation at the ordinary continuation indent; the stable ID is retained while its placement is corrected.

[c2-l001] Nacani. *Fato que vai no caualo entre os dous*
[c2-l001 note] Nacani is the load carried on a horse between the two side loads. The final s of dous is visible against the right rule and is restored from the incomplete OCR dou.
[c2-l002 >] *coſtais.*
[c2-l002 note] Coſtais completes the two side loads or panniers flanking the central burden. This refers to their placement, complementing Nacaje in the preceding column.
[c2-l003] Nacanimo. *Adu. Eſpecialmente.*
[c2-l003 note] Nacanimo means especially or particularly. The dictionary labels it an adverb and provides the direct Portuguese equivalent.
[c2-l004] Nacanzzucu. *Adu. Eſpecialmente.*
[c2-l004 note] Nacanzzucu is another expression for especially, corresponding to nakanzuku. The double z is part of the printed historical spelling and is not simplified.
[c2-l005] Nacara. *Mea medida de qualquer couſa.*
[c2-l005 note] Nacara denotes half a measure of anything. The vowel sequence in qualquer corrects OCR quelquer without altering the historical feminine mea.
[c2-l006] Nacarabi. *Marè mea vazia. Vt,* Nacara-
[c2-l006 note] Nacarabi first describes a tide halfway out. Enlargement shows a small grave over the final e of Marè; the repeated Japanese form is divided after Nacara-.
[c2-l007 >] bi de gozaru. *He marẽ mea vazia. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l007 note] Completes nacarabi de gozaru, saying that the tide is half out. The small mark over marẽ is worn; the aligned OCR's tilde is provisionally retained, rather than copied from the preceding grave-marked occurrence.
[c2-l008 >] *Couſa que eſta mea enxuta, ou ſeca. Vt,* Na-
[c2-l008 note] Adds the sense of something half dried. The example starts with Na- and continues on the next row; the two senses share incomplete drying or withdrawal of water.
[c2-l009 >] carabini fita. *Eſtar algũa couſa mea enxuta,*
[c2-l009 note] Completes nacarabini fita, illustrating the half-dry state. The printed fita is retained as the form associated with drying, not replaced by an anticipated xita; the Portuguese directly confirms the state described.
[c2-l010 >] *ou ſeca.*
[c2-l010 note] Ou seca completes the alternative Portuguese wording for being half dry. It belongs to the preceding example rather than introducing a separate sense.
[c2-l011] Nacatagai. *O quebrar a amizade.*
[c2-l011 note] Nacatagai denotes a falling-out or rupture of friendship. Its gloss connects directly with the earlier Naca example about breaking relations.
[c2-l012] Nacatçugui. *Eſteira de dous juncos de largo,*
[c2-l012 note] This Nacatçugui names a mat made from two lengths of rushes across its width. The following row explains how the rushes are joined at the middle.
[c2-l013 >] *juntos, & tecidos no meo do aueſo. Vt,*
[c2-l013 note] The rushes are joined and woven at the centre on the reverse side. The single printed long s in aueſo is preserved rather than doubled from modern avesso.
[c2-l014 >] Nacatçuguino vomote. *Idem.*
[c2-l014 note] Nacatçugui no omote repeats the name for this mat surface. The initial vowel is a, not OCR u; the printed vomote spelling is preserved.
[c2-l015] Nacatçugui. *Certa boceta redonda vruxada*
[c2-l015 note] A separate homonymous entry describes a particular round lacquered container. It must not be merged with the preceding mat entry merely because the headword is identical.
[c2-l016 >] *que ſerue de ter Cha moido.*
[c2-l016 note] The container holds powdered tea. The scan reads ſerue, not OCR ſerae; Cha is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese explanation and is recorded for optional whole-word typeface review.
[c2-l017] Nacate. *Segũda nouidade do arroz, ou arroz*
[c2-l017 note] Nacate denotes rice maturing after the earliest crop, here described as the second harvest or crop. The nasal abbreviation in Segũda remains unexpanded.
[c2-l018 >] *que vem mais tarde, logo depois do primej-*
[c2-l018 note] Specifies rice arriving later, immediately after the first. The printed primej- is retained at the division before ro, including its descending final letter rather than modernizing primeiro.
[c2-l019 >] *ro. O mais temporão ſe diz, Vaxe. O mais*
[c2-l019 note] Completes the preceding word and names the earliest-ripening rice Vaxe, corresponding to wase. Vaxe is embedded within Portuguese naming syntax, so it is included in the typeface-term mapping.
[c2-l020 >] *ſorodeo, Vocute.*
[c2-l020 note] Names the late-ripening rice Vocute, corresponding to okute. The Portuguese ſorodeo contrasts with temporão above; Vocute is another embedded Japanese term rather than a new headword here.
[c2-l021] Nacauobi. *Vt,* Nacauobino tei. l, nacauo-
[c2-l021 note] Nacauobi introduces examples of being dressed with a sash but without outer garments. The upright l supplies an alternative expression, whose nacauo- continues below.
[c2-l022 >] bi bacaride. *O eſtar ſomente cingido mas*
[c2-l022 note] Completes nacauobi bacaride, being only girt with a sash. Enlargement shows no tilde over the o of ſomente; the OCR's nasal mark is removed.
[c2-l023 >] *ſem calçoẽs, & ſem Dobuco.*
[c2-l023 note] Specifies the absence of trousers and a Dobuco outer garment. The word Dobuco is embedded Japanese within the Portuguese clause; the broken ascender of b is not supplied as an accent on o.
[c2-l024] Nacauori. *Papel aßi chamado.*
[c2-l024 note] Nacauori is identified simply as a kind of paper with this name. The gloss does not justify supplying a particular manufacturing method or modern paper category.
[c2-l025] Nacazaxi. *Certa laya de frechas mais esti-*
[c2-l025 note] Nacazaxi names a particular, more highly valued kind of arrow. The Portuguese adjective is divided after esti-; its provisional st classification is retained.
[c2-l026 >] *madas.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes estimadas, valued or esteemed, qualifying the arrows above. The short continuation is real body text despite the nearby ink blemish at its beginning.
[c2-l027] Nacazora. *Região do ar.*
[c2-l027 note] Nacazora denotes the region of the air or mid-sky. The explanation is spatial and supplies no additional cosmological classification.
[c2-l028] Nacazorano cumo. *Nuuens do ceo, ou do ar.*
[c2-l028 note] Nacazora no cumo means clouds in the sky or air. The Portuguese historical spellings Nuuens and ceo are preserved.
[c2-l029] Nacazumi. *Riſca que ſe deita pello meo do*
[c2-l029 note] Nacazumi is an inked centre line. The description begins with drawing a line through the middle and identifies the wooden surface on the following row.
[c2-l030 >] *pao, ou taboa com tinta.*
[c2-l030 note] The line is drawn with ink along a piece of wood or board. This completes the carpenter's marking sense rather than referring simply to black coloration.
[c2-l031] Naccu. *Adu. Que tem varias ſignificaçoens,*
[c2-l031 note] Naccu is labelled an adverb with several meanings and uses. The ensuing examples concern unexpectedly early or easy completion and arrival; the double c is retained.
[c2-l032 >] *& vſo. Vt,* Naccu xifataita. *Acabei*
[c2-l032 note] Naccu xifataita means finishing sooner or more easily than expected, as the continuation explains. The scan has Naccu, not OCR Xaccu; xifataita retains the historical s-stem past formation.
[c2-l033 >] *quando menos cuidaua, ou mais cedo, & com*
[c2-l033 note] The speaker finished when least expecting it, or sooner, and with greater ease. The unfinished comparison continues onto the next physical row.
[c2-l034 >] *mais facilidade do que ſe eſperaua. ¶* Nac-
[c2-l034 note] Completes the comparison with what had been expected. The next illustration begins with the divided Nac-, which is not a separate lexical form.
[c2-l035 >] cu tçuita. *Cheguei mais depreſſa do que*
[c2-l035 note] Completes Naccu tçuita, arriving sooner than expected. The example's arrival verb contrasts with the preceding completion verb while illustrating the same adverb.
[c2-l036 >] *cuidaua.*
[c2-l036 note] Cuidaua completes the comparison with the speaker's expectation. The Portuguese verb here concerns thinking or expecting, not taking care of someone.
[c2-l037] Nacǒdo. *Terceiro.*
[c2-l037 note] Nacǒdo is a go-between or intermediary, not the ordinal third in this context. The open-long-o caron is visible and is not replaced by the modern nakoudo spelling.
[c2-l038] Nada. *Borda do mar, ou junto da terra.* Na-
[c2-l038 note] Nada is glossed as the sea edge or water close to land. The example begins with Na-, retained at the physical division rather than generalized to every modern use of nada.
[c2-l039 >] dauo noru. *Nauegar ao longo da terra.*
[c2-l039 note] Nada wo noru is sailing along the coast. The visible word is longo, correcting OCR lengo; the gloss specifically keeps the vessel near land.
[c2-l040] Nadamari, u, atta. *Mitigarſe, ou aplacarſe.*
[c2-l040 note] Nadamari denotes becoming pacified or subsiding. The reflexive Portuguese verbs distinguish this intransitive entry from the transitive Nadame that follows.
[c2-l041 >] *¶* Icariga nadamaru. *Abrandarſe a ira.*
[c2-l041 note] Icari ga nadamaru means anger subsiding. Enlargement supports Abrandarſe, correcting the OCR's Abrandas ſe; the Portuguese infinitive describes the calming of the anger itself.
[c2-l042] Nadame, uru, eta. *Mitigar, ou abrandar.*
[c2-l042 note] Nadame means soothing or mitigating something. The printed principal-part abbreviation uru is preserved rather than rewritten to the modern verb form.
[c2-l043 >] *¶* Fitono cocorouo nadamuru. *Apla-*
[c2-l043 note] Fito no cocoro wo nadamuru means soothing another person's heart or feelings. The object-marked construction supplies the transitive counterpart to anger subsiding above.
[c2-l044 >] *car, ou conſolar a alguem.*
[c2-l044 note] Completes Aplacar and adds comforting someone. The Portuguese translation confirms that the preceding Japanese example is emotional reassurance, not flattening a physical surface.
[c2-l045] Nadaracana. *Couſa igual, & plana. ¶ Permet.*
[c2-l045 note] Nadaracana first describes something even and level, then announces a metaphorical extension. The medial r is visible; OCR Nadatacana is corrected without altering the printed Permet. abbreviation.
[c2-l046 >] Nadaracana fito. *Homem brando, &*
[c2-l046 note] Nadaracana fito applies the adjective to a gentle person. The following short row completes the pair of Portuguese character descriptions.
[c2-l047 >] *manſo.*
[c2-l047 note] Manſo completes the metaphorical sense as mild or meek. The signature and Nad below are page furniture, not continuations of this gloss.
