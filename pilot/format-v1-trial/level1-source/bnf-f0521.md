## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Tôtǒdo. *Adu. Modo de estarem muitos à*
[c1-l001 note] Tootoodo describes the manner in which many people sit or remain comfortably at ease. The first long-o mark is a circumflex and the second a caron; the Portuguese construction continues below.
[c1-l002 >] *ſua vontade. Vt,* Tôtǒdo iru. *Eſtarem*
[c1-l002 note] Tôtǒdo iru illustrates remaining at ease. Sua vontade completes the preceding Portuguese phrase, and the new translation begins with estarem before continuing on the next row.
[c1-l003 >] *muitos à ſua vontade aßentados.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes the example as many people seated at their ease. The seated posture in assentados clarifies the Japanese iru rather than supplying a separate headword.
[c1-l004] Tǒtoi. *Couſa ſanta, & marauilhoſa.*
[c1-l004 note] Tootoi is glossed as holy and wondrous. The initial is a damaged T, matching neighboring headwords, rather than OCR F; the Portuguese noun is Couſa.
[c1-l005 >] Tǒtô.
[c1-l005 note] This short indented row gives the contracted adverbial form belonging to tootoi above. Both the caron and final circumflex are retained as printed.
[c1-l006 >] Tôtoſa.
[c1-l006 note] The corresponding abstract noun denotes holiness or venerableness. The first o has only an acute-like surviving stroke; the supplied circumflex is retained provisionally rather than treated as a secure acute. The detached spot near the final a may belong to the broken hook of ſ, so no extra accent is introduced.
[c1-l007] Tǒtono. *Tono, ou ſenhor da terra mancebo ẽ*
[c1-l007 note] Tootono designates the young lord of the territory in contrast with his still-living father. The Japanese title Tono is embedded in the Portuguese explanation and remains italic as printed; the abbreviated ẽ begins the comparison continued below.
[c1-l008 >] *comparação do pay que ainda eſtà viuo mas de-*
[c1-l008 note] The contrast is with the father who is still alive but has relinquished government. The final de- is the first part of dexou on the next physical row, not an independent preposition.
[c1-l009 >] *xouja o gouerno.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes dexouja o gouerno: the father has already handed over government. The close spacing of ja is retained in the transcription rather than modernized.
[c1-l010] Totonoi, ô, ôta. *Cõcertarſe, ou aparelharſe.*
[c1-l010 note] Totonoi gives the intransitive forms for becoming arranged or ready. Both Portuguese reflexives describe preparation, and the abbreviated cõ- is retained without expanding the tilde.
[c1-l011] Totonouori, u, otta. *Idem. ¶* Zôſacu
[c1-l011 note] Totonouori is equated with the preceding preparation verb. Zoosaku begins the following example and refers to construction or building work.
[c1-l012 >] totonouotta. *Acabaranſe as obras.*
[c1-l012 note] Zoosaku totonowotta means that the construction work was completed. The Portuguese reads Acabaranſe with the printed n; it is not silently modernized to a modern reflexive spelling.
[c1-l013] Totonoye, uru, eta. *Concertar, ou apare-*
[c1-l013 note] Totonoye is the transitive verb for arranging or preparing something, contrasted with the preceding intransitive entries. The Portuguese apare- continues as aparelhar.
[c1-l014 >] *lhar. ¶* Mexiuo totonoyuru. *Fazer,*
[c1-l014 note] Meshi wo totonoyuru illustrates preparing food. The historical yuru ending is retained, and the Portuguese begins its rendering with fazer.
[c1-l015 >] *ou aparelhar o comer.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes the food-preparation example as making or preparing the meal. O comer is the nominal expression for food or eating here.
[c1-l016] Tottçuqe. *Hum certo cordelſinho que eſtà de-*
[c1-l016 note] Tottsuke is identified as a small cord used on horse equipment. The visible diminutive cordelſinho is retained, and de- continues into dependurado.
[c1-l017 >] *pendurado dambas as partes das retrancas do*
[c1-l017 note] The cord hangs on both sides of the breeching or rear harness. Dambas is the printed contraction, and do introduces the horse named on the next row.
[c1-l018 >] *caualo.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes the harness description with the horse as possessor. This short continuation is independent physical text, not part of the next headword.
[c1-l019] Totte. *Aſa de algum vaſo, cesto, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] Totte denotes a handle, such as the handle of a vessel or basket. The Portuguese asa provides the concrete object-part sense.
[c1-l020] Totte cayexi, ſu, ita. *Voltar, ou tornar a*
[c1-l020 note] Totte kaeshi means turning back or wheeling about. The damaged initial is T, supported by the adjacent Totte and the compound, rather than OCR long s.
[c1-l021 >] *virar o que vai fugindo.*
[c1-l021 note] The definition specifies a fleeing person turning back. The relative clause belongs with voltar or tornar a virar above.
[c1-l022] Tottenoqi, u, oita. *Afaſtarſe, ou retirar-*
[c1-l022 note] Totte noku is first glossed as moving away or withdrawing. The inflected oita is the supplied historical principal part; retirar- continues on the next row.
[c1-l023 >] *ſe. ¶ Item, Tomando afaſtarſe, & irſe.*
[c1-l023 note] After completing retirar-se, the entry adds taking something and moving off with it. The final verb is irſe, with r, rather than OCR inſe.
[c1-l024] Totto. *Adu. Muito, ou eficazmente.*
[c1-l024 note] Totto is an adverb of degree or effective action: greatly or effectively. This concise gloss starts a new entry and does not continue the retreating verb.
[c1-l025] Tǒua. Cotaye cataru. *Reposta que hum dà*
[c1-l025 note] Toowa concerns a spoken reply, explained by kotaye kataru. The headword begins with T, not OCR r; the Portuguese introduces the answer someone gives.
[c1-l026 >] *branda, ou riſpida. Vt,* Tǒuano yoi fito.
[c1-l026 note] The reply may be mild or harsh. Toowa no yoi hito supplies the example of a person whose responses are good or agreeable.
[c1-l027 >] *Homem de boas repoſtas.*
[c1-l027 note] The Portuguese completes the example as a man of good replies, confirming that the quality of his responses is meant.
[c1-l028] Tǒvchiua. *Abano redondo da China.*
[c1-l028 note] Too-uchiwa is a round Chinese fan. The printed v in vchiua represents the initial vowel of uchiwa and is preserved.
[c1-l029] Touo. *Numero de dez.*
[c1-l029 note] Touo is the native numeral ten. Its spelling is kept distinct from the distance-related touo- compounds that follow.
[c1-l030] Touoaſa. *Lugar baixo em algum rio, ou mar*
[c1-l030 note] Tooasa denotes a shallow place in a river or the sea. The following line specifies a narrow stretch that can be crossed on foot.
[c1-l031 >] *estreito que ſe pode paßar a vao.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the shallow-water definition: a narrow place that can be forded. The phrase a vao explains passage through shallow water.
[c1-l032] Touobiqi. *Vt,* Touobiqiuo ſuru, l, touo-
[c1-l032 note] Toobiki names a falconry action; the example offers toobiki wo suru and the alternative tooha wo hiku. The headword has initial T, correcting OCR r.
[c1-l033 >] fauo fiqu. *Voar o falcão longe, ou deſgar-*
[c1-l033 note] Completes tooha wo hiku, followed by the falcon flying far away. Enlargement supports Voar and falcão, rather than OCR Voer and faltão; desgar- continues on the next row.
[c1-l034 >] *rarſe a tomar algum paßaro.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes desgarrar-se: the falcon ranges away to take a bird. This supplies the hunting purpose of the flight described above.
[c1-l035] Touodouoto. *Adu. Longe. Vt,* Touo-
[c1-l035 note] Toodooto is the adverb far away. The following example repeats it across the physical break, with Touo- at the end of this row.
[c1-l036 >] douoto ficayeta. *Afastarſe longe não ou-*
[c1-l036 note] Toodooto hikayeta describes holding oneself well back. The Portuguese says to stay at a distance, with ou- beginning ousando below.
[c1-l037 >] *ſando chegar perto por reſpeito.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes the reason for staying back: not daring to approach closely out of respect. The distance is socially deferential in this example.
[c1-l038] Touodouoxij. *Couſa afaſtada, ou alongada.*
[c1-l038 note] Toodooshii describes something remote or distant, matching both Portuguese adjectives. The final ij is the printed adjectival ending.
[c1-l039] Touofa. *Vt,* Touofauo fiqu. *Vide,* To-
[c1-l039 note] Tooha wo hiku is the same falconry expression explained under Toobiki. The damaged first letter is T, not OCR F; Vide introduces the divided cross-reference To-uobiqi.
[c1-l040 >] uobiqi.
[c1-l040 note] Completes the cross-reference Touobiqi from the preceding physical row. It is not a new entry or an omitted Portuguese continuation.
[c1-l041] Touoficata. *Lugar, ou praya onde vaza mui-*
[c1-l041 note] Toohikata describes a shore or place where the tide withdraws a long way. The Portuguese mui- continues into muito below.
[c1-l042 >] *to amarè, & fica muito eſpaço em ſeco.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the tidal description: much ground is left dry. The close spelling amarè and the printed grave accent are retained rather than respaced or modernized.
[c1-l043] Touogaqe. *Acometimento que hum faz na-*
[c1-l043 note] Toogake is an attack made after going a considerable distance in war. The line-end mark after na is visible and retained even though the following guerra is a separate word.
[c1-l044 >] *guerra indo longe adar nos inimigos. Vt,* To-
[c1-l044 note] The attack involves travelling far to strike the enemy. The compact adar is retained; To- begins the repeated Japanese headword in the example.
[c1-l045 >] uogaqeuo ſuru.
[c1-l045 note] Completes toogake wo suru, to conduct such an attack. The final vowel is u, producing ſuru rather than OCR ſuri.
[c1-l046] Touoi. *Couſa alongada, ou que eſtà longe.*
[c1-l046 note] Tooi describes something far away or distant. The next two indented rows give related grammatical forms.
[c1-l047 >] Touô. *Vt,* Touôm iru. *Ver a olonge.*
[c1-l047 note] Touô is the contracted adverbial form of tooi. The example means looking into the distance, corresponding to touô miru. Its m/r strokes are badly broken, so the supplied Touôm iru and its physical gap are retained provisionally rather than silently respaced into the expected phrase; the circumflex of the head form is clear.
[c1-l048 >] Touoſa.
[c1-l048 note] Toosa is the corresponding noun, distance. It is the final body line in column 1, above the bottom rule.

[c2-l001] Touo jeme. *O por em aperto, ou combater*
[c2-l001 note] Toozeme describes putting an opponent under pressure or attacking from afar. The printed separation in Touo jeme is retained.
[c2-l002 >] *de longe, como quando ſe combate a fortaleza*
[c2-l002 note] Explains the distant attack through the example of fighting against a fortress. This develops the military sense of toozeme.
[c2-l003 >] *não chegando muito perto.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes the qualification: the attackers do not approach very closely. The negative establishes that distance is essential to this sense.
[c2-l004] Touomi. *Atalayas, ou eſpias que vão deſcobrir*
[c2-l004 note] Toomi denotes lookouts or scouts who go to reconnoitre. The Portuguese verbs and nouns identify people performing reconnaissance, not simply distant eyesight.
[c2-l005 >] *o campo na guerra. ¶ Itẽ, Na caça q̃ ſe faz cõ-*
[c2-l005 note] Completes military reconnaissance of the field, then adds a falconry use. The abbreviated Itẽ starts with capital I, and cõ- is printed with a terminal mark.
[c2-l006 >] *falcão os que eſpreitão, & notão onde pouſa al-*
[c2-l006 note] In falconry, these are the watchers who observe where a bird settles. The final al- continues into the abbreviated alguma on the next row.
[c2-l007 >] *gũa aue. Vt,* Touomiuo voqu. *Por vi*
[c2-l007 note] Completes alguma ave; toomi wo oku means posting lookouts. The Portuguese vi continues into vigias without a clearly printed line-end hyphen.
[c2-l008 >] *gias, ou eſpias.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes vigias and equates them with scouts or spies. This short row closes the example of posting lookouts.
[c2-l009] Touonǒqe, ru, eta. *Afaſtar algũa couſa, ou*
[c2-l009 note] Toonoke is the transitive verb for putting something far away. Only a tiny triangular remnant survives above the second o: the supplied caron in Touonǒqe is retained provisionally, without treating it as a secure long-vowel reading or erasing the visible mark.
[c2-l010 >] *apartarla longe.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes the transitive definition as separating the object to a distant place. The enclitic object in apartarla is preserved as printed.
[c2-l011] Touonoqi, u, ita. *Alongarſe, ou afaſtarſe.*
[c2-l011 note] Toonoki supplies the corresponding intransitive forms, to move away or become distant. The two Portuguese reflexives contrast with the object-taking definition immediately above.
[c2-l012] Touori. *Teor, ou laya. ¶* Tçucaino to-
[c2-l012 note] Toori here means tenor, nature, or kind. Tsukai no toori begins the example about the substance of a message, divided after to-.
[c2-l013 >] uoriuo yǔ. *Dizer a ſuſtancia, ou teor do reca-*
[c2-l013 note] Tsukai no toori wo yuu means stating the substance or tenor of the message. The caron on yǔ is visible; reca- continues into recado.
[c2-l014 >] *do. ¶* Sonatano touorino bun. *Da meſma*
[c2-l014 note] Sonata no toori no bun introduces a comparison with the addressee or the addressee's stated account. The Portuguese da mesma continues its explanation below.
[c2-l015 >] *maneira, ou teor q̃ vos. Itẽ, O teor do q̃ dizeis.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes the alternatives: in the same manner or tenor as you, or the tenor of what you say. Both abbreviated q̃ forms and Itẽ retain their printed tildes.
[c2-l016] Touori, ru, otta. *Paßar. Vt,* Michiuo
[c2-l016 note] Toori gives the verb to pass or go through. Michi wo begins the example about travelling along a road.
[c2-l017 >] touoru. *Paßar pollo caminho. ¶* Qiǒuo
[c2-l017 note] Michi wo tooru means passing along the road; incidental dark ink crosses the verb without removing its reading. The following Qiǒuo is the object for a scripture or book in the next example: enlargement supports dotted i, not OCR u, and a caron over o.
[c2-l018 >] ichibu mitouoru. *Paßar hum liuro de prin-*
[c2-l018 note] Ichibu mitooru describes going through a whole volume. The Portuguese confirms reading or passing through a book from beginning to end; prin- begins principio.
[c2-l019 >] *cipio atè ocabo. ¶* Côquai cocoroni to-
[c2-l019 note] Completes from beginning to end, preserving the printed ocabo. Kookai kokoro ni tooru then introduces repentance penetrating or reaching the heart.
[c2-l020 >] uoru. *Ter contrição de coração.*
[c2-l020 note] Completes the Japanese tooru and renders the phrase as heartfelt contrition. The spiritual sense is an extension of passage or penetration.
[c2-l021] Touori. *Paßagem, ou o paßar. ¶* Michino
[c2-l021 note] Toori is now the noun passage or passing. Michi no begins an expression for the route or course of a road.
[c2-l022 >] touori. i, Michino ſugi. *Vereda, ou fi-*
[c2-l022 note] Michi no toori is explained by michi no suji, the course of a road. The printed ſugi spelling is retained; fi- begins fieira in the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l023 >] *eira do caminho. ¶ Item, Hũa bebida, ou*
[c2-l023 note] Completes the road's course or line, then gives a drinking-related sense. The following rows explain the social act rather than a variety of beverage.
[c2-l024 >] *beberete que fazem muitos juntos bebendo cada*
[c2-l024 note] The drinking is done together by many people, each taking a turn. Cada introduces the distributive phrase completed by hum below.
[c2-l025 >] *hum, & afaſtando ſe como quando o ſenhor dà*
[c2-l025 note] Each person drinks and then moves away. The comparison introduces a lord giving the drinking cup to his servants.
[c2-l026 >] *o Sacazzuqui aos criados. Mas cõmummente*
[c2-l026 note] The Japanese cup-name Sacazzuqui is embedded in the Portuguese description of the lord serving his retainers. Its type remains italic in this impression; the sentence then introduces the more usual name.
[c2-l027 >] *ſe diz,* Votouori.
[c2-l027 note] Votouori is the more commonly used designation for this shared drinking round. It is an upright Japanese term inside the Portuguese explanation, not a separate headword.
[c2-l028] Touoriauaxe, ſuru, eta. *Paßando encon-*
[c2-l028 note] Tooriawase means encountering something while passing. The supplied ſuru principal form is retained even though it differs from a simple modern stem expansion.
[c2-l029 >] *trarſe com algũa couſa, ou acertar de ver al-*
[c2-l029 note] Continues encountering something or happening to see something. Both alternatives depend on the passing-by context, and al- continues into alguma below.
[c2-l030 >] *gũa couſa paßando, &c.*
[c2-l030 note] Completes the chance-encounter definition: seeing something while going past. The abbreviated gũa is the continuation of al- from the preceding row.
[c2-l031] Touoſaburai. *Lugar, ou caſa determinada on-*
[c2-l031 note] Toosaburai is a designated place or building for certain retainers. The final on- begins onde, locating the activity described next.
[c2-l032 >] *de cuſtumão eſtar os criados honrados dalgum*
[c2-l032 note] Specifies that a lord's honored retainers customarily stay there. The historical cuſtumão and the contraction dalgum are preserved.
[c2-l033 >] *ſenhor.*
[c2-l033 note] Completes the possessive phrase of some lord. This genuine short body line closes the definition of the retainers' room.
[c2-l034] Touoxi, ſu, oita. *Paßar algũa couſa, l, deixar,*
[c2-l034 note] Tooshi is the transitive verb to pass something through, allow it through, or cause it to pass. The l of algũa is largely lost: its upper remnant survives between a and gũa, supporting this chosen restoration of the damaged word.
[c2-l035 >] *ou fazer paßar. Vt,* Nacauo aqete touo-
[c2-l035 note] Completes the causative gloss, then illustrates opening the middle to let someone pass. The Japanese toosu is divided after touo-.
[c2-l036 >] ſu. *Dar caminho a alguem pollo meyo.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes toosu and translates the example as giving someone a path through the middle. The scan has pollo with two l letters, correcting the draft's polo.
[c2-l037 >] *¶* Iqidouoriuo touoſu. *Durar o rancor.*
[c2-l037 note] Ikidoori wo toosu means maintaining resentment or allowing it to persist. The Portuguese durar o rancor identifies the figurative sustained-emotion sense.
[c2-l038 >] *¶* Nozomiuo touoſu. *Ir auante com o*
[c2-l038 note] Nozomi wo toosu means carrying one's desire or intention through. The Portuguese begins proceeding onward with the desire.
[c2-l039 >] *deſejo atè o comprir. ¶* Muneuo touoſu-
[c2-l039 note] Completes pursuing the desire until fulfilling it. The next example begins mune wo toosu, with the printed terminal hyphen retained before yoona.
[c2-l040 >] yǒna itami. *Dores que parece atraueſar o*
[c2-l040 note] Mune wo toosu yoona itami describes pain that seems to pass through the chest. The visible singular parece and single long s in atraueſar are preserved despite modern grammatical or spelling expectations.
[c2-l041 >] *peito, ou coração.*
[c2-l041 note] Completes the penetrating-pain example as pain through the chest or heart. It closes the final illustration of toosu.
[c2-l042] Touozacari, ru, atta. *Afaſtarſe, ou alongarſe.*
[c2-l042 note] Toozakari is to move away or become distant. The principal forms and both Portuguese reflexives point to an intransitive sense.
[c2-l043] Touoxiguiri. *Hũa laya de verruma de Iapão.*
[c2-l043 note] Tooshigiri is a kind of Japanese boring tool or auger. The entry supplies no more precise construction details, so the note does not assign a modern tool subtype.
[c2-l044] Touoya. *Frecha delgada pera voar, ou che-*
[c2-l044 note] Tooya is a slender arrow intended to fly or reach far. The final che- starts chegar; its initial c and tall h replace the OCR reading abe-.
[c2-l045 >] *gar longe.*
[c2-l045 note] Completes chegar longe, reaching a great distance. It belongs to the arrow's purpose, not a separate lexical entry.
[c2-l046] Touoyama. *Monte que eſtà longe, ou*
[c2-l046 note] Tooyama denotes a distant mountain. The Portuguese adjective on the next line restates longe.
[c2-l047 >] *diſtante.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes the mountain definition as distant. The visible long-s/t group restores diſtante from OCR ditante; the short continuation is the final body row, above the separate signature and catchword.
