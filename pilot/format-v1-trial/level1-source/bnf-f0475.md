[c1-l001] Taſo. *Quem, ou quem he? quem eſtà ahi.*
[c1-l001 note] Taſo is an interrogative, who or who is there. The last word is ahi, with h rather than OCR b; the entry confirms the preceding page's Taſo catchword.
[c1-l002] Taſocare doqi. *Entre luſco, & fuſco da noite.*
[c1-l002 note] Taſocare doqi denotes evening twilight. The initial is T, and the Portuguese expression reads luſco and fuſco, correcting OCR Faſocare and foſco without modernizing the Japanese form.
[c1-l003] Taſucari. *Saluação.*
[c1-l003 note] Taſucari is the noun salvation or deliverance. The clear initial T distinguishes the actual headword from the OCR's Faſucari.
[c1-l004] Taſucari, ru, atta. *Saluarſe, ou eſcapar.*
[c1-l004 note] The verb taſucari means to be saved or escape. Its intransitive force contrasts with taſuqe, saving or helping another, later in the column.
[c1-l005 >] *Vt,* goxǒuo taſucaru. *Saluarſe na outra*
[c1-l005 note] Goxǒuo taſucaru is illustrated as salvation in the next life. The lowercase example and the caron belong to the printed form; Vt is italic apparatus.
[c1-l006 >] *vida. ¶* Nanguiuo taſucaru. *Eſcapar*
[c1-l006 note] Vida completes the afterlife example. Nanguiuo taſucaru then introduces escape from danger or hardship, with its Portuguese object continuing below.
[c1-l007 >] *de perigos. ¶* Inochiuo taſucaru. *Sal-*
[c1-l007 note] After completing de perigos, Inochiuo taſucaru means saving one's life. Preserve the printed division of Saluar between this row and the next.
[c1-l008 >] *uar a vida. ¶* Xittçuiga taſucaru. *Pou-*
[c1-l008 note] Uar a vida completes the life-saving gloss. Xittçuiga taſucaru gives an expenditure-saving example; enlargement shows a short horizontal hyphen after Pou, so the divided pouparſe is recorded as Pou-/parſe.
[c1-l009 >] *parſe, ou forrarſe gaſto.*
[c1-l009 note] Parſe completes pouparſe: sparing or saving expenditure. The Portuguese forrarſe supplies a second expression for economizing, not a material lining sense here.
[c1-l010] Taſuqe, uru, eta. *Saluar, ou fazer eſcapar*
[c1-l010 note] Taſuqe is the transitive verb save or enable escape. The recipient, another person, is completed on the following physical row.
[c1-l011 >] *a outro. ¶ Item, Ajudar.*
[c1-l011 note] A outro completes the transitive salvation gloss, and Item adds helping as another meaning. This line clarifies the contrast with the earlier intransitive verb.
[c1-l012] Taſuqe. *Ajuda, ou ſocorro. Vt,* Taſuqe-
[c1-l012 note] The noun Taſuqe means help or relief. Its example begins the compound Taſuqeno tçuuamono, troops sent as reinforcements, across the row boundary.
[c1-l013 >] no tçuuamono. *Soldados de ſocorro.*
[c1-l013 note] No tçuuamono completes the compound for soldiers sent in relief. The Portuguese Soldados de ſocorro confirms the military, rather than household-help, sense.
[c1-l014] Taſuqi. *Fita, ou corda com que atão as mãgas*
[c1-l014 note] Taſuqi is a ribbon or cord for tying up sleeves. The nasal abbreviation in mãgas stands as printed; it is not a different object from mangas.
[c1-l015 >] *arregaçandoas pera trabalhar.*
[c1-l015 note] Arregaçandoas explains raising or tucking up the sleeves for work. The pronoun refers back to the sleeves, completing the definition of the fastening cord.
[c1-l016] Taſuqe bune. *Embarcação em que ſe alguem*
[c1-l016 note] Taſuqe bune is a boat in which someone escapes danger. Its specific shipwreck context is completed on the following row.
[c1-l017 >] *ſalua do naufragio.*
[c1-l017 note] Salua do naufragio specifies rescue from shipwreck. The line finishes the boat definition and does not introduce a new salvation term.
[c1-l018] Taſuqete. *Saluador, ou liurador.*
[c1-l018 note] Taſuqete is defined as a saviour or deliverer. Despite resembling a modern request form, it is treated here as a person-denoting noun by the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l019] Tatacai, ǒ, ǒta. *Pelejar, ou contender.*
[c1-l019 note] Tatacai is the verb to fight or contend. The worn internal t agrees with the immediately following Tatacai noun; OCR Tacacai loses that letter distinction.
[c1-l020] Tatacai. *Peleja, contenda, ou batalha.*
[c1-l020 note] The noun Tatacai means fighting, contention, or battle. Keep it separate from the preceding verb even though the same headword shape occurs.
[c1-l021] Tatacai cutabire, uru, eta. *Canſar de pelejar.*
[c1-l021 note] Tatacai cutabire means to become tired from fighting. The compound and the Portuguese Canſar de pelejar express fatigue resulting from the activity.

[c1-l023 >] *leja, vel, contenda.*

[c1-l024] Tatague. Tanuqino qe. *Cabelos de adibe.*
[c1-l024 note] Tatague is explicitly hair of the tanuki, Tanuqino qe. The initial is T, not OCR F; the Portuguese animal label adibe is retained without imposing a modern zoological equivalence.

[c1-l026 >] *cabelos de adibe.*
[c1-l026 note] Cabelos de adibe completes the brush-material definition. The same animal name as in the headword gloss is repeated, so it should not be independently modernized.
[c1-l027] Tatami. *Colchoens de palha de Iapão.*
[c1-l027 note] Tatami are described as Japanese straw mattresses. The worn country name starts with I, not OCR r; retain the historical spelling Colchoens.
[c1-l028] Tatami, u, ǒda. *Dobrar. ¶* Xiuauo ta-
[c1-l028 note] The verb tatami means to fold. Xiuauo ta- begins the wrinkle example, while the principal part's caron is copied as printed rather than altered from phonological expectation.
[c1-l029 >] tamu. *Ter rugas no roſto. ¶* Iye. l,
[c1-l029 note] Tamu completes Xiuauo tatamu, having facial wrinkles. The next construction offers Iye or Xiro as alternative objects, a house or a fortress.
[c1-l030 >] Xirouo tatamu. *Desfazer caſa, ou forta-*
[c1-l030 note] Xirouo tatamu means dismantling a fortress, alongside the preceding house alternative. The Portuguese Desfazer, not a simple folding action, supplies the sense of this application.
[c1-l031 >] *leza pera ſe mudar a outra parte, &c. ¶* Ta-
[c1-l031 note] Leza completes fortaleza: dismantling it to move elsewhere. The worn word reads parte, with faint t material; Ta- begins the following wave expression.
[c1-l032 >] tamu nami. *Ondas que vem com furia, &*
[c1-l032 note] Tamu completes Tatamu nami, waves piling or folding over one another. The scan supports tamu rather than the OCR tamiu; the Portuguese describes their violent movement.
[c1-l033 >] *como encapelladas.*
[c1-l033 note] Como encapelladas further describes the waves as heaped or crested. This continuation belongs to the wave example, not to the masonry verb below.
[c1-l034] Tatami ague, uru, eta. *Aleuantar pedras*
[c1-l034 note] Tatami ague means to build up or lay stones. The worn vowel before gue is a, not OCR z; the following aguru example confirms the verb family.
[c1-l035 >] *aßentandoas no edificio, &c. Vt,* Ixiuo ta-
[c1-l035 note] The stones are set in a building, followed by Ixiuo ta- as the example's beginning. Preserve the source's aß spelling and the physical Japanese word division.
[c1-l036 >] tami aguru.
[c1-l036 note] Tami aguru completes Ixiuo tatami aguru, building up the stones. The short Japanese-only continuation is not an independent headword and needs no inserted translation.
[c1-l037] Tatami bǒchǒ. *Hũa maneira de cutelo com*
[c1-l037 note] Tatami bǒchǒ names a mat-maker's cutting knife. The damaged second t of Tatami is recoverable from residual strokes; both visible o-marks are carons and remain so.
[c1-l038 >] *q̃ os oficiaes de Tatamis cortão as eſteiras, &c.*
[c1-l038 note] The knife is used by tatami makers to cut the mats. Tatamis is embedded in Portuguese and is italic: its sloping, single-storey a and narrow m contrast with the nearby upright headword types. Preserve the abbreviation q̃ and the Portuguese plural ending.
[c1-l039] Tatami caſane, uru, eta. *Dobrar com mui-*
[c1-l039 note] Tatami caſane means folding with multiple folds or layers. The caſane element contributes repetition or layering, which the Portuguese expands across the line boundary.
[c1-l040 >] *tas dobras.*
[c1-l040 note] Tas completes muitas dobras, many folds. This short Portuguese-only row is the continuation of the folding compound, not part of the next noun.
[c1-l041] Tatami donſu. *Peças de ladrilho.*
[c1-l041 note] Tatami donſu is glossed literally as pieces of brick or tile. Preserve that supplied definition without substituting a textile gloss suggested by the familiar word donsu.
[c1-l042] Tatami ſaxi. *O que faz Tatamis.*
[c1-l042 note] Tatami ſaxi denotes the person who makes tatami. The embedded Tatamis belongs to the italic Portuguese gloss; second-pass comparison with upright headwords corrects the first-pass font separation without changing the word.
[c1-l043] Tatami muxiro. *Eſteira que ſe dobra polo me-*
[c1-l043 note] Tatami muxiro is a mat that can be folded in half. The Portuguese me- begins meyo, its middle, on the next physical row.
[c1-l044 >] *yo ao comprido.*
[c1-l044 note] Yo completes meyo ao comprido, specifying a lengthwise fold. This detail distinguishes the object from an unspecified folding mat.
[c1-l045] Tatami macura. *Almofadas de pao dobradi-*
[c1-l045 note] Tatami macura names folding wooden headrests or pillows. The historical almofadas wording is retained rather than replaced with a modern furniture label.
[c1-l046 >] *ças à maneira de eſtantes.*
[c1-l046 note] Ças completes dobradiças, folding or hinged; the objects are likened to stands. Preserve the grave accent in à and the source's comparison.
[c1-l047] Tatamino dai. *Hũa maneira de catre, ou ban-*
[c1-l047 note] Tatamino dai is a kind of frame or workbench for tatami. The damaged but readable headword replaces the garbled OCR ſaianuino; ban- continues as banca at the top of column 2.
