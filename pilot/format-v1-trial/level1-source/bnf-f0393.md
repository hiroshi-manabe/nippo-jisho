## column-1 [column] Column 1

> Lexical cross-check, after independent scan reading: the supplied *Entry Words Data of Nippojisho*, NINJAL, Hideyuki Ohshima and Taichi Aida, version 202510, licensed CC BY 4.0 (https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/textdb_dataset/en/nipp/index.html; https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The scan-based transcription and English notes are this reviewer's work; the index is not a diplomatic authority.

[c1-l001] Qinnen. Chicaqi toxi. *Annos que ha pou-*
[c1-l001 note] Qinnen is explained as recent years, literally years near at hand. The damaged initial is Q, not OCR D, and Chicaqi supplies the expected 'near'; the Portuguese continues pouco across the row boundary.
[c1-l002 >] *co paſſarão.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes pouco and says that those years passed only a short time ago. The historical spelling paſſarão is retained, not converted to a modern past-tense spelling.
[c1-l003] Qinnicu. Sugi, xiximura. *Neruos, &*
[c1-l003 note] The entry concerns sinews and flesh. Enlargement shows Sugi, with a damaged u and a clear g, rather than OCR Sagi; the form is retained as printed rather than regularized to modern suji. Xiximura supplies the flesh component.
[c1-l004 >] *carne.*
[c1-l004 note] Carne completes the paired Portuguese gloss 'sinews and flesh'; it is a short continuation of Qinnicu, not an independent entry.
[c1-l005] Qinnǒ. Nixiqino fucuro. *Saco de peça fi-*
[c1-l005 note] Nixiqino fucuro identifies a brocade bag. The headword's long-o mark points downward, and the Portuguese begins describing a bag made from fine fabric.
[c1-l006 >] *na, como brocadilho, em que ſe metem couſas*
[c1-l006 note] Completes fina and compares the material to brocade; the bag is used to put things in. The relative clause continues to specify their importance on the next row.
[c1-l007 >] *de importancia.*
[c1-l007 note] The contents are things of importance. This finishes the brocade-bag definition without adding any new Japanese material.
[c1-l008] Qinô. *Ontem.*
[c1-l008 note] Qinô means yesterday, directly matched by Ontem. Its upward circumflex is distinct from the caron on Qinnǒ above.
[c1-l009] Qinomi. *Fruita de aruores.*
[c1-l009 note] Qinomi is tree fruit. The first letter is an upright Q with its descending tail, not an italic O detached from the headword.
[c1-l010] Qinpen. Chicaqi fotori. *Perto. Vt,*
[c1-l010 note] Chicaqi fotori explains the nearby surroundings, and Perto gives 'near'. The italic Vt introduces an example of being nearby.
[c1-l011 >] Qinpenni aru. *Eſtar algũa couſa perto,*
[c1-l011 note] Qinpenni aru means that something is close by. The tilde in algũa is over u; the Japanese headword and its locative ending belong together.
[c1-l012 >] *ou jũto.*
[c1-l012 note] Ou jũto adds 'or adjacent' to the preceding explanation. The abbreviation tilde is preserved rather than expanding the Portuguese word.
[c1-l013] Qinpenxu. *Osque ſeruem immediatamente ao*
[c1-l013 note] This Qinpenxu entry concerns personal attendants who serve someone directly. The source's tight Osque is retained; the object of their service follows below.
[c1-l014 >] *ſenhor, ou andão à ſua ilharga.*
[c1-l014 note] The attendants serve a lord or remain at his side. Ilharga supplies the physical 'side' image behind the close-service sense.
[c1-l015] Qinpenxu. *Vizinhos.*
[c1-l015 note] A separate identically spelled headword is glossed as neighbours. It should not be merged with the preceding definition of a lord's attendants.
[c1-l016] Qinpǒ. Chicaqi catauara. *Lugar perto. Vt,*
[c1-l016 note] Chicaqi catauara explains a nearby place or vicinity. The following example illustrates location, unlike the distinct resisting/prohibiting sense of the next Qinpǒ entry.
[c1-l017 >] Qinpǒni aru. *Eſtar perto, ou junto.*
[c1-l017 note] Qinpǒni aru means being nearby or adjacent. The long-o mark and attached ni agree with the immediately preceding location entry.
[c1-l018] Qinpǒ. Imaxime, fuxegu. *Reſiſtir. Vt,*
[c1-l018 note] This homograph is explained through restraining and defending, then translated as resisting. Fuxegu is the historical spelling corresponding to fusegu; it is not a faulty long-s reading.
[c1-l019 >] Vondeqi yocuyocu qinpǒ xeyo. *Reſiſ-*
[c1-l019 note] The example urges firm resistance against an enemy. The repeated word reads qinpǒ, not OCR qiupǒ, and xeyo is the imperative; the Portuguese verb continues on the next row.
[c1-l020 >] *ti bem ao voßo inimigo prejudicial.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes Reſiſti and translates the exhortation as resisting one's harmful enemy well. The printed voßo is preserved rather than expanded to a different s sequence.
[c1-l021] Qinpon. Firugayeri, u. *O virar, ou re-*
[c1-l021 note] Firugayeri supplies the idea of turning over, which the Portuguese renders as turning or overturning. The abbreviated second form u is retained exactly despite the fuller form a modern reader might expect.
[c1-l022 >] *uoluer.* S.
[c1-l022 note] Uoluer completes reuoluer, 'turn over'. The upright S. is the written-usage label and does not belong to the italic verb.
[c1-l023] Qinpǔ. i, Aqino caje. *Vento do Outono.* S.
[c1-l023 note] Aqino caje explains an autumn wind. The long-u mark is retained, and the final upright S. marks written usage rather than contributing to the Portuguese definition.
[c1-l024] Qinqei. *Certa aue como galinha do mato que*
[c1-l024 note] Qinqei names a bird compared here to a wild hen. The damaged second vowel is i, not OCR u; the next row locates the bird in China, without establishing a modern species in this transcription.
[c1-l025 >] *ha na China.*
[c1-l025 note] This completes the bird description by saying that it occurs in China. China is part of the Portuguese gloss, not an embedded Japanese word.
[c1-l026] Qinqet. *Paços ricos del Rey.*
[c1-l026 note] Qinqet denotes splendid royal palaces. Its final t is retained as the printed checked ending rather than expanded to a modern Japanese syllable.
[c1-l027] Qinqi. *Couſas que ſe prohibem, ou a que hum*
[c1-l027 note] Qinqi first denotes things prohibited or avoided. The second relative clause continues into the explanation of personal aversion on the following row.
[c1-l028 >] *tem aſco por lhe ſerem prejudiciaes pera o eſta-*
[c1-l028 note] The avoidance is attributed to things being harmful to the stomach or health. The scan supports eſta- before mago, so the historical or anomalous vowel is not silently changed to modern estômago.
[c1-l029 >] *mago, ſaude, &c.*
[c1-l029 note] Mago completes the printed eſtamago; ſaude broadens the reference to health. These remain parts of the taboo/avoidance definition.
[c1-l030] Qinqi. Coganeno vtçuuamono. *Vaſo*
[c1-l030 note] This separate Qinqi entry is a gold vessel, explained literally by Coganeno vtçuuamono. Initial v and the doubled u sequence belong to the printed Japanese spelling.
[c1-l031 >] *de ouro.*
[c1-l031 note] De ouro completes 'a vessel of gold'. The short continuation must remain separate from the adverbial entry below.
[c1-l032] Qinqin. Chicaxi, chicaxi. *Adu. Perto.*
[c1-l032 note] The repeated Chicaxi explains nearness, with Adu. identifying adverbial use. The following example shifts the proximity specifically to time: in the near future.
[c1-l033 >] i, *Da qui a pouco tempo. Vt,* Qinqinni
[c1-l033 note] The Portuguese narrows 'near' to a short time from now. Qinqinni begins the Japanese example and continues with the speaker's intended departure below.
[c1-l034 >] macari tatǒzu. *Daqui a poucos dias me*
[c1-l034 note] Macari tatǒzu expresses an intended departure, translated as 'in a few days I will depart'. The historical prospective ending is retained rather than replaced by a modern verb form.
[c1-l035 >] *partirei.*
[c1-l035 note] Partirei completes the first-person future statement. This short row belongs to the temporal example of Qinqin.
[c1-l036] Qinqiocu. Cotono qiocu. *Certa toada de mu-*
[c1-l036 note] Cotono qiocu identifies a musical tune for the koto. The headword begins with roman Q, not OCR italic O; musica is divided after mu-.
[c1-l037 >] *ſica no tanger.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes musica and specifies instrumental performance. Tanger here supplies playing an instrument, not a separate Japanese form.
[c1-l038] Qinqua. Coganeno fana. *Roſas douro.*
[c1-l038 note] Coganeno fana means golden flowers, rendered here as roses of gold. The metallic sense is distinct from the short-lived flower named by the following homograph.
[c1-l039 >] *Vt,* Caqini qinquauo caqe, toniua ſuixǒ
[c1-l039 note] The example describes placing golden flowers on a fence and crystal at the doors. The line ends with the full Japanese ſuixǒ; its object particle continues on the next row without an invented hyphen.
[c1-l040 >] uo tçuranetari. *Na ſebe pos roſas douro,*
[c1-l040 note] The opening uo completes the crystal phrase, and tçuranetari describes arranging it in a row. The Portuguese begins the corresponding account of golden roses placed on the hedge.
[c1-l041 >] *& as portas fez de criſtal.*
[c1-l041 note] The translation concludes that the doors were made of crystal. This completes the ornamental example under the gold-flower sense of Qinqua.
[c1-l042] Qinqua. Aſagauono fana. *Flor aßi chama-*
[c1-l042 note] This Qinqua is explained as the flower of asagao. The Portuguese merely calls it a flower of that name and continues the description below; it is not the golden ornament of the previous entry.
[c1-l043 >] *da que não dura mais que tè ſair o ſol.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes chamada and describes the flower as lasting only until sunrise. The statement is the dictionary's own description, not an independently imposed botanical identification.
[c1-l044] Qinquan. Coganeno camuri. *Coroa de ouro.*
[c1-l044 note] Coganeno camuri explains a golden crown. Camuri is retained in the historical form shown, not modernized to kanmuri.
[c1-l045] Qinrai. i, Chicai coro. *Eſtes tempos atras,*
[c1-l045 note] Qinrai means recently or in these recent times. The scan shows r rather than OCR t in the headword, consistent with Chicai coro and the temporal gloss.
[c1-l046 >] *ou ha pouco tempo.*
[c1-l046 note] The alternative explanation is 'a short time ago'. This completes Qinrai and does not belong to the textile entry below.
[c1-l047] Qinran. *Certa laya de brocadilho da china.*
[c1-l047 note] Qinran names a kind of Chinese brocade. The final character is n, not the extra ni produced by OCR; the printed lowercase china is retained.

[c2-l001] Qinren. *Esteiras ricas que ſe dependurão.*
[c2-l001 note] Qinren is described as rich mats or screens hung up for display. The definition itself supplies the hanging function; no more specific modern furnishing is imposed.
[c2-l002] Qinri. i, Dairi. *Paços del Rey.*
[c2-l002 note] Qinri is equated with Dairi and glossed as the ruler's palace. Dairi is a Japanese synonym, not an embedded term within the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l003] Qinrin. Chicaqi tonari. *Perto, ou vizinho.*
[c2-l003 note] Chicaqi tonari explains proximity or neighbouring location. The example on the next row applies the word to nearby notable flowers.
[c2-l004 >] *¶* Qinrinno meiqua. *Flores inſignes que*
[c2-l004 note] Qinrinno meiqua means notable flowers in the neighbourhood. The scan includes a paragraph mark before the example that the OCR omitted.
[c2-l005 >] *estão perto.*
[c2-l005 note] The relative clause says that the flowers are nearby. This completes the example under Qinrin rather than introducing another sense.
[c2-l006] Qinſan. Coganeno ſacazzuqi. *Taça, ou co-*
[c2-l006 note] Coganeno ſacazzuqi identifies a golden drinking cup. The Portuguese offers two cup terms, dividing copo after co-.
[c2-l007 >] *po de ouro.*
[c2-l007 note] Po completes copo, followed by 'of gold'. This short continuation closes the gold-cup definition.
[c2-l008] Qinsǒ. i, Qizu. *Ferida. ¶ Item, Su-*
[c2-l008 note] Qinsǒ is first equated with Qizu, a wound. The next sense begins the Portuguese word for surgery, divided after Su-, and extends to the practitioner.
[c2-l009 >] *rugia, ou ſurgião q̃ cura feridas. ¶* Qin-
[c2-l009 note] Completes Surugia and adds a surgeon who treats wounds. The repeated Japanese Qinsǒ begins at the right edge and is genuinely hyphenated here.
[c2-l010 >] sǒ ſuru fito. *Surgião.*
[c2-l010 note] Together with Qin- above, the example names a person who practices surgery. The Portuguese Surgião directly identifies that practitioner.
[c2-l011] Qinſocu. *O estar recolhido dentro de caſa ſem*
[c2-l011 note] Qinſocu denotes remaining confined or withdrawn inside one's house. The qualification 'without going out' is completed below.
[c2-l012 >] *ſair fora. Vt,* Qinſocu ſuru.
[c2-l012 note] The example Qinſocu ſuru expresses staying indoors without going out. Vt is part of the italic explanatory apparatus, while the Japanese example is roman.
[c2-l013] Qinſu. Cogane. *Ouro.*
[c2-l013 note] Qinſu is equated with Cogane and translated simply as gold. Neither an additional monetary sense nor a modern spelling is inserted.
[c2-l014] Qintei. i, Dairino niua. *Pateo, ou lugar*
[c2-l014 note] Dairino niua identifies the palace courtyard or garden. The Portuguese starts Pateo, with a clear capital P, correcting OCR Fateo.
[c2-l015 >] *de alegretes del Rey.*
[c2-l015 note] The gloss continues with the ruler's garden beds or planted area. Alegretes is preserved as the printed Portuguese term rather than replaced by a modern garden word.
[c2-l016] Qintet. Cogane, curogane. *Ouro, &*
[c2-l016 note] Qintet combines gold and iron, explicitly supplied by Cogane and curogane. The following example contrasts their values rather than describing an alloy.
[c2-l017 >] *ferro. Vt,* Qintet ataiuo vonajǔ xezu.
[c2-l017 note] The example says that gold and iron do not have the same value. The initial of vonajǔ is read as the printed v; the negative xezu belongs to the historical Japanese construction.
[c2-l018 >] *Ouro, & ferro não tem o meſmo preço.*
[c2-l018 note] The Portuguese explicitly confirms the contrast: gold and iron do not have the same price. This anchors the value comparison in the preceding Japanese sentence.
[c2-l019] Qintǒ. Firugayeri tauoru. *Virandoſe ca-*
[c2-l019 note] Firugayeri tauoru describes turning over and falling. The Portuguese similarly combines turning with cair, divided after ca-.
[c2-l020 >] *ir.* S.
[c2-l020 note] Ir completes cair, 'fall'. The separate upright S. marks written usage, rather than being part of the italic infinitive.
[c2-l021] Qinton. *Certa laya de Mochis redondos com*
[c2-l021 note] Qinton is described as a kind of round Mochis. Enlargement supports Mochis rather than OCR Molbis; the Japanese food name occurs within the Portuguese explanation and takes a Portuguese plural ending.
[c2-l022 >] *aſucar dentro.*
[c2-l022 note] The cakes are said to contain sugar. The printed historical aſucar is retained without adding a cedilla from modern orthography.
[c2-l023] Qinu. *Peça de ſeda delgada.*
[c2-l023 note] Qinu is a piece of fine or thin silk fabric. It is distinct from the following Qin v, despite their similar unaccented letters.
[c2-l024] Qin v. i, Nichirin. *Sol.*
[c2-l024 note] Qin v is equated with Nichirin and translated as the sun. The visibly separated v is preserved; it is not merged into the silk headword Qinu above.
[c2-l025] Qinuguinu. *P.* i, Vacare. *Apartamento.*
[c2-l025 note] Qinuguinu is a poetic expression for parting, equated with Vacare. Apartamento here means separation, not a modern residential apartment; the initial P. is the poetic label.
[c2-l026] Qinuocu. i, Qeccôna iye. *Fermoſas, &*
[c2-l026 note] Qeccôna iye explains splendid houses. The printed Qinuocu is retained without changing its internal u to suit an expected modern romanization.
[c2-l027 >] *ricas caſas.*
[c2-l027 note] The houses are described as rich as well as beautiful. This completes the Portuguese adjective-and-noun phrase begun above.
[c2-l028] Qinuta. *Paos com que batem as peças de ſeda,*
[c2-l028 note] Qinuta is described here through the wooden implements used to beat silk cloth. The terminal mark is a comma, not the OCR period, continuing the list of fabrics below.
[c2-l029 >] *ou cangas, & nonos.*
[c2-l029 note] The continuation adds cangas and nonos to the fabrics beaten with these implements. The historical textile names are preserved rather than replaced with the generic Portuguese panos.
[c2-l030] Qinuya. *Caſa onde ſe fazem, ou tecem peças*
[c2-l030 note] Qinuya is a place where silk fabrics are made or woven. The Portuguese description concerns production rather than explicitly defining a retail shop.
[c2-l031 >] *delgadas de ſeda.*
[c2-l031 note] This finishes the description as fine or thin pieces of silk. It belongs to the workshop entry, not the following textile name.
[c2-l032] Qinxa. *Certa laya de peças delgadas.*
[c2-l032 note] This Qinxa names a kind of thin fabric. Although a dark blot crosses delgadas, the surviving letters support the word; the opening headword is roman Q, not italic O.
[c2-l033] Qinxa. Coganeno iſago. *Area de ouro.*
[c2-l033 note] This homograph means gold sand, explicitly explained by Coganeno iſago. The Portuguese Area is retained as printed rather than modernized to areia.
[c2-l034] Qinxẽ. Coganeno vǒgui. *Abano dourado.*
[c2-l034 note] Qinxẽ denotes a golden fan, with the final nasal abbreviation preserved. The Japanese explanation uses vǒgui; the Portuguese describes a gilded fan.
[c2-l035] Qinxen. Coganeno jeni. *Moedas de ouro.*
[c2-l035 note] Coganeno jeni identifies gold coins. The full -en ending distinguishes this printed headword from the abbreviated fan entry immediately above.
[c2-l036] Qinxenqua. *Certo genero de roſas.*
[c2-l036 note] Qinxenqua names a kind of flower, described by the dictionary as a type of rose. A modern botanical species is not supplied from the headword alone.
[c2-l037] Qinxeqi. Cogane, ixi. *Metal, & pedras.*
[c2-l037 note] The paired Japanese words are metal and stone. The Portuguese likewise uses the broader Metal here, rather than the narrower gold gloss seen in neighbouring entries.
[c2-l038] Qinxet. Coganeno ſuricuzzu. *Pò, ou lima-*
[c2-l038 note] Coganeno ſuricuzzu identifies gold scrapings or filings. The Portuguese offers powder or filings and divides limaduras after lima-.
[c2-l039 >] *duras de ouro. Vt,* Qinxet tattoxito iye-
[c2-l039 note] Completes limaduras and begins the example 'although gold dust is precious'. Two adjacent t types are visible in tattoxito, correcting OCR tatoxito; iyedomo is divided across the physical rows.
[c2-l040 >] domo, manaconi itte yeito naru. *O ou-*
[c2-l040 note] The example continues: when gold dust enters the eye, it becomes an obstruction to sight. The Portuguese translation starts O ouro, divided after ou-.
[c2-l041 >] *ro em pò poſtoque he muito estimado, toda-*
[c2-l041 note] Completes ouro and states the concession: gold in powder form is highly valued. Todavia is divided at the edge as the translation turns to the contrasting consequence.
[c2-l042 >] *uia caindo nos olhos cega, ou faz ſe neuoa dos*
[c2-l042 note] The dictionary's comparison says that entering the eyes nevertheless blinds or becomes a mist over them. The historical spelling neuoa and the printed separation faz ſe are preserved.
[c2-l043 >] *olhos.*
[c2-l043 note] Olhos completes the phrase about a mist over the eyes. This is the last continuation of the gold-dust example.
[c2-l044] Qinxi. Coganeno ito. *Fio douro.*
[c2-l044 note] Coganeno ito identifies gold thread. This Qinxi entry concerns material, not the prohibition sense of the next homograph.
[c2-l045] Qinxi. Imaxime todomuru. *Prohibição.*
[c2-l045 note] The Japanese explanation combines restraining and stopping, matching prohibition. The opening letter is a damaged Q, not the OCR G.
[c2-l046 >] *¶* Qinxi ſuru. *Prohibir.*
[c2-l046 note] Qinxi ſuru is the verbal example, 'to prohibit'. The paragraph mark introduces the example within the same entry.
[c2-l047] Qinxin. Chicaqi xinca. *Criado proprio do*
[c2-l047 note] Chicaqi xinca describes a close retainer. The weak initial retains the tail of Q, correcting OCR C; the Portuguese specifies whose personal servant is meant on the next row.
[c2-l048 >] *Rey.*
[c2-l048 note] Rey completes 'the king's personal servant'. The separate Ddd signature and Qinxit catchword formerly attached by OCR have been moved into furniture zones, not discarded or counted as body text.
