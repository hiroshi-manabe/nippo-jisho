## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Iinbasǒ. *Erua aßi chamada do mar.*
[c1-l001 note] Jinbasoo is identified only as a named marine plant; the Portuguese does not establish a botanical species. The opening capital is damaged, while the final caron is visible.
[c1-l002] Iinben. *Couſa rara, & marauilhoſa. Vt,*
[c1-l002 note] Jinben denotes something rare and marvellous. Italic Vt introduces the example of manifesting such wonders on the following row.
[c1-l003 >] Iinbenuo guenzuru. *Fazer milagres, ou*
[c1-l003 note] Jinben wo genzuru means performing or manifesting wonders. The Portuguese begins the paired gloss “perform miracles or wonders.”
[c1-l004 >] *marauilhas.*
[c1-l004 note] Marauilhas completes the preceding alternative to milagres; this short Portuguese continuation is a separate physical row.
[c1-l005] Iinbenna. *Couſa marauilhoſa.*
[c1-l005 note] Jinbenna is the adjectival form, directly glossed as marvellous. It supplies the semantic base for the two unglossed derivatives below.
[c1-l006 >] Iinbenni.
[c1-l006 note] Jinbenni is the adverbial derivative, “marvellously”; its indentation groups it with Jinbenna rather than with a new Portuguese definition.
[c1-l007 >] Iinbenſa.
[c1-l007 note] Jinbensa is the corresponding abstract noun, marvellousness. The visible long s belongs to the Japanese suffix, not to a Portuguese word.
[c1-l008] Iinbi. *Vt,* Iinbita fito. *Homem graue, mo-*
[c1-l008 note] Jinbita hito describes a mature, dignified person. The Portuguese begins “a grave, modest and cultivated man”; mo- continues into deſto below.
[c1-l009 >] *deſto, & politico.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes modeſto and adds politico, here describing cultivated manners rather than political activity.
[c1-l010] Iinbut. Fito mono. *Aſpecto, ou ſembran-*
[c1-l010 note] Jinbut is explained through hito and mono and then defined as a person’s appearance or countenance; ſembran- continues below.
[c1-l011 >] *te da peßoa. Vt,* Iinbutga yoi. *Bom pa-*
[c1-l011 note] Completes ſembrante da peßoa. Jinbut ga yoi illustrates an attractive appearance, with Portuguese parecer divided across the next boundary.
[c1-l012 >] *recer, ou fermoſura. ¶* Iinbutno yoi fi-
[c1-l012 note] Parecer and fermoſura express good looks or beauty. The next example begins jinbut no yoi hito, retaining its printed fi- division.
[c1-l013 >] to, l, jinbutxa. *Gentil homem, & bem*
[c1-l013 note] To completes hito. Latin l introduces jinbutxa as an alternative designation for a handsome, well-presented man.
[c1-l014 >] *aſombrado.*
[c1-l014 note] Aſombrado completes bem aſombrado, describing a pleasing appearance. The print has a single long s, which is not silently doubled.
[c1-l015] Iinbutxa. *Ordinariamente ſe toma na pratica*
[c1-l015 note] Jinbutxa receives a separate entry stating how the term is ordinarily used in conversation; the actual description follows below.
[c1-l016 >] *por homem de bom parecer, ou bem apeßoado.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes the conversational definition: a good-looking or well-proportioned man. The damaged first vowel of homem is o, not the OCR’s a.
[c1-l017] Iinca. Fitono iye. *Caſa onde habita gente.*
[c1-l017 note] Jinka is paraphrased as hito no iye, a house inhabited by people. The Japanese paraphrase remains upright.
[c1-l018] Iinchi. *Saber de homẽ aprimorado, & cortes.*
[c1-l018 note] Jinchi is the knowledge or understanding of a refined, courteous person. The Portuguese abbreviation homẽ is retained without expansion.
[c1-l019] Iincô. Fitono cuchi. *Bocas dos homẽs. Vt,*
[c1-l019 note] Jinkoo is literally people’s mouths; this first homograph introduces an idiom about being talked about. The circumflex is visible.
[c1-l020 >] Iincôni noru. *Andar nas bocas dos homẽs.*
[c1-l020 note] Jinkoo ni noru means circulating on people’s lips, exactly matching the Portuguese idiom andar nas bocas dos homens.
[c1-l021] Iincô. Fucaqu atçuxi. *Muito, ou grande.*
[c1-l021 note] The second Jinkoo is glossed fukaku atsushi, deeply abundant or great. It is a distinct sense, not a repetition of “mouths.”
[c1-l022 >] *¶* Govon jincôno itari. *Grandes, & mui*
[c1-l022 note] Govon jinkoo no itari expresses the exceptional magnitude of received favours. Mui continues as muitos below without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l023 >] *tos beneficios. S.*
[c1-l023 note] Tos completes muitos beneficios, many favours. The final upright S is the dictionary’s written-register label, not part of the italic gloss.
[c1-l024] Iincun. Fito qimi. *Senhor principal, ou Tono.*
[c1-l024 note] Jinkun, explained as hito kimi, denotes a principal lord. Tono is a Japanese title embedded within the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l025] Iindai. Fanafada fanafadaxij. *Grande cou-*
[c1-l025 note] Jindai is intensively great or extraordinary, as the repeated hanahada paraphrase explains. The scan preserves the initial I omitted by OCR.
[c1-l026 >] *ſa, & extraordinaria, ou profunda.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes grande couſa and adds extraordinary or profound; these are coordinated descriptions of Jindai, not new entries.
[c1-l027] Iindaigi. Tçucuru vôquina chi. *Toda a ter-*
[c1-l027 note] Jindaiji denotes the whole earth. The scan separates vôquina chi; retain printed Tçucuru rather than replacing it with a modern expected form.
[c1-l028 >] *ra, ou todo o mundo.*
[c1-l028 note] Ra completes terra, followed by “or the whole world.” This closes the geographical extent gloss of Jindaiji.
[c1-l029] Iindei. Fucaqi doro. *Atoleiro, ou lama alta.*
[c1-l029 note] Jindei is fukaki doro, deep mud. Atoleiro and lama alta identify a mire or deep muddy ground.
[c1-l030] Iinden. *Varzeas, ou terras dos Camis.*
[c1-l030 note] Jinden denotes fields or lands belonging to the kami. Camis is the Japanese religious term with a Portuguese plural ending inside the gloss.
[c1-l031] Iindô. Yaſaqi. *Certa laya de frechas de ponta*
[c1-l031 note] Jindoo is a kind of arrow; Yasaki is the supplied Japanese equivalent. The initial I omitted by OCR is visible, and the blunt point is described below.
[c1-l032 >] *romba com que tirão aos paſſaros.*
[c1-l032 note] The arrows have blunt points and are used to shoot birds. This completes the definition without adding a modern weapon classification.
[c1-l033] Iinen. *Per ſi, ou naturalmente.*
[c1-l033 note] Jinen is explained as occurring of itself or naturally. Per ſi retains the printed historical phrasing.
[c1-l034] Iingiǒ. i, axita tô. *Polamanhaã cedo.*
[c1-l034 note] Jingyoo is paraphrased as ashita too and means early in the morning. The final a of manhaã carries the tilde.
[c1-l035 >] *¶* Iingiǒno cane xǒjino nemuriuo ſamaſu.
[c1-l035 note] The morning bell awakens the sleep of birth-and-death. The initial mark is a paragraph sign before Iingiǒno, not the OCR’s A.
[c1-l036 >] Feiq. *Lib. 4. O ſino que ſe tange antema*
[c1-l036 note] Feiq. supplies the literary citation; the book number is visibly 4, not a paragraph sign. The translation begins with the bell sounded before dawn.
[c1-l037 >] *nhaã faz eſpertar a lembrança da morte.*
[c1-l037 note] Nhaã completes antemanhaã without an added hyphen above. The Portuguese interprets awakening as recalling death; preserve that interpretation rather than rewriting it from the Japanese.
[c1-l038] Iingiǔ. Fucǒ caſanaru. *Grãde a crecentamento,*
[c1-l038 note] Jingyuu is glossed as deep accumulation or increase. The first letter is I, not F; retain the printed separated a crecentamento and damaged final vowel with caron.
[c1-l039 >] *ou acumulação.*
[c1-l039 note] Ou acumulação provides the alternative “or accumulation,” completing the preceding increase gloss.
[c1-l040] Iingo. Fito cataru. *Pratica dos homẽs. Vt,*
[c1-l040 note] Jingo, paraphrased through human speaking, is people’s discourse. The final letter of homẽs is s, not the OCR’s t.
[c1-l041 >] Vǒmu jingouo manabuni nitari. *He co-*
[c1-l041 note] The example compares something to a parrot learning human speech. The Japanese Vǒmu and Portuguese He co- retain their physical forms and division.
[c1-l042 >] *mo papagayo, que fala como homem mas não*
[c1-l042 note] Completes como and describes the parrot as speaking like a person, but introduces the negative qualification continued below.
[c1-l043 >] *entende o que diz.*
[c1-l043 note] Entende o que diz completes “does not understand what it says,” distinguishing imitation of speech from understanding.
[c1-l044] Iingo. *Termo, ou fim.*
[c1-l044 note] The second Jingo is independently glossed as a limit or end; it must not be merged with the preceding speech entry.
[c1-l045] Iingû. Cami Miya. *Hermida de Cami.*
[c1-l045 note] Jinguu, explained as kami miya, is a kami shrine. The printed circumflex over u is retained; the final Cami occurs inside Portuguese.
[c1-l046] Iingui. *Duas virtudes moraes, & politicas q̃*
[c1-l046 note] Jingi introduces two moral and social virtues. The scan’s initial I corrects OCR F, and q̃ is an unexpanded abbreviation.
[c1-l047 >] *os Iapoẽs, & Chinas muito estimão, que ſão*
[c1-l047 note] The virtues are said to be highly esteemed by Japanese and Chinese people. Their identification continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l006] Iingui. *Vt,* Sanxuno jingui. *Tres layas*

[c2-l007 >] *de couſas precioſas, ou tres peças ricas do Cami.*
[c2-l007 note] The treasures are described as three kinds of precious objects or rich pieces belonging to the kami; no individual objects are enumerated.
[c2-l008] Iinguiquan. *Certa dignidade de Cũgues q̃ tem*
[c2-l008 note] Jingikan is a court office or dignity. The initial Ii sequence is visible; Cũgues names court nobles within the Portuguese, which continues below.
[c2-l009 >] *ſuperintendencia ſobre as couſas dos Camis.*
[c2-l009 note] The office supervises affairs of the kami. This relative clause completes the preceding dignity definition.
[c2-l010] Iinho. *Segunda filha.*
[c2-l010 note] Iinho is explicitly glossed “second daughter.” The printed nh spelling is retained rather than recast as a modern kinship term.
[c2-l011] Iinja. Camino yaxiro. *Caſa, ou ermida dos*
[c2-l011 note] Jinja is paraphrased as kami no yashiro, a shrine. The initial I lost by OCR is visible; the Portuguese complement follows below.
[c2-l012 >] *Camis.*
[c2-l012 note] Camis completes “house or shrine of the kami.” This one-word body continuation remains separate from the following headword.
[c2-l013] Iinji. Camino coto. *Ceremonias, & festas*
[c2-l013 note] Jinji denotes ceremonies and festivals of the kami. The initial is I rather than F, and the gloss ends festas rather than OCR festis.
[c2-l014 >] *dos Camis.*
[c2-l014 note] Dos Camis completes the ritual definition. Camis is part of the Portuguese clause, not a standalone Japanese synonym.
[c2-l015] Iinjin. Fucaqu fanafadaxij. *Couſa grande,*
[c2-l015 note] Jinjin expresses exceptional depth or magnitude. The scan supplies initial Ii and a comma after grande, connecting the following alternative.
[c2-l016 >] *ou profunda. Vt,* Iinjinno chijn. *Grã-*
[c2-l016 note] Jinjin no chijin illustrates deep friendship. The visible ending is chijin, not chiju; Grã- begins the Portuguese “great friend.”
[c2-l017 >] *de amigo. ¶* Iinjinno govon. *Gran-*
[c2-l017 note] Completes grande amigo and begins jinjin no govon, great favours received from a respected person. Gran- continues below.
[c2-l018 >] *des beneficios recebidos de peßoa alta.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes grandes beneficios and specifies that they are received from a person of high standing.
[c2-l019] Iinjit. *Septimo dia dos primeiros do Xǒguachi.*
[c2-l019 note] Jinjit names the seventh day of the first month. The mark in the embedded Xǒguachi is a caron, not the OCR’s tilde.
[c2-l020] Iinin. Vtçucuximu, xinobu. *Piedade, &*
[c2-l020 note] Jinin is paraphrased through compassion and endurance, with Portuguese piedade beginning the paired abstract gloss.
[c2-l021 >] *paciencia. S.*
[c2-l021 note] Paciencia supplies the second virtue, patience. The final upright S labels written usage and is distinct from the italic gloss.
[c2-l022] Iinjǒ. *Policia, modeſtia, grauidade, &c.*
[c2-l022 note] Jinjoo is decorum, modesty and gravity. Policia here concerns polished conduct rather than a modern police institution.
[c2-l023] Iinjǒna. *Couſa politica, graue, modeſta. ¶* Iin
[c2-l023 note] Jinjoona is the corresponding adjective, refined and modest. The example starts Iin at the edge without a supplied hyphen.
[c2-l024 >] jǒna fito. *Homem modeſto, & honeſto em*
[c2-l024 note] Completes jinjoona hito, a modest and honourable person. The scan supports initial h in honeſto, not OCR b; the description continues below.
[c2-l025 >] *ſuas acçoens.*
[c2-l025 note] Suas acçoens completes the description of a person who is modest and honourable in his actions.
[c2-l026 >] Iinjǒni.
[c2-l026 note] Jinjooni is the adverbial derivative, referring to acting with decorum. Its printed indentation connects it with Jinjoona.
[c2-l027 >] Iinjǒſa.
[c2-l027 note] Jinjoosa is the abstract noun for the corresponding decorum or modesty; the caron and long s are preserved.
[c2-l028] Iinme. Camino vma. *Caualo do Cami.*
[c2-l028 note] Jinme, kami no uma, is a horse belonging to a kami. The final Cami is embedded in the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l029] Iinmet. Tçuqi meſſuru. *Total deſtruição. S.*
[c2-l029 note] Jinmet denotes complete destruction, paraphrased through exhaustion and perishing. The final upright S marks written usage.
[c2-l030] Iinmiacu. *Pulſo que reſponde aos rins.*
[c2-l030 note] Jinmiaku is the pulse associated with the kidneys in the dictionary’s medical terminology. The gloss reports that association without anatomical elaboration.
[c2-l031] Iinmiraiſai. *Eternidade, ou tempo que eſtà por*
[c2-l031 note] Jinmiraisai denotes eternity or limitless future time. The Portuguese relative clause continues below; the edge context confirms por is complete.
[c2-l032 >] *vir infinito. S.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes “infinite time to come.” Upright S is the written-register label rather than part of the italic phrase.
[c2-l033] Iinmit. *Couſa muito ſecreta. S.*
[c2-l033 note] Jinmit is something very secret. The following upright S is a usage label, not an additional word of the definition.
[c2-l034] Iinmǒ. Fitono nozomi. *Deſejo dos ho-*
[c2-l034 note] Jinmoo is hito no nozomi, people’s desire. The Portuguese noun homens divides at the visible ho- ending.
[c2-l035 >] *mens. S.*
[c2-l035 note] Mens completes homens, closing the collective-desire gloss; the final S is upright.
[c2-l036] Iinmon. Fitono cado. *Porta, ou fronteſpi-*
[c2-l036 note] Jinmon is hito no kado, a person’s gate or house-front. The first character is I, not long s; fronteſpi- continues below.
[c2-l037 >] *cio da caſa de alguem.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes fronteſpicio da caſa de alguem, the front of someone’s house. The word remains divided at the physical line break.
[c2-l038] Iinmot. *Homens, & as de mais couſas do*
[c2-l038 note] Jinmot collectively denotes people and the other things of the world. Preserve the printed separated de mais.
[c2-l039 >] *mundo.*
[c2-l039 note] Mundo completes the preceding expression “of the world”; this is a genuine one-word body continuation.
[c2-l040] Iin mufen. *Immenſo. S.*
[c2-l040 note] Jin muhen is boundlessness or immensity. The scan retains a space after Iin and an f in mufen; S is upright.
[c2-l041] Iinmuriǒ. *Idem.*
[c2-l041 note] Jinmuryoo is equated with the preceding immensity gloss. The initial is a descending lowercase j, not OCR r; its lowercase form is retained.
[c2-l042] Iinnin. Camino fito. *Homẽs q̃ ſeruẽ aos Camis.*
[c2-l042 note] Jinnin, kami no hito, denotes people serving the kami. The final preposition is aos, not OCR aoe, and both abbreviation tildes remain unexpanded.
[c2-l043] Iinno fu. i, Fǒquǒ. *Bixiga.*
[c2-l043 note] Jin no fu is equated with hookoo and glossed “bladder.” Restore the scan’s initial I and retain the historical Portuguese Bixiga.
[c2-l044] Iinpan. *Homem de varias cores. i*, *Homem*
[c2-l044 note] Jinpan begins with the figurative image of a many-coloured man and then clarifies it as a malicious person. The explanatory i has a comma.
[c2-l045 >] *malicioſo. Vt,* Cofanua miyaſuqu, jin-
[c2-l045 note] The proverb contrasts a tiger’s visible markings with human duplicity. Its second compound jin-pan is divided across this row and the next.
[c2-l046 >] panua miyegataxi. Xix. *O tigre malha*
[c2-l046 note] Completes jinpan wa miegatashi, human duplicity being hard to discern. Xix. is an upright citation label; the Portuguese begins “the spotted tiger.”
[c2-l047 >] *do logo ſe deixa ver, mas o homem malicioſo não*
[c2-l047 note] Do completes malhado. The tiger is readily visible, but the malicious person is not understood; the edge shows full não without a hyphen.
[c2-l047a >] *ha quẽ o entenda.*
[c2-l047a note] Completes não ha quẽ o entenda: no one understands the malicious person. OCR grouped this body continuation with the separate catchword Iinpi.; both are now represented separately.
