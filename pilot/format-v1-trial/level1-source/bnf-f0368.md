[c1-l001] Niyeiri, iru, itta. *Deixar a agoa de ſoar, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Niyeiri describes boiling water ceasing to sound, rather than simply ceasing to boil. The Japanese principal parts and Portuguese ſoar agree with the following reference to noise.
[c1-l002 >] *fazer eſtrondo cõ a feruura.*
[c1-l002 note] This completes the noise made by boiling: the scope of Deixar above includes making a din. The tilde in cõ is kept as an abbreviation, not expanded.
[c1-l003] Niyeyu. *Agoa que ferue.*
[c1-l003 note] Niyeyu is water that is boiling. Its yu element is part of the upright headword; agoa retains the source's spelling.
[c1-l004] Niyetaguiri, u, itta. *Feruer muito, ou fer-*
[c1-l004 note] Niyetaguiri means boiling vigorously. The guiri spelling and the printed inflectional u are retained; fer- continues the Portuguese verb below.
[c1-l005 >] *uer em burbulhoẽs.*
[c1-l005 note] This completes fer-/uer and explains the vigorous boiling as bubbling. The historical burbulhoẽs and its tilde on e are preserved.
[c1-l006] Niyoi, ô, ôta. *Gemer.*
[c1-l006 note] This Niyoi is the verb glossed 'to groan.' Both principal-part marks are circumflexes; the spelling is not adjusted to a different modern verb from the sound alone.
[c1-l007] Niyotte. *Particula que junta com outras pala-*
[c1-l007 note] Niyotte is described as a connective particle used with other words. The physical pala- ending belongs to palauras, not the Japanese entry.
[c1-l008 >] *uras quer dizer porque, ou por quanto.*
[c1-l008 note] The particle's function is explained as 'because' or 'inasmuch as.' This ends the NI material before the separate NO section heading.
