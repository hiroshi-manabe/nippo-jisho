[c1-l001] Qefô. *Ley, ou cuſtume que corre por decen-*
[c1-l001 note] Kehō denotes a family's inherited rule or custom. The final letter of Ley is y, not the OCR's x; the Portuguese word for descent begins here and continues below.
[c1-l002 >] *dencia em algũa caſa, ou familia.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes decendencia: the rule is transmitted within a house or family. The printed historical spelling is retained rather than modernized to descendência.
[c1-l003] Qega. *Erro, ou desaſtre. ¶* Qegano
[c1-l003 note] Kega is glossed as an error or mishap. The large initial is Q, not D; Qegano begins the expression for an unexpectedly fortunate result of a mishap.
[c1-l004 >] cǒmiǒ de gozaru. Mon. *He façanha, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Kega no kōmyō de gozaru describes an accidental achievement or lucky success. Mon. remains the printed citation label; the Portuguese explanation continues on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] *feito do deſaſtre: vſaſe nas couſas que a cer-*
[c1-l005 note] The gloss explains an achievement arising from a mishap, used of things that happen to turn out well. Preserve the printed separation a cer- and its physical continuation.
[c1-l006 >] *tão de ſair bem, & milhoradas por algum ca-*
[c1-l006 note] Completes acertão and specifies a good or improved outcome brought about by some chance event. Milhoradas is the printed historical vowel form; ca- continues as caso.
[c1-l007 >] *ſo. ¶* Qegauo ſuru. l, caqu. *A cõ-*
[c1-l007 note] After completing caso, the entry gives kega wo suru or kaku, suffering a mishap. The spaced Portuguese A cõ- begins acontecer across the line break; l marks an alternative.
[c1-l008 >] *tecer deſaſtre, ou couſa que redunda em dano,*
[c1-l008 note] Completes acontecer and describes an event resulting in harm. The list of consequences continues below, so this is not an independent entry.
[c1-l009 >] *vergonha, &c.*
[c1-l009 note] Shame completes the preceding list of harm caused by a mishap. This short continuation belongs to Qega and must remain a separate physical row.
[c1-l010] Qegarauaxij. *Couſa immunda.*
[c1-l010 note] Kegarawashii means unclean or defiling, directly matching immunda. The following two indented rows give related forms rather than new Portuguese definitions.
[c1-l011 >] Qegarauaxiſa.
[c1-l011 note] Kegarawashisa is the nominal form, uncleanness or impurity, derived from the preceding adjective. The long s belongs to the suffix and is not f.
[c1-l012 >] Qegarauaxǔ.
[c1-l012 note] Kegarawashū is the contracted adverbial form associated with kegarawashii. The final vowel visibly carries a caron; no missing gloss is supplied.
[c1-l013] Qegare, ruru, eta. *Sujarſe. Vt,* Chiuo fu-
[c1-l013 note] Kegare, kegaruru, kegareta gives the intransitive verb for becoming soiled. The example starts chi wo fukunde, with fu- continuing on the next physical row.
[c1-l014 >] cunde fitoni faqeba, mazzu ſono cuchi
[c1-l014 note] The example says that when one spits blood at another person, one's own mouth is affected first. Fukunde and hakeba are coherent verb forms; their printed spelling remains unchanged.
[c1-l015 >] qegaru. *O que tendo ſangue na boca coſpe*
[c1-l015 note] Kegaru completes the statement that one's mouth becomes defiled. The Portuguese translates someone holding blood in the mouth and spitting; its object follows below.
[c1-l016 >] *no outro, elle he o q̃ fica primeiro com ſangue cõ*
[c1-l016 note] The person spitting at another is himself first contaminated with blood. Both abbreviation tildes are retained, including the unhyphenated line-final cõ before taminado.
[c1-l017 >] *taminado. Acommodaſe ao que murmura de*
[c1-l017 note] Completes contaminado and explains the proverb's figurative application to someone who speaks ill of others. The Portuguese verb is murmura, not a Japanese example.
[c1-l018 >] *outros.*
[c1-l018 note] Others completes the explanation of slander: the slanderer stains himself first. The short final word remains on its own physical row.
[c1-l019] Qegare. *Sujidade, ou immundicia.*
[c1-l019 note] Kegare is the noun for dirtiness or impurity, distinct from the preceding inflected verb. The paired Portuguese nouns directly explain this nominal sense.
[c1-l020] Qegarifa. *Gallo, ou galinha que tem penas*
[c1-l020 note] Qegarifa names a cock or hen with feathered feet. The Portuguese capital has the cross-stroke of Gallo, not OCR Callo; the Japanese spelling is retained as printed.
[c1-l021 >] *nos pees atè as vnhas.*
[c1-l021 note] The feathers extend along the feet as far as the claws. Pees and vnhas retain the source's spelling, and the accent in atè is not modernized.
[c1-l022] Qegaxi, ſu, aita. *Sujar. Vt,*
[c1-l022 note] Kegashi, kegasu, kegaita gives the transitive verb for soiling. The historical -aita principal form is retained; it introduces the verse on the following two rows.
[c1-l023 >] Miuagauano qiyoqi nagareni ſuſuguitexi,
[c1-l023 note] The verse recalls washing in the clear current of the Miwa River. Susugiteshi expresses the past washing; the following row supplies the garment's sleeves and a vow not to soil them again.
[c1-l024 >] coromono ſǒdeuo mataua qegaſaji.
[c1-l024 note] The speaker will not again soil the sleeves of the robe. The small mark over the o of ſǒdeuo is retained as supplied and visible, despite the ordinary lexical reading sode; it is not silently regularized.
[c1-l025 >] Fox. *Lib. 1. Tendo lauado as mãgas de meus*
[c1-l025 note] Fox. identifies the quoted source, followed by book 1. The Portuguese translates the washing of the speaker's sleeves; mãgas retains its abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l026 >] *veſtidos neſte rio não as tornarei mais a ſujar.*
[c1-l026 note] The translation promises not to soil those garments again after washing them in this river. This completes the literal interpretation of the preceding verse.
[c1-l027 >] *Entẽdeſe cõ as couſas do mũdo q̃ tinha deixado.*
[c1-l027 note] The editor explains that the verse concerns worldly things the speaker had abandoned. The four printed abbreviation tildes remain unexpanded in the canonical text.
[c1-l028 >] *¶* Nauo qegaſu. *Por nodoa em ſua fama*
[c1-l028 note] Na wo kegasu means staining one's name or reputation. The Portuguese phrase Por nodoa means placing a stain; no modern accent is supplied to the infinitive.
[c1-l029 >] *ou nome. ¶* Facumauo qegaſu. *S.*
[c1-l029 note] After completing the name-and-reputation gloss, Facuma wo kegasu introduces a written-register expression for defiling paper. Its explanation continues on the next two rows. Second-pass enlargement confirms the separate upright S. label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l030 >] *Sujar o papel com ſua ruim letra. He modo de*
[c1-l030 note] The expression depreciates one's own handwriting as soiling the paper. The Portuguese explicitly presents it as a manner of speaking, continued below.
[c1-l031 >] *falar com humildade nas cartas.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the explanation: this is a humble formula used in letters, rather than a literal statement that paper has become dirty.
[c1-l032] Qego. Tauabure cataru. *O falar não de pro*
[c1-l032 note] Kego is explained by tawabure kataru, speaking playfully rather than seriously. The headword begins with Q, not OCR D; pro continues below without a printed hyphen.
[c1-l033 >] *poſito mas como dezombaria, ou por paßa-*
[c1-l033 note] Completes proposito and contrasts serious intention with joking or passing the time. Dezombaria retains the printed joined spelling, and paßa- continues into tempo.
[c1-l034 >] *tempo. ¶* Qego, qeron. *Pratica, ou*
[c1-l034 note] After completing passatempo, the paired terms kego and keron denote conversation or disputation of this joking kind. The Portuguese definition continues below.
[c1-l035 >] *disputa deſta laya. S.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes the description of playful conversation or argument. The final S is the written-register label, not part of the Japanese synonym. Second-pass enlargement confirms the separate upright S. label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l036] Qegomi, iru, ita. *Indo diante ver, & no-*
[c1-l036 note] The verb denotes going ahead to inspect or reconnoitre. No- begins notar, and the extended explanation specifies both military scouts and a thief's preliminary inspection.
[c1-l037 >] *tar, ou eſpiar como ſoldados que vão diante*
[c1-l037 note] Completes notar and compares the action with soldiers scouting in advance. The relative clause remains connected to the following account of surveying terrain.
[c1-l038 >] *a descubrir o campo, ou ver a feição da forta-*
[c1-l038 note] The scouts examine the field or the configuration of a fortress. Descubrir preserves the source's vowel; forta- continues as fortaleza on the next row.
[c1-l039 >] *leza, &c. ou como o ladrão que nota primeiro*
[c1-l039 note] Completes fortaleza and adds the comparison with a thief examining premises beforehand. A visible separation after &c. is retained rather than joining it to ou.
[c1-l040 >] *os cantos da caſa, & o fato que ha, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] The thief notes the corners of the house and the goods present. Fato here belongs to the list of property being inspected, completing the reconnaissance explanation.
[c1-l041] Qegomi. *Eſpia que vai ver, & notar eſtas*
[c1-l041 note] The noun denotes the scout or spy who performs the inspection just described. It is a separate entry following the related verb.
[c1-l042 >] *couſas.*
[c1-l042 note] Things completes the scout's definition by referring back to the terrain, fortress, house, and goods described above.
[c1-l043] Qegon. *Hum dos liuros que compos Xaca.*
[c1-l043 note] Kegon names a Buddhist book, which the gloss attributes to Xaca. That attribution is part of the historical dictionary's wording; Xaca is a Japanese name embedded in Portuguese.
[c1-l044 >] *¶ Item, Hũa ſeita das oito que ha em Ia-*
[c1-l044 note] The second sense identifies Kegon as one of eight sects said to exist in Japan. Ia- continues as the Portuguese place-name Iapão below.
[c1-l045 >] *pão. ¶* Qegonjǔ. *Os deſta ſeita.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes Iapão and gives Kegonjū for members of the sect. The final vowel has a caron, and the Japanese term is an example label rather than embedded Portuguese prose.
[c1-l046] Qegon. i, Qego. *Vide ſuprà. Vt,* Sore
[c1-l046 note] This second Qegon entry refers back to Qego, joking speech, not the Buddhist sect. Sore begins the example whose remainder crosses the column boundary.
[c1-l047 >] cunxiua qegon xezu. *O homem ſabio, &*
[c1-l047 note] The maxim says that a gentleman does not speak frivolously. The Portuguese starts 'the wise and' and is completed by 'prudent' at the top of column 2.

[c2-l006] Qegime. *Couſa contra feita, ou dißimulação.*
[c2-l006 note] The dictionary explains kegime through a feigned thing or dissimulation. Enlargement establishes the ß ligature and cedilla in dißimulação, replacing the garbled OCR ditimulacão.
[c2-l007 >] *¶* Qegimega miyeta. *Apareceo, ou des*
[c2-l007 note] Kegime ga miyeta describes the underlying truth becoming apparent. The printed paragraph mark before the example was omitted by OCR and is restored; des continues below without a hyphen.
[c2-l008 >] *cobrioſe a verdade da couſa paleada.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes descobriose: the truth of the disguised or glossed-over matter was revealed. Paleada is retained as printed, rather than replaced with a modern synonym.
[c2-l009] Qei. *Viſta. Vt,* Tocorono qeiga yoi. *A*
[c2-l009 note] Kei means a view or prospect. Tokoro no kei ga yoi says the place has a good view; its Portuguese translation begins with the final article A.
[c2-l010 >] *viſta do lugar he boa.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes the example as 'the view of the place is good.' The clause is the translation of the preceding Japanese, not a separate sense.
[c2-l011] Qei. *Caſtigo, ou juſtiça. Vt,* Qeino vta-
[c2-l011 note] A different kei means punishment or the execution of justice. The maxim begins kei no utagawashiki, a doubtful matter concerning punishment, and continues below.
[c2-l012 >] gauaxiqiuoba vomonjeyo, côno vtaga-
[c2-l012 note] The maxim says to weigh doubtful punishment carefully, then contrasts a doubtful claim of merit or service. The printed circumflex in côno is retained.
[c2-l013 >] uaxiqiuoba caronjeyo. *Quando ouuer*
[c2-l013 note] Caronjeyo completes the contrasting instruction about doubtful merit. The Portuguese introduces the conditions under which the maxim applies rather than translating each word mechanically.
[c2-l014 >] *duuida ſobre caſtigar, ou matar alguem, exa-*
[c2-l014 note] When there is doubt about punishing or killing someone, examination is required. Exa- begins examina, which is completed on the next physical row.
[c2-l015 >] *mina, & pondera bem: quando ouuer duuida*
[c2-l015 note] Completes the command to examine and weigh matters carefully, then introduces doubt about a service. The scan has duuida, including the u omitted by OCR.
[c2-l016 >] *ſe he ſeruiço dalgũ vaſalo não faças tanto ca-*
[c2-l016 note] The second case concerns whether a vassal has rendered service: the reader is told not to scrutinize it so heavily. Ca- continues into caso below.
[c2-l017 >] *ſo ainda que logo o julgues por ſeruiço.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes caso and allows the deed to be accepted promptly as service. This closes the contrast between caution in punishment and generosity in recognizing merit.
[c2-l018] Qeiba. *Muitos caualos juntos de dous em do-*
[c2-l018 note] Keiba is described as horses brought together in pairs for racing. Dous is divided across the physical line ending and must not be joined in the transcription.
[c2-l019 >] *us pera correr à profia como ſe faz no Miaco*
[c2-l019 note] Completes dous and explains racing in competition, as practised in Miaco. Miaco is a Japanese place-name occurring inside the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l020 >] *em certo tempo. ¶* Camono qeibauo
[c2-l020 note] The races take place at a particular time. Kamo no keiba wo begins the example about observing the races at Kamo, completed by miru below.
[c2-l021 >] miru. *Ver eſta junta de caualos pera correr*
[c2-l021 note] Miru completes the example, seeing the assembly of horses for racing. The Portuguese translation specifies the location on the following row.
[c2-l022 >] *da poſta na quelle lugar Camo.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes the description of horse-racing at Camo. The printed na quelle separation is retained, and Camo is a Japanese name within Portuguese prose.
[c2-l023] Qeibat. *O matar por iuſtiça. Vt,* Qeibat
[c2-l023 note] Keibat denotes execution by judicial authority. The bare final t is retained in both headword and example; the example's verb follows below.
[c2-l024 >] ſuru.
[c2-l024 note] Suru completes keibat suru, carrying out a judicial execution. This short Japanese continuation is a body row, not a catchword.
[c2-l025] Qeiben. *Vara, ou rota com que açoutão na*
[c2-l025 note] Keiben is a rod or cane used for judicial whipping. The Portuguese location is completed by China on the next physical row.
[c2-l026 >] *China por juſtiça.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes the definition as an instrument of judicial punishment in China. This is part of the dictionary's description, not an additional headword.
[c2-l027] Qeibi. *Couſa pouca.*
[c2-l027 note] Keibi denotes something slight or of small quantity. The following adjectival and adverbial forms belong to this same semantic group.
[c2-l028] Qeibina. *Idem.*
[c2-l028 note] Keibina is the adjectival form, with Idem referring to the preceding smallness sense. The initial Q is upright roman type, not the OCR's italic O.
[c2-l029] Qeibini. *Adu.* Qeibini gozaredomo. *Pos-*
[c2-l029 note] Keibini is marked as an adverb; the example concedes that something is slight. The Portuguese begins Posto, with visible s rather than the OCR's r in the line-final fragment.
[c2-l030 >] *to que ſeja pouquidade.*
[c2-l030 note] Completes Posto que seja pouquidade, 'although it be a small amount.' It explains the concessive gozaredomo rather than giving a new entry.
[c2-l031] Qeibo. i, Mamafaua. *Madraſta.*
[c2-l031 note] Keibo is equated with Mamafaua and glossed as stepmother. The initial Q is restored to roman type; the unusual internal spelling of the Japanese synonym is preserved.
[c2-l032] Qeibǒ. *Deſejo importuno com que hum por*
[c2-l032 note] Keibō denotes an importunate desire to obtain something. The caron distinguishes its printed form from the preceding unaccented keibo, stepmother.
[c2-l033 >] *todas as vias quer auer algũa couſa, ou toma-*
[c2-l033 note] The desire pursues possession by every means, even taking the object. Auer is the historical spelling, and toma- is physically divided before the following pronoun.
[c2-l034 >] *la por força. Vt,* Qeibǒ ſuru. *De ſejar*
[c2-l034 note] Completes taking the thing by force, then gives keibō suru. The visible separation De ſejar is retained rather than silently joining the Portuguese infinitive.
[c2-l035 >] *deſta maneira.*
[c2-l035 note] Completes the example's gloss as desiring in the manner just described, namely insistently and potentially by force.
[c2-l036] Qeibut. *Fruita, ou comer que ha em certo,*
[c2-l036 note] Keibut refers to seasonal fruit or food. The printed final t and historical Fruita remain unchanged; the seasonal restriction is developed below.
[c2-l037 >] *& determinado tempo do anno como peras, &*
[c2-l037 note] The food belongs to a fixed time of year, with pears as the first example. This continues the seasonal definition rather than introducing an unrelated list.
[c2-l038 >] *vuas em Setembro, &c. ¶* Toqino qei-
[c2-l038 note] Grapes in September complete the examples. Toki no keibut begins the phrase about assembling seasonal delicacies for hospitality, split after qei-.
[c2-l039 >] butuo ſoroyete motenaſu. *Ajuntando*
[c2-l039 note] The Japanese phrase means entertaining guests with a selection of seasonal foods. The final t before uo is preserved rather than expanded to a modern open syllable.
[c2-l040 >] *todas as fruitas, & iguarias de todos os tem-*
[c2-l040 note] The Portuguese describes gathering fruits and delicacies from the various seasons. Tem- continues as tempos; no text is missing at the column edge.
[c2-l041 >] *pos do anno agaſalhar, & conuidar.*
[c2-l041 note] Completes tempos do anno and the act of welcoming and entertaining guests. The seasonal-food example ends here.
[c2-l042] Qeichô. Niuatori. *Gallo, ou galinha. S.*
[c2-l042 note] Keichō is equated with niwatori and glossed as a cock or hen. The circumflex and the written-register label are preserved. Second-pass enlargement confirms the separate upright S. label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l043] Qeico. *Exercicio, ou enſayo de couſas que hum*
[c2-l043 note] Keiko means practice or rehearsal of things already learned. The relative clause continues below and makes the acquired-skill sense explicit.
[c2-l044 >] *tem aprendido. Vt,* Qeico ſuru.
[c2-l044 note] Completes the practice definition and gives keiko suru, to practise. The Japanese example remains roman, distinct from the italic Portuguese introduction.
[c2-l045] Qeicocu. Cuni catamuqu. I*r ſe o reino deſ-*
[c2-l045 note] Keikoku is explained by kuni katamuku, a kingdom falling into ruin. The Portuguese verb is divided after deſ- at the physical line ending.
[c2-l046 >] *truindo. S.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes destruindo and marks the expression as written usage. This short row belongs to the political sense, not the following homograph. Second-pass enlargement confirms the separate upright S. label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l047] Qeicocu. Tani, tani. *Valles. S.*
[c2-l047 note] A separate keikoku means valleys, explained by the repeated tani. The lower-right Qeidacu is a catchword for the next page and is not part of this body row.
