[c1-l001] Gugo. i, Mexi. *Arroz cozido. He pala-*
[c1-l001 note] Gugo is equated with meshi, cooked rice. The gloss begins a usage restriction with He pala-; the preceding page's Gugo- is only its catchword and is not added to this headword.
[c1-l002 >] *ura de molheres.*
[c1-l002 note] Ura completes palaura, followed by de molheres. The dictionary describes this rice-word as women's vocabulary; that historical register attribution is retained.
[c1-l003] Gugoriǒ. *Renda que tem o Dairi particular*
[c1-l003 note] Gugoriǒ denotes the Dairi's specially assigned revenue for his table. Dairi is a Japanese title inside a Portuguese sentence, not an additional headword.
[c1-l004 >] *pera arroz de ſua meſa.*
[c1-l004 note] The revenue specifically supplies rice for the ruler's table. This completes the connection between Gugoriǒ and the preceding word for cooked rice.
[c1-l005] Guguan. *Ignorancia.*
[c1-l005 note] Guguan is glossed simply as ignorance. The guan spelling is retained without expanding or modernizing the historical syllable sequence.
[c1-l006] Gu-i. Vorocana cocoro. i, Vaga zonbun.
[c1-l006 note] Gu-i, literally a foolish mind or intention, is explained by waga zonbun, one's own opinion. The internal hyphen distinguishes separate gu and i syllables and must not be discarded as a line-end mark.
[c1-l007 >] *Meu parecer falando deſi humildemente.*
[c1-l007 note] The Portuguese makes Gu-i a humble expression for my opinion. Deſi remains the printed spelling and spacing; the meaning is self-deprecation rather than condemnation of another person's view.
[c1-l008] Gu ichi. *Cinco, & as nos dados.*
[c1-l008 note] Gu ichi names five and an ace on dice. Portuguese as denotes the one-pip face; this is a dice combination rather than the ordinary Japanese counting sequence.
[c1-l009] Gǔjen. Tamatama. *Raro.* S.
[c1-l009 note] Gǔjen is paired with tamatama and glossed raro, rarely or occasionally. S. marks written usage; the caron belongs over u, not over either vowel of the Japanese synonym.
[c1-l010] Gujǒ. i, Vaga fumi. *Minha carta falando*
[c1-l010 note] Gujǒ is waga fumi, my letter. The Portuguese begins the explanation of the humble speaking convention, which is completed below.
[c1-l011 >] *com humildade.*
[c1-l011 note] Com humildade completes the self-deprecating register note for Gujǒ. It is not a separate sense of carta.
[c1-l012] Gumai. Vorocani, curaxi. i, guchi. *Ig-*
[c1-l012 note] Gumai is explained by orokani, kurashi and the synonym guchi, ignorance. Ig- starts the Portuguese noun on the next row; the punctuation between the Japanese paraphrase and synonym is preserved.
[c1-l013 >] *norancia.*
[c1-l013 note] Norancia completes Ig-norancia. This is the continuation of Gumai's gloss and contains no omitted Japanese headword.
[c1-l014] Gumi. *Hũa certa fruita.*
[c1-l014 note] Gumi is described only as a certain fruit. The source's generic botanical statement is retained without importing a species name into either the transcription or the definition.
[c1-l015] Gumô. i, Guchi. *Ignorancia.*
[c1-l015 note] Gumô is another word for guchi, ignorance. The marked long o and the independent entry are retained despite the repeated Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l016] Gun. Icuſa. *Não ſe vſa perſi ſoo, ſe não cõ-*
[c1-l016 note] Gun is explained by Icuſa, warfare or an army. The damaged long-s shoulder appears detached above the following a; Icuſa, rather than the draft's Iculã, is the chosen reading from the surviving stem, shoulder and warfare context. This is a nonblocking glyph uncertainty. The following restriction says gun is used in compounds, not alone.
[c1-l017 >] *poſto. Vt,* Ichigun, nigun. *Certo nu-*
[c1-l017 note] Poſto completes cõ-poſto. Ichigun and nigun illustrate one and two military units, with the Portuguese explanation of their personnel continuing below.
[c1-l018 >] *mero de gente que tem hum eſquadrão, dous,*
[c1-l018 note] Mero completes numero: the gloss concerns the number of people in one squadron, two, and so on. Its wording does not prescribe a fixed troop strength.
[c1-l019 >] *&c.* ¶ Gunbiǒ. *Soldados.*
[c1-l019 note] After closing the numerical examples, Gunbiǒ is introduced with the gloss soldiers. It remains on the same physical row after a paragraph mark.
[c1-l020] Gun. Couori. *Hũa parte, ou comarca do rei-*
[c1-l020 note] The next Gun is the administrative district, paraphrased as kôri in the historical spelling Couori. Rei- begins reino in the Portuguese geographical definition.
[c1-l021 >] *no: não ſe vſa perſi ſe não.* Ichigun, ni-
[c1-l021 note] No completes reino. The entry again restricts use to combinations such as Ichigun and ni-gun; those forms are examples of counting districts.
[c1-l022 >] gun, *&c.*
[c1-l022 note] Gun completes nigun, followed by &c. The row is an example continuation, not a third independent Gun headword.
[c1-l023] Gunamecaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer bulir, ou me-*
[c1-l023 note] Gunamecaxi gives the transitive verb for making something move or stir. Its ſu and aita principal forms are preserved as historical morphology; me- begins menear below.
[c1-l024 >] *near algũa couſa como membro q̃ eſtá como mor*
[c1-l024 note] Near completes menear. The example is a limb that is almost lifeless or slack; the scan has no visible hyphen after mor, so the original unmarked division before tal is retained.
[c1-l025 >] *tal, froxo, &c.*
[c1-l025 note] Tal completes mortal from the unhyphenated preceding row; froxo adds slack or weak. These adjectives explain the sort of limb movement meant by Gunamecaxi.
[c1-l026] Gunameqi, u, eita. *Bulirſe, ou mouerſe de-*
[c1-l026 note] Gunameqi is the corresponding intransitive action, moving or stirring in that manner. The principal forms u and eita are retained without modernizing the inflection.
[c1-l027 >] *ſta maneira.* ¶ Gunagunato ſuru. *Idẽ.*
[c1-l027 note] The phrase Gunagunato ſuru gives the same action with an expressive adverb. The scan shows long s without an f crossbar, correcting furu; Idẽ abbreviates the repeated gloss.
[c1-l028] Gunbai. *Agoureiro da guerra, que diz o dia*
[c1-l028 note] Gunbai is described here as a military diviner who chooses an auspicious day. The source defines a person, not the modern familiar object sense of the commander's fan.
[c1-l029 >] *em que ſe ha de partir, dar batalha, &c.*
[c1-l029 note] The diviner chooses when to depart or fight a battle. This completes the role described under Gunbai, with the original &c. left unexpanded.
[c1-l030] Gunbiǒ. Icuſano tçuuamono. *Soldados, ou*
[c1-l030 note] Gunbiǒ is ikusa no tsuwamono, soldiers or warriors. Enlargement shows tçuuamono, without the extra no in the draft tçuuanomono; the Portuguese confirms the military-personnel sense.
[c1-l031 >] *gente de guerra.*
[c1-l031 note] Gente de guerra completes the gloss soldiers or fighting people. It is continuing Portuguese text rather than another Japanese synonym.
[c1-l032] Gunchô. Muragaru tori. *Bando de paſſaros.*
[c1-l032 note] Gunchô is a flock of birds, paraphrased as muragaru tori. The circumflex is visible in the headword; the revised crop also restores the full left edge of the capital G.
[c1-l033 >] *Vt,* Gunchô ſayezzuru coto mimini michi
[c1-l033 note] The example describes a flock's chirping filling the ears. The scan repeats Gunchô with a circumflex, not the draft's caron; michi continues into tari below.
[c1-l034 >] tari. *O bando de paſſaros faz grande traqui*
[c1-l034 note] Tari completes michitari, being filled. Portuguese traqui continues without a printed hyphen into nada; the absent terminal mark is not supplied merely because the word is divided.
[c1-l035 >] *nada nos ouuidos cantando.* S.
[c1-l035 note] Nada completes traquinada, the noisy bustle of singing birds in the ears. S. is the separate written-usage label, not part of the italic translation.
[c1-l036] Gunchǔ. Icuſano chǔxet. *Seruiço feito na*
[c1-l036 note] This Gunchǔ means loyal service in war, explained by ikusa no chǔxet. Final t is a checked ending in the Japanese synonym, not an incomplete Portuguese word.
[c1-l037 >] *guerra.* S.
[c1-l037 note] Guerra completes the war-service definition. The independent S. marks the expression as written usage and is kept outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l038] Gunchǔ. Icuſano naca. *Dentro na guerra, ou*
[c1-l038 note] A second Gunchǔ means within a war or battle, from ikusa no naka. It is a separate homophone rather than a continuation of the loyal-service meaning.
[c1-l039 >] *batalha.*
[c1-l039 note] Batalha completes the alternative Portuguese wording for war or battle. This short continuation belongs to the second Gunchǔ entry.
[c1-l040] Gunchǔ. Couorino naca. *Meo da comarca,*
[c1-l040 note] A third Gunchǔ is the interior of a district, paraphrased as kôri no naka in the printed Couorino form. The administrative sense of gun is distinct from the military sense above.
[c1-l041 >] *ou parte do reino.*
[c1-l041 note] Ou parte do reino completes the district description. The entry concerns location within an administrative region, not service rendered to it.
[c1-l042] Guncô. Icuſano chǔcô. *Seruiços feitos na*
[c1-l042 note] Guncô denotes services in war, explained by ikusa no chǔcô. Both final o marks are circumflexes in the scan; the draft's caron in chǔcǒ is corrected.
[c1-l043 >] *guerra.* S.
[c1-l043 note] Guerra completes the definition of military services. The following S. is the upright written-register label and remains distinct from the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l044] Guncocu. *Terras, & reinos. Vt,* Gunco-
[c1-l044 note] Guncocu denotes lands and kingdoms. The example begins Gunco-, with a visible terminal hyphen, and continues as the object of mauaru below.
[c1-l045 >] cuuo mauaru. *Correr muitas terras, &*
[c1-l045 note] Guncocu wo mawaru means travelling through many lands and kingdoms. The physical split Gunco-cuuo is retained rather than joining the original lines.
[c1-l046 >] *reinos.*
[c1-l046 note] Reinos completes the travel example's Portuguese list. This is a body continuation, not a column catchword.
[c1-l047] Gundai. Couorino dai. *Feitor dalgũa comar*
[c1-l047 note] Gundai is a district administrator, paraphrased as kôri no dai. The print ends at comar without a hyphen; the crop is widened leftward to include the whole headword and outer-rule context.
[c1-l048 >] *ca do reino.*
[c1-l048 note] Ca completes comarca from the preceding row, finishing the description of an administrator for a district of the kingdom. This is the last body row of column 1.

[c2-l001] Gundô. Muragari vgoqu. *Mouerſe muita*
[c2-l001 note] Gundô is collective movement, paraphrased as muragari ugoku. The final consonant in vgoqu is q, matching the historical spelling of ugoku rather than introducing another g.
[c2-l002 >] *gente junta.* S.
[c2-l002 note] Many people moving together completes the definition. S. is the separate written-usage label, not part of the italic phrase gente junta.
[c2-l003] Gunen. Motomuru. *Alcançar, ou buſcar*
[c2-l003 note] Gunen is glossed by motomuru, seeking or desiring to attain something. The Portuguese alternative buſcar takes its qualifying phrase on the next row.
[c2-l004 >] *com o deſejo.*
[c2-l004 note] Com o deſejo specifies pursuit in desire. This completes the definition rather than asserting that the desired object has already been obtained.
[c2-l005] Gungin. Icuſano gin. *Exercito, ou arrayal.*
[c2-l005 note] Gungin is ikusa no jin, an army or military camp. The spelling gin is retained as printed, rather than normalized to the neighboring Gunjin form with a different meaning.
[c2-l006] Gungǒ. Couorito gǒ. *Comarca, ou parte*
[c2-l006 note] Gungǒ is paraphrased as Couorito gǒ, kôri and gô. The scan's t replaces the draft's r; the following Portuguese distinguishes two levels of territorial division, supporting the conjunction to.
[c2-l007 >] *do reino que ſe chama,* Gun, *& outra parte ſome*
[c2-l007 note] The larger district is called Gun, a Japanese term visibly embedded in the Portuguese explanation. The row ends at ſome without a hyphen; the comparison with the smaller division continues below.
[c2-l008 >] *nos que ſe chama,* Gǒ.
[c2-l008 note] Nos completes ſomenos, describing the smaller territorial division called Gǒ. Gǒ is visibly upright within the Portuguese sentence, and its caron is retained.
[c2-l009] Gunguio. Muragaru vuo. *Cardume de pei-*
[c2-l009 note] Gunguio is a school of fish, explained by muragaru uo. The initial vuo is the historical spelling of fish, and pei- begins Portuguese peixes below.
[c2-l010 >] *xes. Vt,* Gunguio namini vcamu. *O*
[c2-l010 note] Xes completes peixes. The example describes the school floating or moving at the surface of the waves; its Portuguese translation begins with O at the row end.
[c2-l011 >] *cardume de peixe anda ao lume dagoa.* S.
[c2-l011 note] The fish are at the water's surface, ao lume dagoa. The final S. is a written-register label, and the historical joined dagoa is preserved.
[c2-l012] Gunguiocu. Muragaru tama. *Muitas pedras*
[c2-l012 note] Gunguiocu denotes gathered jewels, paraphrased as muragaru tama. The Portuguese expression muitas pedras continues into preciosas on the next row.
[c2-l013 >] *precioſas juntas.*
[c2-l013 note] Precioſas juntas completes the description of many precious stones together. This is a continuation of the collective jewel-name entry.
[c2-l014] Guni. *Dous, & cinco nos dados.*
[c2-l014 note] Guni names the two-and-five combination on dice. Its short gloss is a gaming use, distinct from the expressions with gu and the particle ni on the preceding page.
[c2-l015] Gunin. Vorocana fito. *Ignorante. Vt,*
[c2-l015 note] Gunin is an ignorant or foolish person, explained by orokana hito. Vt introduces the proverbial insect-and-fire comparison below.
[c2-l016 >] Gunin natçuno muxi tonde fini iru. *O*
[c2-l016 note] The fool is likened to a summer insect flying into a fire. The printed Japanese sentence, including fini for hi ni, remains intact rather than being replaced with a modernized proverb.
[c2-l017 >] *neſcio he como a borboleta do eſtio, que che-*
[c2-l017 note] Portuguese describes a summer butterfly approaching the fire. Che- begins chegandoſe, and the insect's action continues below.
[c2-l018 >] *gandoſe ao fogo voando, ſe queima. Enten-*
[c2-l018 note] The flying insect approaches the fire and burns itself. Enten- begins the source's explanation of how the comparison is to be understood.
[c2-l019 >] *deſe de hum cobiçoſo, ladrão, &c. que ain-*
[c2-l019 note] Deſe completes Entendeſe. The source applies the comparison to a greedy person or thief, with ain- starting ainda across the break.
[c2-l020 >] *da que ſabe, que o ande matar, toda via furta.*
[c2-l020 note] Da completes ainda: the thief steals despite knowing he will be killed. Ande and toda via retain the source's historical spelling and spacing rather than being rewritten as modern Portuguese.
[c2-l021] Gunjei. *Gente de guerra, ou multidão de ſol-*
[c2-l021 note] Gunjei denotes fighting people or a multitude of soldiers. The final ſol- begins soldados and has a visible line-end mark.
[c2-l022 >] *dados.*
[c2-l022 note] Dados completes ſoldados. This short row finishes Gunjei's collective-military definition; no separate Japanese entry begins here.
[c2-l023] Gunjen. Icuſano maye. *Antes da guerra.*
[c2-l023 note] Gunjen means before a war, explained by ikusa no mae. The example that follows concerns preparations before fighting, not a location at the front of a formation.
[c2-l024 >] *Vt,* Gunjenni cateuo facaru. *Antes da*
[c2-l024 note] Gunjenni kate wo hakaru means calculating provisions before war. The Portuguese translation starts Antes da and continues with the necessary food supply below.
[c2-l025 >] *guerra lançar conta ao mantimento que he neceſ*
[c2-l025 note] The example concerns calculating the food necessary for the army before war. Neceſ continues into ſario without a visible terminal hyphen; no hyphen is supplied from the expected word.
[c2-l026 >] *ſario pera tanta gente.* S.
[c2-l026 note] Sario completes neceſſario, followed by enough provisions for so many people. S. is the written-usage label, distinct from the italic translation.
[c2-l027] Gunjin. i, Icuſagami. *Marte, ou Cami da*
[c2-l027 note] Gunjin is ikusa-gami, a war deity, compared by the Portuguese compiler to Mars. Cami is a Japanese noun embedded in the Portuguese phrase Cami da guerra, rather than a second headword.
[c2-l028 >] *guerra.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*o homem.*
[c2-l028 note] Guerra completes the war-deity gloss. The parenthesis and o homem at the right belong semantically to the next entry's exclusion of humans, but their displaced physical position is preserved.
[c2-l029] Gunjǒ. *Todos os viuentes ſenſitiuos tirando*
[c2-l029 note] Gunjǒ is defined as all sentient living beings except humans. The excluded o homem is printed at the previous row's right edge, already represented by named displaced spans rather than silently moved here.
[c2-l030] Gunmacu. *Cortinas que ſeruem no exercito eſ-*
[c2-l030 note] Gunmacu denotes military curtains or screens. The Portuguese describes their use in an army, with eſ- beginning eſtendidas on the next row.
[c2-l031 >] *tendidas com ſua diuiſa pintada.* ¶ Gunma-
[c2-l031 note] The screens are stretched out bearing a painted emblem. Gunma- then begins the Japanese example, preserving the visible hyphen at the edge.
[c2-l032 >] cuuo faru. *Eſtender eſtas cortinas.*
[c2-l032 note] Cuuo faru completes gunmaku wo haru, stretching out the military screens. The Portuguese repeats the action with eſtender eſtas cortinas.
[c2-l033] Gunnai. Couorino vchi. *Dentro de hum*
[c2-l033 note] Gunnai means inside a district, paraphrased as kôri no uchi. The Portuguese construction Dentro de hum takes its district noun on the next row.
[c2-l034 >] *Gun. i, Certa comarca do reino.*

[c2-l035] Gunome. *Certa laya de peles.*

[c2-l038 >] *algũa couſa com lauor, ou pintura de cinco*

[c2-l040] Gunpacu. Icuſano macu. *Certas cortinas q̃*

[c2-l042 >] *chos onde ſe aloja o exercito.* S.
[c2-l042 note] Chos completes ranchos, where the army lodges. S. is the separate written-register label following the completed definition.
[c2-l043] Gunpen. Icuſano fotori. *Iunto do arrayal.*

[c2-l044] Gunpi. Muragari tobu. *Bando de paſſaros q̃*
[c2-l044 note] Gunpi describes a flock in flight, explained by muragari tobu. The abbreviated q̃ introduces the relative clause on the following row.
[c2-l045 >] *voão juntos.* S.
[c2-l045 note] The birds fly together, completing the flock definition. S. is the written-use label rather than an italic part of the sentence.
[c2-l046] Gunpǒ. Gun, couori. *Partes, ou comarcas*
[c2-l046 note] Gunpǒ is explained by Gun and couori as parts or districts. These are Japanese synonyms, not terms syntactically embedded in the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l047 >] *do reino.* S.
