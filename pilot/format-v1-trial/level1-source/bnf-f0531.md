[c2-l001] Vchi, vtçu, vtta. *Bater, ou dar pancadas.*
[c2-l001 note] Uchi, utsu, utta introduces the broad verb meaning strike or give blows. The abbreviated principal parts retain the printed initial v.
[c2-l002 >] *¶* Cauouo vtçu. *Dar bofetadas. ¶* Te-
[c2-l002 note] Kao wo utsu means slapping the face. Te- begins teki in the next example.
[c2-l003 >] qiuo vtçu. *Dar nos inimigos matandoos.*
[c2-l003 note] Teki wo utsu means striking or defeating enemies; the Portuguese explicitly includes killing them. The physical Te-qiuo division is preserved.
[c2-l004 >] *¶* Tçuzzumiuo vtçu. *Tanger hum certo*
[c2-l004 note] Tsuzumi wo utsu is playing a drum. The Portuguese description begins with sounding a certain instrument, completed below.
[c2-l005 >] *tabaquinho. ¶* Bacuchiuo vtçu. *Iugar*
[c2-l005 note] Tabaquinho supplies the small-drum gloss; bakuchi wo utsu then begins the gambling example. The historical word and spelling are retained.
[c2-l006 >] *os dados. ¶* Caneuo vtçu. *Iurar cer-*
[c2-l006 note] Completes the gambling gloss as playing dice. Kane wo utsu introduces an oath-taking usage, whose Portuguese translation continues below.
[c2-l007 >] *to juramento batendo com hum ferro noutro.*
[c2-l007 note] Explains the oath as striking one piece of iron against another. This specifies the special sense of kane wo utsu rather than ordinary bell ringing.
[c2-l008 >] *¶* Gouo vtçu. *Iugar hum certo jogo de*
[c2-l008 note] Go wo utsu is playing go. The Portuguese begins an explanation of the game by reference to its many small stones.
[c2-l009 >] *muitas pedrinhas. ¶* Fitouo vtçu. *Eſpã-*
[c2-l009 note] Completes the game description and starts hito wo utsu, striking a person. Eſpã- continues as car on the next row.
[c2-l010 >] *car, ou matar a alguem. ¶* Mizzuuo v-
[c2-l010 note] Completes the gloss as beating or killing someone, then starts mizu wo utsu. A visible space separates matar a, correcting OCR matara.
[c2-l011 >] tçu. i, Soſoqu. *Botar agoa com a mão, ou*
[c2-l011 note] Mizu wo utsu is equated with sosoku and explained as casting water with the hand. The Japanese explanatory synonym remains a separate roman span.
[c2-l012 >] *agoar. ¶ Item, Fazer certas obras de*
[c2-l012 note] Agoar completes the water-sprinkling sense. The entry then extends utsu to making certain kinds of handiwork.
[c2-l013 >] *mãos. Vt,* Vmano curauo vtçu. *Fazer*
[c2-l013 note] Uma no kura wo utsu exemplifies the craft sense by making a horse saddle. The object noun kura is not a blow directed at the animal.
[c2-l014 >] *ſela de caualo. ¶* Yumiuo vtçu. *Fazer*
[c2-l014 note] Completes the saddle gloss, then gives yumi wo utsu, making a bow. Its Portuguese object continues below.
[c2-l015 >] *arcos de atirar. ¶* Yumi vchi. *Official*
[c2-l015 note] Completes the bow-making gloss and gives yumi uchi as the bow maker. The scan has capital Y in Yumi and a single final l in Official, correcting OCR vumi and Officiall.
[c2-l016 >] *de arcos. ¶* Muxirouo vtçu. *Fazer*
[c2-l016 note] De arcos completes the bow-maker description; mushiro wo utsu begins the example of making mats.
[c2-l017 >] *esteiras, &c. ¶* Vouo vtçu. *Fazer*
[c2-l017 note] Esteiras completes the mat-making gloss. O wo utsu introduces cord or braid making, explained on the following row.
[c2-l018 >] *cordões, ou trança, &c. ¶* Catanauo v-
[c2-l018 note] The cords or braid complete the preceding example. Katana wo u- begins the sword-making example continued below.
[c2-l019 >] tçu. *Fazer eſpadas. ¶* Tauo vtçu. *La*
[c2-l019 note] Completes katana wo utsu as making swords, then gives ta wo utsu for working a field. The printed La has no supplied terminal hyphen before urar below.
[c2-l020 >] *urar, ou cauar as varzeas. ¶* Qirimu-
[c2-l020 note] Completes laurar as ploughing or digging low-lying fields. Kirimu- begins kirimugi in the following example.
[c2-l021 >] guiuo vtçu. *Fazer a letria de trigo.*
[c2-l021 note] Kirimugi wo utsu describes making wheat noodles, rendered a letria de trigo. The printed word spacing is retained.
[c2-l022 >] *¶* Fiuo vtçu. *Ferir fogo. ¶* Tori-
[c2-l022 note] Hi wo utsu means striking fire. Tori- begins the example of catching birds on the next row.
[c2-l023 >] uo vtçu. *Tomar com rede marrecas que ſe*
[c2-l023 note] Tori wo utsu is explained as catching ducks or teal in a net. The relative clause describes the birds' movement below.
[c2-l024 >] *leuantão do chão, & vão voando. ¶* Tori-
[c2-l024 note] The birds rise from the ground and fly; tori- then starts the compound toriuchi naming a catching place.
[c2-l025 >] vchi. *Lugar onde tomão eſtas marrecas com*
[c2-l025 note] Toriuchi is a place where these ducks are caught. The definition continues with the means of catching them.
[c2-l026 >] *rede que ſe lhes lança em cima. ¶* Fatouo
[c2-l026 note] The net is thrown over the birds. Hato wo begins the following example concerning pigeons.
[c2-l027 >] vtçu. *Tomar pombas com rede de tombo.*
[c2-l027 note] Hato wo utsu is catching pigeons with a falling or overturning net. The Portuguese rede de tombo specifies the mechanism.
[c2-l028 >] *¶* Vchiami. *Rede de tombo de tomar paſ*
[c2-l028 note] Uchiami names such a cast or falling net. The printed paſ ends without an added hyphen before ſaros on the next physical row.
[c2-l029 >] *ſaros, ou rede como tarrafa pera tomar peixe.*
[c2-l029 note] Completes paſſaros and extends the net definition to a cast net for fish. The shared net mechanism links the bird-catching and fishing senses.
[c2-l030 >] *¶* Tçuruuo vtçu. *Tocar na corda do arco*
[c2-l030 note] Tsuru wo utsu means plucking or touching a bowstring. The Portuguese explains how its sound is tested below.
[c2-l031 >] *puxando por ella pera ver ſe ſoa bem. Item,*
[c2-l031 note] The string is pulled to determine whether it sounds well. Item begins a figurative extension of this testing action.
[c2-l032 >] *per met. Ver, & palpar algũa couſa que hum*
[c2-l032 note] The figurative sense is looking at and feeling something in order to learn about it. The abbreviation per met. is retained without expansion.
[c2-l033 >] *quer ſaber, &c. ¶ Este verbo ſe ajunta, & cõ-*
[c2-l033 note] Completes the wish to know about the object. The lexicographic comment then says this verb joins and compounds with many others.
[c2-l034 >] *poem com muitos outros. Algũas vezes dà*
[c2-l034 note] Completes compoem and introduces the effects that utsu can have in compounds. The divided cõ-poem remains literal.
[c2-l035 >] *mais força, & enfaſi: & outras no mais que*
[c2-l035 note] Sometimes the compound adds force or emphasis; sometimes it adds no more than elegance. The printed no is retained rather than silently normalized to não.
[c2-l036 >] *elegancia, como ſe verà a baixo.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes the grammatical comment by directing the reader to the following entries for examples of that elegance.
[c2-l037] Vchiague, uru, eta. *Lançar pera cima. Vt,*
[c2-l037 note] Uchiage, uchiaguru, uchiageta means throwing upward. The following example illustrates throwing a stone high.

[c2-l044] Vchiai, ǒ, ǒta. *Encontrar com algũa couſa.*
[c2-l044 note] Uchiai and its listed principal forms mean meeting or coming into contact with something. The scan supports Vchiai and final a in couſa, correcting OCR Vehiai and couſo.
[c2-l045 >] *¶ Item, Darſe de pancadas, ou cutiladas hũ*
[c2-l045 note] Adds the reciprocal sense of exchanging blows or sword cuts. The visible t supports cutiladas rather than OCR cuiladas.
[c2-l046 >] *ao outro.*
[c2-l046 note] Ao outro completes the reciprocal phrase, one against the other. It closes uchiau before the next entry.
[c2-l047] Vchiaqe, uru, eta. *Abrir de todo. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l047 note] Uchiake, uchiakuru, uchiaketa means opening completely. Itẽ introduces a further sense that continues on the next page; Deſpejar below is the catchword, not another body line.
