[c1-l006 initial=2] IA. *Retribuição: ſempre ſe vſa com algũa cõ*

[c1-l008 note] Fôja ſuru is a second example of rendering recompense. The Portuguese Retribuir is recoverable despite abrasion of its early letters; no word space is inserted inside it.
[c1-l009] Ia. *Serpente.*
[c1-l009 note] This separate Ia means snake or serpent. It is consonantal I, read with a j-like value, not the OCR long s.
[c1-l010] ia. Yocoxima. *Couſa mà. Vt,* Iauo firuga-
[c1-l010 note] Another Ia means evil or wrong, explained by Yocoxima. The example begins turning away from evil; its verb continues on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] yexi xǒni qiſu. *Emendar os vicios, &*
[c1-l011 note] The opening yexi completes firugayexi, followed by the separately printed xǒni qiſu, returning to what is right. The Portuguese begins correcting vices; its conjunction leads into the next line.
[c1-l012 >] *darſe à virtude, & justiça.* Xix.
[c1-l012 note] Darſe means to devote oneself, completing the explanation as turning to virtue and justice. The apparent t in the draft is a long s; Xix. is the citation label and remains roman.
[c1-l013] Iaacu Yocoxima acu. *Couſa peruerſa, & mà*

[c1-l014 >] *ou deſordenada.*

[c1-l015] Iabi. Iano vo. *Rabo de ſerpente.*

[c1-l016 note] Iacago denotes stone-filled basket structures. The Portuguese starts describing baskets like large hampers with stones inside; den- continues as dentro below.

[c1-l017 note] The baskets are placed along rivers, with ter mão beginning the explanation of holding the earth in place. The nasal abbreviation q̃ is retained rather than expanded to que.
[c1-l018 >] *na terra, que não ſe desfaça, ou pera repre-*
[c1-l018 note] This line explains preventing the earth from coming apart, then begins the alternative purpose of damming water. Repre- must be joined semantically with ſar on the following physical row.
[c1-l019 >] *ſar a agoa, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] The fragment ſar completes repreſar: retaining or damming the water. Agoa is the printed historical spelling, not an error to modernize to água.
[c1-l020] Iacǒ. *Animal que dà almiſcre. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l020 note] Iacǒ first denotes an animal that yields musk; the next sense is the musk itself. The scan supports almiſcre, with c after the long s rather than the OCR e, and a consonantal initial I in the headword.

[c1-l021 note] This is the second Iacǒ sense: the musk itself. Almiſcre matches the preceding line; the draft’s alinſere loses the m and mistakes c for e.
[c1-l022] Iacǒguan. *Hũa mezinha.*
[c1-l022 note] Iacǒguan is identified simply as a medicine. The entry does not specify its ingredients here, so the gloss is not expanded into a modern pharmaceutical identification.
[c1-l023] Iacot. Iano fone. *Oßo de ſerpente que ſerue*
[c1-l023 note] Iacot is snake bone, explained by Iano fone. The n of Iano is faint but fits the surviving shape and the genitive phrase; final t and the printed Oßo ligature are preserved.
[c1-l024 >] *de mezinha.*
[c1-l024 note] The snake bone is described as serving medicinally. The damaged consonant in mezinha is n, consistent with the same word clearly printed two rows earlier.
[c1-l025] Iacqet. Ybara. *Aruore deſpinhos aßi chamada.*
[c1-l025 note] Iacqet is explained by Ybara as a thorny tree so called. The scan gives Aruore and aßi rather than the OCR Atuore and aße; the joined deſpinhos and the ß ligature remain diplomatic.
[c1-l026] Iacu. Youaxi. *Couſa fraca, ou fraqueza.*

[c1-l028] Iacufai. Vacaqi tomogara. *Mancebo, ou de*
[c1-l028 note] Iacufai describes someone young, expanded as Vacaqi tomogara. The Portuguese begins a young man or someone of little age, continuing on the following row.
[c1-l029 >] *pouca idade. ¶ Item, Serue pera ſe humi-*

[c1-l030 note] The humble use presents the speaker as having little knowledge and age. The terminal po belongs to posto on the next row; no unprinted hyphen is supplied.

[c1-l031 note] The speaker may use Iacufai humbly despite actually being of considerable age. The example Iacufaina cotouo starts on this row, with its verb below.
[c1-l032 >] yǔ. *Dizer couſas de homem de pouca idade,*

[c1-l033 >] *ou pouco ſaber.*
[c1-l033 note] Ou pouco ſaber extends the example to speech betraying little knowledge. This short continuation completes Iacufai before a new quietness-related entry.
[c1-l034] Iacujacuto. *Adu. Quieta, & ſoſegadamẽ*
[c1-l034 note] Iacujacuto is an adverb meaning quietly or peacefully. The abbreviation is Adu., not Alu.; ſoſegadamẽ continues with te on the next physical row.

[c1-l036] Iacujǒ. Xizzucani, xizzuca nari. *Paz, ou*
[c1-l036 note] Iacujǒ is explained with Xizzucani and xizzuca nari as peace or quietness. Its final quietação is physically displaced to the preceding row’s right end and is encoded there.
[c1-l037] Iacujǔ. Youaxi, yauaraca. *Fraqueza, ou cou*
[c1-l037 note] Iacujǔ combines the ideas of weakness and softness, explained by Youaxi and yauaraca. The scan supports c in yauaraca, not the draft’s t; the initial I and unhyphenated cou are retained.
[c1-l038 >] *ſa fraca.*
[c1-l038 note] The opening ſa completes couſa, yielding weakness or something weak. It is a continuation of Iacujǔ, not an independent definition.
[c1-l039] Iacumacu. Xizzucana coto. *Couſa quieta, ou*
[c1-l039 note] Iacumacu is a quiet state or quietness, explained by Xizzucana coto. The Portuguese alternative introduced by ou continues below.
[c1-l040 >] *quietação. ¶* Iacumacu muninjǒ. *Quie-*
[c1-l040 note] Quietação completes the general definition and is printed with i, omitted in the draft. Iacumacu muninjǒ introduces a more specific quiet condition, whose Portuguese explanation starts with the divided Quie-.
[c1-l041 >] *tação, & repouſo ſem ouuir vozes, ou eſtron*
[c1-l041 note] The compound means quiet and repose without hearing voices or human noise. The scan supports ouuir, not the OCR ouair; eſtron continues into eſtrondo on the next row without a supplied hyphen.
[c1-l042 >] *do de gente.*
[c1-l042 note] Do completes eſtrondo, followed by de gente: the noise of people. The physical division is preserved even though the word is interpreted continuously.
[c1-l043] Iacumet. Xizzucanari meſſu. i, Monono
[c1-l043 note] Iacumet concerns cessation or extinction, explained by the following Japanese phrase. The faint initial I is retained, and the scan shows two long-s sorts in meſſu; Monono begins the explanatory alternative completed below.
[c1-l044 >] meſsuru. *Acabar, ou morrer.* Iacumet
[c1-l044 note] Meſsuru completes Monono meſsuru, glossed as ending or dying. The final Iacumet begins the next example without the spurious stop supplied by OCR; its object particle is on the following row.
[c1-l045 >] uo tonǒ. *Idem.* Bup.
[c1-l045 note] This completes Iacumet uo tonǒ, likewise glossed as ending or dying and marked for Buddhist usage. The source’s tonǒ is retained, and the terminal stop belongs with roman Bup., not an isolated italic run.
