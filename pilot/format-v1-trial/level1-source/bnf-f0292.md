[c1-l001] Iǒ. *Teima, ou opinião teſa. Vt,* Iǒuo faru.
[c1-l001 note] Joo denotes obstinacy or a fixed opinion. Joo wo haru illustrates insisting on one's position; the initial I and its caroned o are retained from the scan.
[c1-l002 >] *Ter mão ẽ ſua opinião, ou ſer teimoſo. ¶* Iǒ-
[c1-l002 note] Explains holding firmly to one's opinion or being stubborn. The next Japanese phrase begins Iǒ- at the line end; ẽ remains an unexpanded nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l003 >] no couai fito. *Homem teimoſo, & afer-*
[c1-l003 note] Completes joo no kowai hito, a stubborn person. Teimoſo is a single printed word, not the OCR's tei moſo; afer- continues below.
[c1-l004 >] *rado a ſeu parecer. ¶ Item, Nos liuros*
[c1-l004 note] Completes aferrado a seu parecer, firmly attached to one's view. Item introduces a different sense found in books.
[c1-l005 >] *ſe toma por amor, ou piedade.*
[c1-l005 note] The written usage is love or compassion. This sense belongs to the same joo entry, rather than to the following old-person homophone.
[c1-l006] Iô. Toxiyori. *Velho.*
[c1-l006 note] Joo here means an old person, toshiyori. The visible circumflex is retained, despite the different mark on the surrounding obstinacy entry.
[c1-l007] Iô. *Cinza em que ſe deſfaz a braſa, ou car-*
[c1-l007 note] Another joo denotes ash into which glowing coal breaks down. The circumflex is visible; car- begins carvão on the next line.
[c1-l008 >] *uão aceſo ainda que fica de baixo algum fogo,*
[c1-l008 note] Completes carvão and explains that the coal may still contain some fire underneath. De baixo remains the printed two-word form.
[c1-l009 >] *ou braſa. ¶* Voqi mina jôni natta. *Ia*
[c1-l009 note] The explanation adds remaining embers. Oki mina joo ni natta says the embers have all turned to ash, with the circumflex repeated in jôni.
[c1-l010 >] *a braſa esta deſfeita toda em cinza. ¶* No
[c1-l010 note] Completes the statement that all the embers have become ash. No begins a Portuguese explanation continued by chanoyuu; its upright type is retained rather than changed solely because it is Portuguese.
[c1-l011 >] chanoyǔ *ſe diz, Sumi nadareta. Estarem*
[c1-l011 note] The Portuguese says that in chanoyuu one uses sumi nadareta for the subsiding embers. Chanoyǔ is upright, while Sumi nadareta is visibly italic in this impression; both are Japanese expressions embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l012 >] *as braſas ja baixas, & des feitas quaſi ẽ cinza.*
[c1-l012 note] Explains the expression as embers already low and almost reduced to ash. The gap before ẽ is visible; the separate des feitas is retained rather than normalized.
[c1-l013] Iǒ. *Cadeado. Vt,* Iǒuo voroſu. *Lançar*
[c1-l013 note] Joo here means a padlock. Joo wo orosu means fastening or putting on the lock, with the Portuguese object supplied below.
[c1-l014 >] *cadeado. ¶* Iǒuo aquru. *Abrir o ca-*
[c1-l014 note] Completes lançar cadeado and contrasts opening it, joo wo akuru. The damaged but identifiable Iǒuo replaces the OCR's Muo.
[c1-l015 >] *deado.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes cadeado, the object of abrir. This short row belongs to the lock example.
[c1-l016] Iǒban. Xirono ban. *Vigia da fortaleza.*
[c1-l016 note] Jooban is the guard of a fortress, explicitly shiro no ban. The missing initial I in the OCR is present in the scan.
[c1-l017] Iǒban. *Vigia ordinaria, & que não ſe troca.*
[c1-l017 note] This jooban is a standing or regular guard that is not replaced in rotation. It is distinct from the fortress-guard sense immediately above.
[c1-l018] Iǒbita. *Couſa excelente, perfeita, &c. Vt,*
[c1-l018 note] Joobita describes something excellent or accomplished. The following examples apply it first to a person's bearing, then to hospitality.
[c1-l019 >] Iǒbita fito. *Laya de homem honrado, ou*
[c1-l019 note] Joobita hito is a person of honourable or excellent character. The damaged medial y of Laya is supported by its surviving strokes; OCR Laga does not match the impression.
[c1-l020 >] *excelente em ſua peßoa, & trato. ¶* Iǒbita fu-
[c1-l020 note] Completes the description of excellence in person and conduct. The mark after trato is a paragraph sign, not q; fu- begins furumai below.
[c1-l021 >] rumai. *Banquete eſplendido.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes joobita furumai, splendid hospitality or a magnificent feast. The Portuguese explicitly gives banquete, distinguishing this example's sense from personal bearing.
[c1-l022] Iôbitaqi. *Aueſinha aßi chamada.*
[c1-l022 note] Joobitaki is identified simply as a bird of that name. The scan reads Aueſinha, repairing OCR Aueſmha; the definition does not itself supply a modern species name.
[c1-l023] Iǒbon. Vyeno xina. *Eſpecie, ou ordem de*
[c1-l023 note] Joobon is a high class or grade, explained by ue no shina. The Portuguese introduces a class or order of excellent things.
[c1-l024 >] *couſas excelentes, & ſupremas.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the high-grade definition with excellent and supreme things. It belongs to joobon, not the next volume-related entry.
[c1-l025] Iǒbu. *Primeira parte, ou tomo dalgum liuro.*
[c1-l025 note] Joobu is the first part or volume of a book. The source has the initial I omitted by OCR.
[c1-l026 >] *¶ Item, Suprema, ou primeira parte, como*
[c1-l026 note] Adds the highest or first portion in a spatial division. The scan supports primeira, not the OCR's nasalized prãmeira.
[c1-l027 >] *da regiam do ar, &c.*
[c1-l027 note] The region of the air illustrates this upper-portion sense. Regiam is retained as the printed historical spelling.
[c1-l028] Iǒbun. Vyeno bun. *Parte ſuperior.*
[c1-l028 note] Joobun is an upper portion, explicitly ue no bun. It is a separate headword from the following report-to-a-superior sense.
[c1-l029] Iǒbun. *Meliùs,* Xǒbun. *Fazer ouuir, ou*
[c1-l029 note] The dictionary prefers shoobun for making something heard by, or reporting to, a superior. Meliùs is italic editorial apparatus; the alternative Japanese form remains roman.
[c1-l030 >] *dizer a peßoa alta, & nobre, como Cubǒ,*
[c1-l030 note] Continues reporting to a high-ranking person, with Kuboo as an example. Cubǒ is a Japanese title embedded in the Portuguese explanation; its visibly italic type is retained.
[c1-l031 >] *Yacata, &c. Vt,* Iǒbunni taſſuru. *Fa-*
[c1-l031 note] Yacata adds another lordly title, visibly italic as a whole rather than the OCR's split styling. Joobun ni tassuru illustrates addressing such a superior; Fa- begins falar.
[c1-l032 >] *lar a eſtas peſſoas.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes falar a estas pessoas, speaking to those high-ranking people. The two long-s forms in peſſoas are retained.
[c1-l033] Iôbun. *Ouuir de peßoa alta, & nobre o que*
[c1-l033 note] This joobun is hearing what a high-ranking person says, contrasting with reporting to one in the preceding entry. The circumflex is retained from the printed headword.
[c1-l034 >] *lhe diz, ou manda.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the hearing gloss: what the superior says or commands. It is not a continuation of the earlier speaking-to-a-superior example.
[c1-l035] Iǒbut. Fotoqeni naru. *Fazerſe Fotoque, ou*
[c1-l035 note] Joobutsu means becoming a Buddha, hotoke ni naru. The embedded Fotoque in the Portuguese explanation is printed italic here; the final t in Iǒbut is historical checked-coda notation.
[c1-l036 >] *ſaluarſe.*
[c1-l036 note] Adds being saved as the Portuguese interpretation of attaining Buddhahood. It completes the preceding religious entry.
[c1-l037] Iôbut. Norimono. *Hũa maneira de andas*
[c1-l037 note] A homophonous joobutsu denotes a conveyance, norimono, described as a kind of litter. Its circumflex contrasts with the preceding religious headword's caron.
[c1-l038 >] *que leuão homẽs aos ombros, ou nas mãos.*
[c1-l038 note] The litter is carried by men on their shoulders or in their hands. Homẽs retains the nasal abbreviation in the scan.
[c1-l039] Iǒcai. Bup. *Mundo ſuperior, ou ſobre os Ce-*
[c1-l039 note] Jookai is the upper world, marked as Buddhist vocabulary by upright Bup. The initial I corrects OCR F; Ce- begins Ceos.
[c1-l040 >] *os. Vt,* Iǒcai guecai. *Ceos, & terra, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Completes Ceos and gives jookai gekai, the upper and lower worlds. The Portuguese pairs heavens and earth.
[c1-l041 >] *mundo ſuperior, & eſte inferior.*
[c1-l041 note] Completes the contrast between the higher world and this lower one. The demonstrative eſte refers to the present world.
[c1-l042] Iǒcai. Qiyoi caimon. *Ley, ou mandamentos*
[c1-l042 note] This jookai denotes pure precepts, explained by kiyoi kaimon. The scan's large Q is clear and corrects OCR Giyoi.
[c1-l043 >] *puros, & rectos.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes the definition of the law or commandments as pure and right. It belongs to the precept entry rather than the upper-world homophone.
[c1-l044] Iǒchacu. Nobori tçuqu. *Chegar a algum lu-*
[c1-l044 note] Joochaku means arriving after going up, nobori tsuku. The Portuguese begins arrival at a place conventionally described as upward.
[c1-l045 >] *gar em reſpeito do qual ſe diz ſubir, como ao*
[c1-l045 note] Completes lugar and explains the relational use of 'go up', not necessarily a literal climb. The scan reads como ao rather than OCR cõmo co.
[c1-l046 >] *Miaco, &c.*
[c1-l046 note] The capital, Miaco, is the example of a destination one goes up to. This completes the preceding arrival definition.
[c1-l047] Iǒchǔ. Xirono vchi. *Dentro da fortaleza.*
[c1-l047 note] Joochuu is inside a fortress, explicitly shiro no uchi. The checked headword keeps its two visible vowel marks.

[c2-l001] Iǒ, chǔ, gue. Vye, naca, xita. *Couſa ſu-*
[c2-l001 note] Joo, chuu, ge are upper, middle and lower, explicitly ue, naka and shita. The Portuguese ſu- begins ſuprema; the initial I was misread as l in OCR.
[c2-l002 >] *prema, meoã, & infima. ¶ Item, Em*
[c2-l002 note] Completes the three grades: supreme, middle and lowest. The source's meoã is retained with the tilde over its final a; Item adds a spatial paraphrase.
[c2-l003 >] *cima, no meo, em baixo.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes em cima and gives in the middle and below. This is the spatial use of the same three terms.
[c2-l004] Iǒco. *Capitão dalgũa fortaleza.*
[c2-l004 note] Jooko is the commander of a fortress. The Portuguese dalgũa keeps its visible nasal sign.
[c2-l005] Iǒcocu. *Bom reino. i, Onde ha riquezas, &*
[c2-l005 note] Jookoku is a good or prosperous country, explained as one with riches. The explanatory i, belongs to the italic Portuguese apparatus.
[c2-l006 >] *boas couſas.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes the prosperity gloss with good things. It belongs to jookoku rather than the following physical-vigour entry.
[c2-l007] Iǒcon. *Boa, & forte diſpoſição natural. Vt,*
[c2-l007 note] Jookon denotes a good, strong natural constitution. The initial is I, not the OCR long s.
[c2-l008 >] Areua jǒconno fitogia. *Aquelle he homẽ*
[c2-l008 note] Are wa jookon no hito ja means that person has a strong constitution. The scan prints Aquelle as one word, not OCR A quelle.
[c2-l009 >] *bem deſpoſto, & forte.*
[c2-l009 note] Completes the description of that man as well constituted and strong. It is the predicate of the preceding Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l010] Iocu acu. Nigoru, acu. *Maldade, ou pecca-*
[c2-l010 note] Joku aku concerns impurity and evil, explained by nigoru and aku. The Portuguese calls it wickedness or sin; pecca- continues below.
[c2-l011 >] *do. Vt,* Iocu acu xecai. *Mundo immũdo.*
[c2-l011 note] Completes peccado and illustrates joku aku sekai, an impure world. Immũdo preserves the printed nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l012] Iocuqet. Nigotta chi. *Sãgue ruim, & podre.*
[c2-l012 note] Jokuketsu is corrupted blood, literally nigotta chi. The scan supports initial I rather than OCR r; final t is the historical checked-coda notation.
[c2-l013] Iôdacu. Vqetamauari, vqegô. *Ouuir, &*
[c2-l013 note] Joodaku denotes hearing and assenting, with uketamawari and ukegoo as explanations. The initial I corrects OCR F, and both circumflexes remain as printed.
[c2-l014 >] *conſentir com o que lhe dizem.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes the definition: consenting to what one is told. It belongs to joodaku, not the historical-age entry below.
[c2-l015] Iǒdai. *Idade antigua, pacifica, & florente.*
[c2-l015 note] Joodai is an ancient age described as peaceful and flourishing. Antigua is the printed historical form.
[c2-l016 >] *¶* Imaua jǒdaini natta. *Eſtà eſte tempo,*
[c2-l016 note] Ima wa joodai ni natta says the present has become like that flourishing ancient age. The Portuguese translation continues on the next row.
[c2-l017 >] *ou idade como a antigua florente, &c.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes the comparison of the present age with the flourishing ancient one. Antigua, not OCR amigua, is supported by the scan.
[c2-l018] Iǒdan. Vyeno dan. *Supremo degrao.*
[c2-l018 note] Joodan is the highest level, literally ue no dan. The following extension describes an elevated seating place.
[c2-l019 >] *¶ Item, Lugar mais alto que ſe faz no Za-*
[c2-l019 note] Adds a raised place made in a zashiki for the lord. The embedded word begins with a visible capital Z, Za-, not the OCR's lowercase z. Za- is the first printed fragment of the embedded Japanese room-name Zaxiqui.
[c2-l020 >] *xiqui pera o ſenhor eſtar, &c.*
[c2-l020 note] Completes Zaxiqui across the line break and explains that the raised place is for the lord to occupy. The Japanese room-name remains in the italic type actually printed here. The opening xiqui completes the preceding Za-; both fragments remain italic as printed.
[c2-l021] Iǒdǒ. Dǒuo jǒzuru. *Fazerſe, ou chegar*
[c2-l021 note] Joodoo is attaining the way, doo wo joozuru. The Portuguese begins becoming or reaching Buddhahood; the damaged initial of Fazerſe is F, not OCR P.
[c2-l022 >] *à dignidade de Fotoque.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes the attainment gloss as reaching the dignity of a Buddha. Fotoque is a Japanese word embedded in the italic Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l023] Iǒdo. Qiyoi cuni. *Terra limpa. i, Paraiſo*
[c2-l023 note] Joodo is the pure land, explained by qiyoi cuni. The Portuguese gloss proceeds from clean land to paradise; the explanatory i, is retained where the punctuation is locally ink-filled and irregular, rather than inventing an additional mark.
[c2-l024 >] *do Fotoque.*
[c2-l024 note] Completes the phrase as the Buddha's paradise. Fotoque is embedded in the Portuguese gloss, not a separate Japanese example.
[c2-l025] Iǒdo. Vyeno nori. *Ley.*
[c2-l025 note] This separate joodo is explained by ue no nori and glossed simply as law. The initial I corrects OCR F; the wording is retained without imposing a more specific modern doctrinal sense.
[c2-l026] Iǒdoxǔ. *Os da ſeita de* Iǒdo.
[c2-l026 note] Joodoshuu denotes adherents of the Joodo school. The embedded school-name Iǒdo is upright within the italic Portuguese. I retain the baseline's capital I: its tall stem is interrupted by a white break rather than showing the small, separate dot of the neighboring lowercase i; the damaged impression makes capitalization a minor caveat.
[c2-l027] Iǒfacumai. *Arroz muito branco, & bem*
[c2-l027 note] Joohakumai is very white, thoroughly prepared rice. Arroz corrects the OCR's Anroz, with the description completed by pilado below.
[c2-l028 >] *pilado.*
[c2-l028 note] Pilado specifies rice well pounded or milled. This short row completes the preceding rice definition.
[c2-l029] Iǒfai. Vyeno tomogara. *Maiores, ou prin-*
[c2-l029 note] Joohai means the higher-ranking people, ue no tomogara. The Portuguese prin- begins principais.
[c2-l030 >] *cipais. Vt,* Iǒfaiua guefaiuo auaremu.
[c2-l030 note] Completes principais and gives joohai wa gehai wo awaremu, the higher ranks showing compassion toward the lower ranks.
[c2-l031 >] *Os grandes tem miſericordia dos pequenos.*
[c2-l031 note] Translates the preceding social comparison: the great have mercy on the small. The historical unaccented tem is retained.
[c2-l032] Iǒfen. Xirono fotori. *Ao longo, ou a par da*
[c2-l032 note] Joohen is the vicinity of a fortress, shiro no hotori. The Portuguese describes being alongside it, with the noun completed on the next row.
[c2-l033 >] *fortaleza.*
[c2-l033 note] Completes the description of being beside the fortress. The scan prints fortaleza as one word, not OCR forta leza.
[c2-l034] Iǒfit. *Bom eſcriuão, ou boa letra, ou boa mão*
[c2-l034 note] Joohitsu denotes a good writer, fine handwriting, or a good writing hand. These alternatives are supplied directly by the Portuguese definition.
[c2-l035 >] *pera eſcreuer.*
[c2-l035 note] Completes boa mão pera escrever, skill at writing. This is part of the joohitsu definition.
[c2-l036] Iofit. Taſuqe fude. *O ajudar a eſcreuer.*
[c2-l036 note] Johitsu is assistance with writing, literally tasuke fude. Fude means brush; the visible f and the writing gloss support fude rather than OCR ſude.
[c2-l037] Iǒgiǔ. *Sempre. Vt,* Iǒgiǔ fudanno co-
[c2-l037 note] Joojuu means always or continually. The initial I is present despite its loss in OCR; co- begins koto in the example.
[c2-l038 >] tonari. *He couſa ordinaria.*
[c2-l038 note] Completes joojuu fudan no koto nari, something constant or habitual. The Portuguese renders it as an ordinary or regular matter.
[c2-l039] Iǒgiǔgui. *Veſtido de cote.* Iǒgiǔguini ſu-
[c2-l039 note] Joojuugi is everyday clothing. De cote supplies the habitual sense; the Japanese example ends with the first part of suru.
[c2-l040 >] ru. *Trazer algum veſtido de cote.*
[c2-l040 note] Completes suru and translates wearing a garment regularly. Trazer is used of wearing, not merely transporting, the clothing.
[c2-l041] Iǒgiǔmot. *Couſa continua, & ordinaria.*
[c2-l041 note] Joojuumotsu denotes something constant or ordinary. The final t remains the dictionary's checked-coda spelling.
[c2-l042] Iǒgô. Tçune tçune. *Couſa ordinaria.*
[c2-l042 note] Joogoo is something usual or recurring, explained by tsune tsune. The first long-o mark is a caron and the final one a circumflex.
[c2-l043] Iǒgo. *Bebedor, ou amigo de vinho.*
[c2-l043 note] Joogo is a drinker or someone fond of alcoholic drink. The dictionary's vinho is retained without narrowing it to grape wine.
[c2-l044] Iǒgo. *Funil. ¶* Iǒgode ſaqeuo tçugu.
[c2-l044 note] This joogo is a funnel. Joogo de sake wo tsugu illustrates pouring drink through one; the headword begins with I, not OCR l.
[c2-l045 >] *Botar vinho por funil.*
[c2-l045 note] Translates the funnel example: pour the drink through a funnel. It belongs to the implement sense, not the preceding drinker sense.
[c2-l046] Iogon. Taſuqe cotoba. *Ajuda de palauras,*
[c2-l046 note] Jogon is verbal assistance or advice, explained by tasuke kotoba. The Portuguese definition continues into an example spanning the page boundary.
[c2-l047 >] *como conſelho, &c. Vt,* Goxǒguini jo-
[c2-l047 note] Conſelho, advice, repairs the OCR's final e. Goxǒguini begins an example about speaking during go and shogi; jo- continues as gon at the top of f293, while the bottom gon is only the catchword.
