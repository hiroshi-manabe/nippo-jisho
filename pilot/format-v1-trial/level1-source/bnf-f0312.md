[c1-l004] Me. *Femea, ou molher caſada.*

[c1-l005] Me. *Hũs limos do mar.*

[c1-l010] Me. *He particula que posta com as raizes dal-*

[c1-l011 note] The suffix denotes the endpoint or resulting mark of the verbs concerned. The scan supports ſignifica, not the provisional ſignifira.
[c1-l012 >] *verbos. Vt,* Qirime. *Sinal da ferida.*
[c1-l012 note] Kirime illustrates the suffix as a wound or cut-mark. Vt belongs to the italic explanatory apparatus; Qirime remains roman.
[c1-l013 >] *¶* Cauarime. *Conjunção, ou principio de*
[c1-l013 note] Kawarime is a juncture or the start of a change. Its definition continues on a physical row omitted by the provisional OCR, now restored as c1-l013a.

[c1-l017] Me. *He particula que abaixa os nomes com que*

[c1-l019 >] *madraço, &c.*

[c1-l020] Meate. *Aluo, ou ſinal qualquer a que ſe tira cõ*
[c1-l020 note] Meate is a target or mark aimed at. The Portuguese instrumental phrase starts with the abbreviated cõ and continues with bow or firearm.

[c1-l023 >] Ano fitouo meateni xite mǒxita. *Falei*
[c1-l023 note] Ano hito wo meate ni shite mooshita means speaking with that person as the reference or intended target. The translation here emphasizes taking him as one’s standard.
[c1-l024 >] *tomando aquelle homẽ por regra, ou methodo.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the translation: taking that man as a rule or method. The scan reads homẽ; the provisional homtẽ inserted a consonant not present in the print.
[c1-l025 >] *¶* Meatega chigǒta. *Errou o aluo.*

[c1-l026 note] The same expression is extended metaphorically to being mistaken in one’s supposition. The object of that mistaken judgment continues below.
[c1-l027 >] *tinha viſto, & percebido. ¶* Focutouo
[c1-l027 note] Completes the figurative explanation with what one had seen and perceived. Focutouo begins the next Japanese example, concerning a northern celestial guide.
[c1-l028 >] meateni xite funeuo noru. *Nauegar tomã-*

[c1-l029 note] Completes tomando and identifies the guide as the north star. The gloss’s description is retained without silently equating the Japanese celestial name with a modern astronomical identification.
[c1-l030] Mebayana. *Peſſoa que alcança bem com a vi-*
[c1-l030 note] Mebayana describes someone quick or keen of sight. The printed final na belongs to the adjectival form rather than being an erroneous extra syllable.
[c1-l031 >] *ſta, ou enxerga logo com os olhos algũa couſa*
[c1-l031 note] Completes viſta and explains immediately noticing something with the eyes. The tilde belongs to u in algũa; the faint mark after couſa is not promoted to secure punctuation.
[c1-l032] Mebǒ. *Inſtrumento de metal pera por mezi-*
[c1-l032 note] Mebǒ is a metal instrument for applying eye medicine. The visibly downward mark over o is retained as a caron, and mezi- continues as mezinhas below.
[c1-l033 >] *nhas nos olhos.*

[c1-l034 note] Meboshi denotes the lights or “stars” perceived in the eyes. The Portuguese explains them in terms of vital spirits and begins the diminutive lumeſi- across the line break.

[c1-l037 note] Completes eſtes and places the vital spirits within the eyes. The final B. is an upright register label, outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l038] Mecaqe. *Manceba.*

[c1-l039] Meco. *Molher, & filhos.*
[c1-l039 note] Meko denotes wife and children together. The Portuguese molher and filhos explicitly establish this collective family sense.
[c1-l040] Mecqi. *Certa feição de dourar com folha dou-*
[c1-l040 note] Mekki is a method of gilding with gold leaf. The word ouro is divided after dou- in the contracted phrase folha douro and continues on the next physical row.
[c1-l041 >] *ro. ¶* Mecqiuo ſaſu. *Dourar de folha.*
[c1-l041 note] Ro completes the gold-leaf definition. Mekki wo sasu is applying that gilding, directly translated as Dourar de folha; the long s forms are not f.
[c1-l042] Mecqiacu. *Destruição. ¶* Mecqiacu ſu-
[c1-l042 note] Mekkyaku means destruction. The following Mekkyaku su- begins the verbal example, completed by ru on the next row.
[c1-l043 >] ru *Deſtruir.*
[c1-l043 note] Ru completes suru, making “destroy,” as the Portuguese Deſtruir states. No period is supplied between ru and the gloss because none is securely visible.
[c1-l044] Mecqi mecqito. *Adu. Certa maneira de ſõ*
[c1-l044 note] Mekki mekki to is marked as an adverb and describes a particular creaking or cracking sound. The phonetic form is not the unrelated gilding noun just above.
[c1-l045 >] *que faz a caſa quando treme com algum vento*
[c1-l045 note] The sound is illustrated by a house shaking in wind. The adjective describing the wind continues on the next line.

[c1-l046 note] Rijo completes the strong-wind example, followed by a comparison with wood breaking. The next column adds another usage of the same reduplicated expression.

[c2-l001 >] *¶* Fuxin mecqi mecqito michiga iqu. *A*
[c2-l001 note] The construction work advances rapidly or visibly: this is another example of mekki mekki to. The first repetition prints mecqi, not provisional meeqi; michiga iqu is retained as the printed phrase.
[c2-l002 >] *obra vai a olhos viſtos por diante.*
[c2-l002 note] Completes the Portuguese sentence: the work goes forward before one’s eyes. This is a figurative-progress sense, not a further description of cracking timber.
[c2-l003] Mecuchi cauaqi. *Diz ſe de hũa peßoa eſperta*
[c2-l003 note] Mekuchi kawaki describes an alert or quick-witted person. The supplied NINJAL index independently lists Mecuchi cauaqi; the unfamiliar wording is therefore not silently changed to a more familiar Japanese expression.
[c2-l004 >] *viua, ſagaz, &c.*
[c2-l004 note] Viva and sagaz complete the characterization as lively and shrewd. They belong to the same person introduced on the previous row.
[c2-l005] Mecugui. *Hum preguinho de pao que tem mão*
[c2-l005 note] Mekugi is a small wooden retaining peg. The Portuguese expression tem mão describes its holding or securing function, completed by the reference to the sword hilt.
[c2-l006 >] *nos punhos da Catana.*
[c2-l006 note] The peg holds at the katana’s hilt. Catana is the sword-name occurring within the Portuguese explanation; the printed type is retained for optional whole-word review.
[c2-l007] Mecugui ana. *Buraco por onde metem este*
[c2-l007 note] Mekugi ana is the hole through which that retaining peg is inserted. The printed space before ana is preserved rather than joining the compound.
[c2-l008 >] *prego.*
[c2-l008 note] Prego completes the definition of the peg-hole. This short continuation is not an independent entry or a displaced catchword.
[c2-l009] Mecura. *Cego.*
[c2-l009 note] Mekura denotes blindness or a blind person. The Portuguese vowel is e in Cego, not the provisional Cago.
[c2-l010] Mecujira. *Balea femea: mais ſe vſa por,* Yo-
[c2-l010 note] Mekujira is a female whale. The gloss says the word occurs more as a reading of a written term than in ordinary use; Yo- begins the roman Japanese word Yomi.
[c2-l011 >] mi *de* Qeiguei, *que por vſo de pratica.*
[c2-l011 note] Mi completes Yomi, and Qeiguei is the term whose reading is being explained. The Portuguese prints pratica, not provisional pracica; both Japanese terms remain separate from the italic apparatus.
[c2-l012] Mecurumecu. S. Meno mǒ coto. *Do-*
[c2-l012 note] Mekurumeku concerns dizziness, explained by me no moo koto. The S. is an upright written-register label; the visually clear mǒ is retained rather than normalized to a modern verbal form.
[c2-l013 >] *ença de andar a cabeça à roda, ou irſe o lume*
[c2-l013 note] Ença completes Doença: the head spins, or the light of vision goes away. Both clauses describe dizziness or visual dimming.
[c2-l014 >] *dos olhos. Não ſe vſa tanto em pratica co-*
[c2-l014 note] Completes the reference to the eyes and begins the usage restriction: this is less a conversational word than a reading of a written expression.
[c2-l015 >] *mo por,* Yomi, *de* Qen vn. *A palaura*
[c2-l015 note] Yomi and Qen vn are roman terms embedded in the Portuguese explanation. The printed initial Q and the internal space in Qen vn are preserved rather than regularized from the expected Japanese lexical identification.
[c2-l016 >] *corrente he,* Memaiqe.
[c2-l016 note] Memaiqe is supplied as the current spoken word. It belongs inside the Portuguese sentence “the current word is,” rather than forming a separate headword here.
[c2-l017] Medachi, tçu, atta. *Brotar a aruore, ou ſair*
[c2-l017 note] Medachi, medatsu, medatta here means a tree putting forth growth. The Portuguese brotar fixes the budding sense, not the modern prominent or conspicuous sense of medatsu.
[c2-l018 >] *com gomos, & folhas nouas.*
[c2-l018 note] New buds and leaves complete the description of the tree’s sprouting. Gomos is the botanical noun in the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l019] Medachi. *Folhas, ou gomos tenros das aruores*
[c2-l019 note] The noun medachi denotes the tender leaves or buds themselves. It is separately entered after the verb’s principal forms.
[c2-l020 >] *quando começão a brotar.*
[c2-l020 note] The leaves or buds are those appearing when trees first begin to sprout. The small mark above the opening word is not taken as an abbreviation replacing the fully printed quando.
[c2-l021] Mede, zzuru, eta. *Deleitarſe, ou compra-*
[c2-l021 note] Mede, medzuru, medeta means taking delight or pleasure in something. Printed compra- continues as comprazerſe below; it is not an independent word.
[c2-l022 >] *zerſe dalgũa couſa. Vt,* Cujacuuo fajimete
[c2-l022 note] Completes comprazerſe dalgũa couſa and introduces the peacock example. Cujacuuo fajimete means seeing a peacock for the first time in the sentence continued below.
[c2-l023 >] miqeru fitoua, ſono itçucuxiſani mede-
[c2-l023 note] The people who saw it were captivated by its beauty: miqeru fitoua and itçucuxiſani replace the malformed OCR. Fitoua is abraded but identifiable; the t after i in itçucuxiſani is nearly lost, so that letter remains a nonblocking contextual reconstruction rather than a fully clear impression.
[c2-l024 >] te qimouo qexi, aqire fatçurubacari nari.
[c2-l024 note] Te completes medete, followed by losing one’s composure and becoming utterly astonished. The phrase’s historical forms are retained rather than recast into modern Japanese.
[c2-l025 >] *Os que virão a primeira vez o pauão recrea-*
[c2-l025 note] The Portuguese translates the people’s first sight of the peacock. Recrea- begins the description of their delight; virão retains the historical printed ending.
[c2-l026 >] *dos, & leuados de ſua fermoſura ficarão ato-*
[c2-l026 note] They are delighted and carried away by its beauty, and become ato- across the line break. Fermoſura is the source’s spelling of beauty.
[c2-l027 >] *nitos. ¶* Fiqidemononi mezzuru. *Ser*
[c2-l027 note] Nitos completes atonitos, astonished. Hikidemono ni medzuru then introduces a different application: being swayed by gifts.
[c2-l028 >] *mouido de dadiuas, ou ſobornado de peitas.*
[c2-l028 note] The Portuguese makes the inducement explicit: moved by gifts or bribed with peitas. This is the morally negative extension of taking pleasure in something.
[c2-l029 >] *¶* Fanano iro cani mezzuru. *Goſtar de*
[c2-l029 note] Hana no iro ka ni medzuru means delighting in flowers’ color and fragrance. The printed separation iro cani is retained, and the translation continues below.
[c2-l030 >] *ver, & cheirar as flores, & roſas.*
[c2-l030 note] Seeing and smelling flowers and roses complete the illustration of delight. The two senses correspond to color and fragrance in the Japanese phrase.
[c2-l031] Medetai. *Couſa alegre, ou de bom ſucceßo.*
[c2-l031 note] Medetai describes something joyful or fortunate. The print has ſucceßo, without the extra t in the provisional ſucceßto.
[c2-l032 >] *¶ Item, Palaura com que ſe dão as graças*
[c2-l032 note] A second usage treats medetai as a formula of thanks or congratulation. Its Portuguese construction continues with para bens on the next row.
[c2-l033 >] *e para bens dalgũa couſa.*
[c2-l033 note] Completes the congratulatory usage, with the historical separated para bens. Dalgũa couſa states what the congratulations concern.
[c2-l034 >] Medetaſa.
[c2-l034 note] Medetasa is the abstract noun corresponding to medetai, joyfulness or felicity. Its separate indented physical line is preserved without adding a gloss.
[c2-l035 >] Medetǒ.
[c2-l035 note] Medetǒ gives the corresponding contracted adverbial or formulaic form. The clearly downward accent remains a caron; no explanatory words are supplied to the source text.
[c2-l036] Medo. *O adeuinhar, ou conjeiturar com cere-*
[c2-l036 note] Medo concerns divination or conjecture performed through ceremonies. The Portuguese cere- continues as ceremonias on the next row.
[c2-l037 >] *monias ſuperſticioſas ſobre a vida, fortuna,*
[c2-l037 note] The ceremonies concern a person’s life and fortune. The evaluative adjective ſuperſticioſas is the dictionary’s own characterization.
[c2-l038 >] *ou ſucceßo dalguem. ¶* Medouo toru.
[c2-l038 note] The list concludes with someone’s outcome or success. Medo wo toru begins an example of performing this kind of divination.
[c2-l039 >] *Ver, ou cõjeiturar a vida, ou ſucceßo decada hũ*
[c2-l039 note] The example is translated as discerning or conjecturing each person’s life or outcome. The abbreviated cõjeiturar and hũ are preserved without expansion.
[c2-l040 >] *cõ estas ceremonias, & obſeruãdo certos põtos,*
[c2-l040 note] The prediction uses ceremonies and observation of certain points. The tilde abbreviations cõ, obſeruãdo and põtos remain on their printed carriers.
[c2-l041 >] *ou linhas judiciarias q̃ cõſiderão os Astrologos.*
[c2-l041 note] The observed marks are divinatory lines considered by astrologers. The abbreviations q̃ and cõſiderão are retained, without adopting the gloss’s theory as a factual explanation.
[c2-l042] Medori. *Aue femea.*
[c2-l042 note] Medori is a female bird. The gloss is generic ave femea, not a specific claim that only a domestic hen is intended.
[c2-l043] Mefajiqi. *Meliùs, Memaje. O dar de olho.*
[c2-l043 note] Mehajiki denotes signaling with the eye. Meliùs recommends Memaje as the preferable form; Memaje is a Japanese term embedded in the italic Portuguese apparatus.
[c2-l044 >] *¶* Mefajiqiuo ſuru. *Dar ſinal com o olho.*
[c2-l044 note] Mehajiki wo suru means giving a sign with the eye. The visible opening paragraph sign was omitted by the draft and is restored before the Japanese example.
[c2-l045] Mefitçuji. *Ouelha.*
[c2-l045 note] Mehitsuji is a female sheep, directly glossed Ouelha. The historical tç spelling is retained.
[c2-l046] Mega. *Cerua, ou veado femea.*
[c2-l046 note] Mega is a female deer, described by both cerua and veado femea. This is another female-animal formation rather than the eye-plus-particle sequence of earlier examples.
[c2-l047] Megaqete. *Pondo os olhos em algũa couſa. Vt,*
[c2-l047 note] Megakete means directing one’s eyes or aim toward something. Vt introduces an example that continues on f313; the separate Mega catchword is page furniture, not an extra body row.
