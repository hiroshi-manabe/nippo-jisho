[c1-l001] Racurui. Votçuru namida. *Lagrimas que ca-*
[c1-l001 note] Racurui denotes falling tears, matching the Japanese paraphrase. The initial is R rather than OCR C; this headword also identifies the preceding page's catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *yem dos olhos.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes ca-/yem dos olhos, tears falling from the eyes. The initial has the descending stroke of y, not the OCR v; the historical cayem is retained rather than modernized to caem.
[c1-l003] Racuſacu. *Sobejos como de comer, &c. que fi-*
[c1-l003 note] Racusacu names leftovers, particularly food scraps. The following rows explain what remains after dividing or carving something.
[c1-l004 >] *cão dalgũa couſa que ſe parte, ou trincha depo-*
[c1-l004 note] Completes ficão and describes leftovers from something cut or carved. The printed division depo-/is and the tilde carrier in dalgũa are preserved.
[c1-l005 >] *is de tirar o melhor. ¶* Racuſacuuo furu-
[c1-l005 note] Completes depois and specifies what remains after the best has been taken. The Japanese example begins offering these leftovers to someone.
[c1-l006 >] mǒ. *Conuidar a alguem com eſtes ſobejos.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes furumǒ, entertaining or treating someone with the leftovers. The long-o mark is a caron and the Portuguese supplies the hospitality sense.
[c1-l007] Racuxa. Tanoximu mono. i, Racuuo ſuru
[c1-l007 note] Racuxa is a person who enjoys pleasure, with two synonymous Japanese descriptions. Its initial R corrects OCR B; the second description continues below.
[c1-l008 >] mono. *Peßoa dada à deleites, & goſtos.*
[c1-l008 note] Mono completes the person-denoting phrase. The Portuguese characterizes someone devoted to delights and pleasures, retaining its printed à.
[c1-l009] Racuxo. Votçuru tocoro. *Lugar pera onde*
[c1-l009 note] Racuxo first denotes the place to which someone has fled or taken refuge. The Japanese falling-place paraphrase is interpreted through the following Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l010 >] *hum ſe acolheo, ou fugio.* Racuxo ſomoſan.
[c1-l010 note] Completes the refuge definition and gives a Japanese question about the fugitive's whereabouts. The question is a standalone example, not an embedded Portuguese typeface-toggle term.
[c1-l011 >] *Onde estarà o q̃ ſe acolheo, & fugio? ¶ Item,*
[c1-l011 note] Translates the question as asking where the person who fled may be. The paragraph sign introduces an additional figurative sense below.
[c1-l012 >] *per met. Falta, ou erro em que hum cahio.*
[c1-l012 note] The figurative sense is a fault or error into which someone has fallen. Enlargement distinguishes the shorter, cross-stroked t in Falta from the preceding l; this corrects the provisional Falla rather than modernizing the wording.
[c1-l013] Racuxo. Caqivotoſu. *Papel eſcrito, como*
[c1-l013 note] A separate Racuxo concerns a written paper or placard. Its explanation continues with a publicly posted lampoon, rather than the refuge sense above.
[c1-l014 >] *paſquim que ſe poem em publico contra alguem.*
[c1-l014 note] Specifies a pasquim displayed publicly against someone. The printed spelling poem is retained without supplying a modern accent.
[c1-l015 >] *Vt,* Racuxouo tatçuru. *Por paſquim.*
[c1-l015 note] The Japanese example means putting up the lampoon. Portuguese Por is the infinitive for placing here, not the preposition in an unrelated phrase.
[c1-l016] Racuyǒ. Miyaco miyaco. *Corte, ou cidade*
[c1-l016 note] Racuyǒ is glossed as the court or principal city. The repeated Miyaco miyaco is printed as the Japanese explanation and is not removed as duplication.
[c1-l017 >] *principal do reino.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes the description of the court as the kingdom's principal city. This is the capital-city sense of Racuyǒ.
[c1-l018] Racuyô. Votçuru fa. *Folha caida, ou o cair das*
[c1-l018 note] This distinct Racuyô denotes a fallen leaf or the falling of leaves. Its final circumflex differs from the preceding entry's caron; cair das has a real word boundary.
[c1-l019 >] *folhas.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes the falling-of-leaves definition. The single-word line remains separate from the new thunder entry.
[c1-l020] Rai. Caminari, l, icazzuchi. *Coriſco, ou trouão.*
[c1-l020 note] Rai is thunder or a thunderbolt, with two Japanese synonyms. The damaged a and medial m are restored from surviving strokes and the coherent forms; the headword is upright throughout, not partly italic.
[c1-l021 >] *¶* Raiga vochicacaru. *Cair coriſco ſobre*
[c1-l021 note] The example describes a thunderbolt falling upon something. Raiga, with a damaged a, replaces OCR Reniga; the attached ga is the subject particle.
[c1-l022 >] *algũa couſa.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes the object of the lightning strike as some thing. The faint interior vowel is read as u in couſa rather than the OCR's a.
[c1-l023] Rai. i, Rei. *Reuerencia, ou adoração. Vt,*
[c1-l023 note] A separate Rai is equated with Rei and means reverence or worship. The example marker Vt is italic with the Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l024 >] Raiſuru. *Adorar, ou reuerenciar.*
[c1-l024 note] Rai suru means to worship or revere. The repeated stem's a is abraded but recoverable; the printed joining Raiſuru is preserved.
[c1-l025] Raibin. Qitaru tayori. *Menſageiro que vem,*
[c1-l025 note] Raibin denotes an incoming messenger or opportunity for communication. The explanatory tayori is one word; the apparent internal gap comes from abrasion, not a separate ta.
[c1-l026 >] *ou portador que ha de vir.* S.
[c1-l026 note] Adds a bearer who is to come, completing the messenger definition. The final S. is an upright written-register label.
[c1-l027] Raibiǒ. i, Cattaino yamai. *Doença de lepra.*
[c1-l027 note] Raibiǒ is explained by the historical Japanese name for leprosy. The Portuguese confirms the disease identification; the historical wording is retained as source text.
[c1-l028] Raibon. Suricobachi. *Hũa manèira de gral*
[c1-l028 note] Raibon is a grinding bowl or mortar. The initial syllables read Rai-, not OCR Rui-. Enlargement confirms the small grave over e in manèira; it is preserved as a local printed accent rather than normalized away.
[c1-l029 >] *largo de barro em q̃ moẽ Miſo, moſtarda, &c.*
[c1-l029 note] Describes a broad earthenware bowl for grinding miso, mustard, and similar substances. Miſo is Japanese embedded in the Portuguese explanation and is marked for optional whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l030] Raica. Qitaru norimono. *O ir, ou vir em*
[c1-l030 note] This Raica denotes an honoured person's going or coming in a conveyance. The gloss continues with the litter or sedan-chair description below.
[c1-l031 >] *andas peßoa honrada.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the explanation as an honoured person travelling in a litter. The phrase remains Portuguese, not a separate Japanese example.
[c1-l032] Raica. Qitaru natçu. *O verão que vem.*
[c1-l032 note] A separate Raica means the coming summer. The explanatory initial is a damaged Q, not G; natçu provides the seasonal meaning.
[c1-l033] Raichô. *O vir ao reino de Iapão.* S.
[c1-l033 note] Raichô denotes coming to Japan. The headword bears a circumflex; the final S. is upright, while Portuguese Iapão stays in the italic gloss.
[c1-l034] Raicǒ. Qitari mucǒ. *Vinda, ou aparição de*
[c1-l034 note] Raicǒ is explained as coming to meet, introducing a religious appearance or reception. The two carons are preserved independently of modern romanization.
[c1-l035 >] *Amida, ou outro Fotoque a receber as almas. Po*
[c1-l035 note] Specifies Amida or another Buddha appearing to receive souls. Amida and Fotoque are Japanese names within Portuguese explanation; Po starts Pode without a secure edge hyphen.
[c1-l036 >] *de ſe accõmodar eſta palaura a Deos, & Anjos.*
[c1-l036 note] Completes Pode and says the expression can be adapted to God and angels. This is the compilers' proposed Christian usage, not an editorial replacement of the Buddhist sense.
[c1-l037] Raiden. Caminari, inabicari. *Trouão, ou*
[c1-l037 note] Raiden combines thunder and lightning in the Japanese explanation. The scan supports Caminari and Portuguese Trouão, rather than OCR Camimari and Tronão.
[c1-l038 >] *coriſco, & relampago. ¶* Raidenga ſu-
[c1-l038 note] Completes the storm terminology and begins the Japanese example Raidenga suru. The verb is physically divided ſu-/ru.
[c1-l039 >] ru. *Auer trouões, & relampagos.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes suru and translates the occurrence of thunder and lightning. Auer retains the historical spelling rather than modern haver.
[c1-l040] Raifai. *O adorar, ou fazer reuerencia.*
[c1-l040 note] Raifai denotes worship or paying reverence. Its medial f belongs to the historical Japanese spelling and is not replaced by long s.
[c1-l041] Raifan. *Hum como estrado, ou aßento redon-*
[c1-l041 note] Raifan is described as a platform or round seat used for worship. The shape adjective is divided redon-/do at the physical boundary.
[c1-l042 >] *do hum pouco alto, ſobre que fazem adoração*
[c1-l042 note] Completes round and says the seat is slightly raised, with worship performed upon it. The religious setting is completed on the next row.
[c1-l043 >] *diante do Fotoque.*
[c1-l043 note] Locates the act of worship before the Buddha. Fotoque is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese sentence, not a standalone headword here.
[c1-l044] Raifaru. *Meliùs,* Raixun. i, Qitaru faru.
[c1-l044 note] Raifaru means the coming spring; the apparatus recommends Raixun as the better form. The italic Meliùs and its grave are retained, while the recommended headword begins R, not B.
[c1-l045 >] *A primauera que vem.*
[c1-l045 note] Translates the coming spring. The worn interior vowel supports primauera; the OCR's primau ra represented abrasion, not two printed words.
[c1-l046] Raifǔ. Qitaru caje. *Vento que vem vẽ-*
[c1-l046 note] Raifǔ denotes an approaching wind. The scan supplies the missing vowel and caron in the headword; the Portuguese vẽ-/tando retains abbreviation and physical division.
[c1-l047 >] *tando.* S.
[c1-l047 note] Completes the wind-blowing description. The short final row is body text followed by an upright register label, not bottom furniture.

[c2-l022] Raiqua. Icazzuchino fi. *Coriſco, ou relam-*

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *ſe*
