[c1-l001 >] *Item, Qualquer couſa de apetite quando ſe*
[c1-l001 note] This continues Sacana from the preceding page with another sense: an appetizing food eaten in connection with drinking. Item is printed in full here.
[c1-l002 >] *come pera beber ſobre ella.*
[c1-l002 note] The food is eaten so that one may drink afterward or with it. This completes the broader drinking-accompaniment sense, rather than restricting Sacana to fish.
[c1-l003] Sacanna. *Couſa prospera, & florente, ou que*
[c1-l003 note] Sacanna describes something flourishing, prosperous, or vigorous. Prospera is a continuous printed word, correcting the draft's artificial split pros pera; its short s is retained.
[c1-l004 >] *eſtà em ſeu vigor. ¶* Toxino ſacanna fi-
[c1-l004 note] The definition continues with being in full vigor. The Japanese example begins a phrase for a person in the prime of life, with fito divided at the margin.
[c1-l005 >] to. *Homem que eſta naflor da idade.*
[c1-l005 note] To completes fito. The Portuguese describes a man in the flower of his age; the scan reads flor, not fior, while the tightly set naflor is retained.
[c1-l006 >] *¶* Ixei ſacanni gozaru. *Eſtar naforça da*
[c1-l006 note] The example describes being at the height of power or prosperity. The printed joining naforça is preserved instead of silently regularizing the Portuguese spacing.
[c1-l007 >] *proſperidade, & bonança.*
[c1-l007 note] Prosperity and favorable circumstances complete the gloss of the preceding Japanese example. Bonança here describes good fortune rather than a literal calm at sea.
[c1-l008] Sacanobori, ru, otta. *Ir contra a corrente da-*
[c1-l008 note] Sacanobori means ascending against a river's current. The historical principal parts and the printed line-end division da-/goa are retained.
[c1-l009 >] *goa em algum rio, ou correrem as agoas pera*
[c1-l009 note] The gloss covers both traveling upriver and the water itself flowing upward. Agoa retains the dictionary's spelling, here divided after the preceding da-.
[c1-l010 >] *cima com a força da marẽ, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] The water can run upstream under tidal pressure. The draft's marẽ is retained: the small, thickened mark is ambiguous between a nasal mark and an accent, so its precise shape remains uncertain.
[c1-l011] Sacaqi. i, Camino mayeni tatçuru qi. *Hũ*
[c1-l011 note] Sacaqi is explained in Japanese as a tree set before a deity. The synonym phrase is roman, while Hũ begins the Portuguese definition without expanding its nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l012 >] *pao, ou aruore q̃ ſe aleuanta, ou planta diãte*
[c1-l012 note] The Portuguese describes a piece of timber or tree raised or planted before a sacred place. The abbreviated q̃ and diãte remain literal.
[c1-l013 >] *dos Camis, ou diante deſua ermida.*
[c1-l013 note] The location is before the Camis or their shrine. Camis is embedded in the Portuguese explanation; enlargement supports retaining the draft's tightly set deſua rather than adding a word space.
[c1-l014] Sacari. *Força, & vigor dalgũa couſa. Vt,*
[c1-l014 note] Sacari is the strength or prime condition of something. The gloss introduces examples of this peak or full-vigor sense rather than the separate animal-heat entry below.
[c1-l015 >] Fana zacari. l, fanano ſacari. *Tempo*
[c1-l015 note] Fana zacari and fanano ſacari name the height of flowering. The alternative is introduced by the roman l, and the first form's printed word separation is retained.
[c1-l016 >] *em que eſtão em ſeu vigor as roſas, ou flores.*
[c1-l016 note] The Portuguese defines the time when roses or flowers are in full bloom. This completes the botanical example of Sacari.
[c1-l017 >] *¶* Toxino ſacari. *Flor, & vigor daida-*
[c1-l017 note] A visible paragraph mark introduces Toxino ſacari, the prime of life; it was omitted from the draft. The Portuguese's tightly set daida- continues as de below.
[c1-l018 >] *de, &c.*
[c1-l018 note] This finishes idade and closes the preceding example with &c. The final abbreviation contains c, correcting the draft's &e without joining the physical lines.
[c1-l019] Sacari, ru, atta. *Vir, ou ſer o tempo do cio dos*
[c1-l019 note] This verbal entry concerns animals coming into heat. Its printed principal parts include ru, unlike the abbreviated neighboring entry below.
[c1-l020 >] *animaes. Vt,* Necoga ſacaru. *Andar o-*
[c1-l020 note] Necoga ſacaru illustrates a cat being in heat. The visible terminal mark after o is retained even though it falls after the Portuguese article.
[c1-l021 >] *gato com ocio.*
[c1-l021 note] The cat example ends with com ocio, meaning being in heat in this context. The draft's joined spacing is retained; it is not interpreted as idleness.
[c1-l022] Sacari, u, atta. *Vt,* Cuchiga ſacaru. *Sẽ-*
[c1-l022 note] This entry describes an unpleasant astringent sensation in the mouth. The scan really prints the abbreviated second principal part u; no expected r is supplied.
[c1-l023 >] *tir a boca desabrida, & com desſabor dalgũa*
[c1-l023 note] The mouth feels unpleasant or ill-tasting. Desabrida has a visible short s, correcting the draft's r; the neighboring mixed short-s/long-s spelling desſabor is preserved.
[c1-l024 >] *couſa que ſe come azeda, &c.*
[c1-l024 note] The sensation is attributed to something sour that one eats. This closes the mouth-related sense and distinguishes it from the preceding animal-heat sense.
[c1-l025] Sacaſama. l, Sacaſamani. *Adu. Ao reues,*
[c1-l025 note] Sacaſama and Sacaſamani describe reversal or an upside-down orientation. The adverb label Adu. belongs to the italic Portuguese apparatus; the alternative forms remain roman.
[c1-l026 >] *ou às aueßas, ou cabeça abaixo.*
[c1-l026 note] The alternatives mean the wrong way round or head downward. The printed double-s form in aueßas is retained rather than expanded or modernized.
[c1-l027] Sacatçubo. *Iarra, ou boyão de vinho.*
[c1-l027 note] Sacatçubo denotes a wine jar or pot. Iarra preserves the printed initial I used where modern Portuguese writes J, and boyão is not normalized.
[c1-l028] Sacate. *O que ſe paga pollo vinho.*
[c1-l028 note] This Sacate is the money paid for wine. It is a distinct sense from the reversed handgrip introduced in the next entry.
[c1-l029] Sacate. *O tomar algũa couſa na mão ao modo*
[c1-l029 note] The second Sacate describes holding something in the hand in a particular reversed grip. The explanatory comparison with a dagger follows on the next line.
[c1-l030 >] *de apunhar com adaga. Vt,* Vaqizaxiuo
[c1-l030 note] The grip is likened to grasping a dagger for stabbing. Vaqizaxiuo begins the Japanese example, with the object marker joined as printed.
[c1-l031 >] ſacateni nuqu. *Arrancar a adaga deſta ma*
[c1-l031 note] The example means drawing the short sword with this reversed grip. The Portuguese word maneira continues below, but no terminal hyphen is visible after ma.
[c1-l032 >] *neira como quem quer dar de punhaladas.*
[c1-l032 note] The grip is described as that of someone about to deliver dagger thrusts. This completes maneira and the example without changing the physical line division.
[c1-l033] Sacate vtçu. *P. Batendo as palmas rogar pragas.*
[c1-l033 note] Sacate vtçu is explained as clapping the hands while uttering curses. P. introduces the poetic-use explanation and is italic here, not a detached roman terminal label.
[c1-l034] Saca uoqe. *Certo vaſo como tina ou barça pe-*
[c1-l034 note] Saca uoqe names a vessel for wine, compared with a tub or similar container. The printed separation within the Japanese headword is retained.
[c1-l035 >] *ra vinho.*
[c1-l035 note] This finishes pera and identifies the vessel's use for wine. The short physical continuation belongs to Saca uoqe, not to the adjective below.
[c1-l036] Sacaxij. *Valente, ſão, & forte, &c.*
[c1-l036 note] Sacaxij is first glossed as vigorous, healthy, and strong. Its next sense is explicitly regional and should not replace this initial physical-strength meaning.
[c1-l037 >] *¶ Item, No Cami he o meſmo q̃ Caxicoi, pru-*
[c1-l037 note] In the Cami region the word is said to mean Caxicoi, clever or prudent. Both Japanese terms occur within the Portuguese explanation and retain its visible italic setting.
[c1-l038 >] *dente, & ſagaz.*
[c1-l038 note] The regional gloss ends with prudent and sagacious. Dente completes the divided prudente rather than constituting a separate word or entry.
[c1-l039 >] Sacaxiſa.
[c1-l039 note] This is the nominal derivative of Sacaxij, expressing the corresponding quality. Its separate indented physical line and roman type are preserved.
[c1-l040 >] Sacaxǔ.
[c1-l040 note] This is the corresponding adverbial form. The visible caron over u is retained, rather than substituted with a circumflex or modern long-vowel notation.
[c1-l041] Sacaxira. i, Nacagoto. *Discordias, ou*
[c1-l041 note] Sacaxira is equated with Nacagoto and introduced as discord or ill will. The synonym after i, is an independent Japanese expression, not an embedded Portuguese term.
[c1-l042 >] *mal querença que hum cauſa entre outros. Vt,*
[c1-l042 note] The discord is specifically that which someone causes between other people. The two-word spelling mal querença is preserved before the example marker.
[c1-l043 >] Sacaxirauo yǔ. *Semear diſcordias.*
[c1-l043 note] The example means sowing discord. The Japanese object phrase remains joined as printed, and yǔ retains its caron.
[c1-l044] Sacaya. *Adega, ou tauerna, & caſa onde ſe faz,*
[c1-l044 note] Sacaya can be a wine storehouse, tavern, or establishment where wine is made. The Portuguese uses vinho for the local alcoholic drink without requiring a grape-wine identification.
[c1-l045 >] *ou vende vinho.*
[c1-l045 note] Selling wine completes the alternative functions of a Sacaya. This continuation belongs to the shop or brewery entry, not to the following anatomical headword.
[c1-l046] Sacayaqi. *Parte da cabeça tirando as fontes,*
[c1-l046 note] Sacayaqi names the head area excluding the temples, with another excluded part following below. The faint final letters of the headword agree with its repeated examples.
[c1-l047 >] *& toutiço. ¶* Sacayaqiuo ſoru. *Rapar*
[c1-l047 note] The nape is also excluded, and the example means shaving this part of the head. Sacayaqiuo replaces the draft's Sacayaqico; its worn u is supported by the clear repetition in the next column.

[c2-l001 >] *esta parte da cabeça, ſ. moleira, & cucuruta.*

[c2-l005] Sacaye, uru, eta. *Eſtarem as aruores, &*
[c2-l005 note] This Sacaye means flourishing, initially of trees and vegetation. The plural Portuguese construction begins with trees and continues with herbs or grasses.
[c2-l006 >] *eruas verdes, & viçoſas. ¶ Item, per-*
[c2-l006 note] The plants are green and vigorous. A new paragraph introduces a metaphorical extension; the line-end hyphen after per is visible and retained.
[c2-l007 >] *met. Proſperar. Vt,* Ixeini ſacayuru. *Es-*
[c2-l007 note] The figurative sense is prospering, illustrated by flourishing in power or status. The print divides Estar as Es-/tar, retaining the short s of the draft.
[c2-l008 >] *tar proſpero, & com poder. ¶* Tomi ſa-
[c2-l008 note] The first example means being prosperous and powerful. A second begins Tomi ſacayuru, flourishing in wealth, with the Japanese verb divided at the margin.
[c2-l009 >] cayuru. *Florecer em riquezas.*
[c2-l009 note] This completes the example of flourishing in riches. Florecer is retained with one sibilant-free stem as printed, rather than modernized to florescer.
[c2-l010] Sacaye, uru, eta. *Meliùs,* Toriſacayuru. *Im-*
[c2-l010 note] A separate Sacaye entry concerns intervening in a fight, with Toriſacayuru preferred. Meliùs is italic editorial language; the complete Im- of Impedir is also italic, correcting the split font assignment.
[c2-l011 >] *pedir, ou apartaraos que pelejão.*
[c2-l011 note] The gloss means preventing or separating people who are fighting. The line is physically indented, correcting its placement in the draft; the tightly printed apartaraos remains unchanged.
[c2-l012] Sacayuqi, u. *Ir florecendo, ou proſperando.*
[c2-l012 note] Sacayuqi describes continuing to flourish or prosper. The scan supports florecendo, correcting the draft's flarecendo, while the abbreviated principal part u is preserved.
[c2-l013 >] *Vt,* Miyomo ſacayuqu. *Ir ſe dilatando,*
[c2-l013 note] The example speaks of the realm or reign continuing to flourish. The Portuguese begins with expansion and completes the prosperity sense on the next line.
[c2-l014 >] *& proſperando o imperio.*
[c2-l014 note] The empire's prosperity completes the preceding example. The sentence remains a continuation, not a new entry or independent assertion about a historical ruler.
[c2-l015] Sacazzuqi. *Taça, ou copo. ¶* Sacazzu-
[c2-l015 note] Sacazzuqi denotes a cup or drinking vessel. The example repeats the headword across the physical Sacazzu-/qi division, which is not silently joined.
[c2-l016 >] qiuo catamuquru. i, Nomu. *Beber*
[c2-l016 note] Tilting the cup is equated with Nomu, to drink. The separator after i has a visible comma tail, correcting the draft's period; the Japanese synonym remains roman.
[c2-l017 >] *pello Sacazzuqui.* S.
[c2-l017 note] The Portuguese says to drink from the Sacazzuqui, retaining this embedded spelling with qui rather than the headword's qi. The separate S. label is roman.
[c2-l018] Sacoſo. *Adu. Prouauelmente aßi, ou como*
[c2-l018 note] Sacoſo is glossed as probably so, or in that manner. The adverb label and explanation are italic, while the headword is roman.
[c2-l019 >] *ißo. Vt,* Sacoſo nagoriuoxǔ vomôtçu-
[c2-l019 note] The example concerns the sorrow of parting. The marked final vowel of nagoriuoxǔ is u with a caron, not the draft's i; the compound verb continues below.
[c2-l020 >] rǒ naredomo. Mon. *Parece que aßi de-*
[c2-l020 note] Rǒ completes the preceding divided Japanese verb. Mon. is a roman citation label, followed by a Portuguese translation expressing an inference and then a contrast.
[c2-l021 >] *uia de ter ſaudades, porem, &c.*
[c2-l021 note] The gloss says that there must have been longing or regret, but leaves the contrast abbreviated with &c. No missing conclusion is supplied.
[c2-l022] Sacu. *Couſa de engenho como dito ſutil, &c.*
[c2-l022 note] This Sacu is an ingenious production, such as a clever saying. The following example illustrates an invention or remark devised by the speaker.
[c2-l023 >] *Vt,* Vare fitotçuno ſacuuo iuǒzu. Mon.
[c2-l023 note] The speaker proposes to utter one ingenious saying. The future-intention form iuǒzu and the attached object marker are retained; Mon. is a separate citation label.
[c2-l024 >] *Eu direi hum dito, ou hũa inuenção minha.*
[c2-l024 note] The translation is that the speaker will offer a saying or an invention of their own. Both hum and the nasal abbreviation hũa preserve the printed forms.
[c2-l025 >] *¶ Item, Sementeira, ou nouidade. ¶* Co-
[c2-l025 note] Another Sacu sense is a sowing or crop. Nouidade here means the agricultural yield, not news; the example begins the divided word Cotoxino.
[c2-l026 >] toxino ſacuga yoi. l, varui. *Ser a noui-*
[c2-l026 note] The alternatives describe this year's crop as good or bad. Yoi and varui are linked by l, while the Portuguese translation divides nouidade across the margin.
[c2-l027 >] *dade deſte anno boa, ou ruim. ¶ Item, O-*
[c2-l027 note] The crop example concludes with a good or poor harvest this year. A new paragraph introduces the sense of a work or something made, with Obra divided as O-/bra.
[c2-l028 >] *bra, ou feitura. Vt,* Coreua miga ſacu-
[c2-l028 note] Sacu is also a work or creation. Coreua miga ſacugia begins an example asserting that something is the speaker's work; the visible division is retained.
[c2-l029 >] gia. *Iſto he obra minha, ou feito por minha*
[c2-l029 note] Gia completes the Japanese assertion. The translation says that the object is the speaker's own work or was made by the speaker's hand.
[c2-l030 >] *mão. ¶* Deusnogoſacu. *Obra de Deos.*
[c2-l030 note] The first gloss ends with mão, then Deusnogoſacu illustrates God's work. The Japanese phrase incorporates Deus but is a standalone example, not an embedded term in Portuguese prose.
[c2-l031] Sacu. *Tranqueira, ou cerca que ſe faz de pao*
[c2-l031 note] A distinct Sacu entry denotes a wooden palisade or enclosure. The material is explicitly timber, and its defensive placement follows below.
[c2-l032 >] *ao derredor da fortaleza, ou de outro lugar.*
[c2-l032 note] The wooden barrier surrounds a fortress or another place. This completes the first enclosure sense before the construction example and river-barrier extension.
[c2-l033 >] *Vt,* Sacuuo yui mauaſu. *Fazer eſta trã-*
[c2-l033 note] The example describes constructing a palisade around a place. The Portuguese abbreviation and division trã-/queira are retained, without expanding the nasal mark.
[c2-l034 >] *queira, ou cerca em torno. ¶ Item, Esta-*
[c2-l034 note] The first example ends with making the surrounding enclosure. A new sense begins a stake barrier, divided as Esta-/cada at the margin.
[c2-l035 >] *cada que ſe faz nos rios pera não deixar paßar*
[c2-l035 note] The stake barrier is built in rivers to prevent passage. A final r is visible in paßar, correcting the truncated paßa of the draft.
[c2-l036 >] *embarcações. ¶* Sacuuo ſuru. *Fazer eſ-*
[c2-l036 note] The obstruction prevents boats from passing. Sacuuo ſuru illustrates making such a barrier; the translation continues across eſ-/ta.
[c2-l037 >] *ta eſtacada.*
[c2-l037 note] This completes the example meaning to make the river stake barrier. The short continuation retains both its indentation and the printed physical division.
[c2-l038] Sacubo. Qinôno cure. *Ontem à tarde, ou on-*
[c2-l038 note] Sacubo is equated with Qinôno cure, yesterday evening. The Portuguese includes late afternoon and night, with ontem divided across the margin.
[c2-l039 >] *tem à noite.*
[c2-l039 note] The alternative time is yesterday night. This finishes the Portuguese explanation without changing the accent on à or joining the divided ontem.
[c2-l040] Sacuban. Qinôno cure. *Idem.*
[c2-l040 note] Sacuban is another expression for yesterday evening or last night. Idem explicitly refers back to the immediately preceding definition.
[c2-l041] Sacubiǒ. Tçucuri yamai. *Doença fingida.*
[c2-l041 note] Sacubiǒ is a feigned illness, explained by the Japanese synonym Tçucuri yamai. The caron over o is retained in the headword.
[c2-l042 >] *¶* Sacubiǒuo ſuru. *Fingir doença.*
[c2-l042 note] The example means pretending to be ill. The repeated headword and joined object marker are roman, followed by the concise italic Portuguese translation.
[c2-l043] Sacubun. *Parte dalgũas terras que hum por ſi*
[c2-l043 note] Sacubun is a portion of land that someone cultivates personally. The last pronoun reads ſi, not the draft's ſe, matching the visible narrow final letter.
[c2-l044 >] *cultiua, ou ſemea.*
[c2-l044 note] The activities are cultivating or sowing that portion of land. This finishes the definition without adding any unstated legal or ownership condition.
[c2-l045] Sacuchǒ. Qinôno aſa. *Ontem polamenhaã.*
[c2-l045 note] Sacuchǒ means yesterday morning. Qinôno is one word despite the large capital Q; the final Portuguese word has two a types with the tilde on the last, and no extra final s is retained.
[c2-l046] Sacufan. *Meya nouidade. Vt,* Tauo ſa-
[c2-l046 note] Sacufan is a half share of the crop. Nouidade has its agricultural sense, and the Japanese example begins a statement about working a rice field on shares.
[c2-l047 >] cufanni ſuru. *Laurar a varzea de meas.*
[c2-l047 note] The example means cultivating the paddy field on a half-share basis. The older Portuguese de meas is preserved, and the running text ends before the separate catchword.
