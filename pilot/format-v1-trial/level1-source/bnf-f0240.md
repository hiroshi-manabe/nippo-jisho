[c2-l001] Guiǒgacu. Voconai, gacumon. *Virtude,*
[c2-l001 note] Gyogaku combines conduct or practice with learning, as Voconai and gacumon indicate. The Portuguese pair 'virtue and letters' continues across this row and the next.

[c2-l003] Guiogai. Catachi, cabane. *Figura, esta*

[c2-l004 >] *tua, ou corpo.*

[c2-l005] Guiogan. Vuono manaco. *Olhos de pei-*
[c2-l005 note] Gyogan is a fish's eye, explicitly explained by uwo no manako. Pei- begins the Portuguese peixe continued below.

[c2-l007 note] The fish's eyes resemble precious stones. The tight printed compedras is retained; the terminal S. is a roman usage label for written language, outside the italic gloss.
[c2-l008] Guiǒgiǔ. Yuqu. Giǔ ſuru. i, Tachij. *An-*
[c2-l008 note] Gyoju joins going and remaining, with tachii as an explanation. The enlarged middle Giǔ has a caron above its damaged u rather than the OCR grave; An- begins andar below.
[c2-l009 >] *dar, & estar. Vt,* Guiǒgiǔ zagua. *An-*
[c2-l009 note] Andar and estar translate going and remaining. The example adds zagua, extending the expression to the customary postures or activities.
[c2-l010 >] *dando, & eſtando dormindo, &c. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l010 note] The translation continues with walking, remaining, sleeping, and so forth. Item then introduces the broader temporal sense of the expression.
[c2-l011 >] *Sempre, ou em todo tempo.*
[c2-l011 note] Sempre and em todo tempo give the extended sense 'always' or 'at all times'. This completes the gyoju entry.
[c2-l012] Guiǒgǒ. i, Teiuǒno miyuqi. *Ida, ou ſai-*
[c2-l012 note] Gyogo is an imperial outing, explained by teiwo no miyuki. The final sai- continues as saida in the next row.
[c2-l013 >] *da del Rey, q̃ ſe faz cõ grande ſolennidade.*
[c2-l013 note] Da completes saida: the king's departure is conducted with great solemnity. The scan supports da, not OCR du; the abbreviated q̃ and cõ and the tight ſefaz are retained.
[c2-l014] Guiǒgô. Voconai xiuaza. *Obras, ou exer-*
[c2-l014 note] This differently marked Guiǒgô refers to deeds or exercises, explained by conduct and action. Its final circumflex must not be copied from the preceding entry's caron.
[c2-l015 >] *cicios.*
[c2-l015 note] Cicios completes exercicios. The one-word fragment belongs to the definition of deeds or exercises.
[c2-l016] Guiǒguan. Voconai negǒ. *Obras de vir-*
[c2-l016 note] Gyogan here concerns virtuous works performed with an aspiration. The o and its mark in the headword are almost erased but have compatible traces; Guiǒguan is chosen over the OCR's broken Gui guan, with support from the gloss and supplied lexical index.
[c2-l017 >] *tude que hum faz com deſejo da ſaluação.*
[c2-l017 note] Vir- plus tude forms virtude. The works are done with a desire for salvation; the long s in deſejo corrects OCR dejejo.
[c2-l018] Guiǒgui. *Modo de viuer, ou obras. Vt,*
[c2-l018 note] Gyogi denotes a way of living or one's deeds. The clear headword and its repetition below support Guiǒgui rather than OCR Gurǒgui.
[c2-l019 >] Guiǒguino tadaxij, l, yoi fito. *Homẽ*
[c2-l019 note] The example describes a person whose conduct is upright or good. Latin l introduces the alternative adjective; there is no printed paragraph sign at the beginning of this row.
[c2-l020 >] *recto, & virtuoso, ou de boa vida.*
[c2-l020 note] The Portuguese completes the example as an upright, virtuous person of good life. The sense is moral conduct rather than mere outward etiquette.
[c2-l021] Guiôgui. i, Itçuuari. *Mentira.*
[c2-l021 note] The distinct Guiôgui is glossed as deception or a lie through itsuwari and Mentira. The circumflex differentiates its printed form from the preceding conduct entry.
[c2-l022] Guiôguiôxij. i, Vobitataxij coto. *Couſa*
[c2-l022 note] Gyogyoshii describes something imposing, exaggerated, or extraordinary. The Japanese obitataxii explanation agrees with the Portuguese continuation.
[c2-l023 >] *grande, & façanhoſa, ou extraordinaria.*
[c2-l023 note] The Portuguese expands the sense as great, remarkable, or extraordinary. These adjectives complete the preceding headword's definition.
[c2-l024 >] *¶* Guiôguiôxij fito. *Homem encarece-*
[c2-l024 note] The example applies the adjective to a person who exaggerates or magnifies things. The scan reads fito, 'person', with f rather than OCR long s.
[c2-l025 >] *dor das couſas.*

[c2-l026] Guioi. Von coromo. *Veſtido del Rey.*
[c2-l026 note] Gyoi here means royal clothing, explained by on koromo. The scan supports coromo and Rey, correcting the OCR's corono and Ry.
[c2-l027] Guioi. Micocoro. *Mandado de peßoa nobre.*
[c2-l027 note] The second gyoi denotes the will or command of a noble person. Micocoro supplies the Japanese honorific explanation, and the headword is Guioi rather than Gutoi.
[c2-l028 >] *¶* Guioini macaſuru. *Conformarſe com*
[c2-l028 note] Gyoi ni makasuru means conforming to or leaving matters to the honoured person's will. The Portuguese construction continues with its object below.
[c2-l029 >] *a vontade, & mandado do ſenhor, ou peſſoa*
[c2-l029 note] The object is the will and command of a lord or honoured person. The final peſſoa belongs to this same Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l030 >] *honrada. ¶* Guioi xidai. *Como mãdar,*
[c2-l030 note] Honrada completes the preceding phrase. Gyoi shidai means as the lord commands or wishes; the initial h corrects OCR b, and the tilde in mãdar is retained.
[c2-l031 >] *ou quiſer o ſenhor, &c. ¶* Guioini ca-
[c2-l031 note] The first translation ends with the lord's wishes. A new example begins guioi ni ca-, continued as caqeraruru on the next row.
[c2-l032 >] qeraruru. *Vir peßoa nobre a minha caſa,*
[c2-l032 note] Qeraruru completes the Japanese verb. The gloss describes a noble person coming to one's house, with another favour added below.
[c2-l033 >] *ou dar me algũa couſa. ¶* Guioiuo vru.
[c2-l033 note] Giving the speaker something is the alternative favour. A further example, gyoi wo uru, introduces receiving counsel or consulting someone of rank.
[c2-l034 >] *Receber conſelhos, comunicarſe, ou perguntar*
[c2-l034 note] The gloss lists receiving advice, communicating, and asking. Its honoured interlocutor is specified on the next row.
[c2-l035 >] *a algum ſenhor, ou peßoa honrada. ¶* Guioi-
[c2-l035 note] The consultation is with a lord or honoured person. The following example evidently concerns gyoi ni iru, but the damaged printed start looks like Guiui-; that u-shaped vowel is retained rather than silently regularized to Guioi-.
[c2-l036 >] ni iru. *Estar em graça de algũa peßoa no-*
[c2-l036 note] Ni iru completes the example of entering a noble person's favour. The Portuguese no- continues as nobre below.
[c2-l037 >] *bre, ou agradarlhe. ¶* Guioini, l, guioi-
[c2-l037 note] The translation adds pleasing the noble person. A contrasting example then offers guioini or guioi- with a different particle in the continuation.
[c2-l038 >] uo ſomuqu. *Quebrantar o mandado, ou*
[c2-l038 note] Uo, not OCR no, completes guioi-uo somuku, the alternative to guioini somuku. Both refer to disobeying the honoured person's command.
[c2-l039 >] *deſcontentar ao ſenhor, &c.*
[c2-l039 note] The alternative Portuguese gloss is displeasing the lord. This closes the long entry on the honoured person's will.
[c2-l040] Guiǒja. Voconǒ mono. *Homem que ſe*
[c2-l040 note] Gyoja is a practitioner, explained as one who performs religious or virtuous acts. The Portuguese reflexive is ſe, not OCR ſo.
[c2-l041 >] *exercita na virtude, ou penitencia.*
[c2-l041 note] The practitioner exercises himself in virtue or penitence. The verb is exercita, correcting OCR exereita, and completes the relative clause above.
[c2-l042] Guiǒji. i, Voconai. *Officios, ou ceremonias.*
[c2-l042 note] Gyoji is explained by okonai and glossed as offices or ceremonies. This entry is distinct from the administrative-duty sense immediately below.
[c2-l043] Guiǒji. *Officio, ou cargo como de procura-*
[c2-l043 note] The next gyoji is an office, charge, or duty. Procura- continues as procurador on the following row.
[c2-l044 >] *dor, ou miſter dalgum pouo. ¶* Guioji-
[c2-l044 note] The gloss gives the example of a procurator or an office in a community. Guioji- begins the next Japanese phrase; the unmarked o in this occurrence is retained.
[c2-l045 >] ni ataru, l, guiǒjide gozaru. *Caber a*
[c2-l045 note] The alternatives mean having the duty fall to one, or holding that office. The scan reads gozaru with o, not OCR guzaru; Caber a begins the translation below.
[c2-l046 >] *alguẽ este officio, ou ter eſte cargo. ¶* Gua-
[c2-l046 note] The translation describes an office falling to someone or being held by him. Gua- begins a further compound continued on the final body row.
[c2-l047 >] chi guiǒji. *Officio, ou cargo deſta laya que*
[c2-l047 note] Gua-chi identifies the monthly duty, and the definition continues on the following page. The printed laya is one word; the separate bottom dura is a catchword, not another body row.
