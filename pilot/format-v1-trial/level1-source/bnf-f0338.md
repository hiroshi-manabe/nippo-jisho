## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Mucǒne. *O tornar a dormir depois de eſperto,*
[c1-l001 note] Mucǒne is returning to sleep after having awakened. The headword begins at the normal entry margin, not at the continuation indent.
[c1-l002 >] *& aleuantado.*
[c1-l002 note] The definition adds that the person had also got up; this short continuation belongs to the preceding sleep entry.
[c1-l003] Mucǒ qizu. *Feridas por diante. ¶* Mucǒ
[c1-l003 note] Mucǒ qizu denotes wounds received on the front of the body. The scan separates por diante; the example starts at the paragraph sign and continues below.
[c1-l004 >] qizuuo cǒmuru. *Receber as feridas no roſ-*
[c1-l004 note] The example with cǒmuru means to receive wounds to the face or front. The Portuguese word rosto is physically divided as roſ- / to.
[c1-l005 >] *to, ou por diante.*
[c1-l005 note] This completes rosto and repeats the alternative description “on the front”; it is not an independent entry.
[c1-l006] Mucǒzaſu. *Hum pao como aſna que ſe acrecẽ-*
[c1-l006 note] Mucǒzaſu is first explained as a timber like a rafter added to a beam. The printed entry starts flush with the other headwords.
[c1-l007 >] *ta à traue, que atraueſa na caſa, & não che-*
[c1-l007 note] The beam runs across the house; the added piece does not reach the location named on the next line. Preserve the printed atraueſa rather than regularizing its consonants.
[c1-l008 >] *ga tè a varanda. ¶ Item, Emulo, ou cõ-*
[c1-l008 note] The building sense ends with the veranda. A paragraph sign introduces a separate sense, a rival or opponent; the scan supports the grave accent in tè.
[c1-l009 >] *trario, que não he inimigo principal.*
[c1-l009 note] The second sense is qualified as an opponent who is not the principal enemy. The initial trario completes cõ- / trario across the physical line break.
[c1-l010] Mucǒzune. *Canelas das pernas.*
[c1-l010 note] Mucǒzune denotes the shins. The Portuguese explicitly says the shins of the legs, so the apparent resemblance to the preceding timber word must not collapse the entries.
[c1-l011] Mucu. l, mucuno qi. *Aruore aßi chamada.*
[c1-l011 note] Mucu, alternatively mucuno qi, names a tree. The Portuguese gives no species identification; retain the historical spelling rather than supplying a modern botanical label.
[c1-l012] Mucuchi. l, mucuchina. *Homem de pouco*
[c1-l012 note] Mucuchi and mucuchina describe someone of few words. The printed l, introduces the alternative form.
[c1-l013 >] *falar, ou que não ſabe falar.*
[c1-l013 note] The definition also allows someone who does not know how to speak; both alternatives qualify the preceding person, rather than describing a separate action.
[c1-l014] Mucugue. *Hũa certa roſa.*
[c1-l014 note] Mucugue is glossed as a particular rose or rose-like flower. The source does not specify the species in Portuguese, so no botanical expansion is inserted.
[c1-l015] Mucui, ǔ, ǔta. *Retribuir, ou darſe o me-*
[c1-l015 note] Mucui, with the supplied inflected forms, means to repay or give what is deserved. The gloss continues through the split me- / recido.
[c1-l016 >] *recido. Vt,* Toga nai codomoni chichi-
[c1-l016 note] After completing merecido, an example begins with innocent children and a father; chichi- is continued by no on the following physical row.
[c1-l017 >] no togauo mucǔ coto tada curumano me-
[c1-l017 note] The Japanese describes requiting the father’s fault upon the children and likens it to a turning wheel. The final me- belongs to meguru on the next line.
[c1-l018 >] guruga gotoqugia. Qir. *O cairem os*
[c1-l018 note] The wheel comparison ends here, followed by the upright citation label Qir. The Portuguese translation begins with the sins falling upon someone.
[c1-l019 >] *peccados dos pais por caſtigo ſobre os filhos in-*
[c1-l019 note] The Portuguese makes the inherited punishment explicit: the parents’ sins fall as punishment upon innocent children. The last word continues as in- / nocentes.
[c1-l020 >] *nocentes he como roda que anda às voltas.*
[c1-l020 note] The translation completes the image of a wheel continually turning. Its wording is retained as the dictionary’s moral comparison, not expanded into a doctrinal explanation.
[c1-l021 >] *¶* Tocuuo mucǔ. *Retribuir, ou dar pre-*
[c1-l021 note] Tocuuo mucǔ is to recompense goodness or virtue. The Portuguese begins the equivalent “give a reward,” with pre- continued below.
[c1-l022 >] *mio pollo bem que hũ fez. ¶* Vonuo mu-
[c1-l022 note] The reward is for good that someone has done. A new example, Vonuo mu- / cǔ, then turns to repaying benefits or favors.
[c1-l023 >] cǔ. *Retribuir os beneficios. ¶* Atauo
[c1-l023 note] The benefits example closes before the next paragraph sign. Atauo begins the contrasting construction about responding to harm.
[c1-l024 >] mucǔni tocuuo motteſu. *Auendo de pa-*
[c1-l024 note] The Japanese joins recompensing harm with the use of goodness. The Portuguese begins an explanation contrasting repayment in evil with doing good.
[c1-l025 >] *gar com males, antes fazer bem.*
[c1-l025 note] The contrast is completed: rather than paying back with evils, do good. The initial gar completes pa- / gar across the preceding line break.
[c1-l026] Mucui. *Retribuição. Vt,* Ien, acuno mu-
[c1-l026 note] The noun Mucui means repayment or recompense. Its example contrasts good and evil; the printed initial of Ien is retained, without substituting a modern romanization.
[c1-l027 >] cui. *Paga, ou retribuição dos bens, ou males.*
[c1-l027 note] This finishes the divided example word mucui and translates it as recompense for good or evil. The nominal entry is distinct from the verb above.
[c1-l028] Mucui cayexi, ſu, eita. *Retribuir, ou vin-*
[c1-l028 note] Mucui cayexi means to repay in return or avenge, with inflection in su / eita as printed. The Portuguese vingar is physically divided vin- / gar, not two words.
[c1-l029 >] *gar. ¶* Core ſono inguauo mucui ca-
[c1-l029 note] The example begins an explanation of repayment for a karmic cause. The damaged area after mucui does not support the OCR’s extra t; the scan shows mucui ca- continuing as cayesǒ.
[c1-l030 >] yesǒga tamede atta. Qir. *Iſto era pe-*
[c1-l030 note] The Japanese ends with a purpose construction and past atta, then the Qir. citation. The Portuguese explains that this was in order to repay something.
[c1-l031 >] *ra retribuir, ou ſatisfazer o que fez na idade,*
[c1-l031 note] The repayment concerns what someone did in a previous life. The scan supports ſatisfazer, with the small t overlooked by the draft recognition; the old wording is otherwise retained.
[c1-l032 >] *ou nacimento paßado, ſegundo a opinião dos*
[c1-l032 note] The Portuguese explicitly refers to a previous birth and attributes the belief to the group named on the next line. Keep nacimento as printed.
[c1-l033 >] *Bonzos.*
[c1-l033 note] Bonzos completes the attribution to Buddhist religious figures. This is a short running-text continuation, not a label or a new entry.
[c1-l034] Mucuinu. *Cão felpudo, ou cabeludo.*
[c1-l034 note] Mucuinu is a shaggy or long-haired dog. The two Portuguese adjectives explain its coat rather than naming a particular breed.
[c1-l035] Mucumeqi, u, eita. *Bulir, ou mouer o corpo,*
[c1-l035 note] Mucumeqi describes bodily stirring or movement. The following comparisons clarify movement beneath a covering or beneath the ground.
[c1-l036 >] *como quẽ està dormindo de baixo do fato, tou-*
[c1-l036 note] The first comparison is a sleeper stirring beneath bedding or covering. The last tou- begins the mole comparison completed on the next line.
[c1-l037 >] *peira de baixo da terra, &c. Vt,* Voſaye-
[c1-l037 note] The second comparison is a mole beneath the earth. An example then begins with Voſaye- / taru, something held or pressed down.
[c1-l038 >] taru fizauo aguento mucumeqi qeru aida,
[c1-l038 note] The example describes stirring to raise a knee that had been pressed down. Preserve the connected historical phrase and its printed final comma.
[c1-l039 >] &c. Taif. *Lib. 24. Mouendo o joe-*
[c1-l039 note] The example ends with &c., followed by the upright Taif. citation and italic book number 24. Its Portuguese translation begins with moving the knee.
[c1-l040 >] *lho ſobre que punha amão, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] The translation finishes “the knee on which he put his hand.” The scan supports the joined amão of the baseline; no modern word spacing is imposed.
[c1-l041] Mucuno cǒgai. *Hum inſtrumento de ouro co-*
[c1-l041 note] Mucuno cǒgai names a gold implement, described by comparison with a small knife. This is a different use of mucu from the adjacent tree entries.
[c1-l042 >] *mo caniuete que metem em hũa parte da bai-*
[c1-l042 note] The implement is placed in part of a sword sheath. The physical division bai- / nha is preserved and completed on the next line.
[c1-l043 >] *nha das Catanas.*
[c1-l043 note] Catanas specifies the swords whose sheaths hold the implement. The Japanese-derived term is embedded in the Portuguese explanation and remains italic as printed.
[c1-l044] Mucuno mi. *Fruita da aruore chamada Mucu.*
[c1-l044 note] Mucuno mi is the fruit of the tree called Mucu. The second occurrence of Mucu is embedded in Portuguese and is printed in the italic explanatory run.
[c1-l045] Mucunomi iro. *Azul eſcuro.*
[c1-l045 note] Mucunomi iro is a dark-blue color, expressed by reference to the tree’s fruit. No additional shade terminology is supplied by the Portuguese.
[c1-l046] Mucuno qi. *Aruore aſsi chamada.*
[c1-l046 note] Mucuno qi again identifies the tree by name. The printed aſsi is retained; it is not normalized to the different s-form of aßi elsewhere.
[c1-l047] Mucurenji. *Hũa fruita cuia caſca ſerue de*
[c1-l047 note] Mucurenji names a fruit whose shell serves as soap; the definition crosses to the top of column 2. The scan supports initial Mucu- and caſca, not the draft’s Macu- and cuſca.

[c2-l001 >] *ſabão. ¶ Item, A meſma aruore.*
[c2-l001 note] This completes the soap use from the preceding column. The paragraph sign adds the tree itself as a second referent of Mucurenji.
[c2-l002] Mucure, uru, eta. *Eſfolarſe, ou eſcascarſe*
[c2-l002 note] Mucure is the intransitive peeling or flaking verb. Its headword is flush with the entry margin; the draft continuation indent has no counterpart in the scan.
[c2-l003 >] *como caſca daruore, codea de parede, &c.*
[c2-l003 note] Examples are bark coming off a tree and a crust or surface layer coming off a wall. Keep daruore joined as printed.
[c2-l004] Mucuri, u, utta. *Actiuo. Eſcaſcar, &c.*
[c2-l004 note] Mucuri is explicitly marked active and means to peel or strip. It contrasts with the intransitive verb above and is a new flush-left entry.
[c2-l005] Mucuro. *Corpo ſem cabeça.*
[c2-l005 note] Mucuro is here defined specifically as a body without a head, rather than expanded to all senses of the modern word.
[c2-l006] Mucurôji. *Hũa fruita, cuja caſca ſerue de*
[c2-l006 note] Mucurôji names a fruit whose shell is used as soap. The scan supports caſca; the circumflex-shaped mark over o is retained from the aligned reading.
[c2-l007 >] *ſabão.*
[c2-l007 note] The single word ſabão completes the fruit’s stated use. It remains a body continuation, not a cross-reference or furniture.
[c2-l008] Mucutçuqe. *Vt,* Mucutçuqena mono. l,
[c2-l008 note] Mucutçuqe is illustrated by attributive forms with mono and, on the next row, votoco. The printed l, links the two example expressions.
[c2-l009 >] mucutçuqe votoco. *Homem que tem as*
[c2-l009 note] The gloss describes a man with the features specified on the following line. The worn word is Homem, not the OCR’s Honem.
[c2-l010 >] *feições agrestes, & que à viſta parece que*
[c2-l010 note] The man has rough or uncouth features and an alarming appearance. The Portuguese agrestes belongs to the source’s evaluation of those features.
[c2-l011 >] *mete medo.*
[c2-l011 note] The short continuation completes the frightening appearance: he seems to inspire fear. It closes the Mucutçuqe definition.
[c2-l012] Mudai. *Meliùs,* Mutai. Taimo nai. *Sem*
[c2-l012 note] Mudai is dispreferred in favor of Mutai, with Taimo nai as an explanation. The Latin editorial word reads Meliùs, not Meliàs; the Portuguese gloss continues below.
[c2-l013 >] *rezão, & ſem fundamento.*
[c2-l013 note] The definition is “without reason and without foundation.” This explains the Mutai expression, rather than the unrelated modern sense of mudai as untitled.
[c2-l014] Mudaini. *Meliùs,* Mutaini. *Adu. Sem*
[c2-l014 note] Mudaini is redirected to Mutaini and labeled an adverb. Reinspection prompted by the NINJAL lexical index confirms the faint i between a and n, omitted by the OCR; the spelling here is Mudaini, not Mudani.
[c2-l015 >] *razão, ou injuſtamente.*
[c2-l015 note] The adverb is explained as without reason or unjustly. The printed razão here is retained alongside rezão in neighboring entries.
[c2-l016] Mudaina. *Meliùs,* Mutaina. *Couſa ſem*
[c2-l016 note] Mudaina is redirected to Mutaina. The definition applies to either an unreasonable thing or, in the continuation, a person.
[c2-l017 >] *rezão, ou homem deſarezoado.*
[c2-l017 note] The person is described as unreasonable. Preserve deſarezoado and the source’s variation in the spelling of reason-related words.
[c2-l018] Mudame. *Primeiro ponto, ou riſca do Daihẽ onde*
[c2-l018 note] Mudame is the first mark of a weighing beam at which the counterweight balances it. The embedded scale name is read Daihẽ: the i is heavily abraded, and the following italic h is b-like. This chosen reading remains uncertain, rather than treating the OCR’s Dalhẽ as secure.
[c2-l019 >] *poſto o peſo carrega igualmente.*
[c2-l019 note] The counterweight placed at that first mark loads the balance equally. This completes the scale definition, not an example about an ordinary burden.
[c2-l020] Mudǒxin. *Coração pouco pio, & deuoto.*
[c2-l020 note] Mudǒxin is a heart lacking piety or devotion. The following example applies the term to religious life.
[c2-l021 >] *¶* Mudǒxinni xite xucqeua togue gata-
[c2-l021 note] The Japanese says that without a devotional heart it is difficult to fulfill the religious vocation. The last word is divided gata- / xi across the rows.
[c2-l022 >] xi. *Não auendo deuação, & coração pio,*
[c2-l022 note] The Portuguese begins “not having devotion and a pious heart.” Despite worn letters, auendo is a single word, not the draft’s a vendo.
[c2-l023 >] *ou religioſo não poderà durar.*
[c2-l023 note] The translation concludes that the religious person will not be able to persevere. Preserve the printed wording without forcing a word-for-word alignment with the Japanese.
[c2-l024] Mudoxinna. *Homem que não deſeja a ſalua-*
[c2-l024 note] Mudoxinna describes a person not seeking salvation. The scan shows a terminal hyphen in ſalua-, continuing as ção, rather than a sentence-ending period.
[c2-l025 >] *ção, nem tem deuação, nem obſeruancia da*
[c2-l025 note] The definition adds absence of devotion and observance. The tall letter in obſeruancia is long s, not the OCR’s f.
[c2-l026 >] *ley, &c.*
[c2-l026 note] The phrase closes with observance of the law. It is the final continuation of the irreligious-person definition.
[c2-l027] Mufan. *O não comer. Vt,* Qeſaua mada
[c2-l027 note] Mufan means not eating. The example begins “this morning, still ...,” leading to the statement of not yet having eaten.
[c2-l028 >] mufan degozaru. *Ainda esta menhãa não*
[c2-l028 note] The Japanese says the speaker is still without food, and the Portuguese specifies this morning. Enlargement places the tilde over the first of the two a letters in menhãa, despite the reference guide’s usual menhaã pattern. Retain this local spelling and the joined degozaru.
[c2-l029 >] *comi nada, ou eſtou em jejum.*
[c2-l029 note] The translation offers “I have eaten nothing” or “I am fasting.” The entry does not itself require a deliberate religious fast.
[c2-l030] Mufen. Fotori naxi. *Sem termino. Vt,*
[c2-l030 note] Mufen is explained by Fotori naxi and means without a boundary or limit. An example of boundless magnitude follows.
[c2-l031 >] Mufen quǒdai nari. *He infinito, & ſem*
[c2-l031 note] Mufen quǒdai nari is translated as infinite and without limit. The faint internal i in infinito is present; the OCR’s infinto omitted it.
[c2-l032 >] *termino.*
[c2-l032 note] Termino completes the limitlessness gloss. This one-word body line is not a new headword.
[c2-l033] Mufit. *O não ſaber eſcreuer. ¶ Item, Peſ-*
[c2-l033 note] Mufit denotes not knowing how to write. A second sense, beginning at the paragraph sign, denotes the person with that limitation.
[c2-l034 >] *ſoa que não ſabe eſcreuer. ¶* Mufit de-
[c2-l034 note] The person sense is completed and an example begins Mufit de- / gozaru. Preserve the printed split even though it cuts the polite copular expression.
[c2-l035 >] gozaru. *Não ſei eſcreuer.*
[c2-l035 note] The example is translated “I do not know how to write.” The short Japanese continuation gozaru belongs to de- on the preceding row.
[c2-l036] Mufitna. *Peſſoa que não ſabe eſcreuer.*
[c2-l036 note] Mufitna describes a person unable to write. The worn f agrees with Mufit immediately above, rather than the OCR’s r in Muritna.
[c2-l037] Mufo. i, Fôni fazzururu. *Diſcrepar da ley.*
[c2-l037 note] Mufo is glossed as departing from the law; Fôni fazzururu contains ni, not the OCR’s mi. The first headword vowel is worn and its baseline unaccented o is retained rather than supplying a mark by analogy.
[c2-l038 >] *Vt,* Mufouo voconǒ. *Fazer couſas cõ-*
[c2-l038 note] The example means performing unlawful actions. Preserve unaccented Mufouo as printed here and the caron in voconǒ; neither is harmonized with neighboring forms.
[c2-l039 >] *tra a ley.*
[c2-l039 note] This completes cõ- / tra a ley, “against the law.” It is an indented continuation, not a flush-left new entry.
[c2-l040] Mufǒjin. *Homem que não guarda nem tem*
[c2-l040 note] Mufǒjin is a person who does not observe the law or precepts. The worn f and visible caron support this reading, not Murǒjin.
[c2-l041 >] *deuer com ley ou preceitos.*
[c2-l041 note] The continuation specifies lack of regard for law or precepts. The initial of ley is lowercase l, not the OCR’s capital I.
[c2-l042] Mufǒna. *Idem.*
[c2-l042 note] Mufǒna has the same meaning as the preceding lawless-person entry. Both the u and f are supported by the surviving strokes, and the caron remains visible.
[c2-l043] Mufon. T*raição. ¶* Mufonuo cuuatatçu-
[c2-l043 note] Mufon means treason. The final consonant is n, not u; the following T begins Portuguese Traição, not a Japanese fragment. The example cuuatatçu- continues on the next row.
[c2-l044 >] ru. l, tacumu. *Vrdir, ou machinar traição.*
[c2-l044 note] The example gives cuuatatçuru or tacumu for devising treason. The Portuguese says to contrive or plot it; the line is a continuation despite the draft’s flush-left placement.
[c2-l045] Mufonjin. *Tredo.*
[c2-l045 note] Mufonjin is the treacherous person. The scan separates the final i and n rather than the OCR’s m, and Tredo is retained as the historical Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l046] Mufunbet. Funbet naxi. *Sem entẽdimento,*
[c2-l046 note] Mufunbet, explained by Funbet naxi, means lacking understanding. The gloss adds prudence on the next row.
[c2-l047 >] *& prudencia. ¶* Chicagoro mufũbetno
[c2-l047 note] After the prudence gloss, Chicagoro mufũbetno begins an emphatic example about a very imprudent person. The scan abbreviates n with a tilde in mufũbetno; do not expand it to mufunbetno.
