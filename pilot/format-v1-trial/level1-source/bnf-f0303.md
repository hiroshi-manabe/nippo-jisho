[c1-l001] Mairiai, ǒ, ǒta. *Encontrarſe no caminho cõ*
[c1-l001 note] Mairiai describes meeting someone while coming or travelling. The two carons in the principal parts are visible and must not be assimilated to the circumflexes in nearby verbs.
[c1-l002 >] *alguem. ¶ Item, Vir a tempo alguem de*
[c1-l002 note] Alguem completes the person encountered on the road. The second sense begins arriving at an opportune time, rather than a second unrelated meaning of the verb.
[c1-l003 >] *maneira que encontre com o que deſeja, como*
[c1-l003 note] The timely arrival allows one to encounter what one desires. The examples on the following row specify the occasion; encontre and deſeja belong to the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l004 >] *mißa, prègação, &c.*
[c1-l004 note] Mass and preaching exemplify the desired occasion reached in time. The historical spelling and the printed accent in prègação are retained, as is the double-s type in mißa.
[c1-l005] Mairiauaxe, ſuru, eta. *Vindo encontrarſe a*
[c1-l005 note] Mairiawase means coming upon something by chance. The printed principal parts ſuru and eta are kept rather than recast as a modern conjugation.
[c1-l006 >] *caſo cõ algũa couſa, como algũa feſta, mißa, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] Caſo completes a caſo from the preceding row. A feast or mass illustrates the chance encounter; the visible initial c restores como, lost in OCR omo.
[c1-l007] Mairicayoi, ô, ôta. *Ir, & vir.*
[c1-l007 note] Mairikayoi is going and coming, or attending repeatedly. The Portuguese Ir, & vir is a complete gloss; the circumflexed principal parts differ graphically from those in Mairiai.
[c1-l008] Mairitçudoi, ô, ôta. *Virem hũs depois dos*
[c1-l008 note] Mairitsudoi describes people coming together successively. Hũs depois dos begins the Portuguese construction meaning some after the others, continued on the next physical line.
[c1-l009 >] *outros com grande frequencia.*
[c1-l009 note] Outros completes hũs depois dos outros, with grande frequencia emphasizing repeated or numerous arrivals. This short continuation belongs to Mairitçudoi, not Mairitçuqi.
[c1-l010] Mairitçuqi, u, uita. *Vindo chegar.*
[c1-l010 note] Mairitsuki means reaching a destination on coming. Vindo chegar directly matches arrival; the abbreviated u and uita are retained as the printed principal-part endings.
[c1-l011] Maiſu. i, Fitouo taraſu mono. *Embaidor,*
[c1-l011 note] Maisu is a deceiver, explained by hito wo tarasu mono, someone who beguiles people. The Japanese synonym and Portuguese Embaidor identify a person, not the modern counting suffix mai.
[c1-l012 >] *ou enganador. ¶* Maiſuuo yǔ. *Embair,*
[c1-l012 note] Enganador completes the deceptive-person gloss. Maisu wo yuu then illustrates deceptive speech; the Portuguese Embair means to beguile, not to embark or send away.
[c1-l013 >] *ou enganar falando à võtade muitas couſas.*
[c1-l013 note] The example concerns deceiving by freely saying many things. À võtade is part of the Portuguese account of the speaker's behaviour; its nasal abbreviation is not expanded.
[c1-l014] Maiſumaxi, ſu, aita. *Bailar muito bem, ou*
[c1-l014 note] Mai-sumashi is dancing excellently or completing a dance. The aita principal part reflects the historical inflection and should not be rewritten as modern sumashita.
[c1-l015 >] *acabar de bailar. Vt,* Cocoromo voyo-
[c1-l015 note] The alternative sense is finishing the dance. Kokoro mo oyobanu begins an expression meaning beyond imagination; the scan shows bailar with i, not OCR ballar, and a real hyphen after voyo-.
[c1-l016 >] banu fodo mai ſumaita. Feiq. *Lib. 2.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes kokoro mo oyobanu hodo mai sumaita: the dance surpassed what one could imagine. Feiq. is the upright source citation; the following book reference is italic.
[c1-l017 >] *Bailou melhor do que ſe podia imaginar.*
[c1-l017 note] The Portuguese explicitly says that the person danced better than could be imagined. A visible dotted i supports Bailou rather than OCR Ballou.
[c1-l018] Maitan. *Cada menhaam. S.*
[c1-l018 note] Maitan means every morning. Menhaam is retained in its printed historical form, without replacing it by manhaã; the final upright S. marks written usage.
[c1-l019] Maixeqi. Yǔſagotoni. *Todas as tardes.*
[c1-l019 note] Maiseki is every evening, paraphrased by yuu-sa gotoni. The older Japanese evening expression is coherent here and should not be regularized to a modern yuugata phrase.
[c1-l020 >] *Vt,* Maixeqi idete ſuzumu. *Saindo ca-*
[c1-l020 note] Maiseki idete suzumu means going out each evening to enjoy the cool air. The italic Vt introduces the example; ca- begins cada on the following line.
[c1-l021 >] *da tarde tomar o freſco.*
[c1-l021 note] Da completes cada from the previous row. Tomar o freſco explains suzumu as enjoying the cool air, completing the evening-outing example.
[c1-l022] Maixǔ. Aqigotoni. *Em todos os Outonos,*
[c1-l022 note] Maishuu means every autumn, as aki gotoni and the Portuguese gloss confirm. The scan restores the i in Maixǔ, q in Aqigotoni, and t in todos; these are OCR errors, not historical variants.
[c1-l023 >] *ou cada Outono.*
[c1-l023 note] Ou cada Outono restates the preceding gloss as each autumn. It continues the same entry and must retain its own physical row.
[c1-l024] Maixun. Farugotoni. *Todas as primaſueras.*
[c1-l024 note] Maishun means every spring, paraphrased by haru gotoni. The headword visibly contains i. Enlargement confirms the anomalous long ſ in primaſueras; it is preserved instead of silently emending the print to primaueras.
[c1-l025] Maiya. Yogoto. *Cada noite. ¶* Maiya
[c1-l025 note] Maiya and yogoto both express every night. The repeated Maiya begins the following example, whose action is keeping a vigil at a temple.
[c1-l026 >] midǒni tçuyauo mǒſu. *Paſsar todas as*
[c1-l026 note] Maiya midou ni tsuya wo mousu describes nightly temple vigils. The printed long-vowel marks and Paſsar spelling are preserved; the Portuguese object continues on the next line.
[c1-l027 >] *noites vigiando no templo.*
[c1-l027 note] Noites vigiando no templo completes the explanation as spending the nights keeping watch in a temple. This is the conclusion of Maiya, not part of the following wind-name entry.
[c1-l028] Maje. *Vento ſul. No Ximo ſe diz,* Faye
[c1-l028 note] Maje denotes the south wind. The j descends below the line, unlike OCR Maie; the explanation gives Faye no caje as the Ximo regional expression. Ximo occurs within the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l029 >] no caje.
[c1-l029 note] No caje completes Faye no caje, the regional name of the south wind. This Japanese continuation belongs to the preceding gloss rather than constituting an independent entry.
[c1-l030] Maje, zuru, eta. *Misturar.*
[c1-l030 note] Maje, mazuru, mazeta gives the verb to mix. It is a separate homographic entry from the wind-name above; the Portuguese Misturar makes the distinction explicit.
[c1-l031] Majemono. *Miſtura, ou couſa confuſa, &*
[c1-l031 note] Mazemono is a mixture or mixed material. The gloss begins Miſtura, with a visible u rather than OCR a, and continues its description after the conjunction.
[c1-l032 >] *misturada.*
[c1-l032 note] Misturada completes couſa confuſa, & misturada: something confused or mixed together. The single-word continuation remains a separate physical line.
[c1-l033] Maji. l, majij. i, Mai. *He particula de fu*
[c1-l033 note] Maji and majii are equated with mai and described as a negative-future particle. The lowercase l introduces an alternative; fu continues as futuro without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l034 >] *turo negatiuo na pratica.* Majiqi, *He pera a*
[c1-l034 note] Turo completes futuro. The compiler contrasts conversational maji/majii with majiqi for writing; this is the dictionary's grammatical characterization, not an editorial modernization.
[c1-l035 >] *eſcritura. ¶* Arumajij cotono aruua qi-
[c1-l035 note] Eſcritura completes the written-register explanation. Arumajii koto no aru wa kiquaina koto ja then begins an example about something seemingly impossible occurring; qi- belongs to the divided adjective.
[c1-l036 >] quaina cotogia. *Auer couſa q̃ parece im-*
[c1-l036 note] Quaina completes qiquaina, strange or extraordinary. The Portuguese begins explaining the occurrence of something that appears impossible, with the abbreviated q̃ left unexpanded.
[c1-l037 >] *poßiuel he couſa duuidoſa, & que não ſe en-*
[c1-l037 note] Poßiuel completes impoßiuel from the previous row. Duuidoſa describes the event as doubtful or puzzling; both words are visibly recoverable from OCR piſtiuel and diuidoſa, without modernizing their historical spellings.
[c1-l038 >] *tende.*
[c1-l038 note] Tende completes entende, concluding that the puzzling event is not understood. This short fragment is body text and closes the grammatical example.
[c1-l039] Majinai. *Deprecaçoẽs, & ceremonias gentili-*
[c1-l039 note] Majinai denotes charms or incantations. The Portuguese labels them prayers and non-Christian ceremonies; that religious assessment belongs to the historical dictionary, not to the reviewer.
[c1-l040 >] *cas, ou feitiçarias.*
[c1-l040 note] Cas completes gentilicas. Feitiçarias adds sorcery or magical practices as the alternative gloss, completing the noun entry before the cognate verb.
[c1-l041] Majinai, ǒ, ǒta. *Fazer eſtas deprecaçoẽs.*
[c1-l041 note] Majinai is now the verb for performing the charms or prayers just described. The scan has a caron on ǒ but no corresponding mark on ota; the latter is not silently regularized.
[c1-l042] Majiri. *Canto do olho da parte de fora antre as*
[c1-l042 note] Majiri here names the outer corner of the eye. The Portuguese locates it between the upper and lower lash lines, rather than referring to mixture as in the later homograph.
[c1-l043 >] *duas pestanas. ¶* Magaxira. *He o ou-*
[c1-l043 note] The reference to the two eyelids or lash lines completes the outer-corner definition. Magaxira names the other corner of the eye; it is a Japanese contrasting term, not embedded Portuguese vocabulary.
[c1-l044 >] *tro canto do olho.*
[c1-l044 note] Tro completes outro: Magaxira is the other, inner corner of the eye. The short row concludes the anatomical comparison.
[c1-l045] Majiri. *Miſtura. ¶* Majiriuo naſu. *Mi-*
[c1-l045 note] Majiri is a mixture, and majiri wo nasu means to mix. Both occurrences visibly begin Majiri, correcting OCR Mairi and Najiriuo. The weak terminal mark after Mi is retained as the baseline hyphen; its precise shape remains uncertain.
[c1-l046 >] *ſturar.*
[c1-l046 note] ſturar completes Miſturar from the preceding row, translating majiri wo nasu. The fragment remains in place rather than being joined into an artificial single line.
[c1-l047] Majiri, u, itta. *Misturarſe. Vt,* Auoba-
[c1-l047 note] Majiri, majiru, majitta is the intransitive verb for becoming mixed. The example begins aoba, green leaves, and continues at the top of column 2; OCR Najiri misread the initial M.

[c2-l010 >] *lhos, ou peſtenejar.*

[c2-l012 >] miru. *Olhar com os olhos pera baixo.*

[c2-l014 >] ſuru. *Ter ẽtre ſi grãde amizade, l*, *liança.*
[c2-l014 note] ſuru completes the example: to have a close friendship or alliance with one another. The printed l introduces the alternative liança; neither nasal abbreviation is expanded.
[c2-l015 >] *¶* Majiuariuo naſu. *Ter copula carnal.*
[c2-l015 note] Majiwari wo nasu supplies the sexual sense, glossed as having carnal intercourse. It is an illustrative phrase under the same noun, not a separate conjugated headword.
[c2-l016] Majiuari, u, atta. *Miſturarſe. ¶* Xequẽ-
[c2-l016 note] Majiwari, majiwaru, majiwatta gives the verb to mingle. Xequẽ- begins seken, the worldly or social sphere, with an abbreviation tilde rather than an extra long-vowel mark.
[c2-l017 >] no chirini majiuaru. *Miſturarſe, ou an-*

[c2-l018 >] *dar de miſtura com as couſas vãas do mundo.*

[c2-l022] Majiye. *Miſtura.*
