[c1a-l001] Fiyori. i, Tenqi. *Conjunção de tempo. ¶* Fi
[c1a-l001 note] Hiyori is weather or favourable conditions, glossed by tenki. Conjunção de tempo refers to a conjunction of weather circumstances, not a grammatical conjunction; the final Fi continues on the next line.
[c1a-l002 >] yorigayoi, l, varui. *Ser bom, ou ruim*
[c1a-l002 note] With the preceding Fi, hiyori ga yoi / warui means that the weather is good or bad. The contrast is explicit in bom / ruim; the short printed alternative marker l is not Japanese.
[c1a-l003 >] *tempo pera nauegar, &c.*
[c1a-l003 note] The weather is suitable or unsuitable for sailing. Nauegar is navegar in modern spelling; pera and the historical u/v usage remain unmodernized.
[c1a-l004] Fiyu. Tatoye. *Comparação.*
[c1a-l004 note] Hiyu, explained by tatoye, is a comparison or simile: 比喩 and たとえ. The Portuguese comparação confirms that this is not the plant named in the next entry.
[c1a-l005] Fiyu. *Bredos.*
[c1a-l005 note] A separate hiyu means an edible leafy plant, matched with Portuguese bredos (amaranth greens). The identical Japanese spelling does not mean the two entries should be merged.
[c1a-l006] Fiyuna. *Idem.*
[c1a-l006 note] Hiyuna adds na, a vegetable/greens element, to the preceding plant name. The scan reads Idem, referring to bredos; OCR's Ident was not a different botanical description.
[c1a-l007] Fiza. *Ioelhos. ¶* Fizauo caganuru. *Do*
[c1a-l007 note] Hiza is the knee. Hiza wo kaganuru means to bend the knees; the historical verb form should not be replaced with modern kagameru. Do continues as dobrar below.
[c1a-l008 >] *brar os joelhos, ou abaixarſe hum pouco.*
[c1a-l008 note] The Portuguese explains bending the knees or lowering oneself a little. Hum is the historical form of um; the line does not prescribe a particular seated posture yet.
[c1a-l009 >] *¶* Fizauo tatçuru. *Porſe de joelhos ao*
[c1a-l009 note] Hiza wo tatsuru introduces a kneeling posture. The following lines are needed to understand what the dictionary calls the Japanese manner, rather than substituting a modern posture label.
[c1a-l010 >] *modo de Iapão que he estribãdo nos joelhos, &*
[c1a-l010 note] The posture supports the body on the knees, according to the dictionary's description. Estribãdo represents estribando; its nasal abbreviation and printed s form are retained.
[c1a-l011 >] *nas pontas dos pès deſcanſando as partes poſte-*
[c1a-l011 note] Support on the tips of the feet is also mentioned, with the posterior parts resting farther back. The adjective poste-riores continues into the next line; no missing Japanese phrase is implied.
[c1a-l012 >] *riores ſobre os calcanhares. ¶* Fizauo vo-
[c1a-l012 note] Calcanhares are heels, completing the posture description. The next Japanese example begins hiza wo wo-ru, bend the knees; vo represents o, while uo marks the object particle.
[c1a-l013 >] ru, l, cuſſuru. *Dobrar os joelhos. ¶* Fi-
[c1a-l013 note] The continuation completes woru, and kussuru is offered as an alternative, both translated as bending the knees. The two long s characters in cuſſuru belong to Japanese roman type, not the italic ß convention.
[c1a-l014 >] zauo cumu. *Aßentarſe com as pernas cru*
[c1a-l014 note] Hiza wo kumu means sitting with crossed legs. Aßentarſe is the Portuguese reflexive sit down, and cru continues as cruzadas; the unprinted line-end hyphen is not supplied.
[c1a-l015 >] *zadas. ¶* Fizagumiuo ſuru. *Idem.*
[c1a-l015 note] Hizagumi wo suru gives a noun-plus-suru expression for the same crossed-leg posture. Idem points back to the preceding explanation, not to the earlier kneeling description.
[c1a-l016 >] *¶* Tacafizauo tatçuru. *Pòrſe de joelhos*
[c1a-l016 note] Takahiza wo tatsuru introduces another kneeling posture. The visible paragraph sign and the faint r in Pòrſe were recovered; the next line specifies the European manner.
[c1a-l017 >] *ao modo de Europa. ¶* Fizamazzuqu.
[c1a-l017 note] Europa, not Eucopa, is legible and contrasts with Iapão above. Hizamazzuku is another expression for kneeling; the printed zz belongs to the historical dz series.
[c1a-l018 >] *Idem. ¶* Fizauo noburu. *Eſtender os*
[c1a-l018 note] Idem closes the preceding kneeling example. Hiza wo noburu then means stretching the knees or legs; noburu is a historical form and should not be modernized to nobasu in the transcription.
[c1a-l019 >] *joelhos, ou as pernas. ¶* Fizauo nauoſu. *Pòrſe*
[c1a-l019 note] The phrase hiza wo nawosu changes the subject to adjusting one's posture. Portuguese Pòrſe a sua vontade, continued below, means making oneself comfortable rather than medical treatment of the knee.
[c1a-l020 >] *a ſua vontade. Vt,* Fizauo nauoſaxerarei.
[c1a-l020 note] Hiza wo nawosaxerarei is a polite invitation to relax one's knees/posture. The historical honorific ending is supported by the following courteous Portuguese imperative; Vt is italic.
[c1a-l021 >] *Estai a voßa vontade deſcanſando os joelhos.*
[c1a-l021 note] Estai a voßa vontade tells the addressee to be at ease, resting the knees. This confirms the pragmatic, polite sense of the preceding Japanese example.
[c1a-l022] Fizabuxi. *Nòs, ou rodelas dos joelhos.*
[c1a-l022 note] Hizabushi names the joints or round structures of the knees. Nòs here means knots/joints, not the first-person pronoun nós; rodelas provides a second anatomical description.
[c1a-l023] Fizagaxira. *Rodelas dos joelhos.*
[c1a-l023 note] Hizagashira is the kneecap, literally the head of the knee. The Portuguese again uses rodelas dos joelhos, associating it with the preceding anatomical entry.
[c1a-l024] Fizaguchi. i, Fizano cuchi. *Idem.*
[c1a-l024 note] Hizaguchi is explained as hiza no kuchi, literally the knee's opening/mouth. Idem equates its referent with the kneecap terms above without requiring us to normalize the Japanese compound.
[c1a-l025] Fizamazzuqi, u, uita. *Pòrſe de joelhos.*
[c1a-l025 note] Hizamazzuki gives the conjugational series of kneel. The abbreviated u and uita are dictionary endings, not independent words; the full verb is the same as Fizamazzuqu above.
[c1a-l026] Fizamoto. *Iunto dos joelhos.*
[c1a-l026 note] Hizamoto means beside or at someone's knees. Iunto dos joelhos is a spatial explanation and leads naturally into the figurative court-attendant expression below.
[c1a-l027] Fizamotoſarazu. *O que ſempre eſtà a ilhar-*
[c1a-l027 note] Hizamoto sarazu is literally not leaving the vicinity of the knees. The definition describes someone always at a lord's side; ilhar-ga is split across lines.
[c1a-l028 >] *ga do ſenhor como priuado.*
[c1a-l028 note] The attendant is described as a privado, an intimate or favourite of a lord. This completes the figurative sense rather than adding a new headword.
[c1a-l029] Fizano ſara. *Rodela do joelho.*
[c1a-l029 note] Hiza no sara, the knee's plate, names the kneecap. The scan's sara initial is long ſ; the Portuguese singular rodela do joelho agrees with this anatomical sense.
[c1a-l030] Fizaxi. *O determinar o dia. ¶* Fizaxiuo
[c1a-l030 note] Hizashi here concerns fixing a day, not sunlight. O determinar o dia makes that homophony clear; hizashi wo continues into the next line's suru.
[c1a-l031 >] ſuru. *Determinar dia certo.*
[c1a-l031 note] Hizashi wo suru means to determine a particular day. The Portuguese dia certo confirms appointment/date-setting rather than an action involving knees.
[c1a-l032] Fizori, u, otta. *Empenarſe como taboa, &c.*
[c1a-l032 note] Hizori, with its printed conjugation series, is warping such as a board's. Empenarſe supports the intransitive sense; the unusual-looking spelling is not grounds for replacing the headword.
[c1a-l033] Fizzumacaxi, ſu, aita. *Entortar.*
[c1a-l033 note] Hizzumakashi is a causative/transitive form, to make something crooked. Its aita ending illustrates historical s-verb i-onbin; it should not be regularized to an ashita ending.
[c1a-l034] Fizzume. *Vnhas de caualo, & de outros ani-*
[c1a-l034 note] Hizzume is the hoof, described as the nails of a horse and other animals. This literal Portuguese wording is broader than the modern English distinction between nail and hoof.
[c1a-l035 >] *mais. ¶* Fizzumeno vareta qedamono.
[c1a-l035 note] Hizzume no wareta kedamono is an animal with a divided hoof. Wareta modifies the hoof, not a broken animal; the split Portuguese animais belongs to the preceding definition.
[c1a-l036 >] *Animal de vnha fendida.*
[c1a-l036 note] Animal de vnha fendida confirms a cloven/divided hoof. The singular vnha is the historical u-initial spelling, not a Japanese word needing a reading hint.
[c1a-l037] Fizzumi, u, unda. *Entortarſe, ou estar torto.*
[c1a-l037 note] Hizzumi is the intransitive counterpart, to become or be crooked. Entortarſe / estar torto distinguish process and state, and match the Japanese verb rather than the preceding hoof noun.
[c1a-l038] Fizzuqe. *Dia da data que ſe eſcreue na carta,*
[c1a-l038 note] Hizzuke is the written date on a letter. Dia da data clarifies that this is dating a document, not the act of deciding a day described under Fizaxi.
[c1a-l039 >] *ou em outra eſcritura. ¶* Fizzuqeuo ſu-
[c1a-l039 note] Other written documents are included. Hizzuke wo su-ru continues below, a normal noun-plus-suru construction; its line-end hyphen is visible.
[c1a-l040 >] ru. *Eſcreuer o dia do mes em carta, &c.*
[c1a-l040 note] The example means to write the day of the month on a letter. Historical eſcreuer and mes are retained; the final &c. is part of the explanation.
[c1a-l041 >] *¶* Iccano fizzuqeca? *Qual he o dia da*
[c1a-l041 note] The example asks what date the document bears. Iccano is retained as the scan's uncertain-looking spelling rather than silently changed to an expected interrogative such as itcuno; the visible paragraph mark was restored.
[c1a-l042 >] *data, ou quando foi eſcrita a carta? &c.*
[c1a-l042 note] The Portuguese restates the question as when the letter was written. The ending is carta? in the scan, not the OCR string cariae; the question closes before &c.

[c2-l001] Fo. *Eſpiga de trigo, ou de outro legume, ou erua.*
[c2-l001 note] Fo is the ear or spike of a plant, 穂, here illustrated by wheat and other plants. Legume has the dictionary's broad historical plant/seed usage; this is not the sailing sense introduced later.
[c2-l002] Fǒ. *Lei, ou cuſtume. Vt,* Sadamatta fǒgia.
[c2-l002 note] Fǒ is a rule or established custom. Sadamatta fǒ gia means that it is a settled rule; the word after sadamatta starts with f, not r. The caron spelling is retained rather than harmonized with the next headword's circumflex.
[c2-l003 >] *He lei posta, ou cuſtume ordinario. ¶* Fǒuo
[c2-l003 note] Lei poſta means an established law, paired with ordinary custom. The abraded ſt sequence explains OCR's poila; fǒ wo continues into the next line's verb.
[c2-l004 >] vocaſu. *Quebrar a lei, ou cuſtume.*
[c2-l004 note] Fǒ wo wokasu is to violate a rule. Portuguese quebrar a lei ou cuſtume confirms the sense, and the missing long ſ in cuſtume was restored from the scan.
[c2-l005] Fô. Nori. *Lei.* Fôuo yaburu, l, ſomu-
[c2-l005 note] Fô is glossed nori, law or doctrine. Yaburu and somuku are alternatives for breaking or disobeying it; the faint Portuguese word is Lei, not Lti.
[c2-l006 >] qu. *Quebrar a lei. ¶* Fôuo firomuru.
[c2-l006 note] Qu completes somuku. Fô wo hiromuru then means to spread the law or teaching, with the historical hiromuru form preserved rather than modern hiromeru.
[c2-l007 >] *Dilatar a lei. ¶* Fôuo toqu. *Prègar a lei.*
[c2-l007 note] Dilatar a lei explains spreading the teaching, while fô wo toku is expounding it. The final Portuguese is Pregar a lei., preach the doctrine, with a visible period rather than the OCR hyphen.
[c2-l008] Fô. Nori. i, Cuſurino fô. *Regimento, ou*
[c2-l008 note] Fô here is a method or prescription, specified as kusuri no fô. Regimento introduces a regimen or set of instructions; the small explanatory i has a visible comma.
[c2-l009 >] *receita de mezinhas.*
[c2-l009 note] Receita de mezinhas completes the medicinal-prescription sense. These are historical lexicographic meanings, not an endorsement of a medical regimen.
[c2-l010] Fô. *Maçaã do roſto. ¶* Fôuo vtçu. *Dar*
[c2-l010 note] A different fô means the cheek. Maçaã do roſto is the Portuguese apple-of-the-face expression; the first abraded vowel after ç is a, not u. Fô wo utsu introduces striking the cheek.
[c2-l011 >] *bofetada no rosto.*
[c2-l011 note] Dar bofetada no rosto means to slap the face. Bofetada is printed as one word; the OCR's internal space was not a meaningful word boundary.
[c2-l012] Fo. *Vela. ¶* Fouo aguru, l, caquru, l,
[c2-l012 note] Fo now means a sail, 帆. Fo wo aguru, kakuru and hiku are alternatives for setting or hoisting it; the absence of a long-vowel accent distinguishes it from nearby fô entries.
[c2-l013 >] fiqu. *Dar a vela. ¶* Fouo voroſu, l,
[c2-l013 note] Hiku completes the hoisting alternatives. Fo wo worosu means to lower the sail: the scan has Fouo, without the extra i supplied by OCR, and the Portuguese contrast supports this reading.
[c2-l014 >] ſaguru. *Amainar a vela.*
[c2-l014 note] Saguru is the alternate lowering verb. Amainar a vela is the corresponding Portuguese sailing expression, not a reference to reducing the earlier law or doctrine.
[c2-l015] Fôate. *Viſeira. ¶* Fôateuo ſuru. *Pòr*
[c2-l015 note] Fôate is a face/cheek guard, described as a viseira. The headword's visible mark is a circumflex like that in Fôateuo, not a caron; the example means putting on the guard.
[c2-l016 >] *viſeira no rosto.*
[c2-l016 note] The guard is placed on the face. Rosto remains in its printed short-s form; semantic interpretation does not justify normalizing that allograph.
[c2-l017] Fôbare. *Doença que faz inchar o roſto. No*
[c2-l017 note] Fôbare names a condition of swollen cheeks/face. The dictionary supplies a regional alternative next; the description is historical and does not establish a modern clinical diagnosis.
[c2-l018 >] *Cami ſe diz,* Fôfare. *¶* Fôbareuo ya-
[c2-l018 note] In the Cami the form is fôfare, an unvoiced counterpart to fôbare. Both the alternative and the following Fôbareuo show circumflex marks in the scan; Cami itself is italic within the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l019 >] mu. *Estar com o roſto inchado desta doença.*
[c2-l019 note] Ya-mu completes the phrase for suffering from this swelling. Portuguese estar com o rosto inchado explicitly links it to the preceding condition rather than a general sorrow sense of yamu.
[c2-l020] Fǒbai. *Companheiro.*
[c2-l020 note] Fǒbai is a companion or fellow, 傍輩. Its caron is visible and should not be assimilated to the adjacent cheek compounds with circumflexes.
[c2-l021] Fobaxira. *Maſto. ¶* Fobaxirauo tatçu-
[c2-l021 note] Fobashira, literally sail-pillar, is a mast. The example begins fo-bashira wo tatsuru, raising it; the historical Portuguese masto is retained rather than changed to mastro.
[c2-l022 >] ru, l, voxitatçuru. *Aleuantar o masto.*
[c2-l022 note] Woshitatsuru is an alternative for erecting the mast. Aleuantar o masto supplies a direct semantic check on both Japanese verbs.
[c2-l023] Fǒben. *Ardil, ou modo de enſinar o Fotoque*
[c2-l023 note] Fǒben is an expedient way of teaching, 方便. The Portuguese describes it polemically as an ardil; this is the compiler's framing, not a neutral definition of Buddhist doctrine. Fotoque is visibly upright inside the italic explanation.
[c2-l024 >] *com doctrina apparente pera que o pouo ſe affei*
[c2-l024 note] The explanation says the teaching is presented so that the people become attached to it. Affei-çoe is split without a printed hyphen; the final i is visible in the enlarged scan, replacing OCR's r.
[c2-l025 >] *çoe, poſto que em ſecreto tenha, & enſine ou-*
[c2-l025 note] The compiler contrasts the public teaching with what is supposedly held and taught in secret. This is part of the same polemical definition, not a statement independently verified by the transcription project.
[c2-l026 >] *tra couſa.*
[c2-l026 note] Tra completes outra couſa, something else, and closes the fǒben definition. The line is short but real body text; it should not be merged geometrically with the following cosmetic entry.
[c2-l027] Fôbeni. *Rebique do rosto. ¶* Fôbeniuo
[c2-l027 note] Fôbeni is cheek rouge, with beni providing the colour/cosmetic element. The headword's e was misread as c by OCR; rebique is a historical Portuguese term for face colouring.
[c2-l028 >] ſaſu, tçuquru, nuru. *Pòr rebique no roſto.*
[c2-l028 note] Sasu, tsukuru and nuru are listed for applying the rouge. Portuguese Pòr rebique confirms the cosmetic noun; its damaged b should not be transcribed as o.
[c2-l029] Fôbera. *Guelras do peixe. ¶ Item, Par*
[c2-l029 note] Fôbera first names fish gills, then a region of the human face. Par continues into parte below, without adding an editorial line-end hyphen.
[c2-l030 >] *te do roſto das meçaãs pera baixo. i,* Fôta-
[c2-l030 note] The second sense extends downward from the cheeks, with fôta-bura supplied as a Japanese equivalent. The printed maçaã-type historical spelling is not normalized to modern maçãs.
[c2-l031 >] bura. *B.*
[c2-l031 note] Bura completes fôtabura. The following B. is visibly roman, a usage label rather than part of the Japanese word or a new entry.
[c2-l032] Fôbi. Fome itçucuximu. *Louuor, ou pre-*
[c2-l032 note] Fôbi means praise or reward, 褒美, explained by home itsukushimu. Louuor / premio supports this interpretation; the circumflex belongs to the source spelling, not to a modern kana normalization.
[c2-l033 >] *mio. ¶* Fôbiſuru. *Dar premio por al-*

[c2-l034 >] *gum ſeruiço. ¶* Fôbiuo naſu. *Idem.*

[c2-l037] Fǒbǒ. *Todas as partes, ou varias partes.*

[c2-l038 >] *¶* Fǒbǒuo caqete ariqu. *Andar por*

[c2-l039 >] *diuerſas partes, & lugares.*

[c2-l041 >] *¶* Fobo mǒxi ireta. *Pollamòr parte, ou*

[c2-l042 note] Pouco mais ou menos lhe diſſe means that one told it more or less approximately. The word is mais, not mats; the source's two-long-s spelling in diſſe is retained.

## catchword [catchword] Catchword

[cw-l001] **Fôbu-**
