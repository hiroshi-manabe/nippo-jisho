[c1-l001 >] *met.* Mutenna fito. *Peßoa que não ſe en*
[c1-l001 note] Met. completes per met. from f342. Mutenna hito applies the unpointed-text image to a person who is not easily understood; the Portuguese en continues without a printed hyphen.
[c1-l002 >] *tende, nem ſe ſabe declarar bem. ¶* Mu-
[c1-l002 note] The person cannot make himself understood or explain himself well. Mu- begins a second example of the same adjectival expression.
[c1-l003 >] tenna cotouo yǔ. l, ſuru. *Dizer, ou fa-*
[c1-l003 note] Mutenna koto wo yuu or suru means saying or doing unintelligible things. The alternative verbs belong to the same example.
[c1-l004 >] *zer couſas, que não ſe entendem.*
[c1-l004 note] Zer completes fazer; the Portuguese defines the actions as things that cannot be understood. This closes the preceding page’s entry.
[c1-l005] Mutǒ. Catana naxi. *Sẽ eſpada Vt,* Mu-
[c1-l005 note] Mutǒ is explained by katana nashi, without a sword. Sẽ is an unexpanded abbreviation, and no punctuation is supplied after eſpada.
[c1-l006 >] tǒni xite teqini vǒ. *Encontrarſe com o*
[c1-l006 note] Mutǒ ni shite teki ni vǒ describes meeting an enemy without a sword. The printed vǒ is retained instead of modernizing the Japanese verb.
[c1-l007 >] *inimigo ſem Catana. ¶* Mutǒni mairi
[c1-l007 note] Catana occurs within the Portuguese translation. The next Japanese example repeats Mutǒni, whose damaged initial M was misrecognized as A.
[c1-l008 >] vǒ. *Encontrarſe ſem armas.*
[c1-l008 note] Vǒ completes mutǒni mairi vǒ, glossed as encountering someone unarmed. The caron remains part of the printed verb form.
[c1-l009] Mutocu. Tocu naxi. *Sem proueito, ou ſem*
[c1-l009 note] Mutoku is explained by toku nashi, without benefit or profit. The Portuguese offers two closely related constructions.
[c1-l010 >] *receber proueito.*
[c1-l010 note] Receber proueito completes “without receiving benefit.” This short continuation belongs to mutoku, not to the next adverb.
[c1-l011] Muttato. *Adu. Inconſideradamente, ou im-*
[c1-l011 note] Muttato is an adverb for inconsiderate or disorderly action. The scan retains the e in Inconſideradamente, which OCR omitted.
[c1-l012 >] *perfeitamente, & ſem ordem. B.*
[c1-l012 note] The gloss completes imperfectamente and adds “without order.” The low-register label B. is italic here: its narrow sloping capital matches italic Bicho, unlike the upright B of Bup. elsewhere on this page.
[c1-l013] Muxa. Taqei mono. *Soldado armado.*
[c1-l013 note] Musha is explained by takei mono and glossed as an armed soldier. The Japanese explanatory phrase remains separate from the Portuguese.
[c1-l014 >] *¶* Muxaburiga yoi. *Ter boa aparencia,*
[c1-l014 note] Mushaburi ga yoi describes a good soldierly appearance. A visible paragraph mark, omitted by OCR, introduces the example.
[c1-l015 >] *& geito de ſoldado.*
[c1-l015 note] The gloss adds the bearing or manner of a soldier. This completes the illustration of mushaburi.
[c1-l016] Muxabet. *O não auer differença.*
[c1-l016 note] Muxabet is glossed as absence of difference. The final bare t is retained; it must not be expanded into an extra printed syllable.
[c1-l017] Muxabuguiǒ. *Preſidente como ſargento, que*
[c1-l017 note] Mushabugyǒ is an officer compared with a sergeant. The circumstantial description of commanding soldiers continues below.
[c1-l018 >] *gouerna os ſoldados.*
[c1-l018 note] The officer governs or directs the soldiers. This completes the military-office definition rather than introducing a new headword.
[c1-l019] Muxabune. *Embarcação de ſoldados.*
[c1-l019 note] Mushabune is a vessel carrying soldiers. The visible x restores the headword from OCR Mu,abune.
[c1-l020] Muxaburi. *Vide,* Muxa.
[c1-l020 note] Mushaburi refers the reader to Muxa, where the example concerning soldierly bearing occurs. Vide is italic, while the referenced Japanese word is upright.
[c1-l021] Muxavarangi. *Certa laya de calçado de palha*
[c1-l021 note] Mushawarangi names a kind of straw footwear. The historical warangi sequence is preserved rather than assimilated to a modern spelling.
[c1-l022 >] *como alparcas de que vſão os ſoldados.*
[c1-l022 note] The footwear is compared with alparcas worn by soldiers. The scan’s initial a corrects OCR olparcas.
[c1-l023] Muxaye. *Pintura de guerras, ou batalhas.*
[c1-l023 note] Mushae is a picture of wars or battles. The final ye is the printed form for the picture-related element.
[c1-l024] Muxe, ſuru, eta. *Engaſgar, ou dar no goto*
[c1-l024 note] Muxe describes choking or something going down the wrong way. The Portuguese phrase has the visible separation no goto; the principal form ſuru remains literal.
[c1-l025 >] *quando ſe bebe vinho. agoa, &c. ¶* Qe-
[c1-l025 note] The choking occurs while drinking wine or water. The scan reads agoa, not OCR agna; the small separator after vinho is retained as a period. Qe- begins kemuri.
[c1-l026 >] murini muſuru. *Afogarſe com fumo.*
[c1-l026 note] Kemuri ni muſuru means choking on smoke. Preserve the example’s long ſ despite x in Muxe and muxete elsewhere in the entry.
[c1-l027 >] *¶* Namidani muxete. *Não podendo fa-*
[c1-l027 note] Namida ni muxete is choking with tears. The Portuguese explains inability to speak and continues its divided verb below.
[c1-l028 >] *lar com lagrimas.*
[c1-l028 note] Lar completes falar, giving “unable to speak with tears.” This closes the crying example.
[c1-l029] Muxebi, u. *Idem. Com as meſmas fraſes.*
[c1-l029 note] Muxebi has the same meaning and is said to occur in the same phrases. No additional principal forms are supplied beyond the printed u.
[c1-l030] Muxi, ſu, uita. *Cozer algũa couſa com o ba-*
[c1-l030 note] Muxi is steaming food rather than choking. The principal part uita is preserved as historical morphology, and ba- starts Portuguese bafo.
[c1-l031 >] *fo dagoa quente. Vt,* Mangiǔuo muſu.
[c1-l031 note] The cooking uses the vapour of hot water. Mangiǔ wo musu illustrates steaming buns; the historical g spelling is retained.
[c1-l032 >] *Cozer desta maneira hũs pãosſinhos de trigo.*
[c1-l032 note] The buns are described as small wheat breads cooked in this way. Hũs has a tilde over u; pãosſinhos retains the visible s/ſ sequence. Its n is heavily abraded and supplied from surviving lower strokes and the word’s context.
[c1-l033] Muxi. Fajime naxi. *Sẽ principio. ¶* Mu-
[c1-l033 note] Muxi here means without a beginning, as Fajime naxi and Sẽ principio explain. Enlargement shows a word space before naxi.
[c1-l034 >] xi mujǒ. *Couſa eterna, ſẽ principio nẽ fim.*
[c1-l034 note] Muxi mujǒ is rendered as something eternal, without beginning or end. The printed mujǒ is retained rather than regularized to an expected modern expression.
[c1-l035] Muxi. *Bicho, lombrigas, &c. ¶* Muxi-
[c1-l035 note] A separate muxi entry means an insect or intestinal worms. The following example begins with the object muxi-.
[c1-l036 >] uo vazzurǒ. *Estar doente de lombrigas.*
[c1-l036 note] Muxi wo vazzurǒ means suffering from worms. The printed vazzurǒ retains both z types and its caron.
[c1-l037 >] *¶* Muxiga vocoru. *Adoecer de lombri-*
[c1-l037 note] Muxi ga vocoru describes an attack of worm-related illness. The initial v in vocoru is retained as printed.
[c1-l038 >] *gas, ou dor de estamago. ¶* Muxiga xe-
[c1-l038 note] The explanation adds stomach pain. Muxi ga xe- begins another expression, completed by qu on the following row.
[c1-l039 >] qu. X. *Apertarem, & atormentarem*
[c1-l039 note] Muxi ga xequ is marked X. for regional usage. The upright label is followed by Portuguese describing the worms pressing and tormenting someone.
[c1-l040 >] *as lombrigas a alguem, ou dor de barriga, ou*
[c1-l040 note] The Portuguese describes torment from worms or pain in the abdomen. The final ou introduces the alternative organ on the next row.
[c1-l041 >] *eſtamago.*
[c1-l041 note] Eſtamago completes the reference to stomach pain. This short row remains part of the same explanatory example.
[c1-l042] Muxibami, u, ǒda. *Roer, ou comer o bicho*
[c1-l042 note] Muxibami describes an insect gnawing or eating something. The principal part ǒda is preserved rather than changed to a modern past ending.
[c1-l043 >] *algũa couſa.*
[c1-l043 note] Algũa couſa supplies the object of the insect’s eating. The preceding Japanese verb is therefore transitive in this gloss.
[c1-l044] Muxicui. *Raſto, ou ſinal que deixa o bicho na*
[c1-l044 note] Muxicui first denotes the trace or mark left by an insect. The material bearing the mark is specified below.
[c1-l045 >] *madeira comida, ou notra couſa. ¶ Itẽ a meſ-*
[c1-l045 note] The mark occurs in eaten wood or another object; notra is retained as printed. Itẽ then introduces the affected object itself as another sense.
[c1-l046 >] *ma couſa como fruita, &c. comida do bicho.*
[c1-l046 note] The second sense is the insect-eaten thing itself, such as fruit. This completes the distinction between damage marks and damaged material.
[c1-l047] Muximochi. *Certa laya de Mochis cozidos*
[c1-l047 note] Muximochi here denotes a steamed preparation of Mochis. Mochis is a Japanese-derived word embedded in the Portuguese explanation, unlike the headword.
[c1-l048 >] *com o bafo de agoa quente.*
[c1-l048 note] The preparation is cooked with hot-water vapour. The m of com is heavily abraded; its surviving lower strokes and the phrase support com rather than OCR cou.

[c2-l001] Muximochi. *Peßoa que tem lombrigas, ou dor*
[c2-l001 note] This muximochi denotes a person with intestinal worms or abdominal pain, not the steamed food at the end of the other column.
[c2-l002 >] *de barriga.*
[c2-l002 note] De barriga completes the alternative symptom, abdominal pain. The short continuation closes this distinct muximochi entry.
[c2-l003] Muximugui. *Certa laya de letria cozida com*
[c2-l003 note] Muximugui is described as a kind of noodle-like preparation. Its method of cooking is specified on the next row.
[c2-l004 >] *o bafo dagoa quente.*
[c2-l004 note] The preparation is cooked with hot-water vapour. This completes the food definition and distinguishes it from the surrounding homophonous entries.
[c2-l005] Muxin. Cocoro naxi. *Couſa inanimada, &*
[c2-l005 note] Muxin is explained by kokoro nashi and first glossed as inanimate. The Portuguese list of examples continues below.
[c2-l006 >] *ſem ſentido, como aruores, pedras, &c.*
[c2-l006 note] The first sense is lacking sensation, as with trees and stones. A separate, previously omitted physical row introduces another sense immediately below.
[c2-l006a >] *¶ Item, Pejo, & repugnancia que hum de-*
[c2-l006a note] This complete printed row was omitted by OCR. It introduces the shame or reluctance one ought to feel when making a request; de- joins uia below. Both text and a native-image crop are restored under a fresh ID.
[c2-l007 >] *uia ter quando pede a alguem que dè, ou faça*
[c2-l007 note] Uia completes deuia: the shame one should feel when asking someone to give or do something. This clause depends on the restored preceding row.
[c2-l008 >] *algũa couſa. Vt,* Muxinno xomǒ. *De-*
[c2-l008 note] Muxin no shomǒ illustrates a request liable to impose on another person. De- begins the Portuguese desejo across the next line.
[c2-l009 >] *ſejo, ou petição com que hum pode molestar a*
[c2-l009 note] The wish or petition may trouble another person. This explains the social reluctance described in the restored row.
[c2-l010 >] *outro. ¶* Fitoni muxin, l, muxinna
[c2-l010 note] After completing “another,” the entry starts hito ni muxin or muxinna koto wo yuu, asking someone for something with reluctance.
[c2-l011 >] cotouo yǔ. *Dizer a alguem couſas que hũ*
[c2-l011 note] The example means saying to someone things one is reluctant to say. Dizer a has a visible word boundary; the damaged final abbreviation is read as hũ, not OCR hoi.
[c2-l012 >] *repugna, ou deuia de ter pejo por o não enfadar*
[c2-l012 note] The speaker hesitates or ought to feel shame, so as not to annoy the other person. Pejo repeats the noun in the restored line, rather than OCR peyo.
[c2-l013 >] *ou moleſtar.*
[c2-l013 note] Ou moleſtar completes the concern about troubling the person addressed. This closes the extended request-related sense of muxin.
[c2-l014] Muxiqi. Iro naxi. *Sem cor.*
[c2-l014 note] Muxiqi is explained by iro nashi and glossed “without colour.” The Japanese explanatory phrase is not a Portuguese-embedded term.
[c2-l015] Muxiqina. *couſa ſem cor.*
[c2-l015 note] Muxiqina is the adjective for something colourless. The gloss begins with a visibly lowercase c, retained rather than capitalized.
[c2-l016] Muxiri, u, itta. *Depenar. Vt,* Toriuo mu-
[c2-l016 note] Muxiri means plucking. Tori wo mu- begins the first example, plucking a bird; the principal form itta is retained.
[c2-l017 >] xiru. *Depenar a aue. ¶* Vatauo mu-
[c2-l017 note] Tori wo muxiru is plucking a bird. Vata wo mu- then begins an example involving cotton or loose fibre.
[c2-l018 >] xiru. *Eſcarpear a borra. ¶* Cuſauo mu-
[c2-l018 note] Vata wo muxiru is teasing or separating fibre. The enlarged word has two r types in borra, though the preceding o is weak; kusa wo muxiru begins the next example.
[c2-l019 >] xiru. *Tirar eruas. ¶* Fanauo muxiru.
[c2-l019 note] Kusa wo muxiru is pulling herbs or grass; hana wo muxiru starts the corresponding example for a flower.
[c2-l020 >] *Tirar com a mão as folhas da roſa, ou depinicar*
[c2-l020 note] The flower example is explained as pulling the rose’s leaves or petals off by hand. The coordinated phrase continues below.
[c2-l021 >] *a roſa. ¶* Qeuo muxiru. *Arrancar os*
[c2-l021 note] After completing the rose example, ke wo muxiru introduces pulling out hair. The large-tailed Q is ordinary printed letterform, not a new section initial.
[c2-l022 >] *cabelos.*
[c2-l022 note] Cabelos supplies the object of arrancar, completing the hair-plucking example. This is the final continuation of muxiri.
[c2-l023] Muxiro. *Esteira. ¶* Muxirouo vtçu. *Fa*
[c2-l023 note] Muxiro is a mat. Muxiro wo utsu means making mats; the Portuguese Fa continues without a printed terminal hyphen.
[c2-l024 >] *zer esteiras. ¶* Muxirouo xiqu. *Eſtẽ-*
[c2-l024 note] Zer completes fazer, making mats. Muxiro wo shiku introduces spreading a mat, with Eſtẽ- beginning its translation.
[c2-l025 >] *der a eſteira no chão. ¶* Muxironi tçuqu.
[c2-l025 note] The translation is spreading the mat on the floor. Enlargement shows faint upper and lower strokes of e between t and i in eſteira, followed by the separate no; muxiro ni tsuku begins the next example.
[c2-l026 >] *Deitarſe em cama.*
[c2-l026 note] Muxiro ni tsuku is glossed as lying down in bed. The mat expression is interpreted through its use as bedding.
[c2-l027] Muxiroxiqi. *Manceba, palaura pouco vſada.*
[c2-l027 note] Muxiroxiqi is glossed as a mistress or concubine and described as little used. Palaura is one printed word, not OCR palaur a.
[c2-l028] Muxit. *Mentira, ou falſo teſtemunho.*
[c2-l028 note] Muxit denotes a lie or false testimony. The final t is retained as a checked ending, without supplying a modern extra syllable.
[c2-l029 >] *¶* Fitoni muxituo iy caquru. *Aleuan-*
[c2-l029 note] Hito ni muxit wo iy kakuru means directing a false accusation at someone. In muxituo the object particle follows the printed final t.
[c2-l030 >] *tar falſo testemunho a alguem.*
[c2-l030 note] Tar completes aleuantar: raising false testimony against someone. The Portuguese precisely explains the accusation sense of the example.
[c2-l031] Muxizzu. *Azia que ſe ſente na boca por cau-*
[c2-l031 note] Muxizzu is described as an acid sensation in the mouth. The cause is explained in the continuation below.
[c2-l032 >] *ſa do ruim eſtamago, ou comer indigesto.*
[c2-l032 note] The sensation is attributed to an upset stomach or indigestible food. The historical eſtamago spelling is retained.
[c2-l033 >] *¶* Muxizzuqiga faxiru. *Ter esta azia,*
[c2-l033 note] The printed example describes this acid sensation arising. Preserve Muxizzuqiga literally rather than deleting its qi to match a familiar modern expression; the paragraph mark is visible.
[c2-l034 >] *ou vir à boca eſte azedume.*
[c2-l034 note] The translation adds acid coming up into the mouth. The grave in à is a Portuguese accent, distinct from Japanese vowel-length marks.
[c2-l035] Muxǒ. Xǒ naxi. *Couſa que não tem ſubſtan-*
[c2-l035 note] Muxǒ is explained by shǒ nashi, lacking substance or vitality. The scan has Muxǒ, not Mixǒ, and the divided Portuguese word ends in -tan-, not -tar-.
[c2-l036 >] *cia, & vigor. ¶* Muxǒna fito. *Ho-*
[c2-l036 note] Cia completes substancia, followed by vigour. Muxǒna hito begins an example applying this lack of animation to a person.
[c2-l037 >] *mem que està fora de ſeu juizo, & ſentido por*
[c2-l037 note] The person is outside his normal judgement or awareness. The word is ſeu, with long s, not OCR feu.
[c2-l038 >] *doença, ſono, &c. ¶* Muxǒni naru.
[c2-l038 note] Illness or sleep supplies the cause. Muxǒni naru then illustrates entering such a state.
[c2-l039 >] *Ficar fora de ſi, ou perder o juizo.*
[c2-l039 note] The translation describes becoming beside oneself or losing judgement. It completes the state-change example rather than introducing another headword.
[c2-l040] Muxo. *Sepultura, ou lugar de ſepulturas.*
[c2-l040 note] Muxo, without a printed long-vowel mark, denotes a grave or burial place. Its spelling is kept distinct from adjacent Muxǒ entries.
[c2-l041] Muxǒ. Sauari naxi. *Sẽ impedimento. Vt,*
[c2-l041 note] This Muxǒ means absence of obstruction, as sawari nashi explains. The Portuguese abbreviation is Sẽ, not Sè.
[c2-l042 >] Muxǒ mugue. *Sem impedimento, nem*
[c2-l042 note] Muxǒ mugue means without impediment or hindrance. The mark over the first o is lightly printed and retained as the OCR-aligned caron.
[c2-l043 >] *estoruo.* S.
[c2-l043 note] Estoruo completes the no-hindrance gloss. The final S. is an upright written-language label.
[c2-l044] Muxǒ muxi. *O não nacer, nẽ morrer.* Bup.
[c2-l044 note] Muxǒ muxi is glossed as neither being born nor dying and marked Bup. The printed nacer is preserved rather than modernized.
[c2-l045] Muxoſa. *Ocioſidade, ou não fazer nada. ¶* Mu-
[c2-l045 note] Muxoſa denotes idleness or doing nothing. Mu- begins the illustrative phrase continued on the next row.
[c2-l046 >] xoſani xite iru. *Eſtar ocioſo ſem fazer na-*
[c2-l046 note] Muxoſani shite iru means remaining idle. The translation’s final na- belongs to nada below.
[c2-l047 >] *da.*
[c2-l047 note] Da completes nada and is the only running body text on this row. OCR’s attached Miuxi, was a duplicate misreading of the separately retained catchword Muxu., not a lost headword.
