[c1-l001] Nitonito. *Adu. Modo de pegar algũacouſa*
[c1-l001 note] Nitonito is an adverb describing the adhesion of something soft and sticky, continuing the vocabulary of the preceding page. The close printed algũacouſa is retained rather than automatically respaced.
[c1-l002 >] *molle, & viſcoſa. Vt,* Nito nito ſuru.
[c1-l002 note] Completes the soft, sticky description and gives nito nito suru as the example. The repetition is spaced here even though the headword Nitonito is not.
[c1-l003] Nitten. *Vt,* Nittenji. *Sol, falando com re-*
[c1-l003 note] Nitten, illustrated by Nittenji, is a reverential designation for the sun. Re- continues reverencia on the next physical line.
[c1-l004 >] *uerencia. Como cuſtumão os gentios, tendo que*
[c1-l004 note] Completes reverencia and attributes the usage to the people the compilers call gentios. Their belief about the sun follows below; the note reports the dictionary's terminology rather than endorsing it.
[c1-l005 >] *o ſol he* Fotoque.
[c1-l005 note] Completes the reported belief that the sun is a Fotoque. The Japanese religious term is embedded in a Portuguese sentence and is visibly upright, unlike the surrounding italic words.
[c1-l006] Nittǒ. *Paſſar à China. Vt,* Nittǒ ſuru.
[c1-l006 note] Nittō means going over to China, with nittō suru as the example. The second t in the repeated word is worn but agrees with the visible headword; OCR Nitiǒ misread it as i.
[c1-l007] Niua. *Pateo, ou lugar de alegretes.*
[c1-l007 note] Niwa is a courtyard or a place for ornamental garden beds. The Portuguese alegretes is preserved rather than replaced by a modern generic word for a garden.
[c1-l008] Niua. i, Fiyori. *Tempo bom, ou ruim pera*
[c1-l008 note] This separate niwa is explained by hiyori and refers to favorable or unfavorable weather. Its particular application to navigation follows below.
[c1-l009 >] *nauegar.*
[c1-l009 note] Nauegar completes the weather definition as conditions for sailing. The row belongs to the second Niua entry rather than the following ceremonial-fire noun.
[c1-l010] Niuabi. *Fogo que ſe faz diante do Cami por*
[c1-l010 note] Niwabi is a fire made before a kami for a ceremony. The initial is N, correcting OCR Miuabi; Cami is an embedded Japanese religious term, retaining its printed italic type.
[c1-l011 >] *ceremonia.*
[c1-l011 note] Ceremonia completes the purpose of the fire. It is a genuine one-word continuation of Niuabi.
[c1-l012] Niuabǒqi. *Vaſoura com que ſe varre o pateo.*
[c1-l012 note] Niwabōki is a broom for sweeping the courtyard. The headword's caron and the single visible long ſ in Vaſoura are preserved.
[c1-l013] Niuacagoto. i, Niuacana coto. *Couſa re-*
[c1-l013 note] Niwakagoto is explained by niwakana koto, a sudden occurrence. The Portuguese re- begins repentina below.
[c1-l014 >] *pentina.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes repentina, sudden. The short fragment is not a standalone definition or a missing-row artifact.
[c1-l015] Niuacana. l, Niuacano. *Couſa repentina.*
[c1-l015 note] Niwakana and niwakano are alternative modifiers for something sudden. The Latin l marks the alternative and does not belong to either Japanese form.
[c1-l016] Niuacani. *Adu.*
[c1-l016 note] Niwakani is labeled only as an adverb, understood with the preceding suddenness entries. No unprinted Portuguese gloss is supplied after Adu.
[c1-l017] Niuamuxiro. *Eſteira großa de palha, que ſe*
[c1-l017 note] Niwamushiro is a thick straw mat. The Portuguese clause explains where it is spread on the next row, with the ß-shaped sort retained in großa.
[c1-l018 >] *eſtende nos pateos.*
[c1-l018 note] The mat is spread in courtyards. This completes the relative clause and links the compound's niwa element to the earlier courtyard definition.
[c1-l019] Niuanori. *Certo modo de caualgar no caualo*
[c1-l019 note] Niwanori is a particular way of riding a horse. The setting, rather than a different animal or action, is supplied on the next row.
[c1-l020 >] *dentro do pateo.*
[c1-l020 note] The riding takes place inside the courtyard. The phrase completes Niuanori without introducing a separate entry.
[c1-l021] Niuatataqi. i, Xeqirei. *Arueloa.*
[c1-l021 note] Niwatataki is equated with sekirei and the Portuguese bird name Arueloa, a wagtail. The historical Japanese and Portuguese spellings remain unnormalized.
[c1-l022] Niuatazzumi. *Agoa repreſada que fica no*
[c1-l022 note] Niwatazumi is standing or retained water. The following line specifies that it remains in a courtyard.
[c1-l023 >] *pateo.*
[c1-l023 note] Pateo completes the definition of water left standing in the yard. Its separate physical row is retained despite containing only one word.
[c1-l024] Niuatçutçuji. *Flores aßi chamadas, q̃ ſe dão*
[c1-l024 note] Niwatsutsuji names flowers borne on small trees or shrubs. The visible abbreviated q̃ introduces the relative clause and is not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l025 >] *em hũas aruoreſinhas.*
[c1-l025 note] The flowers grow on small trees. Aruoreſinhas corrects OCR aruoreſinlhas: the apparent extra l belongs to the h-shaped stroke sequence, not another consonant.
[c1-l026] Niuatoco. *Aruore, ou pao aſsi chamado.*
[c1-l026 note] Niwatoko is identified as a tree or wood of that name. The mixed aſsi spelling is retained rather than made identical to nearby aßi occurrences.
[c1-l027] Niuatori. *Gallinha, ou gallo.*
[c1-l027 note] Niwatori includes both hen and cock. The two Portuguese nouns make the non-sex-specific scope of the Japanese headword explicit.
[c1-l028] Niuazacura. *Certa laya de cereijeiras pequenas q̃*
[c1-l028 note] Niwazakura denotes a kind of small cherry tree. Enlargement supports cereijeiras, with the weak r and the additional e missed by OCR cerijeiras; this historical spelling is not replaced with modern cerejeiras.
[c1-l029 >] *ſe poem nas Niuas.*
[c1-l029 note] The small trees are planted in gardens or courtyards. Niuas is the Japanese garden term used with a Portuguese plural ending inside an otherwise Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l030] Niuma. *Caualo de carga. ¶* Nivmani niuo
[c1-l030 note] Niuma is a packhorse; the example begins loading goods onto it. The printed Nivmani preserves the internal v allograph rather than being silently changed to u.
[c1-l031 >] vôſuru. *Carregar o caualo.*
[c1-l031 note] The verb completes the packhorse example, glossed as loading the horse. Its initial v and circumflex are preserved in vôſuru rather than normalized to a modern spelling.
[c1-l032] Niuǒ. *Duas figuras grandes como gigantes, ou*
[c1-l032 note] Niō denotes two large guardian figures, compared with giants. The long vowel has a caron; the objects and their location are further specified below.
[c1-l033 >] *eſtatuas que ſe poem nas portas das varelas.*
[c1-l033 note] The figures are statues placed at temple gates. Varelas is retained as the Portuguese term used here, without replacing it with a modern Japanese building name.
[c1-l034] Niuo. *Adem domar aßi chamada.*
[c1-l034 note] Nio is described as a sea duck of that name. The final i of aßi corrects OCR aßa; domar retains the source's close spacing, and no precise modern bird species is imposed.
[c1-l035] Niuoi. *Cheiro.*
[c1-l035 note] Nioi is the noun for smell or fragrance. It is a separate entry from the inflected verb that follows.
[c1-l036] Niuoi, ô, ôta. *Cheirar. Vt,* Fanaua iroiro
[c1-l036 note] The niou verb is glossed as giving off a smell. The example begins with flowers in various forms or colors, and the two circumflexes in the principal parts remain distinct from nearby carons.
[c1-l037 >] niuoyedomo, arujito tanomu fitomonǒ,
[c1-l037 note] The concessive niwoyedomo describes the flowers' fragrance, followed by the absence of a master on whom to depend. The source's fitomonǒ spelling and attached particles are retained rather than respaced or modernized.
[c1-l038 >] &c. Feiq. *Lib. 4. Posto que as roſas chei-*
[c1-l038 note] Feiq. is the upright source label, followed by an italic book reference and the translation. The scan has chei-, beginning cheirão, not OCR chet-; Posto retains the baseline s/t classification.
[c1-l039 >] *rão, toda via não tendo dono nem ſenhor, a que*
[c1-l039 note] Completes cheirão and says the flowers have neither owner nor lord. The printed toda via remains two words, and the relative clause continues below.
[c1-l040 >] *ſe encomendem, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes the absence of a patron to whom the flowers can entrust themselves. This closes the cited illustration of niuoi rather than adding another verb sense.
[c1-l041] Niuoicata. *Tecidura de armas de fio verme-*
[c1-l041 note] Nioikata names an armor-lacing arrangement. The Portuguese begins the color specification with verme-, continued as vermelho below.
[c1-l042 >] *lho entre dous brancos.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes vermelho and specifies a red thread between two white ones. The color arrangement, not an odor, explains this use of the nioi-related term.
[c1-l043] Niuoivma. i, Conida. *Caualo de carga.*
[c1-l043 note] Nioiuma is equated with konida and glossed as a packhorse. The final a is heavily abraded but retains its right-hand stroke without an i dot; enlargement and the supplied NINJAL entry support Niuoivma over OCR Niuoivmi.
[c1-l044] Nixe. *Dous mundos. i, Eſte preſente, & o*
[c1-l044 note] Nise here means two worlds or lives, namely the present and the future. Dous corrects OCR Dour, and the explanatory i remains part of the italic Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l045 >] *futuro.*
[c1-l045 note] Futuro supplies the second of the two worlds, completing the definition. It is a separate physical row, not a page catchword.
[c1-l046] Nixe, ſuru, eta. *Aſemelhar, imitar, ou cõ-*
[c1-l046 note] Niseru means making something resemble another, imitating, or counterfeiting it. The printed single long ſ in Aſemelhar and abbreviated cõ- are preserved.
[c1-l047 >] *trafazer. ¶* Fitono teuo niſuru. *Ar-*
[c1-l047 note] Completes contrafazer and introduces imitating another person's handwriting. Ar- continues as Arremedar at the top of column 2, so the text must not be treated as a page-ending catchword.

[c2-l008 >] *da por outra.*

[c2-l009] Nixeſoconai, ǒ, ǒta. *Não aſemelhar bem*
[c2-l009 note] Nixeſoconai is failure to imitate successfully; the inflectional forms carry carons, not circumflexes. The Portuguese clause continues with its object below.
[c2-l010 >] *a couſa. Vt,* Dochimo nixeſoconǒte muſ-
[c2-l010 note] The example describes failing in the imitation on both sides. The line-final muſ- joins ſato on the next line, so neither long s is discarded.
[c2-l011 >] ſato xita monode gozaru. Mon. *Errã-*
[c2-l011 note] The Japanese concludes with a judgment about the person, followed by the upright citation Mon. Portuguese Errã- begins the explanation using a nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l012 >] *do em imitar ambas as partes. i, Nem imi-*
[c2-l012 note] The gloss explains the failure to imitate either side adequately. The scan's explanatory i, ends in a comma, correcting the OCR period; imi- continues the following nasalized verb.
[c2-l013 >] *tãdo bem hũ nẽ outro, he homẽ pera pouco, ou*
[c2-l013 note] Neither one nor the other is imitated well, and the person is described as of little ability. The marks belong to nẽ and homẽ, not the OCR grave-accented nè and malformed homò.
[c2-l014 >] *de pouco tomo.*
[c2-l014 note] De pouco tomo supplies a second expression for little standing or worth, completing the preceding assessment rather than naming another object.
[c2-l015] Nixi. *Poente.*
[c2-l015 note] This Nixi is the direction west, defined by Portuguese Poente; it is distinct from the shellfish homograph below.
[c2-l016] Nixi. *Certo genero de mariſco, como buzeos.*
[c2-l016 note] The second Nixi is a kind of shellfish compared with whelk-like shells. The general comparison does not establish a modern species, and buzeos is kept as printed.
[c2-l017] Nixigara. *Caſca deste mariſco.*
[c2-l017 note] Nixigara means the shell of that shellfish; deste explicitly links the definition to the preceding homograph.
[c2-l018] Niximuqi. *Pera a partedo Poente. Vt,* Ni-
[c2-l018 note] Niximuqi means facing or toward the west. The initial is visibly N, correcting Xiximuqi; the close printed partedo is retained, and Ni- starts the example across the line break.
[c2-l019 >] ximuqini nauoru. *Virarſe, ou mudarſe*
[c2-l019 note] The full example Niximuqini nauoru means turning or changing orientation westward. The historical nauoru form is retained rather than modernized.
[c2-l020 >] *pera a parte do Occidente.*
[c2-l020 note] This completes the direction of the turn: toward the west. Occidente is the Portuguese counterpart of Nixi in this entry.
[c2-l021] Nixin. Futavoya. *Pay, & mãy.*
[c2-l021 note] Nixin, paraphrased as Futavoya, means the two parents, father and mother. Both Japanese expressions are upright entry material, not font-toggle terms embedded in Portuguese.
[c2-l022] Nixin. Futagocoro. *Coração refalſado, ou*
[c2-l022 note] Here Nixin is a double or divided heart, with Futagocoro as its Japanese synonym. The Portuguese describes a false or treacherous heart; refalſado is retained literally.
[c2-l023 >] *atreiçoado.*
[c2-l023 note] Atreiçoado completes the alternative description 'treacherous.' Its historical initial a and spelling are not replaced with a modern form.
[c2-l024] Nixin. *Nome de hum peixe do mar.*
[c2-l024 note] This third Nixin is the name of a sea fish. The printed definition gives no further identifying details, so the note does not insert a species into the text.
[c2-l025] Nixiqi. *Potencia, ou ſentido de ouuir. S.*
[c2-l025 note] This Nixiqi is the faculty or sense of hearing, distinct from the textile homograph. The final register label S. is upright, outside the italic Portuguese explanation. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l026] Nixiqi. *Certa laya de peças como brocadilho.*
[c2-l026 note] The second Nixiqi refers to a kind of fabric compared with brocade. The historical laya and brocadilho are preserved, with no added textile classification.
[c2-l027] Nixiqigui. *P. Pao pintado que ſe punha à*
[c2-l027 note] Nixiqigui is painted wood used in a former courting custom. The introductory P. is printed in the italic explanatory run; the final à leads into the placement below.
[c2-l028 >] *porta antigamente da molher que hum pretẽdia.*
[c2-l028 note] The painted wood was placed at the door of the woman being courted. The abbreviation mark in pretẽdia is on e and remains unexpanded.
[c2-l029] Nixǒ. *Duas geraçoẽs.*
[c2-l029 note] Nixǒ is glossed as two generations. The caron in the Japanese and the tilde on e in geraçoẽs are distinct marks and are preserved.

[c2-l031 note] The ceremony occurs in religious services or funeral observances. Officios and ſaimentos complete the definition without introducing a further Japanese example.

[c2-l032 note] Nixxocu means a solar eclipse. The visible cl sequence gives Eclypſe, correcting OCR Echypſe; the roman ſu- begins the verb ſuru across the break.

[c2-l041 note] After the literal cooking sense, Per met. introduces the figurative Xinyuo niyaſu, becoming angry. The Portuguese label is uniformly italic; Xinyuo and niyaſu are upright example text.
[c2-l042 >] *colorizarſe.*

[c2-l044 >] *Feruer ſoando. Vt,* Cono camaua yô ni-

[c2-l045 >] yuru. *Esta panela do Chanoyu coze, l, faz*
[c2-l045 note] This completes ni-/yuru and describes a tea-ceremony kettle that boils water well. The scan's tall initial C gives Chanoyu; the embedded Japanese term remains italic with the Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l046 >] *feruer bem a agoa.*
[c2-l046 note] The clause completes the alternate wording, 'makes the water boil well.' Agoa is the historical printed spelling, not modern água.
[c2-l047] Niyedono. *Cozinha.*
[c2-l047 note] Niyedono is defined as a kitchen. It is the final body entry; the separate Niye- below is the catchword for the following page, not part of this definition.
