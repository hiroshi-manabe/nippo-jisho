[c1-l001] Guenqe. *Caſa, ou familia dos Guenjis.*
[c1-l001 note] Genke is the house or family of the Genji. The scan supports G and familia without OCR's extra m; Guenjis is Japanese lineage vocabulary embedded in Portuguese.
[c1-l002] Guenqi. *O ſarar, ou milhorar da infirmida-*
[c1-l002 note] Genki is recovery or improvement from illness. The verb is ſarar, with long s, not farar; infirmida- continues below.
[c1-l003 >] *de. Vt,* Guenqiuo vru.
[c1-l003 note] Completes infirmidade and gives genki wo uru, to regain health. The historical uru spelling is vru in the source.
[c1-l004] Guenqina, l, guẽqini gozaru. *Eſtar ſão, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Genki na or genki ni gozaru means being healthy or improved. The OCR's lagueqini is resolved as the alternative marker l followed by guẽqini; the tilde abbreviates n.
[c1-l005 >] *milhor da infirmidade.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes being better after illness. The historical milhor spelling is preserved rather than changed to modern melhor.
[c1-l006] Guenqua. Qibixij toga. *Crime graue.*
[c1-l006 note] Genkwa, kibishii toga, is a grave crime or severe offence. The printed qua sequence is retained in the headword.
[c1-l007 >] *¶* Guenquani voconauaruru, l, xoxera-
[c1-l007 note] The examples concern being dealt with or punished for a grave offence. Xoxera- continues into xoxeraruru on the next row.
[c1-l008 >] ruru. *Condenar, ou ſer condenado a morte,*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the Japanese passive form and glosses condemning or being condemned to death. The right-edge punctuation is a comma, leading into the alternative below.
[c1-l009 >] *ou justiçado.*
[c1-l009 note] Ou justiçado adds being executed or judicially punished. This completes the severe-penalty explanation, retaining the source's st-type.
[c1-l010] Guenquan. *Entrada, ou porta do caminho por*
[c1-l010 note] Genkan first means an entrance or gateway on the route to a tea gathering. The Portuguese relative clause continues below.
[c1-l011 >] *onde vão ao Chanoyu. ¶ Item, Porta in-*
[c1-l011 note] The route leads to chanoyu; a second sense begins an inner doorway. Chanoyu is Japanese vocabulary within the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l012 >] *terior por onde ſe entra em caſa dalguem.*
[c1-l012 note] Completes the inner door through which one enters someone's house. This is the second architectural sense of Guenquan.
[c1-l013] Guenquan. Arauaruru cuda, ſanyôno noco-
[c1-l013 note] A homonymous genkan concerns the outstanding balance in accounts. The printed Japanese begins Arauaruru cuda; the c has no clear ascender, and cuda is retained literally despite its unresolved lexical identification. The following sanyō phrase and Portuguese clarify the accounting sense; a targeted external lookup supplied no clarifying parallel.
[c1-l014 >] ru guiuo yǔ. *Reſtante das contas.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes the Japanese explanation as referring to what remains in the reckoning. The gloss explicitly says the remainder of the accounts; the mark in yǔ is retained as a caron.
[c1-l015] Guenrai. Miye qitaru. *Couſa que ſe manda*
[c1-l015 note] Genrai is something sent onward as it arrived from elsewhere. Miye kitaru supplies the Japanese paraphrase; the explanation continues below.
[c1-l016 >] *aßi como veo de fora: vſaſe nas cartas, & ẽ*
[c1-l016 note] The object is forwarded just as it came, a usage found in letters and conversation. The source's aßi and abbreviated ẽ are preserved.
[c1-l017 >] *pratica. Vt,* Guenraini macaxe xinji ſo-
[c1-l017 note] Genrai ni makase shinji soro is the example of forwarding or offering something as received. The polite ending soro is split across the physical rows.
[c1-l018 >] ro. *Aßi como veo volo offereço, ou mando.*
[c1-l018 note] Ro completes soro; the translation says I offer or send it to you just as it came. The historical object combination volo remains unchanged.
[c1-l019 >] *¶* Guenrai tçucamatçutta fodoni, xinjǒ
[c1-l019 note] A second example says that, having received it, the speaker now presents it. The scan supports Guenrai, correcting OCR Guemiai; xinjǒ continues into itasu below.
[c1-l020 >] itaſu. *Idem.*
[c1-l020 note] Itasu completes shinjō itasu, to present or offer respectfully. Idem assigns the same sense as the preceding forwarding example.
[c1-l021] Guenrǒ. *Vt,* Guenrǒ chixin nari. *He*
[c1-l021 note] Genrō is illustrated by an old and wise retainer. The initial is G, not C; the caron in both occurrences is retained.
[c1-l022 >] *criado antigo, & ſabio.*
[c1-l022 note] The Portuguese describes an old or long-serving and wise retainer. This completes the predicate begun by He above.
[c1-l023] Guenſon. Feri, ſenzuru. *Diminuição, &*
[c1-l023 note] Genson is diminution or loss, paraphrased heri and senzuru. The headword's long s and the printed Japanese forms are preserved.
[c1-l024 >] *perda como de fazenda, &c.* S.
[c1-l024 note] The loss may concern property or goods. The terminal S. is a roman written-register label and is separated from the italic gloss.
[c1-l025] Guentan. Xǒguachino axita. *Primeira*
[c1-l025 note] Gantan is the first morning of the New Year, paraphrased shōgachi no ashita. The headword's n and the caron in Xǒguachino are retained.
[c1-l026 >] *manhãa do Xǒguachi.* S.
[c1-l026 note] Completes the first morning of shōgachi. On the second pass the tilde lies over or very close to the first of the two a types, so the aligned baseline manhãa is retained provisionally rather than imposing the more usual manhaã pattern. Xǒguachi is italic within the Portuguese; S. is roman.
[c1-l027] Guẽtǒ. *Este mũdo, ou vida preſente & futura.*
[c1-l027 note] Gentō denotes the present and future lives or worlds. Reinspection shows a faint broken mark above e, interpreted as an abbreviation tilde: Guẽtǒ, not a silently supplied full n. The following examples spell Guentǒ, and the supplied NINJAL entry supports this identification; the damaged mark remains uncertain.
[c1-l028 >] *¶* Guentǒ nixeno vongouo auogu ta-
[c1-l028 note] Gentō nise no ongo wo aogu tatematsuru asks reverently for protection in the two lives or worlds. Ta- begins the honorific auxiliary completed below.
[c1-l029 >] tematçuru. *Pedir a proteição deſte mundo,*
[c1-l029 note] Tematçuru completes tatematsuru; the Portuguese begins asking protection for this world. The source's proteição spelling is retained.
[c1-l030 >] *& do outro. ¶* Guentǒno tçumiuo fa-
[c1-l030 note] Adds the other world, then introduces gentō no tsumi wo hatasu, discharging sins associated with the two lives. Fa- continues into fatasu below.
[c1-l031 >] taſu. *Pagar aqui, ou ſer castigado pollos*
[c1-l031 note] Tasu completes hatasu; the Portuguese speaks of paying here or being punished for sins. The cast-type is retained without a global long-s change.
[c1-l032 >] *peccados feitos neſta vida, & que bum auia*
[c1-l032 note] These are sins committed in this life that one would otherwise have to pay for in the next. On reinspection the initial of bum has a closed b-like bowl, unlike the clear h of hum at c2-l003. Retain baseline bum as a possible printed error for hum; an over-inked h cannot be entirely excluded.
[c1-l033 >] *de pagar na outra.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes the contrast: sins incurred here would have to be paid for in the other life. The Portuguese sentence ends on this physical line.
[c1-l034] Guentô. *Meliùs,* Quẽtô. Qibixij fuyu.
[c1-l034 note] Gentō is redirected to kentō, severe winter, with kibishii fuyu as the Japanese paraphrase. Both o-vowels carry circumflexes; the t in abbreviated Quẽtô is poorly inked and is read from residual traces and context.
[c1-l035 >] *Coração, ou força do inuerno.*
[c1-l035 note] The Portuguese describes the heart or greatest severity of winter, completing the entry above rather than introducing another sense of heart.
[c1-l036] Guenxa. *Vide,* Guenja.
[c1-l036 note] Genxa redirects to genja, the practitioner described on the preceding page. The printed x/j variation is preserved.
[c1-l037] Guenxi. Moto fajime. *Principio.*
[c1-l037 note] Genshi means an origin or beginning, paraphrased moto hajime. The definition Principio confirms that sense.
[c1-l038] Guenxi. *Rezão, ou miſterio profundo.* Bup.
[c1-l038 note] A separate genshi denotes a profound principle or mystery. Bup. labels the Buddhist context and remains roman.
[c1-l039] Guenzai. *Este mundo, ou esta vida preſente.*
[c1-l039 note] Genzai is the present world or present life. The two Portuguese descriptions are alternatives within one entry.
[c1-l040] Guenzan. *Ver a alguem como viſitando, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] Genzan denotes seeing someone in a visit or audience. The damaged initial is G, not the OCR C, in agreement with the examples below.
[c1-l041 >] *¶* Guenzanni iru, l, guenzan ſuru. *Ver,*
[c1-l041 note] Genzan ni iru and genzan suru are alternative expressions for an audience or visit. The Portuguese infinitive is Ver, not Vor, and continues onto the next line.
[c1-l042 >] *ou encontrarſe depropoſito com alguem.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the gloss: seeing or deliberately meeting someone. The close form depropoſito is preserved rather than respaced to modern Portuguese.
[c1-l043] Guenzǒ. *Meliùs,* guenzan. *Ver a alguem.*
[c1-l043 note] Genzō is redirected to the preferred genzan, seeing someone. Meliùs is entirely italic; its internal OCR font breaks have been removed.
[c1-l044 >] *Vt,* Guenzǒ ſuru.
[c1-l044 note] Genzō suru is the example for the preceding entry. It is a standalone Japanese expression, not a Japanese word embedded in Portuguese.
[c1-l045] Guenzocu. Zocuni cayeru. *Tornarſe o*
[c1-l045 note] Genzoku means returning to secular life, paraphrased zoku ni kaeru. The first vowel in Zocuni is o, despite damaged printing; the Portuguese subject continues below.
[c1-l046 >] *Religioſo a fazer ſecular.*
[c1-l046 note] The religious person becomes secular again, completing the definition of genzoku. An ink blot obscures the middle of ſecular; the surviving letters and contrast with Religioſo support this reading, not the OCR faeular.
[c1-l047] Guenzǒnin. *Peßoa conhecida de viſta, ou q̃*
[c1-l047 note] Genzōnin is someone known by sight. The headword has n and a caron-bearing o; viſta is the sight word, not the OCR vißta, and q̃ remains an abbreviation.
[c1-l048 >] *ſe vio ja com outra.*
[c1-l048 note] Adds someone previously seen in another person's company. The short continuation belongs to genzōnin and reaches the lower body rule without an omitted row.

[c2-l001] Gueô. *Tẽpo, ou idade de algum Rey. Vt,*
[c2-l001 note] The royal-period sense of Gueô denotes the time or age of a king. Tẽpo abbreviates tempo with a tilde over e; it is not the OCR Tãpo. The historical Gueô spelling is not normalized to the following Guiô cross-reference.
[c2-l002 >] Aru micadono gueôni. *No tẽpo, ou ida-*
[c2-l002 note] Aru micadono gueôni is the example meaning in the time of a certain sovereign. The scan reads micadono, not nicaono, and the Portuguese ends with the first part of idade.
[c2-l003 >] *de de hum Rey.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes idade and specifies a king. Rey belongs wholly to the Portuguese italic gloss; the isolated roman e was an OCR font error.
[c2-l004] Gueô. *Vide,* Guiô.
[c2-l004 note] The second Gueô redirects to Guiô. This is a cross-reference entry distinct from the royal-period sense immediately above.
[c2-l005] Gueôqi. *Vide,* Guiôqi.
[c2-l005 note] Gueôqi redirects to Guiôqi. Both circumflex-bearing o-vowels and the q in the damaged headword are retained from the scan.
[c2-l006] Gueôraxij. *Vide,* Guiôraxij.
[c2-l006 note] Gueôraxij redirects to Guiôraxij, preserving the historical -raxij spelling. No independent Portuguese definition is supplied here.
[c2-l007] Gueôtai. *Vide,* Guiôtai
[c2-l007 note] Gueôtai redirects to Guiôtai. The terminal period is not clearly printed, so none is added to the baseline.
[c2-l008] Gueôxǒ. *Vide,* Guiôxǒ.
[c2-l008 note] Gueôxǒ redirects to Guiôxǒ. The initial G and the distinction between circumflex ô and caron ǒ are visible; OCR Cueôxǒ is corrected.
[c2-l009] Gueppacu. Tçuqi, xemaru. i, Tçuqino fa-
[c2-l009 note] Geppaku is paraphrased tsuki semaru and tsuki no hate. The latter phrase is divided after fa- and is completed below, where the year-end sense is made explicit.
[c2-l010 >] te. *Fim dos meſes que he* Xiuaſu.
[c2-l010 note] Completes hate and identifies the end of the sequence of months as Shiwasu. Xiuaſu is a Japanese month name embedded within the Portuguese definition and remains roman as printed.
[c2-l011] Gueqicon. *Vide,* Guecon.
[c2-l011 note] Gekikon redirects to gekon. The second vowel of Gueqicon is e, not the OCR u; the Japanese headwords are not embedded Portuguese terms.
[c2-l012] Gueqifa. Sacaxima nami. *Ondas aleuanta-*
[c2-l012 note] Gekiha is paraphrased sakashima nami, opposing or rising waves. The Portuguese aleuanta- is divided here and completed on the next physical line.
[c2-l013 >] *das que vem por proa.*
[c2-l013 note] These are raised waves coming against the bow of a vessel. The damaged initial d of das is included within the crop and continues aleuantadas above.
[c2-l014] Gueqiǒ. *Çururgia, ou çururgião.*
[c2-l014 note] Gekyō denotes surgery or a surgeon. The source spells the Portuguese words with ç and with -rurgia/-rurgião, not the OCR -ruegia/-rurgião confusion; the headword retains caron ǒ.
[c2-l015] Gueqiǒja. *Çururgião.*
[c2-l015 note] Gekyōja specifically denotes the surgeon. The capital Ç has a visible lower stroke and is retained rather than modernized to Cirurgião.
[c2-l016] Gueqio. *Mal derins,*
[c2-l016 note] Gekyo is defined as a disease of the kidneys. The printed close form derins and the comma-like terminal mark are retained; this entry lacks the long-vowel sign of the two preceding surgical terms.
[c2-l017] Gueqiran. Sacaſamani midaruru. *Grande*
[c2-l017 note] Gekiran means great disorder, paraphrased sakasama ni midaruru. The adjective Grande carries over into the Portuguese nouns on the next line.
[c2-l018 >] *perturbação, ou reuoltas.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes the definition as disturbance or revolts. The second noun reads reuoltas, with t, not the OCR reuolias.
[c2-l019] Gueqiri. Axida cutçu. *Tamãcos.*
[c2-l019 note] Gekiri is equated with ashida and kutsu and translated as wooden clogs. Tamãcos retains the printed nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l020] Gueqirin. *Agaſtamento de peſſoa real.*
[c2-l020 note] Gekirin denotes the anger of a royal person. The Portuguese peſſoa has two s-elements, not l followed by long s as in OCR.
[c2-l021 >] *¶* Gueqirin aru, l, naſaruru. *Agaſtar*
[c2-l021 note] Gekirin aru or nasaruru describes a ruler becoming angry. The headword ends in n, and the apparent OCR colon at the right is not separable from the broken column rule; no terminal punctuation is supplied.
[c2-l022 >] *ſe el Rey.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes the reflexive Agaſtarſe and supplies the royal subject. The spurious final roman e was caused by the damaged ending and is removed.
[c2-l023] Gueqirǒ. Sacaxima nami. *Ondas aleuanta*
[c2-l023 note] Gekirō again denotes opposing waves. The corrected headword has e and a caron-bearing final o; the definition ends mid-word without a secure printed hyphen.
[c2-l024 >] *das por proa. ¶ Item, Permet. Pertur-*
[c2-l024 note] Completes the waves coming against the bow, then introduces a metaphorical sense of disturbance. Permet. is the printed abbreviated marker, with final t rather than OCR s; Pertur- continues below.
[c2-l025 >] *bação. ¶* Xicaino gueqirǒuo xizzumu-
[c2-l025 note] Completes perturbação and gives shikai no gekirō wo shizumuru, calming disturbances throughout the realm. The scan supplies the missing n in Xicaino; xizzumu- continues below.
[c2-l026 >] ru. *Aquietar as perturbações do mundo. i,*
[c2-l026 note] Ru completes shizumuru, followed by the Portuguese translation about quieting disturbances in the world. The final i, introduces the narrower explanation on the next line.
[c2-l027 >] *do imperio.*
[c2-l027 note] Specifies that world here means the empire or realm. This is a gloss continuation, not a separate entry or an omitted Japanese fragment.
[c2-l028] Gueqito. Sacaxima tomogara. *Inimigos,*
[c2-l028 note] Gekito is paraphrased sakashima tomogara and begins a definition of enemies. The following line specifies rebellion against a lord.
[c2-l029 >] *ou aleuantados contra o ſenhor.*
[c2-l029 note] Completes the definition as those risen against their lord. The historical spelling aleuantados and the long s in ſenhor are retained.
[c2-l030] Gueqixin. Sacaximano xinca. *Criado trai-*
[c2-l030 note] Gekishin is a rebellious retainer, paraphrased sakashima no shinka. The Portuguese divides trai-dor at this line boundary.
[c2-l031 >] *dor, ou aleuantado.*
[c2-l031 note] Dor completes traidor, and the alternative aleuantado again expresses rebellion. The text remains a continuation of the retainer entry.
[c2-l032] Guequan. *Meliùs,* Caquan. i, Fiqij cu-
[c2-l032 note] Gekwan is redirected to the preferred kakwan and paraphrased hikii kurai, low rank. The Japanese cu- at the right is continued by rai on the next line.
[c2-l033 >] rai. *Dignidade baixa. Vt,* Iǒquan gue-
[c2-l033 note] Completes kurai, low rank, then contrasts jōkwan and gekwan. The example reads Iǒquan, not the OCR Iǒquaſi; gue- continues below.
[c2-l034 >] quan. *Suprema, & infima dignidade.*
[c2-l034 note] Quan completes gekwan. The Portuguese explicitly contrasts the highest and lowest dignity or rank, confirming the paired Japanese terms above.
[c2-l035] Gueracu. *Decer, ou vir do Miyaco pera outra*
[c2-l035 note] Geraku means going down or coming from Miyako to another place. Miyaco is embedded in the italic Portuguese explanation, not presented as a headword or standalone synonym.
[c2-l036 >] *parte.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes the destination as another place. The short line is a genuine body continuation with no additional hidden entry in its blank right-hand area.
[c2-l037] Gueriacu. *Potiùs,* Cariacu. Xitauo ria
[c2-l037 note] Geryaku is redirected to karyaku and paraphrased shita wo ryakusu. The scan begins with G, not C; ria ends the line without a securely printed hyphen.
[c2-l038 >] cuſu. *O abreujar, ou deixar de tocar cer-*
[c2-l038 note] Cusu completes ryakusu. The Portuguese explains abbreviation or omission and breaks cer-tas at the right margin.
[c2-l039 >] *tas pancadas do cabo no tanger do tabaquinho*
[c2-l039 note] Completes certas and describes omitting final strokes while playing a small Japanese drum. The instrument word is the continuous tabaquinho, not the OCR Iaba quinho.
[c2-l040 >] *de Iapão, &c. ¶ Item, Sincopar a pa-*
[c2-l040 note] Completes the Japanese instrument description, then introduces another sense: shortening a word at its end. The pa- fragment continues as palavra below.
[c2-l041 >] *laura no cabo. Vt,* Iſabelno cauarini, Iſa.
[c2-l041 note] Completes palavra and illustrates final shortening: Isa in place of Isabel. The Japanese metalinguistic example is roman throughout, including l; the shortened name visibly begins with a capital I.
[c2-l042] Gueret. *Baixeza. ¶* Gueretna fito. *Ho-*
[c2-l042 note] Geretsu denotes baseness or low status; geretsu na hito gives a lowly person. The scan supports G in both forms and t followed by n in Gueretna, not the OCR m.
[c2-l043 >] *mem baixo.*
[c2-l043 note] Mem completes homem, translating the preceding Japanese phrase as a lowly man. This continuation does not introduce another entry.
[c2-l044] Gueri. *Doença de camaras.*
[c2-l044 note] Geri is described as a bowel disease. The initial of Doença is D, not OCR C; the historical expression de camaras is retained rather than replaced by a modern diagnosis.
[c2-l045] Guerǒ. *Peſſòa ſomenos, ou inferior entre*
[c2-l045 note] Gerō is defined as an inferior or lesser person. The description explicitly begins a comparison within social groups, continued below; the provisional accent in Peſſòa is retained.
[c2-l046 >] *nobres, ou tambem entre gente baixa.*
[c2-l046 note] The comparison applies both among nobles and among people of lower standing. This completes gerō and does not alter the separate gesu entry beneath it.
[c2-l047] Gueſu. i, Iyaxij mono. *Eſcrauo, ou eſcraua.*
[c2-l047 note] Gesu is paraphrased iyashii mono and translated here as a male or female slave. The corrected initial is G and Eſcrauo has long s, not OCR j; the lower-right Gue- is a separate catchword.

## catchword [catchword] Catchword
