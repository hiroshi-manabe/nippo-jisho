[c1-l001 >] *preßa a fugir decà pera là.*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding page's description of settlements taking hurried flight, scattering here and there. Preßa is running text here; its occurrence in the preceding page's lower margin is only a catchword.
[c1-l002] Niguejini. *O morrer fugindo. ¶* Nigue-
[c1-l002 note] Nigejini means dying while fleeing. The headword is visibly continuous, rather than the draft's Nigue jini; the following example divides the same compound at the line end.
[c1-l003 >] jiniuo ſuru. *Idem.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes nigejini wo suru, to die in flight. Idem refers back to the headword's definition.
[c1-l004] Niguejiri. *Vt,* Niguejiriuo qiru. l, qira-
[c1-l004 note] Nigejiri is illustrated by cutting someone from behind while fleeing, with the alternative passive kiraruru. The passive form is divided after qira-; Latin l is not part of the Japanese.
[c1-l005 >] ruru. *Ferir, ou ſer ferido por de tras.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes kiraruru and translates the active/passive pair as wounding or being wounded from behind. The printed de tras spacing is retained.
[c1-l006] Niguejitacu. *Aparelho, ou geito de fugir.*
[c1-l006 note] Nigejitaku means preparation or arrangements for flight. The Portuguese geito is retained rather than modernized to jeito.
[c1-l007] Niguemauari, u, atta. *Fugir por diuerſas par-*
[c1-l007 note] Nigemawari means moving about in flight through different places. The physical division par-tes begins its extended Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l008 >] *tes, ou furtar o corpo de muitas maneiras pera*
[c1-l008 note] Completes partes and adds evading someone in many ways. The purpose of this evasive movement follows below.
[c1-l009 >] *ſe não encontrar com alguem.*
[c1-l009 note] The purpose is to avoid encountering someone. This closes the nigemawari gloss rather than introducing a separate reflexive verb entry.
[c1-l010] Niguemayoi, ô, ôta. *Fugindo andar deſgar-*
[c1-l010 note] Nigemayoi means wandering astray while fleeing. Both principal-part o marks visibly point upward, so the draft's first ǒ is corrected to ô; the Portuguese adjective continues below.
[c1-l011 >] *rado. Vt,* Sôzocu nannho tôzaini nigue
[c1-l011 note] Completes desgarrado and begins an example of religious and lay people, men and women, fleeing east and west. The spelling nannho is retained rather than normalized to a modern reading.
[c1-l012 >] mayô. Taif. *Lib. 28. Religioſos,*
[c1-l012 note] Mayoo completes nigemayoo. The upright Taif. citation is followed by italic book 28 and the translation beginning with religious people; the citation's final stop is kept outside the italic apparatus.
[c1-l013 >] *& ſeculares homẽs, & molheres andauão fugi-*
[c1-l013 note] The translation includes lay people as well as religious people, men as well as women, all fleeing. Homẽs retains its printed abbreviation mark.
[c1-l014 >] *dos, & diſperſos ſem ſaber poronde hião.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes fugidos and describes them as scattered, not knowing where they were going. The close-set poronde and historical hião remain unchanged.
[c1-l015] Niguen. l, nigon. Futatçuno cotoba. *Duas*
[c1-l015 note] Nigen, alternatively nigon, is explained as futatsu no kotoba, two utterances. The Portuguese definition continues below and connects the term with repeating one's words.
[c1-l016 >] *palauras, ou o repetir de palaura. ¶* Cũ-
[c1-l016 note] The gloss gives two words or repetition of an utterance. Cũ- begins kunshi in the example below; the tilde abbreviates the nasal and the entire Japanese fragment is upright, not partly italic.
[c1-l017 >] xini niguen naxi. *O homem ſabio, & pru*
[c1-l017 note] Kunshi ni nigen nashi says that a gentleman or wise person does not speak with two voices. The Portuguese renders the person as wise and prudent; pru continues below without an inferred hyphen.
[c1-l018 >] *dente não tem muitas palauras.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes prudente and says that such a person does not have many words. This is the dictionary's paraphrase, not an editorial replacement by a more familiar modern proverb translation.
[c1-l019] Niguenobi, bu, l, uru, ita. *Ir fugindo muito*
[c1-l019 note] Nigenobi means getting far ahead while escaping. The two printed dictionary-form alternatives bu and uru are retained as given.
[c1-l020 >] *diãte dos que vem no alcance.*
[c1-l020 note] The fugitives are far ahead of those pursuing them. The contracted diãte completes the preceding muito and is not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l021] Nigueſari, u, atta. *Fugindo acolherſe, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Nigueſari denotes withdrawing or getting away in flight; the Portuguese offers taking refuge and, on the next row, moving away. The three verb forms remain on one physical line.
[c1-l022 >] *apartarſe.*
[c1-l022 note] Apartarſe completes the alternative definition of Nigueſari: separating oneself or withdrawing. This is a short indented continuation, not a new entry.
[c1-l023] Nigue vxe, ſuru, eta. *Tugindo deſaparecer.*
[c1-l023 note] Nigue vxe combines flight with disappearance. The first Portuguese letter survives in a T-like form, so the aligned draft's Tugindo is retained. Fugindo is clearly intended by the sense and neighboring entries, but a worn F cannot be distinguished securely from a printed T here; the uncertainty is not silently normalized.
[c1-l024] Nigui. i, Tenchi. *Ceo, & terra. ¶* Ni-
[c1-l024 note] Nigui is equated with Tenchi, heaven and earth. The pilcrow starts a further expression whose first element is physically divided Ni-/gui across the row boundary.
[c1-l025 >] gui caifiacu. *Deſda criação do mundo. S.*
[c1-l025 note] Ni-/gui caifiacu is explained as dating from the creation of the world. The old Portuguese spacing Deſda and the separately printed upright S. are preserved. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l026] Niguiniguito. *Adu. Com apparato de gẽ-*
[c1-l026 note] Niguiniguito is labeled an adverb and describes a display or bustle of people. The tilde and terminal hyphen in gẽ- belong to the printed abbreviation and continuation.
[c1-l027 >] *te, & festa, &c. Vt,* Qenaimo nigui-
[c1-l027 note] The gloss continues with people and festivity, then introduces a Japanese example concerning the inside of a household. The roman example is physically divided at nigui-.
[c1-l028 >] niguito miyeta. *Dentro da caſa parecia mo-*
[c1-l028 note] This completes the example's niguiniguito miyeta. The Portuguese begins describing how the interior of the house appeared; mo- continues as modo on the next row.
[c1-l029 >] *do de aparato, tumulto de gente, & abaſtan*
[c1-l029 note] The house presents an appearance of display, bustling people and abundance. The final abaſtan continues into ça below without a visible printed hyphen, which has not been supplied.
[c1-l030 >] *ça, &c.*
[c1-l030 note] Ça completes abaſtança, abundance, and closes the household example. The short continuation remains a separate indented physical row.
[c1-l031] Niguiniguixij. *Couſa de concurſo de gente, a-*
[c1-l031 note] Niguiniguixij describes something crowded, lively or festive. The Portuguese begins with a gathering of people and divides a-/parato across the next boundary.
[c1-l032 >] *parato, feſta, &c.*
[c1-l032 note] Aparato and feſta complete the description with display and festivity. This is the continuation of the adjective's gloss, not a separate definition.
[c1-l033 >] Nigui niguixiſa.
[c1-l033 note] This indented derivative is the corresponding noun in -ſa, liveliness or festive bustle. The visible internal space is retained rather than joining it to match the main headword.
[c1-l034 >] Nigui niguixǔ.
[c1-l034 note] The second indented derivative gives the adverbial form in -xǔ. The opening stroke of the repeated element is worn, but the printed spacing and marked final vowel remain recoverable.
[c1-l035] Niguiri, u, itta. *Apertar com a mão.*
[c1-l035 note] Niguiri means gripping or squeezing with the hand. The entry supplies the usual three verb forms, followed by a figurative example on the next row.
[c1-l036 >] *¶* Xecaiuo tanagocoroni niguiru. *Senho-*
[c1-l036 note] The example pictures holding the world in one's palm. Its Portuguese interpretation begins Senho-, continuing into the verb for ruling or mastering below.
[c1-l037 >] *rear todo o mundo.*
[c1-l037 note] Together with Senho- above, this says to rule the whole world. It completes the figurative application of gripping something in one's hand.
[c1-l038] Niguiri. *Lugar por onde tomão o arco cõ a mão*
[c1-l038 note] This separate noun entry denotes the grip of a bow, the place held with the hand. The nasal abbreviation cõ is retained, and the specification of which hand continues below.
[c1-l039 >] *eſquerda forrado com couro.*
[c1-l039 note] The grip is held with the left hand and is covered in leather. This finishes the definition of the bow part rather than describing a different item.
[c1-l040] Niguiricobuxi. *Punho ſeco. ¶* Niguiri-
[c1-l040 note] Niguiricobuxi denotes a clenched fist. The Portuguese phrase Punho ſeco is followed by an example whose noun is divided Niguiri-/cobuxi across rows.
[c1-l041 >] cobuxide vtçu. *Dar depunhadas, ou com*
[c1-l041 note] The example means striking with a fist. Depunhadas retains the source's close spacing; the alternative Portuguese wording is completed on the following row.
[c1-l042 >] *punho ſeco.*
[c1-l042 note] This repeats the clenched-fist expression. The partly abraded vowel in punho is read as u, consistent with its surviving strokes and the clearer occurrence two rows above, rather than the draft's penho.
[c1-l043] Niguiuai. *Aparato de gente, & abastança.*
[c1-l043 note] Niguiuai is the noun for bustle or prosperity, glossed through a display of people and abundance. The faint middle letters are retained as the same stem used in the next entry.
[c1-l044] Niguiuai, ǒ, ǒta. *Auer aparato. & abaſtança.*
[c1-l044 note] The corresponding verb means having bustle, display and abundance. Both marked o forms show the downward-pointing accent of ǒ. Enlarged reinspection shows a point after aparato rather than a descending comma; that unusual punctuation is preserved.
[c1-l045] Niguiyacana. *Couſa festiual de gente, apara-*
[c1-l045 note] Niguiyacana describes a lively or festive scene involving people and display. The printed apara- continues into apparatus or display on the next physical row.
[c1-l046 >] *to, &c.*
[c1-l046 note] To completes aparato and closes the adjective's Portuguese explanation. Its short length does not make it page furniture or an independent entry.
[c1-l047 >] Niguiyacani. Niguiyacaſa.
[c1-l047 note] The two indented forms give the adverb in -ni and the noun in -ſa associated with Niguiyacana. They are printed together on one row without additional Portuguese glosses.
[c1-l048] Nigura. *Albarda.*
[c1-l048 note] Nigura is glossed as a pack saddle. This final left-column entry stands above the bottom rule and is body text, distinct from the signature and catchword elsewhere below the page.

[c2-l001] Nigin. Mimino chiri. *Ciſco, ou pò dos ou-*
[c2-l001 note] Nigin is paraphrased as Mimino chiri, dust of the ears. The Portuguese likewise gives dirt or dust in the ears; chiri has h rather than the draft's b, and ou- continues below.
[c2-l002 >] *uidos. ¶ Per met.* Niginuo farǒte qiqu.

[c2-l003 note] The Portuguese interprets the figurative expression as listening attentively. The final S. is upright and separate from the italic translation. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l004] Niji. *Arco do ceo. ¶* Nijiga tatçu. *Fazer-*
[c2-l004 note] Niji means a rainbow, literally the sky's bow in the Portuguese gloss. Nijiga tatçu introduces its appearance or formation, with Fazer- continued on the next row.
[c2-l005 >] *ſe eſte arco. ¶* Nijiga qiyuru. *Desfa-*
[c2-l005 note] The first phrase finishes the rainbow's formation; the second describes its disappearance. Both the intervening pilcrow and the terminal division Desfa- are preserved.
[c2-l006 >] *zerſe o arco do ceo.*
[c2-l006 note] Joined to Desfa- above, this means the rainbow dissolving or disappearing. The continuation closes the second example under Niji.
[c2-l007] Nijigata. *Feição de arco do ceo. Vt,* Mado
[c2-l007 note] Nijigata denotes a rainbow-like or arched shape. The example starts with Mado, a window, in roman type at the right end of the same row.
[c2-l008 >] uo nijigatani aquru. *Abrir janella à fei-*
[c2-l008 note] The example describes making a window opening in an arched shape. The Portuguese repeats that construction and divides fei-/ção across the physical rows.
[c2-l009 >] *ção de arco do ceo.*
[c2-l009 note] This completes the phrase describing the window's rainbow or arch shape. It remains the continuation of the Nijigata example.
[c2-l010] Nijiri, u, itta. *Irſeroçando pollo chão.*
[c2-l010 note] Nijiri describes moving while sliding or rubbing along the ground. The close-set Irſeroçando is retained without modernizing the spacing or the historical contraction pollo.
[c2-l011] Nijiriyori, u, otta. *Irſe chegando roçandoſe*
[c2-l011 note] Nijiriyori adds approaching to the sliding motion of the previous entry. The Portuguese describes drawing nearer while rubbing or sliding along a surface specified below.
[c2-l012 >] *pollo chão.*
[c2-l012 note] The surface is the ground. This short continuation completes the definition of Nijiriyori and retains pollo as printed.
[c2-l013] Nijiruxi. *Sinal, ou diuiſa que ſe pinta ſobre*
[c2-l013 note] Nijiruxi is an identifying mark or device painted on baggage. The scan supplies the t in pinta, omitted by the draft's pina; the object carrying the mark follows below.
[c2-l014 >] *o fato.*
[c2-l014 note] Fato here is the baggage or load on which the identifying mark is painted. This closes the Nijiruxi entry.
[c2-l015] Nijit. *Dous ſoẽs. Vt,* Tenni nijitnaqu,
[c2-l015 note] Nijit means two suns. The example begins the maxim that there are not two suns in heaven. The close-set nijitnaqu is retained as in the aligned draft: its narrow internal gap does not securely establish a printed word space.
[c2-l016 >] chini nixu naxi. *No ceo não ha dous ſo-*
[c2-l016 note] The Japanese continues with there not being two rulers on earth. The Portuguese starts with the parallel statement about heaven and divides ſo-/ẽs between rows.
[c2-l017 >] *ẽs, nem na terra, ou reino dous ſenhores.*
[c2-l017 note] This completes the maxim: neither two suns in heaven nor two lords in a land or kingdom. The wording is the dictionary's explanation, not a supplied expansion of the Japanese.
[c2-l018] Nijǔfaxxucu. *28. Estrellas, ou ſignos celeſtes*
[c2-l018 note] The entry describes twenty-eight stars or celestial signs. The mark over u is visibly downward-pointing, so Nijǔ replaces the draft's grave-marked Nijù without otherwise changing the headword.
[c2-l019 >] *que os Iapoẽs, & Chinas nomeão por nomes de*
[c2-l019 note] The dictionary attributes the naming of these celestial signs to Japanese and Chinese people. The printed nasal marks and historical forms Iapoẽs and Chinas are retained.
[c2-l020 >] *animaes.*
[c2-l020 note] The names are those of animals, completing the dictionary's explanation of Nijǔfaxxucu. This single-word line is a body continuation, not a label or catchword.
[c2-l021] Nimame. *Grãos cozidos.*
[c2-l021 note] Nimame is glossed as cooked grains or pulses. The nasal mark in Grãos belongs to the Portuguese word and has not been expanded into an extra letter.
[c2-l022] Nimô. Mimiqiqu. *Ouuir. S.*
[c2-l022 note] Nimô is paraphrased by Mimiqiqu and glossed as hearing. The clearly upward-pointing mark over o is retained, along with the final S. label in the gloss's typeface.
[c2-l023] Nimochi. *O que leua o fato.*
[c2-l023 note] Nimochi denotes the person who carries the baggage or load. The Portuguese is a complete single-row explanation.
[c2-l024] Nimot. *Fato.*
[c2-l024 note] Nimot names the baggage itself, contrasting with the bearer in the preceding entry. The historical final t is retained rather than expanding the word to a modern Japanese form.
[c2-l025] Nina. *Caramujos.*
[c2-l025 note] Nina is glossed as snails. Enlarged reinspection shows the dotted letter after u descending below the baseline with a hook, so Caramujos replaces the draft's Caramuios on graphic evidence, not through a general modernization of consonantal i.
[c2-l026] Nin-ai. *Trato, & conuerſação humana. Vt,*
[c2-l026 note] Nin-ai concerns human dealings and sociability. Its internal hyphen is printed within the headword, not a physical line-break sign; Vt introduces the example below.
[c2-l027 >] Nin aino yoi fito. *Homem brando, & de boa*
[c2-l027 note] The example describes a person good to deal with, rendered in Portuguese as gentle and of good conversation or company. The Japanese's spacing differs from the hyphenated headword and is preserved.
[c2-l028 >] *conuerſação.*
[c2-l028 note] This completes the description of someone agreeable to converse or associate with. It is the end of the Nin-ai example rather than a separate social category.
[c2-l029] Ninai. *Baldes de carretar agoa.*
[c2-l029 note] This noun Ninai denotes buckets used for carrying water. It is distinct from the social term immediately above and is followed by the related carrying verb.
[c2-l030] Ninai, ǒ, ǒta. *Leuar algũa couſa aos ombros*
[c2-l030 note] The verb describes carrying a load on the shoulders. The final s of ombros is very worn beside the right rule but has surviving ink; that chosen plural reading is a nonblocking graphic uncertainty.
[c2-l031 >] *que tenha peſo diante, & de tras.*
[c2-l031 note] The carried object has weight in front and behind. This qualifies the shoulder-carried load in the Ninai definition and retains the printed separation de tras.
[c2-l032] Ninaigoxi. *Andas que leuão às costas, ou aos*

[c2-l034] Ninaua. *Corda de amarrar o fato.*

[c2-l037] Ninbet. *Finta, ou impoſição que paga cada*

[c2-l039 >] *eſta finta a cada peßoa.*

[c2-l041 >] *acarreta o fato.*
[c2-l041 note] The relative clause specifies a person who carries baggage. This completes the explanation of Ninbu and Ninſocu.
[c2-l042] Ninchi. i, Fitono chiye. *Saber humano.*

[c2-l043 >] *¶* Ninchi tenſainimo voyobanu. *Não*

[c2-l044 >] *chegar, ou abrãger o ſaber humano, nẽ celestial.*
[c2-l044 note] The translation describes something beyond the reach or compass of human and celestial understanding. The nasal abbreviations abrãger and nẽ remain unexpanded.
[c2-l045] Ninchicu. i, Ninguento, chicuxǒ. *Home*
[c2-l045 note] Ninchicu combines human beings and animals; the Japanese paraphrase names both categories. The scan supports x in chicuxǒ, not the draft's a; the short Portuguese Home is retained as printed at the right edge.
[c2-l046 >] *& animal.*
[c2-l046 note] And animal completes the definition begun with Home above. This row belongs to Ninchicu, not to the next entry involving heavenly beings.
[c2-l047] Nindẽ. i, Ninguẽto, tẽnin. *Homẽ, & Anio*
[c2-l047 note] Nindẽ is paraphrased as humans and heavenly beings, translated by the dictionary as man and angel. The scan restores the damaged OCR's headword, abbreviation marks and initial H in Homẽ; Anio retains the printed consonantal i, and no final stop is supplied.
