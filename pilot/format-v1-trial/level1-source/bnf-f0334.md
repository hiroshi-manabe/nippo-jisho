[c1-l001] Môtô. *Hum cabelinho da cabeça. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l001 note] Môtô denotes a single hair of the head, then introduces a transferred sense of something minute. Both circumflexes are visible; this is the headword anticipated by the previous page's catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *Couſa muito pouca como ponta de hum cabelo.*
[c1-l002 note] The transferred sense is a very small amount, compared with the tip of a hair. This completes the Môtô entry.
[c1-l003] Moto. *Principio, ou raiz. Vt,* Qino mo-
[c1-l003 note] Moto means a beginning or root. Qino mo- starts the example ki no moto, a tree's root, with its printed line-end division.
[c1-l004 >] to. *Raiz daruore. ¶ Item, Dantes, ou*
[c1-l004 note] To completes moto and its tree-root gloss. A new sense follows, meaning formerly or from the beginning.
[c1-l005 >] *des do principio.*
[c1-l005 note] Des do principio completes the temporal sense. The historical separate des do is preserved rather than replaced by modern desde.
[c1-l006] Motobu. *Hũa doença das mãos com que in-*
[c1-l006 note] Motobu names a hand ailment. The definition begins the description of swelling, physically dividing inchão across two rows; no modern diagnosis is inferred.

[c1-l007 note] Chão os dedos completes the explanation that the fingers swell. It is the continuation of in- above, not a reference to the ground.

[c1-l017] Motodori. *Cabelos do toutiço que cuſtumão ter,*

[c1-l042] Mottainai. *Couſa inſofriuel, ou que não conuẽ.*
[c1-l042 note] Mottainai is explained here as intolerable or improper. The damaged second t remains recoverable; the scan also supports continuous inſofriuel and the separate words que não conuẽ, correcting the garbled OCR.
