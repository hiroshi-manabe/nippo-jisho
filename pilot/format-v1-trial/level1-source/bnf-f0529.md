[c1-l001] Vataco. *Veſtido feito todo de borra tirando as*
[c1-l001 note] Watako is a garment made of silk floss or waste silk; the Portuguese excludes its borders, continued below. The damaged d of Veſtido and the word boundary before tirando are restored from the scan.
[c1-l002 >] *bordas, & debruns.*
[c1-l002 note] The edges and bindings are the exceptions to the garment's floss construction. This is the continuation of tirando as, not a separate definition.
[c1-l003] Vatacuxi. *Eu. ¶* Vye, vatacuxi. *Se-*
[c1-l003 note] Watakushi is the first-person expression glossed Eu. Uye, watakushi contrasts a superior with his dependants or with the speaker, as the next row explains.
[c1-l004 >] *nhor, & criados, ou ſenhor, & eu. ¶* Va-
[c1-l004 note] Completes Senhor: lord and servants, or lord and I. The final Va- begins the following Japanese example; the printed division is retained.
[c1-l005 >] tacuxina cotouo yǔ. *Dizer couſas propri-*
[c1-l005 note] Watakushina koto wo yuu means stating private or personal opinions. The divided opening completes Va- above, and the Portuguese adjective continues on the next physical row.
[c1-l006 >] *as, ou de ſua opinião que não tem autoridade,*
[c1-l006 note] The private opinions lack authority, according to the Portuguese explanation. The scan supports autoridade rather than OCR autoricade; no modern wording is substituted.
[c1-l007 >] *nem valor em pubrico.*
[c1-l007 note] The opinions also have no public weight. The printed form pubrico is retained with r rather than silently modernized to publico; OCR puhrico misread the b.
[c1-l008] Vatagami. l, Vatanome. *Fios de borra que*
[c1-l008 note] Watagami and watanome name fibres of floss protruding from a garment. The l is the alternative-form marker, while the relative clause continues below.
[c1-l009 >] *ſaem pollo veſtido. ¶* Vatagami. l, Va-
[c1-l009 note] The fibres emerge through the garment. The example repeats watagami and begins its alternative watanome across the row boundary.
[c1-l010 >] tano mega deta. *Sairem os fios da borra po-*
[c1-l010 note] Watanome ga deta describes the floss fibres appearing outside the cloth. The visible borra corrects OCR boira; the source's separation tano mega is retained.
[c1-l011 >] *llo veſtidoralo, ou delgado.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes pollo: the fibres emerge through thin or open-textured clothing. The tightly set veſtidoralo is preserved rather than imposing a modern word gap.
[c1-l012] Vatagami. *Armas dos ombros.*
[c1-l012 note] This distinct watagami denotes armour for the shoulders. It is a separate homograph, not another description of the garment's projecting fibres.
[c1-l013] Vatague. *Penugem de paßaro, que eſtà no ninho.*
[c1-l013 note] Watage is the down of a bird still in the nest. Enlargement shows the damaged double-s ligature in paßaro, matching the clearer repeated word on the next row.
[c1-l014 >] *¶ Item, O meſmo paßaro cõ penugem.*
[c1-l014 note] The same word can denote the down-covered young bird itself. The nasal abbreviation cõ remains unexpanded.
[c1-l015 >] *¶* Norigue. *He o falcão, que eſtà ainda*
[c1-l015 note] Norige is specified as a falcon still possessing down. The Portuguese qualification continues on the next row, distinguishing this bird-specific use.
[c1-l016 >] *em penugem.*
[c1-l016 note] The phrase completes the description of the young falcon as still in down. Its short length does not change its status as a separate physical line.
[c1-l017] Vatagomi. *Molho de palha brãda com ſal, que*
[c1-l017 note] Watagomi is a bundle of soft straw mixed with salt, used for preserving a gutted animal. The scan and repeated gomiuo below support gomi, also corroborated by the supplied NINJAL headword index; brãda is nasal-abbreviated branda.
[c1-l018 >] *metem na barriga dalgũa aue, ou peixe depois*
[c1-l018 note] The straw is placed inside a bird or fish. The visible peixe corrects OCR prixe; depois introduces the prior removal of the entrails below.
[c1-l019 >] *de lhe tirar em as tripas pera o fazer duras mui-*
[c1-l019 note] The entrails are removed so that the animal will keep for a long time. The print appears to give duras, although the intended infinitive is durar; this anomaly and the supplied tirar em spacing are retained.
[c1-l020 >] *to tempo, porque ſe não corrompa. Vt,* Vata-
[c1-l020 note] Completes muito tempo and explains the preservation purpose: preventing decomposition. Vt introduces the verb phrase divided at Vata-.
[c1-l021 >] gomiuo ſuru.
[c1-l021 note] Completes watagomi wo suru, to carry out the straw-and-salt preservation procedure. It belongs to the preceding definition rather than beginning a separate gomi entry.
[c1-l022] Vataito. *Fio de berra. ¶ Itẽ aliquando,*
[c1-l022 note] Wataito names a thread made of floss. The weak second letter in berra is retained as the aligned OCR reading, although borra is expected from the surrounding entries; the next clause adds another occasional sense.
[c1-l023 >] *Seda branda, ou cozida: poſto que mais corrẽ-*
[c1-l023 note] The occasional sense is soft or boiled silk. The explanation then contrasts this with the more usual terminology, continuing corrente across the row boundary.
[c1-l024 >] *te he,* Tçuqeito, l, Neriguri.
[c1-l024 note] Completes corrente and supplies the more usual terms Tçuqeito and Neriguri for the softened silk. The faint e after q and the following i replace the malformed OCR Tçuqrito; the visible forms are preserved without modernization.
[c1-l025] Vatamaxi. *Mudãça pera a caſa noua. ¶* Va-
[c1-l025 note] Watamashi denotes moving into a new house. The example repeats that noun across the physical division Va-/tamaxi below.
[c1-l026 >] tamaxiuo ſuru. *Fazer eſta mudança.*
[c1-l026 note] Watamashi wo suru is to make that move. The Portuguese explicitly links eſta mudança to the preceding house-changing definition.
[c1-l027] Vatanome. *Fios da borra que ſam fora do*
[c1-l027 note] Watanome again names floss fibres showing outside clothing. This standalone entry supplies the alternative previously given under watagami.
[c1-l028 >] *veſtido.*
[c1-l028 note] Veſtido completes fora do veſtido, specifying that the loose fibres are outside the garment. It remains a distinct physical continuation row.
[c1-l029] Vatari, u, atta. *Paſſar de hũa parte a outra,*
[c1-l029 note] Watari, wataru, watatta denotes crossing from one side to another. The principal parts and doubled long s in the Portuguese Paſſar are visible; the places crossed follow below.
[c1-l030 >] *como ponte, rio, &c. ¶* Faxiuo vataru.
[c1-l030 note] Bridges and rivers exemplify the crossing. Hashi wo wataru gives the bridge example, with its Portuguese equivalent on the following line.
[c1-l031 >] *Paſſar a põte. ¶* Tacocuye vataru. *Paſ-*
[c1-l031 note] The bridge example is translated first; takoku ye wataru then means going across to another country. The final Paſ- belongs to its Portuguese translation below.
[c1-l032 >] *ſar a outro reino. ¶* Vmi, l, yeuo vata-
[c1-l032 note] Completes the foreign-country gloss. Umi or ye, sea or inlet, supplies the object of the next wataru example, divided at vata-.
[c1-l033 >] ru. *Nauegar por mar, ou enſeada.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes wataru, here sailing across the sea or an inlet. The gloss confirms the two alternative objects in the preceding Japanese example.
[c1-l034] Vatari. *Paſſagem. Vt,* Vatariuo ſuru.
[c1-l034 note] The noun watari denotes a passage or crossing. Watari wo suru illustrates making the crossing, distinct from the preceding verb's principal parts.
[c1-l035] Vatariai, yǒ, yǒta. *Encontrarenſe os q̃ paſ-*
[c1-l035 note] Watariai denotes meeting while passing, with a more specific hostile sense explained below. The scan restores t in Encontrarenſe; the abbreviation q̃ and long-o carons are retained.
[c1-l036 >] *são: mas mais propriamente ſe diz dos inimi-*
[c1-l036 note] Completes paſsão and narrows the meaning to enemies. The visible round s and divided inimi- replace the OCR's cão and inimut-; the mixed s forms across paſ-/são are retained.
[c1-l037 >] *gos que ſe encontrão, & ferem hus aos outros.*
[c1-l037 note] Enemies encounter and strike one another. The printed hus is retained without adding a nasal mark merely because hũs would be expected.
[c1-l038 >] *Vt,* Teqini vatariyǒ.
[c1-l038 note] Teki ni watariyoo illustrates engaging an enemy. The contracted long-vowel form matches the principal parts of watariai, with the printed caron retained.
[c1-l039] Vatariauaxe, ſuru, eta. *Idem.*
[c1-l039 note] Watariawase carries the same encounter or combat sense as watariai. Idem refers back to that entry, while ſuru and eta give the supplied principal parts.
[c1-l040] Vataridate. *Rodela leue, & meneauel de que*
[c1-l040 note] Wataridate is described as a light, easily handled shield. The relative clause continues into its historical use against arrows below.
[c1-l041 >] *vſauão os Iapões contra as frechas.*
[c1-l041 note] The shield was used by the Japanese against arrows. Iapões, with its visible nasal mark and e, replaces the OCR's malformed Iapies.
[c1-l042] Varidono. *Hũs paßadiços, que ha fora dos pa-*
[c1-l042 note] Varidono denotes covered passages outside a palace or shrine. The print omits the ta of expected wataridono; the supplied NINJAL index independently flags this as a misprint, so the visible headword is preserved.
[c1-l043 >] *ços do Dairi, ou das ermidas dos Camis.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes paços and locates the passages outside the imperial palace or the shrines of the kami. Dairi and Camis are Japanese cultural terms embedded in the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l044] Vataxi. l, vataxigane. *Hum instrumento de*
[c1-l044 note] Watashi or watashigane names a metal implement. The following description explains its use in the equipment for blackening teeth.
[c1-l045 >] *metal bem laurado, & polido de que vſão as*
[c1-l045 note] The implement is carefully worked and polished metal. The relative clause identifies its users on the next physical row.
[c1-l046 >] *molheres atraueſado ſobre hũa ſelha, ſobre que*
[c1-l046 note] Women use the implement laid across a tub, supporting the objects described next. The abraded atraueſado and ſobre are restored from their visible strokes and the surrounding construction.
[c1-l047 >] *poem algũs petrechos com que fazem os den-*
[c1-l047 note] They set utensils on the support for the tooth-blackening procedure. The open c in petrechos replaces OCR l; the final den- continues dentes below.
[c1-l048 >] *tes pretos.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes dentes pretos, the blackened teeth. The initial t and continuation indentation are restored; the line belongs to the watashigane definition.
