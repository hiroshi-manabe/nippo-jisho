[c1-l001 >] gon ſuruua qenquano motoigia. *No jo-*
[c1-l001 note] Gon completes jo-gon from f292, not a new headword. The sentence says that giving advice during the games is a cause of quarrels; the printed motoigia is retained rather than simplified to a modern moto ja.
[c1-l002 >] *go do Go, & enxadrez o falar, ou terçar por*
[c1-l002 note] Completes jogo from the preceding row. The Portuguese explains talking or taking a side during go and chess; Go is a Japanese game-name embedded in this italic explanation.
[c1-l003 >] *hũa parte he ocaſião de brigas.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes the warning that siding with one player gives occasion for quarrels. The opening three rows are continuations and are indented like the surrounding continuation text.
[c1-l004] Iǒgouai. *Meliùs,* Iǒno couai. *Pertinaz, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Joogowai means obstinate; the dictionary prefers the separated expression joo no kowai. The initial is I, not OCR long s; Meliùs is italic editorial apparatus.
[c1-l005 >] *teimoſo.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes the obstinacy gloss with stubborn. It is not a separate entry.
[c1-l006] Iǒgouana. *Idem.*
[c1-l006 note] Joogowana is an adjectival alternative with the same meaning. Idem refers to the immediately preceding stubbornness definition.
[c1-l007] Iǒgouani. *Adu. Pertinazmente.*
[c1-l007 note] Joogowani is the corresponding adverb, stubbornly. Adu. is the italic grammatical label; the initial I lost by OCR is present.
[c1-l008] Iǒgu. Vyeuo motomuru. *Saluação. Vt,*
[c1-l008 note] Joogu is seeking what is higher, ue wo motomuru, rendered by the Portuguese as salvation. The object marker uo should not be interpreted as possessive no.
[c1-l009 >] Iǒgu bǒdaino tçutomeni vocotarazare.
[c1-l009 note] The example enjoins not neglecting the practice of seeking enlightenment. Vocotarazare is the printed negative imperative; it is not modernized.
[c1-l010 >] *Não vos deſcuideis no negocio da ſaluação.* S.
[c1-l010 note] Translates the injunction as not becoming negligent in the business of salvation. S. is an upright written-register label.
[c1-l011] Iǒguai. Xirono foca. *Fora da fortaleza.*
[c1-l011 note] Joogai is outside the fortress, shiro no hoka. The initial I omitted by OCR is visible.
[c1-l012] Iǒguan. Vyeno maqi. *Primeiro liuro, ou*
[c1-l012 note] Joogan is the first book or upper volume, ue no maki. The Portuguese explanation continues with its place in a larger work.
[c1-l013 >] *parte dalgum corpo, & volume de liuro.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the description as part of a collected body or volume of a book. It belongs to joogan.
[c1-l014] Iǒgue. Vye xita. *Em cima, & em baixo.*
[c1-l014 note] Jooge is above and below, explicitly ue and shita. The headword's initial I corrects the OCR lowercase i.
[c1-l015 >] *¶ Item, Altos, & baixos. Vt,* Iǒgue
[c1-l015 note] Adds the social sense, the high and the low. The example begins with jooge and is completed on the next line.
[c1-l016 >] banmin. *Todos, altos, & baixos. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l016 note] Completes jooge banmin, all people, high and low. Enlargement supports n in banmin rather than OCR u; Itẽ is an abbreviation of Item followed by a comma, not a word broken by a terminal hyphen.
[c1-l017 >] Nobori, cudaru. *Subir, & decer. Vt,*
[c1-l017 note] Adds the verbal interpretation of jooge: going up and coming down. The paired Japanese forms are nobori and kudaru; Vt introduces a travel example.
[c1-l018 >] Camiye jǒgueuo xiguiô itaſu. *Ir, & vir*
[c1-l018 note] The example concerns repeatedly going up to and returning from the capital. Camiye is the directional phrase, while the Portuguese continuation makes the frequency explicit.
[c1-l019 >] *frequentemente ao Miyaco.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes the translation as frequent travel to and from Miyako. Miyaco is italic in the Portuguese sentence and is not normalized to the Miaco spelling used elsewhere.
[c1-l020] Iǒguen. Vye moto. *Os 15. da primeira lũa.*
[c1-l020 note] Joogen, explained by ue moto, refers to the fifteenth day of the first lunar month. The printed Portuguese date expression and abbreviated lũa are retained without expanding them.
[c1-l021] Iǒguiǒ. Iſaguiyoqu voconǒ. *Boas obras, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Joogyoo is pure conduct or good works, explained by isagiyoku okonoo. The scan's long-o marks are preserved; the Portuguese qualifies the works further below.
[c1-l022 >] *puras.* Bup.
[c1-l022 note] Completes the definition as pure works. Bup. is the upright Buddhist-register label, including its final punctuation.
[c1-l023] Iǒ-i. *Mandado do* Cubǒ. *¶ Item, O meſ-*
[c1-l023 note] Jooi is an order of the Kuboo. Cubǒ is upright within the Portuguese explanation; meſ- starts the additional sense, the ruler himself.
[c1-l024 >] *mo* Cubǒ. *¶ Item, Mandado dalgum ſe-*
[c1-l024 note] Completes meſmo Cubǒ, the Kuboo himself, then broadens the meaning to an order of another great lord. The second title is also upright.
[c1-l025 >] *nhor grande, ou Tono.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes senhor grande and supplies Tono as another lordly designation. Tono is a Japanese title embedded in the Portuguese explanation, regardless of its printed type.
[c1-l026] Iǒ-i. *Bom, ou excelente fiſico.*
[c1-l026 note] This separate jooi denotes a good or excellent physician. Fiſico has its historical medical sense, not the modern sense of a physicist.
[c1-l027] Iôji, uru, ita. Noru. *Caualgar, ou embar-*
[c1-l027 note] Jooji, joozuru, joojita means mounting or boarding, explicitly noru. The principal-part endings are retained rather than replaced with modernized forms.
[c1-l028 >] *carſe. ¶ Item, Estar enleuado, ou abſor-*
[c1-l028 note] Completes embarcarſe and adds being engrossed or absorbed. The second sense is figurative and continues below.
[c1-l029 >] *to em algũa couſa. Vt,* Quanguen qiôni
[c1-l029 note] Completes abſorto em alguma cousa. Kangen kyoo ni begins an example about absorption in the delight of music.
[c1-l030 >] jôzuru. *Eſtar embebido no gosto da muſica.*
[c1-l030 note] Joozuru completes the music example. The Portuguese says to be engrossed in the enjoyment of music; the circumflex is retained.
[c1-l031] Iôji. *Peßoas que ſeruem na Tera, ou aos Bõ-*
[c1-l031 note] Jooji denotes people serving at a temple or attending the Bonzos. The scan reads ſeruem na Tera, not ſeruemu na Pera. On the second pass, the lowercase letters of Tera clearly match the surrounding italic type; the word is retained in that italic span rather than normalized to roman. The capital T alone is less diagnostic, so its typeface remains a minor caveat for the whole-word toggle.
[c1-l032 >] *zos. ¶ Item, Homem que tem por offi-*
[c1-l032 note] Completes Bõzos and adds a person whose occupation is specified below. The terminal offi- visibly divides officio.
[c1-l033 >] *cio tanger o ſino.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes the preceding definition of Iôji: a person whose occupation is to ring the bell; the initial cio belongs to the divided Portuguese offi-cio.
[c1-l034] Iǒjǒ. Vye vye. *Couſa ſuprema, ou excelẽte.*
[c1-l034 note] Vye vye, 'above above', explains the superlative jōjō; the Portuguese says supreme or excellent. Retain the tilde in excelẽte and distinguish the two caroned vowels in the headword.
[c1-l035] Iǒjo. *Hum dos cinco liuros, que enſinão a go-*
[c1-l035 note] The book title is identified as one of five books teaching government. The scan supports initial I, not long ſ, and a space between a and the divided go-; the final vowel of the headword is unmarked.
[c1-l036 >] *uernar.*
[c1-l036 note] Completes go-uernar, 'to govern', from the preceding physical line; this is not a separate entry.
[c1-l037] Iojǒ. Taſuqe naſu. *Saluar, & liurar de pe-*
[c1-l037 note] Taſuqe naſu supplies the sense of helping or bringing rescue; the Portuguese says save and deliver from dangers. The first o of Iojǒ is unmarked, unlike its final ǒ.
[c1-l038 >] *rigos, ou ſocorrer em neceßidades, &c. Vt,*
[c1-l038 note] Completes pe-rigos, then adds assistance in needs or difficulties. Retain the printed ß in neceßidades and the example introducer Vt.
[c1-l039 >] Gojojǒuo tanomi zonzuru. *Peço que me*
[c1-l039 note] The honorific Japanese request asks for assistance, matching 'I ask that you ...'. A visible word space separates Gojojǒuo from tanomi; zonzuru remains a separate word.
[c1-l040 >] *ajudeis, & façais merce.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes the Portuguese request: help me and do me a kindness. Retain the unaccented final merce rather than modernizing its spelling.
[c1-l041] Iǒju. *Perfeiçoar, & acabar. Vt,* Xoguã
[c1-l041 note] Jōju is explained as perfecting or completing; Xoguã begins the Japanese example about a wish. The entire Japanese word is roman, including its final nasalized ã.
[c1-l042 >] uo jǒju tçucamatçuru. *Comprir, ou ef-*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the Japanese example as bringing the wish to accomplishment. The Portuguese gives fulfilment, with the printed ef- continuing onto the next line.
[c1-l043 >] *feituarſe o deſejo dalguem.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes ef-feituarſe and identifies the thing fulfilled as someone's wish. Preserve the historical spelling and long ſ in deſejo rather than regularizing the divided verb.
[c1-l044] Iǒjun. Cami tôca. *Os primeiros dez dias do*
[c1-l044 note] Cami tôca identifies the first ten days, confirmed by the Portuguese definition. The Japanese is Cami, not the OCR Camri; the mark on tôca is a circumflex.
[c1-l045 >] *mes.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes 'the first ten days of the month'; the single word is the continuation of the Iǒjun entry.
[c1-l046] Iǒmai. *Arroz que ſe mete na fortaleza pera*
[c1-l046 note] The headword is Iǒmai, rice put into a fortress. The scan has initial I and no additional i between ǒ and m; this corrects the misleading OCR form.
[c1-l047 >] *mantimento, & vitualha.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes the definition of fortress rice as provisions and victuals. Retain pera from the preceding line and the historical vitualha here.
