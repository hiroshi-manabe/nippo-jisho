[c1-l001] Miyamagacure. *P. Sombra de mõte de mui-*
[c1-l001 note] Miyamagacure denotes shade or concealment in a thickly wooded mountain, marked poetic by P. The scan has a nasal tilde in mõte, not the OCR's grave accent; mui- continues onto the next row.
[c1-l002 >] *to aruoredo.*
[c1-l002 note] To completes muito, describing abundant woodland. This continuation is visibly indented rather than aligned with the headword as in the provisional markup.
[c1-l003] Miyamajitoto. *Vide,* Miyama.
[c1-l003 note] Miyamajitoto is cross-referred to the bird-name entry Miyama on f0324. Vide is italic editorial Latin, while both Japanese names remain upright.
[c1-l004] Miyari, u, atta. *Lançar os olhos pera al-*
[c1-l004 note] Miyari means casting one's eyes in a direction. The past form atta is actually printed; the Portuguese divides alguma after al-.
[c1-l005 >] *gũa parte.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes the direction of the glance as some place or other. This short phrase belongs to Miyari, not to the following royal title.
[c1-l006] Miyaſudocoro. *Rainha que ja tem filho*
[c1-l006 note] Miyasudokoro is explained as a queen who already has a child. The next physical row specifies the child's princely status.
[c1-l007 >] *principe.*
[c1-l007 note] Principe completes filho principe. The dictionary's status-specific definition is retained without replacing it with a broader modern account of the title.
[c1-l008] Miyazzucai. *Seruiço feito a peſſoa nobre. Vt,*
[c1-l008 note] Miyazukai is service rendered to a noble person. The scan shows two long-s types in peſſoa here, unlike the ligature-like form in nearby occurrences.
[c1-l009 >] Miyazzucaino nhôbǒtachi. *Molheres que*
[c1-l009 note] Miyazukai no nyoobootachi denotes the women engaged in such service. The two long-o marks in nhôbǒtachi are different: circumflex first, caron second.
[c1-l010 >] *ſeruem a peßoa nobre principalmente a Cũgues.*
[c1-l010 note] The women serve noble people, especially court nobles. Cũgues is the pluralized Japanese title embedded in Portuguese; the tilde over u is retained.
[c1-l011] Miyazzucai, ǒ, ǒta. *Seruir a peßoa nobre,*
[c1-l011 note] The verbal Miyazzucai means serving a noble person. The supplied long-o principal parts carry carons and are not converted to modern conjugation forms.
[c1-l012 >] *principalmente a cũgues.*
[c1-l012 note] The service is especially to court nobles, completing the verb's gloss. The lower-case cũgues differs in capitalization from the preceding example and is preserved.
[c1-l013] Miye, uru, eta. *Aparecer, ou parecer.*
[c1-l013 note] Miye covers becoming visible or appearing, as well as seeming. The Portuguese pairs aparecer and parecer rather than restricting the entry to physical sight alone.
[c1-l014] Miyeqitari, u, atta. *Aparecer, ou vir de fo-*
[c1-l014 note] Miyekitari is appearing or arriving from outside. The visible initial f of fo- and the continuation ra establish fora, not the OCR's ſora.
[c1-l015 >] *ra. Vt,* Miyeqitariſoro aida xinjiſoro.
[c1-l015 note] Miyekitari-soro aida xinji-soro means offering something because it has arrived. The scan supports xinjiſoro, the giving/offering expression, not the OCR's malformed ximiſoro.
[c1-l016 >] *Ofereço vos isto por me vir agora de fora.*
[c1-l016 note] The speaker offers the object because it has just come from elsewhere. This confirms the offering verb in the Japanese example rather than treating it as an unrelated action.
[c1-l017] Miyeſome, uru, eta. *Começar a aparecer.*
[c1-l017 note] Miyesome is beginning to appear. The entry is a compound with the beginning-of-action element some, directly matched by começar.
[c1-l018] Miyevatari, u, atta. *Verſe algũa couſa ao lõ-*
[c1-l018 note] Miyewatari is being visible far away or across an entire extent. The final lõ- begins the Portuguese longe on the next row.
[c1-l019 >] *ge, ou toda ao comprido. ¶* Farucani to-
[c1-l019 note] The gloss adds visibility along the whole length, then begins a quotation about a great distance. Farucani to- continues into tooki on the next row.
[c1-l020 >] uoqi ſancaino ſuyeua caſumitaredomo,
[c1-l020 note] The remote limits of mountains and sea are said to be hazy. Kasumitaredomo supplies the concessive sense, although they are obscured by haze.
[c1-l021 >] võmeno mayeni cotogotocu miye vataru
[c1-l021 note] Everything is nevertheless visible before the honored eyes. The scan has a nasal tilde in võmeno, not a long-vowel caron; interpreting it as onme no avoids the stale hint's oome reading.
[c1-l022 >] nari. Chr. N. *Lib. 8. Os termi-*
[c1-l022 note] Nari closes the Japanese statement. Chr. N. is retained as the printed upright citation label without conjectural expansion; Lib. 8 and the translation are italic.
[c1-l023 >] *nos do mar, & dos montes muito distantes que*
[c1-l023 note] The translation names the very distant limits of the sea and mountains. Nos completes terminos from the preceding row.
[c1-l024 >] *eſtauão ẽneuoados, todauia aparecerão todos cla-*
[c1-l024 note] Those distant places were shrouded in mist but nevertheless appeared clearly. The printed ẽneuoados is one word, with an abbreviation tilde, not the OCR's separated ẽ neuoados.
[c1-l025 >] *ramente.*
[c1-l025 note] Ramente completes claramente, closing the translation of the long-distance-visibility quotation. It is a genuine short continuation.
[c1-l026] Miyori. *Parte direita do falcão, que he aparte,*
[c1-l026 note] Miyori names the falcon's right side in the handling position described below. The gloss anchors the terminology in falconry rather than in the ordinary sense of family connections.
[c1-l027 >] *ou aſas que ficão vizinhas ao corpo do homem*
[c1-l027 note] This is the side or wings nearest the handler's body. The relative orientation is completed by the description of the arm carrying the bird.
[c1-l028 >] *que o tem no braço eſquerdo.* Tadaſaqi. *He*
[c1-l028 note] The bird is held on the left arm; Tadaſaqi then introduces the contrasting side. The Japanese label is part of the explanatory comparison, followed by Portuguese He.
[c1-l029 >] *aparte, ou aſas eſquerdas.*
[c1-l029 note] Tadasaki is the left side or left wings in this comparison. The printed plural aſas is retained rather than anatomically regularized.
[c1-l030] Miyuqi. P. i, Fucai yuqi. *Neue amontoa-*
[c1-l030 note] The poetic Miyuki means deep snow, explained by fukai yuki. P. is upright in this occurrence; it is a usage label, not an italic fragment of the headword.
[c1-l031 >] *da, ou alta.*
[c1-l031 note] Da completes amontoada, snow piled up or lying high. This closes the snow entry before the distinct royal-travel entry.
[c1-l032] Miyuqi. *Ida, ou ſaida de Rey.*
[c1-l032 note] A separate Miyuki means the ruler's going out or journey. It begins at the headword margin, not at continuation indentation.
[c1-l033] Mizame. *O ver algũa couſa friamente, ou ſẽ*
[c1-l033 note] Mizame describes regarding something coldly or without the earlier enthusiasm. The visible tilde in ſẽ supplies the negative preposition sem; it must not be dropped as in the OCR.
[c1-l034 >] *aquelle feruor cõ q̃ primeiro a via. ¶* Mizame
[c1-l034 note] The lost enthusiasm is that with which the thing was first regarded. Mizame begins the illustrative Japanese expression at the row end.
[c1-l035 >] gaſuru, l, xita. *Idem. No Ximo ſe diz;*
[c1-l035 note] Mizame ga suru, or xita, describes that cooling of interest. The prose then identifies a regional equivalent used in Ximo; the printed final punctuation is a semicolon.
[c1-l036 >] Miſabita. *Idem.*
[c1-l036 note] Misabita supplies that regional expression for the same cooling or loss of freshness in one's regard. The visible long s is preserved, not changed to f.
[c1-l037] Mizauo. *Bambu, ou vara da embarcação.*
[c1-l037 note] Mizao is the bamboo or pole used with a boat. The short definition identifies an implement, not the channel itself.
[c1-l038] Mizo. *Regato, ou cano que vai pello chão.*
[c1-l038 note] Mizo is a ditch or channel running along the ground. Enlargement resolves the OCR's pll as pello, retaining the printed doubled l.
[c1-l039] Mizogaua. *Regato, ou ribeira pequena.*
[c1-l039 note] Mizogawa is a small stream or rivulet, extending mizo into a compound. The Portuguese smallness qualification is explicit.
[c1-l040] Mizore. *Neue que cae miſturada com chuua.*
[c1-l040 note] Mizore is snow falling mixed with rain. The next paragraph applies this mixed appearance figuratively to a drink.
[c1-l041 >] *¶ Item, per met. Certo vinho branco de Na*
[c1-l041 note] The figurative extension is a particular white wine from Nara. Na continues into ra on the next physical row without an added hyphen.
[c1-l042 >] *ra, que tem dentro miſturada a caſca do arroz.*
[c1-l042 note] Ra completes Nara; the drink has rice husk mixed into it according to the gloss. The historical description is retained rather than replaced with a modern brewing explanation.
[c1-l043] Mizoſanzai. *Hum paſſarinho. Vide,* Mi-
[c1-l043 note] Mizosanzai is a small bird, with a cross-reference to Misosanzai. The voiced spelling of this headword and the unvoiced spelling in the reference are distinct as printed.
[c1-l044 >] ſoſanzai.
[c1-l044 note] Completes Mi-ſoſanzai, the cross-referred bird name. This fragment is not a new headword or an embedded word in Portuguese prose.
[c1-l045] Mizôu. Imada catçute arazu. *Couſa não a-*
[c1-l045 note] Mizou is explained by imada katsute arazu, something that has never occurred before. The circumflex and following u are retained rather than condensed to a modern spelling.
[c1-l046 >] *contecida tè gora.* S.
[c1-l046 note] Completes acontecida: not having happened up to now. The closing S. is upright and marks written usage.
[c1-l047] Mizzu. *Agoa. ¶* Mizzuuo ſumaſu. *A-*
[c1-l047 note] Mizzu is water. Mizzuuo ſumaſu means making water clear, leading into A-clarar at the next column's opening; this restores the badly garbled headword, gloss, object particle, and verb without adding a new physical row.

[c2-l001 >] *clarar a agoa. ¶* Mizzuni tçucaru. *Me-*
[c2-l001 note] Clarar completes A-clarar from the previous column, making the water clear. Mizzuni tçucaru then introduces immersing oneself in water; Me- begins its translation.
[c2-l002 >] *terſe na agoa. ¶* Mizzuuo caquru. *Bo-*
[c2-l002 note] Terſe completes meterſe na agoa. Mizzuuo caquru is pouring or casting water on something, with Bo- belonging wholly to the italic Portuguese verb Botar.
[c2-l003 >] *tar agoa em cima dalgũa couſa. ¶* Mizzu-
[c2-l003 note] Tar completes Botar, specifically putting water on top of something. Mizzu- begins the next example and remains divided at this physical line boundary.
[c2-l004 >] ga maſu. *Leuar o rio grande enchente.*
[c2-l004 note] Mizzu-ga masu means the water rises, glossed as a river carrying a large flood. Enlargement supports the separate ga maſu, not the OCR's gaimaſu.
[c2-l005 >] *¶* Mizzuuo cumu. *Tirar agoa. ¶* Miz-
[c2-l005 note] Mizzuuo cumu means drawing water. A new example, beginning Miz-, follows the paragraph mark and is completed on the next row.
[c2-l006 >] zuuo muſubu. P. *Tirar agoa, ou lauar o*
[c2-l006 note] Mizzuuo musubu is a poetic expression for taking water or washing. P. is visibly upright here and labels the Japanese usage before the italic explanation.
[c2-l007 >] *rosto, ou mãos cõ agoa. ¶* Mizzuuo ſoſoqu.
[c2-l007 note] The washing concerns the face or hands. Mizzuuo sosogu introduces sprinkling water; the long-s forms in the Japanese verb are preserved.
[c2-l008 >] *Borrifar com agoa. ¶* Mizzuuo vtçu. *Botar*
[c2-l008 note] The sprinkling gloss ends, then mizzuuo utsu begins an example of casting water. The Portuguese Botar leads into the house-or-courtyard explanation below.
[c2-l009 >] *agoa como quẽ agoa a caſa, ou pateo.*
[c2-l009 note] The water is spread as when watering a house or courtyard. The tilde in quẽ is retained rather than expanded to quem.
[c2-l010] Mizzubixacu. *Coco de tirar agoa.*
[c2-l010 note] Mizubishaku is a water-drawing dipper, described as a coco. The Portuguese material term is not silently replaced with a modern utensil name.
[c2-l011] Mizzubune. *Pia de agoa, ou lauatorio.*
[c2-l011 note] Mizubune denotes a water basin or washing place. The gloss uses pia and lauatorio as explanatory alternatives.
[c2-l012] Mizzucane. *Azougue.*
[c2-l012 note] Mizukane is mercury, glossed by azougue. The entry is a metal name despite its literal association with water and its position among mizzu forms.
[c2-l013] Mizzucara. *Eu meſmo.* S.
[c2-l013 note] Mizucara is the reflexive or emphatic myself, glossed Eu meſmo, not a water compound. The final upright S. marks written usage.
[c2-l014] Mizzucaſa. *Crecente, ou enchente do rio.*
[c2-l014 note] Mizukasa denotes a river's rising water or flood level. The following example describes an actual increase in that level.
[c2-l015 >] *¶* Mizzucaſaga maita. *Veo grande en-*
[c2-l015 note] Mizukaſaga maita describes the water level having risen, translated as a great flood arriving. The printed maita is retained as the historical form rather than changed to a modern -shita ending.
[c2-l016 >] *chente dagoa.*
[c2-l016 note] Chente completes enchente, a flood of water. This short continuation closes the mizukasa example.
[c2-l017] Mizzucoboxi. *Vaſo em que deitão a agoa cõ*
[c2-l017 note] Mizukoboshi is a vessel into which used water is poured. The following row specifies rinsing the porcelain used for tea.
[c2-l018 >] *que enxaguão aporſolana do Cha.* S.
[c2-l018 note] The vessel receives the water used to rinse tea porcelain. Cha is embedded in the Portuguese gloss; S. is an upright usage label, and the printed aporſolana is not respelled.
[c2-l019] Mizzucoxi. *Coador de coar agoa.*
[c2-l019 note] Mizukoshi is a strainer for filtering water. The repeated Portuguese coador and coar expresses the implement and its function.
[c2-l020] Mizzucumi. *Aguadeiro, ou o que tira agoa.*
[c2-l020 note] Mizukumi denotes a water carrier or someone who draws water. This is the person performing the action, not another container.
[c2-l021] Mizzucuroi. *O concertar, & puxar pelo ves-*
[c2-l021 note] Mizukuroi is arranging one's appearance, specifically straightening and tugging at clothing. This is body-grooming vocabulary, not a compound about repairing water.
[c2-l022 >] *tido pera ſe veſtir bem. ¶* Toriga mizzu-
[c2-l022 note] The clothing is adjusted so that one is properly dressed. Tori ga mizzu- begins an analogous example concerning a bird's grooming.
[c2-l023 >] curoiuo ſuru. *Meliùs,* fazzucuroiuo ſu-
[c2-l023 note] The bird performs mizukuroi; fazzukuroi is supplied as the preferable expression. Meliùs is one italic editorial word, not a mixture of Japanese and Portuguese runs.
[c2-l024 >] ru. *O paßaro eſtaſe eſpinicando.*
[c2-l024 note] The Portuguese explains that the bird is preening itself. Ru completes the Japanese suru of the preferred wording from the preceding line.
[c2-l025] Mizzudaru. *Barrijs de pao, ou tinas peque-*
[c2-l025 note] Mizudaru first denotes wooden barrels or small water tubs. The list of vessel-related senses continues below.
[c2-l026 >] *nas de agoa. ¶ Item, Tanque dagoa nas*
[c2-l026 note] The first sense ends with water tubs; the next is a water tank in a vessel. Nas leads into embarcaçoens on the next row.
[c2-l027 >] *embarcaçoens. ¶ Item, Barça de vinho*
[c2-l027 note] The second sense concerns boats, while a third is a container of wine. The following row qualifies that wine as unaccompanied by food.
[c2-l028 >] *ſem Sacana. Vt,* Mizzudaru fitotçuno
[c2-l028 note] Sem Sacana means without food accompanying the drink. The Japanese example begins by referring to even a single mizudaru.
[c2-l029 >] goinxinnimo azzucaranu. *Nem hũa bar-*
[c2-l029 note] The speaker says he has not received even that small courtesy or gift. The Portuguese begins the corresponding negative statement, Nem hũa bar-.
[c2-l030 >] *ça de vinho ſò ſem Sacana medeu.*
[c2-l030 note] Completes barça and explains that not even wine alone, without sakana, was given. Sacana is embedded in Portuguese, while the joined medeu preserves the printed spacing.
[c2-l031] Mizzufajiqi. *Eſguicho dagoa como ſiringa.*
[c2-l031 note] Mizuhajiki is a water-squirting device compared with a syringe. The alternative Japanese term is supplied on the following short row.
[c2-l032 >] l, Mizzutçuqi.
[c2-l032 note] The upright Latin l introduces Mizzutçuqi as the alternative name. Neither the marker nor the Japanese word belongs to an italic Portuguese run.
[c2-l033] Mizzugare. *Certo genero de peixe como lin-*
[c2-l033 note] Mizugare names a fish compared with a sole or flatfish. The comparison is explicit, but the brief gloss does not establish a precise modern species.
[c2-l034 >] *guado.*
[c2-l034 note] Guado completes linguado, the Portuguese comparator. The physical line break is preserved even though it divides a single word.
[c2-l035] Mizzugoromo. *Veſtido de ſeda muito delgada*
[c2-l035 note] Mizugoromo is a garment of very thin silk. The following row specifies its use as an outer layer in theatrical performance.
[c2-l036 >] *que ſe veſte por cima nos autos, &c.*
[c2-l036 note] The thin garment is worn over other clothing in plays or performances. Autos is theatrical here, not a modern sense involving vehicles.
[c2-l037] Mizzuguchi. *Boca por onde ſae agoa.*
[c2-l037 note] Mizuguchi denotes an opening from which water flows. The Portuguese boca is used for the outlet or mouth of the flow.
[c2-l038] Mizzuguqi. *Pena de Iapão.*
[c2-l038 note] Mizuguki is glossed as a Japanese writing pen or brush. Pena is an explanatory instrument term here, not a statement that the object is necessarily a bird's feather.
[c2-l039] Mizzuguruma. *Roda como nora de tirar*
[c2-l039 note] Mizuguruma is a water-lifting wheel compared with a nora. The source from which it lifts water follows on the next row.
[c2-l040 >] *agoa de rios, ou ribeiras.*
[c2-l040 note] The wheel raises water from rivers or streams. This completes the mechanism's function rather than describing a water-driven mill.
[c2-l041] Mizzujeme. *Tormento de agoa. ¶* Mizz u-
[c2-l041 note] Mizuzeme is torture by water. The example starts with the visibly spaced Mizz u-, retained from the print rather than silently closing the gap.
[c2-l042 >] jemeuo ſuru. *Dartormento de agoa.*
[c2-l042 note] Jemeuo suru completes the Japanese example, applying water torture. The Portuguese gives the same action, and its joined Dartormento is retained.
[c2-l043] Mizzuire. *Hũa vaſilha pequenina com que*
[c2-l043 note] Mizzuire is a very small vessel used to pour water. The destination, an ink container, is specified on the next row.
[c2-l044 >] *deitão agoa no tinteiro.*
[c2-l044 note] The water is poured into the inkwell or ink receptacle. This identifies the vessel's writing-related use without replacing the printed terminology.
[c2-l045] Mizzuiro. *Certa cor de azulclaro.*
[c2-l045 note] Mizuiro denotes a light blue color. The Portuguese prints azulclaro together; that spelling is retained.
[c2-l046] Mizzumaſagumo. *Hũas nuuezinhas peque-*
[c2-l046 note] Mizumasagumo names small clouds; the shape and density are described on the next row. The entry starts at the headword margin, so the provisional indentation is removed.
[c2-l047 >] *nas, & ralas, que aparecem como ondas.*
[c2-l047 note] The clouds are thin and look like waves, completing the descriptive gloss. The signature and the catchword below are separate page furniture.
