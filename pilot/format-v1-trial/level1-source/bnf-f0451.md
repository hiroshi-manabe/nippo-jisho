[c1-l001 >] *ver nem falar, ou não correr com amizade.*

[c1-l003 >] *com amizade.*
[c1-l003 note] Com amizade completes the explanation of maintaining friendly relations, here negated by the preceding sentence.
[c1-l004] Soyenoqe. *O acrecẽtar, & tirar. Vt,* Co-
[c1-l004 note] Soyenoqe means adding and taking away. Co- begins cotobano in the illustrative expression about modifying speech.
[c1-l005 >] tobano ſoyenoqeuo ſuru. *O acrecentar,*
[c1-l005 note] Cotobano ſoyenoqeuo ſuru concerns additions and omissions in speaking. The Portuguese infinitive is acrecentar, not OCR acrecentat.
[c1-l006 >] *ou diminuir no falar.*
[c1-l006 note] The continuation adds diminishing what is said. It completes the two-sided definition of adding and subtracting.
[c1-l007] Soyevoqi, u, oita. *Acrecentando, ou ajun-*
[c1-l007 note] Soyevoqi describes placing something as an addition or attachment. The printed v in the headword and the abbreviated principal forms are retained.
[c1-l008 >] *tando por.*
[c1-l008 note] Tando completes ajuntando, followed by por, to put. Together the lines mean putting something down while adding or joining it.
[c1-l009] Sôyǒ. *Moſcas verdes grandes.*
[c1-l009 note] This Sôyǒ denotes large green flies. Both vowel marks are retained independently of the identically spelled entry that follows.
[c1-l010] Sôyǒ. Sônami. *Em comũ, ou como os outros.*
[c1-l010 note] The second Sôyǒ means commonly or like everyone else, with Sônami as its Japanese equivalent. It is separate from the insect name.
[c1-l011] Sǒyô. Cuſano fa. *Folhas de erua.* S.
[c1-l011 note] Sǒyô denotes leaves of grass or herbs, explained by Cuſano fa. The first caron is faint; the surviving mark, botanical gloss and supplied NINJAL entry support the reading.
[c1-l012] Sôyô. Mado. *Ianelas.* S.
[c1-l012 note] Sôyô is explained by Mado and Portuguese Ianelas, windows. The terminal S. is an upright written-register label.
[c1-l013] Sǒyô. Ximo, fa. *Folhas cõ geada em cima.*
[c1-l013 note] Sǒyô here denotes leaves with frost on them. Ximo and fa supply the components, and the accent pattern is not assimilated to the window entry.
[c1-l014] Sǒyocu. Riǒno tçubaſa. *Ambas as aſas.*
[c1-l014 note] Sǒyocu means both wings. Riǒno tçubaſa confirms the paired or double sense.
[c1-l015] Soyogui, gu, yoida. *Bulirem as folhas das*
[c1-l015 note] Soyogui describes leaves moving or rustling. The principal forms gu and yoida are kept as printed, not expanded to a modern conjugation.
[c1-l016 >] *aruores, ou eruas com o vento.*
[c1-l016 note] The movement is caused by wind through trees or herbs. This completes the preceding verb’s explanation.
[c1-l017] Sôza. Tçucaſadoru yaxiro. *Ermida princi-*
[c1-l017 note] Sôza is a principal shrine, paraphrased by Tçucaſadoru yaxiro. The Portuguese adjective is divided after princi-.
[c1-l018 >] *pal dos Camis que he como cabeça das outras.*
[c1-l018 note] The shrine is described as the head of other kami shrines. Camis occurs inside the Portuguese explanation and is recorded for whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l019] Sǒza. Cuſano za. *Aßento de eruas.*
[c1-l019 note] Sǒza is a seat made of grass, explicitly explained by Cuſano za. It is distinct from the preceding principal-shrine term.
[c1-l020] Sôzan. *Todas as teras, ou ermidas dos mon-*
[c1-l020 note] Sôzan collectively denotes temples or religious houses. Teras is the Japanese-derived term embedded in the Portuguese description; mon- continues montes below.
[c1-l021 >] *tes, ou do campo.*
[c1-l021 note] Tes completes montes, followed by the countryside. The collective temple term covers religious houses in both settings.
[c1-l022] Sôzanyô. *Soma, ou cõta em gèral de tudo.*
[c1-l022 note] Sôzanyô means a total or general reckoning of everything. The printed grave accent in gèral and both circumflexes in the headword are retained.
[c1-l023] Sôzô. *Gèralmente, ou por todos. Vt,* Sô-
[c1-l023 note] Sôzô means collectively or in all. The repeated headword begins a question about the total number of people.
[c1-l024 >] zô icutari fodozo. *Por todos quantos ſão?*
[c1-l024 note] Sôzô icutari fodozo asks how many people there are altogether. The Portuguese ends ſão? with a question mark, replacing the garbled OCR ending.
[c1-l025] Sǒzocu. Aitçuzzuqu. *Tradição, ou entrega*
[c1-l025 note] Sǒzocu is transmission or succession, explained by Aitçuzzuqu. The Portuguese definition emphasizes handing something on over time.
[c1-l026 >] *dalgũa couſa que vem continuada por tempos,*
[c1-l026 note] What is handed on remains continuous through time. This relative clause expands the preceding transmission or inheritance sense.
[c1-l027 >] *ou por ſucceſores. ¶* Iyeuo sǒzocu ſu-
[c1-l027 note] The succession can occur through successive holders. Iyeuo sǒzocu ſuru begins the example about the house; the initial s in sǒzocu replaces OCR e.
[c1-l028 >] ru. *Deixar a caſa por herança. ¶* Vo-
[c1-l028 note] The Portuguese renders the house example as leaving a house by inheritance. Vo- begins the second example concerning teaching.
[c1-l029 >] xiyeuo sǒzocu ſuru. *Deixar por tra-*
[c1-l029 note] Voxiyeuo sǒzocu ſuru concerns handing on a teaching. The Portuguese infinitive is Deixar, not OCR Deixir; tra- begins tradição.
[c1-l030 >] *dição algũa doutrina pera que ſe và continu-*
[c1-l030 note] The doctrine is passed down so that it will continue. Both nasal abbreviations and the physical division continu- are retained.
[c1-l031 >] *ando.*
[c1-l031 note] Ando completes continuando, maintaining the transmission of the teaching. The short row is genuine body text.
[c1-l032] Sôzocu. Xucqe, zocu. *Religioſo, &*
[c1-l032 note] Sôzocu combines the religious and secular, explicitly paired as Xucqe and zocu. This is distinct from the preceding succession headword.
[c1-l033 >] *ſecular.*
[c1-l033 note] Secular completes the paired Portuguese description of religious and lay status. It does not begin a new entry.
[c1-l034] Sôzôna. l, *Potiùs,* Sôzôxij. i, Caximaxij.
[c1-l034 note] Sôzôna is qualified by Potiùs, preferring Sôzôxij, with Caximaxij as a Japanese synonym. The Latin editorial word belongs to the italic apparatus.
[c1-l035 >] *Couſa de traquinada, & perturbação.*
[c1-l035 note] The adjective describes noise, commotion or disturbance. The gloss explains the shared sense of the preceding Japanese alternatives.
[c1-l036] Sozoro. *Couſa feita ſem conſideração, & ſem*
[c1-l036 note] Sozoro describes something done without consideration. The Portuguese adds lack of a definite purpose on the following row.
[c1-l037 >] *algum propoſito, ou leuemente. ¶* Sozo-
[c1-l037 note] The action is without purpose or done lightly. Sozo- begins the example sozoro vta rather than another headword.
[c1-l038 >] ro vta. *Cantiga que ſe canta aſi ſem propo-*
[c1-l038 note] Sozoro vta is a song sung casually without a deliberate purpose. The single-s spelling aſi is retained as printed.
[c1-l039 >] *ſito, & aduertencia como os mininos, ou traba-*
[c1-l039 note] The casual singing is compared with that of children or workers. Traba- continues trabalhadores on the next line.
[c1-l040 >] *lhadores fazem.*
[c1-l040 note] Lhadores completes trabalhadores, and fazem closes the comparison with what workers do. This row completes the song example.
[c1-l041] Sozorobiqi, qu, ijta. *Recolherſe oexercito,*
[c1-l041 note] Sozorobiqi describes withdrawing an army. The printed principal forms and the closely joined Portuguese oexercito are retained.
[c1-l042 >] *ou tornar algum tanto pera tras fingidamente.*
[c1-l042 note] The withdrawal may be a feigned movement somewhat backwards. The scan reads pera, not the OCR paera.
[c1-l043 >] *¶* Yumiuo ſozorobiqu. *Puxar hum pouco*
[c1-l043 note] Yumiuo ſozorobiqu means drawing a bow a little. The scan has no extra n after Yumi and no word boundary inside ſozorobiqu.
[c1-l044 >] *pola corda do arco com a frecha poſta como*
[c1-l044 note] The bowstring is pulled with the arrow already set in place, as though preparing to shoot. The scan has long ſ in poſta; pola and the historical wording remain unmodernized.
[c1-l045 >] *quem quer tirar.*
[c1-l045 note] The person acts as though about to shoot. This completes the bow example rather than the earlier military withdrawal.
[c1-l046] Sozorodachi. *Eſtar nalgum lugar como eſpe-*
[c1-l046 note] Sozorodachi means standing somewhere idly, as though waiting. The word eſpe- is physically divided before the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *rando a alguem ſem fazer nada como ſuſpenſo.*
[c1-l047 note] The person waits without doing anything, as if suspended or undecided. The abraded word is ſuſpenſo, not two words ſu penſo; the entry continues in column two.

[c2-l016] Su. *Coroa de area, reſtinga, ou baixo darea fo-*

[c2-l022] Su. *Eſteira rala de caninhas, ou outra couſa,*

[c2-l025 >] *eſtas eſteiras.*

[c2-l026] Suama. *Hũa figura, ou pintura dos veſtidos, à*
[c2-l026 note] Suama denotes a textile design or painted garment pattern. The shape of the pattern is compared with waterways below.
[c2-l027 >] *maneiradas voltas, & entradas q̃ tẽ os rios, ou*
[c2-l027 note] The pattern resembles bends and inlets in rivers or shorelines. The close printed maneiradas is retained; the abbreviated words q̃ tẽ are separated as visible.

[c2-l029] Suaxi. i, Fadaxi. *Descalço. ¶* Suaxi-

[c2-l030 >] de ayumu. *Ir, ou andar descalço.*

[c2-l031] Subacu. *Meliùs,* Sunbacu. *Doença, ou dor*
[c2-l031 note] Subacu is followed by Meliùs, preferring Sunbacu. The historical definition describes an abdominal illness or pain; it is transcribed, not adopted as a modern diagnosis.
[c2-l032 >] *de barriga cauſada de hum bicho comprido,*
[c2-l032 note] The historical explanation attributes the abdominal pain to a long worm. The description continues with its width and supposed movement.
[c2-l033 >] *& largo, o qual pretende ir ter à bolſa dos*

[c2-l034 >] *teſticulos, ou às pernas.*
[c2-l034 note] The destination is specified as the scrotum or the legs. The line completes the historical illness account without replacing it with modern medical terminology.
