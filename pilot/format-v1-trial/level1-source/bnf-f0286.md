[c1-l001 >] Fito mina jibocuuo vodorocaſu. *Todos*
[c1-l001 note] Continues Iibocu on f285: everyone's ears and eyes are startled, idiomatically everyone is astonished. The initial Fito is the word anticipated by f285's catchword, not a repeated catchword on this page.
[c1-l002 >] *paſmão, ou paſmarão.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes Todos with present or past astonishment. The printed alternatives and their tildes are retained rather than forcing one tense.
[c1-l003] Iibun. *Tempo. ¶* Iibun tǒrai ſuru. *Vir*
[c1-l003 note] Jibun here means time or occasion; jibun toorai suru is the arrival of the appropriate time. This is a new entry aligned at the column margin, not an indented continuation.
[c1-l004 >] *o tempo, ou hora de algũa couſa. ¶* Iibũ
[c1-l004 note] Explains the coming of the time or hour for something. Iibũ begins another Japanese example; its abbreviated nasal and tilde belong to the roman word, not an isolated italic vowel.
[c1-l005 >] uo vcagǒ. *Buſcar tempo, ou conjunção.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes jibun wo ukagoo, seeking a suitable time or opportunity. The caroned contracted verb is preserved rather than modernized to ukagau.
[c1-l006 >] Iibunuo motte mairǒzu. *Eu irei a ſeu tẽ-*
[c1-l006 note] Jibun wo motte mairoozu says that the speaker will come at the proper time. The initial is I rather than OCR ſ; tẽ- begins the Portuguese time-word across the line break.
[c1-l007 >] *po. ¶* Sǒtǒno jibun. *Tempo conueni-*
[c1-l007 note] Completes tẽ-po and introduces sootoo no jibun, a suitable occasion. Tempo is wholly in the italic Portuguese apparatus; the OCR's isolated roman T was spurious.
[c1-l008 >] *ente pera fazer algũa couſa. ¶* Iibun faz-
[c1-l008 note] Completes conueni-ente as a suitable time for doing something. The next example begins jibun haz-, whose continuation below describes acting out of season or at the wrong time.
[c1-l009 >] zureni monouo ſuru. *Fazer algũa couſa*
[c1-l009 note] Completes faz-zureni mono wo suru, doing something at an unsuitable time. The middle of algũa is abraded; the surviving letters and repeated local form support this reading rather than OCR algaa.
[c1-l010 >] *fora de tẽpo, ou de horas determinadas, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] The Portuguese specifies doing it out of time or outside the appointed hours. Tẽpo retains the printed abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l011] Iibun. *Eu meſmo. Vt,* Iibunno xinjǒde
[c1-l011 note] This jibun means oneself, not time. The example begins a declaration that an offering is the speaker's own gift.
[c1-l012 >] gozaru. *Eu offereço isto da minha parte.*
[c1-l012 note] Gozaru closes the Japanese statement; the translation says the speaker offers this on his own behalf. The existing short-s st in isto is retained under the project's ligature policy.
[c1-l013 >] *¶ Item, Propria poßibilidade, ou faculdade.*
[c1-l013 note] Adds one's own capacity or ability as another sense of jibun. The paragraph mark introduces a semantic extension of the same entry.
[c1-l014 >] *Vt,* Iibunni canauanu. *Não poſſo por mi*
[c1-l014 note] Jibun ni kanawanu means being unable to accomplish something through one's own means. The Portuguese mi is physically divided from nhas without a visible hyphen.
[c1-l015 >] *nhas forças, ou faculdade.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes mi-nhas and explains inability by one's own strength or capacity. No hyphen is inserted into the canonical preceding row.
[c1-l016] Iibungara. *Tempo, ou conjunção em que ha*
[c1-l016 note] Jibungara is a time characterized by particular circumstances. The Portuguese begins a definition of an occasion on which something notable occurs.
[c1-l017 >] *algũa couſa notauel como tempo de peſte, ou de*
[c1-l017 note] Specifies a notable circumstance, such as a period of plague. The second example of a troubled period continues below.
[c1-l018 >] *perſeguição, &c.*
[c1-l018 note] Persecution completes the list of circumstances illustrating jibungara. This short line closes the entry before the next headword.
[c1-l019] Iicai. Mizzucara imaxime. *Ley, ou prohi-*
[c1-l019 note] Jikai concerns an admonition or rule one observes oneself, as mizukara imashime indicates. Prohi- begins the Portuguese alternative prohibition.
[c1-l020 >] *bição que hum guarda.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes prohi-bição and specifies a prohibition that someone keeps or observes, not merely an order imposed without observance.
[c1-l021] Iiccai. Touono imaxime. *Dez leis, ou mã-*
[c1-l021 note] Jikkai means ten precepts, directly matched by too no imashime and ten laws. The nasal abbreviation in mã- belongs to the divided Portuguese word below.
[c1-l022 >] *damentos.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes mã-damentos, commandments. This does not identify a particular religious list beyond what the entry itself supplies.
[c1-l023] Iichi. Mizzucara xiru. *Proprio ſaber. Vt,*
[c1-l023 note] Jichi is knowing for oneself, explicitly mizukara shiru. The following water example distinguishes firsthand experience from report.
[c1-l024 >] Mizzuuo nôde reidan jichiſu. *Somente*
[c1-l024 note] The example says that on drinking water one knows its coldness or warmth oneself. The circumflex in printed nôde is retained rather than replacing it with an expected nasal spelling.
[c1-l025 >] *o que bebe a agoa diſcerne ſe he fria, ou quẽte.*
[c1-l025 note] Only the person drinking the water can tell whether it is cold or hot. Quẽte preserves the abbreviated nasal, and agoa retains the historical Portuguese spelling.
[c1-l026 >] *i, o que experimenta, eße ſabè das couſas mais*
[c1-l026 note] The explanatory i says that the person with experience knows more about things. The short curved mark in ſabè is retained as a grave from the aligned reading, although a damaged tilde is also possible; it is not normalized away on grammatical grounds.
[c1-l027 >] *que com ouuir.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes the comparison: firsthand experience teaches more than hearing about something. The historical ouuir spelling remains unchanged.
[c1-l028] Iichi iqi. i, *Iapaõ.*
[c1-l028 note] Iichi iqi is given as a name for Japan. The first character is I, not long s, and the gloss's tilde is over final o in Iapaõ.
[c1-l029] Iichimena. l, Iichimena fito. *Peßoa graue,*
[c1-l029 note] Jichimena, also illustrated with hito, describes a serious and trustworthy person. Latin l introduces the alternative expression rather than a Japanese syllable.
[c1-l030 >] *recta, & de verdade.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the characterization as upright and truthful. These qualities explain graue as seriousness or dignity, not physical weight.
[c1-l031] Iichina mono. l, Iichina fito. *Homem graue,*
[c1-l031 note] Jichina mono and jichina hito similarly describe a serious person. Both Japanese alternatives are printed on this physical row.
[c1-l032 >] *ſimples, & de verdade.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes the gloss as straightforward and truthful. In this context ſimples is lack of duplicity, not a diagnosis or a claim about intelligence.
[c1-l033] Iichinimo nai mono. i, Iichino nai. *O cõ-*
[c1-l033 note] The phrases describe someone without sincerity or seriousness, the opposite of Iichina. The tightly inked i+n in Iichino can resemble m or transposed ni; enlargement shows the dot over the first stem, favoring Iichino. The print remains locally difficult.
[c1-l034 >] *trario de* Iichina.
[c1-l034 note] Completes cõ-trario de, opposite of Iichina. Iichina is an upright Japanese word embedded in the Portuguese explanation and is therefore a typeface-review term.
[c1-l035] Iichiraxij. l, Iichina mono. vt Mono mǒſu
[c1-l035 note] Jichirashii describes the appearance of sincerity or seriousness, compared with jichina mono. The locally upright lowercase vt is preserved; the example begins with one's way of speaking.
[c1-l036 >] yǒdaimo jichiraxij. *No modo de falar pa*
[c1-l036 note] Completes the example: even the manner of speaking seems sincere or dignified. Portuguese pa continues as rece without a visible line-end hyphen.
[c1-l037 >] *rece que tem grauidade, & verdade.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes pa-rece, saying that the speaker appears to possess dignity and truthfulness. This explains the appearance nuance of the Japanese adjective.
[c1-l038] Iichǒ. i, Bumaru. *Mariola, ou homem que*
[c1-l038 note] Jichoo is equated with bumaru, a porter or carrier. The Portuguese mariola is clarified by the description of carrying goods below; it is not assigned a modern pejorative sense here.
[c1-l039 >] *carreta fato.*
[c1-l039 note] Describes someone who carries goods or baggage. Fato is material belongings in this gloss, and the short line completes the porter definition.
[c1-l040] Iichǔ. Terano vchi. *Dentro da varela, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Jichuu means within a temple, directly explained as tera no uchi. The Portuguese supplies temple and monastery as alternatives.
[c1-l041 >] *moeſteiro.*
[c1-l041 note] Completes the monastery alternative. The printed moeſteiro vowel order is retained rather than modernized to mosteiro.
[c1-l042] Iichǔ. Iiuo xiruſu. *Declaração, & comento*
[c1-l042 note] This jichuu is an explanatory annotation on a character, not a temple interior. The small mark over u appears caroned on reinspection; the Japanese phrase ji wo shirusu clarifies the gloss.
[c1-l043 >] *ſobre algũa letra.*
[c1-l043 note] Specifies the object of the explanation or commentary: a written character. The tilde remains over u in algũa.
[c1-l044] Iico. Mizzucara vonore. *Eu meſmo.*
[c1-l044 note] Jiko means oneself, given with mizukara and onore and translated as I myself. Initial v in vonore is retained as a historical positional spelling.
[c1-l045] Iicocu. Toqi, qizami. *Hora. ¶* Iico-
[c1-l045 note] Jikoku is a time or hour, explained through toki and kizami. Iico- begins the first Japanese example on the next physical row.
[c1-l046 >] cuga vtçuru. *Paßarſe a hora, ou tempo.*
[c1-l046 note] Completes jikoku ga utsuru, the passing of the hour or time. The printed Paßarſe form is retained without normalizing its double-s representation.
[c1-l047 >] *¶* Iicocu tǒrai ſuru. *Chegarſe a hora.*
[c1-l047 note] A second example, jikoku toorai suru, describes the arrival of the hour. This final row is body text, not a catchword or signature.

[c2-l001] Iicocu. Mizzucarano cuni. *Proprio reino,*
[c2-l001 note] This jikoku is one's own country, explicitly mizukara no kuni, distinct from time in the preceding column. Its Portuguese gloss continues with the country where one is situated.
[c2-l002 >] *ou reino onde hum eſtà. Vt,* Iicocu, taco-
[c2-l002 note] Adds the country where one is currently living. The example contrasts jikoku with takoku, other countries; the visible t in taco- corrects the OCR's caco-.
[c2-l003 >] cu. *Eſte, & outros reinos.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes taco-cu and translates the pair as this and other kingdoms. The row is an indented continuation, not a new unindented entry.
[c2-l004] Iicqen. *Ver o ſenhor, ou capitão na guerra as*
[c2-l004 note] Jikken here is a lord's or commander's inspection of enemy heads in war. This is a historical definition, not the modern experiment sense of the same pronunciation.
[c2-l005 >] *cabeças dos inimigos q̃ os ſeus cortarão. Vt,*
[c2-l005 note] Specifies the heads taken by the commander's own men and introduces an example. Q̃ remains unexpanded; cortarão is retained as the printed verbal form.
[c2-l006 >] Cubiuo jicqen ſaxeraruru.
[c2-l006 note] Kubi wo jikken saseraruru illustrates the respectful construction for inspecting the heads. The Japanese example has no additional Portuguese translation on this row.
[c2-l007] Iicqeô. Macotono voxiye. *Ley, ou doctri-*
[c2-l007 note] Jikkyoo is true teaching, explicitly makoto no oshie. The Portuguese has doctri-, not OCR dostri-, beginning doctrine across the line boundary.
[c2-l008 >] *na verdadeira.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes doctri-na verdadeira, true doctrine. The adjective applies to the teaching just defined.
[c2-l009] Iicqin. Mutçumaxij chicaxi. *Familiar, ou*
[c2-l009 note] Jikkin describes closeness or intimacy, matched by mutsumashii and chikashi. Familiar begins the Portuguese pair of equivalents.
[c2-l010 >] *priuado.*
[c2-l010 note] Priuado completes the sense as an intimate or close associate. It should not automatically be given the modern privacy-related sense.
[c2-l011] Iicun. Futarino qimi. *Dous ſenhores. Vt,*
[c2-l011 note] Jikun means two lords, explicitly futari no kimi. The following maxim concerns serving two masters.
[c2-l012 >] Qenxin jicunni tçucayezu. *Homem in-*
[c2-l012 note] The maxim says that an exemplary retainer does not serve two lords. In- begins the Portuguese characterization of that person as distinguished.
[c2-l013 >] *ſigne, & aprimorado não ſerue a dous ſe-*
[c2-l013 note] Completes in-ſigne and describes an accomplished man who does not serve two lords. Se- begins ſenhores on the next row.
[c2-l014 >] *nhores.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes ſe-nhores, the two masters in the translated maxim. This is a short body continuation.
[c2-l015] Iidai. Toqi, yo. *Idade.*
[c2-l015 note] Jidai is an age or period, matched by toki and yo and Portuguese idade. The definition concerns time, not a person's numerical age specifically.
[c2-l016] Iidanda. *Modo de piſar cõ os pees. Vt,* Ii-
[c2-l016 note] Jidanda is a manner of stamping or treading with the feet. The scan prints pees with two e letters; Ii- starts jidanda in the example below.
[c2-l017 >] dandauo fumu. *Vide,* Fumi, u.
[c2-l017 note] Completes jidanda wo fumu, stamping one's feet. Vide introduces the reference Fumi, u.; this is an upright Japanese cross-reference within Portuguese apparatus, not another independent headword.
[c2-l018] Iidaracu, l, jidaracuna. *Deſmazelado, ou*
[c2-l018 note] Jidaraku and its adjectival form describe someone careless or disorderly. The Portuguese begins with desmazelado; the printed comma after the headword is retained.
[c2-l019 >] *deſconcertado. Vt,* Iidaracuna mono. *Ho*
[c2-l019 note] Adds disorderliness, then exemplifies a careless person. Ho begins homem on the next physical row; no terminal hyphen is visible, and both letters are italic.
[c2-l020 >] *mem deſcuidado, & deſmazelado em concer-*
[c2-l020 note] Completes Ho-mem and describes a person careless about arranging things. The visible hyphen after concer- belongs to the division of concertar.
[c2-l021 >] *tar, & por por ordem as couſas, &c.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes concertar and describes putting things in order. The two occurrences of por are retained: the first is the verb meaning put, the second the preposition in por ordem.
[c2-l022] Iidô. Chigo, varanbe. *Minino.*
[c2-l022 note] Jidō is glossed with chigo and historical varanbe, a child. Minino is the printed Portuguese spelling and is not modernized to menino.
[c2-l023] Iidocu. Mizzucara yomu. *Ler eu. Vt,*
[c2-l023 note] Jidoku means reading for oneself, explicitly mizukara yomu. The example below concerns learning a book without a teacher.
[c2-l024 >] Cono qiǒ xixǒ nǒte tada jidocu, itaita.
[c2-l024 note] The speaker says that he read or learned this book independently, without a master. The carons and the small comma after jidocu are retained as printed, rather than normalizing the Japanese sentence.
[c2-l025 >] *Eſte liuro ſem meſtre ſo por mi o aprẽdi, ou li.*
[c2-l025 note] The Portuguese translates the example as learning or reading this book by oneself without a master. The abbreviation in aprẽdi is retained; the tiny marks near so and final li are not expanded into conjectural accents.
[c2-l026] Iifi. *Miſericordia, ou eſmola. ¶* Iifiuo
[c2-l026 note] Jihi denotes compassion, mercy, or almsgiving. The scan supports Miſericordia, not the OCR's Muſericordia; Iifiuo begins the Japanese example.
[c2-l027 >] mopparato ſuru. *Ter por couſa muito neceßa*
[c2-l027 note] Completes jihi wo moppara to suru, making charitable giving a principal concern. Neceßa continues into ria without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c2-l028 >] *ria, & principal fazer eſmolas. ¶* Iifiuo
[c2-l028 note] Completes neceßaria and identifies giving alms as a necessary, principal undertaking. Principal is one word in the scan; the next example begins after the pilcrow.
[c2-l029 >] taruru, l, ſuru. *Fazer eſmolas. ¶* Iifi
[c2-l029 note] The alternatives jihi wo taruru and jihi wo suru are glossed as giving alms. The final Iifi starts the contrasting example about receiving charity.
[c2-l030 >] uo vquru. *Receber eſmolas.*
[c2-l030 note] Completes jihi wo ukuru, receiving alms. This is the continuation of the previous example, not a new entry.
[c2-l031] Iifijin. *Eſmoler, ou amigo de fazer eſmolas.*
[c2-l031 note] Jihijin is a charitable person, explained as an almsgiver or someone fond of giving alms. The f-like letter in the historical romanization is retained consistently with Iifi.
[c2-l032] Iifina. *Couſa de eſmola, ou peßoa eſmoler.*
[c2-l032 note] Jihi na describes something charitable or a person who gives alms. The Portuguese expressly allows both a thing and a person as referent.
[c2-l033] Iifit. *Letra da propria mão, ou couſa eſcrita*
[c2-l033 note] Jihitsu means one's own handwriting or something written by one's own hand. The printed headword Iifit is kept rather than completing it to a modern romanization.
[c2-l034 >] *de ſua mão.*
[c2-l034 note] Completes the explanation of autograph writing as something written by the person's own hand. This belongs to Iifit, not to the charitable-giving entries around it.
[c2-l035] Iifixa. *Homem eſmoler, ou amigo de fazer*
[c2-l035 note] Jihisha is a charitable person, here an almsgiver or someone who likes giving alms. The Portuguese definition continues on the next row.
[c2-l036 >] *eſmolas.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes fazer eſmolas, giving alms. The isolated word is a body continuation rather than a catchword.
[c2-l037] Iifixin. Iifino cocoro. *Coração piadoſo.*
[c2-l037 note] Jihishin is a compassionate heart, explicitly jihi no kokoro. The Portuguese piadoſo has the sense of merciful or compassionate in this context.
[c2-l038 >] *¶* Iifixinuo vocoſu. *Mouerſe a piedade,*
[c2-l038 note] Jihishin wo okosu means arousing compassion. The gloss describes being moved to pity; the initial pilcrow introduces the example.
[c2-l039 >] *ou miſericordia.*
[c2-l039 note] Adds mercy as an equivalent to pity in the preceding example. The spelling miſericordia is retained without modern accentuation.
[c2-l040] Iifixinna. *Homem de bem fazer, ou eſmoler.*
[c2-l040 note] Jihishin na describes a benevolent or charitable person. Bem fazer is printed as two words and is not joined into a modern lexical form.
[c2-l041] Iifiya. *Caſa de Miſericordia, ou hoſpital.*
[c2-l041 note] Jihiya denotes a charitable house or hospital. The apparent b in the OCR is the italic h of hoſpital; the institutional Portuguese expression is retained as printed.
[c2-l042] Iifǒ. Terano fatto. *Regras do moesteiro.*
[c2-l042 note] Jihō means the rules of a temple or monastery, explicitly tera no hatto. The printed Portuguese vowel order moesteiro is retained; it is not modernized to mosteiro.
[c2-l043 >] *¶* Iifǒuo tamotçu. *Guardar as regras do*
[c2-l043 note] Jihō wo tamotsu means observing the monastery's rules. The Portuguese translation continues with the institution on the next row.
[c2-l044 >] *conuento.*
[c2-l044 note] Completes the phrase about keeping the convent's rules. Conuento is the historical spelling of convento and is not a separate headword.
[c2-l045] Iifu. Itçucuximu chichi. *Pay piadoſo.*
[c2-l045 note] Jifu means a compassionate father, matched by itsukushimu chichi. The historical spellings Itçucuximu and Pay are preserved.
[c2-l046] Iiga, l, jigabachi. *Certa laya de abelhas. Vt,*
[c2-l046 note] Jiga and jigabachi name a kind of stinging insect, described here as a kind of bee. The initial is capital I, not long s; the historical Portuguese classification is not silently changed to a modern zoological one.
[c2-l047 >] Iigaua noqini ſuuo caquru. *Aquellas abe-*
[c2-l047 note] Jiga wa noki ni su wo kakuru describes those insects building a nest under the eaves. The scan reads Iigaua and ſuuo, not OCR Iigana and ſiuo. Aquellas abe- continues into the next page; the separate italic footer lhas is its catchword.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *lhas*
