[c1-l001] Toriſoroye, uru, eta. *Tomando ajuntar, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Torisoroye means gathering things together or putting them in order; the Portuguese gives joining or equalizing them. The scan contains the ordinary initial T omitted by OCR; it is not an enlarged initial.
[c1-l002 >] *igualar. ¶* Taru, ſacanauo toriſoroyuru.
[c1-l002 note] Taru and sakana name the wine vessels and accompanying food that are being arranged. Torisoroyuru is the historical finite form corresponding to the headword, and igualar completes the preceding Portuguese alternative.
[c1-l003 >] *Por por ordẽ as barças de vinho, & Sacanas.*
[c1-l003 note] The Portuguese explains arranging the wine vessels and sakana in order. Sacanas is a Japanese food term embedded in the Portuguese sentence; its printed plural ending and italic setting are retained.
[c1-l004] Toriſoye, uru, eta. *Acrecẽtar, ou tomar hũa*
[c1-l004 note] Torisoye means adding or taking something together with another item. The following illustration specifies a vessel and its accompanying drinking cup; Acrecẽtar retains the printed abbreviation.
[c1-l005 >] *couſa, & outra como o bule, & taça, ou copo.*
[c1-l005 note] The paired objects are the bule and a taça or copo, a pot and a drinking vessel. This completes the idea of taking two associated objects together, rather than introducing another entry.
[c1-l006] Toriſucume, uru, eta. *Forçar, ou conſtrãger.*
[c1-l006 note] Torisucume is glossed as forcing or constraining. The Portuguese conſtrãger is abbreviated in print and is preserved without supplying the missing nasal letter.
[c1-l007] Toriſute, tçuru, eta. *Lançar, ou deitar fora*
[c1-l007 note] Torisute means throwing or casting away. The historical principal form tçuru belongs to this verb and should not be replaced by a modern teru form.
[c1-l008 >] *com a mão, &c.*
[c1-l008 note] Com a mão specifies casting something away by hand and completes the preceding definition. The short continuation is an independent physical row.
[c1-l009] Toritate, tçuru, eta. *Edificar, ou aleuantar*
[c1-l009 note] Toritate first means building or raising houses. The following rows extend the verb to restoring or advancing a person socially.
[c1-l010 >] *caſas. ¶ Item, Tornar por a alguem no ſeu*
[c1-l010 note] After completing caſas, the gloss adds restoring someone to his own former condition. Tornar belongs to the Portuguese italic explanation, including its capital T.
[c1-l011 >] *proprio eſtado. ¶* Fitono iyeuo tori ta-
[c1-l011 note] The example begins hito no ie wo toritatsuru, concerning raising a person’s house or household. Proprio eſtado completes the preceding statement about restoring his position.
[c1-l012 >] tçuru. *Aleuantar, & por empès a alguẽ,*
[c1-l012 note] Tçuru completes toritatsuru; the Portuguese gloss explains raising someone and setting him on his feet. The expression therefore allows a social or household sense as well as literal construction.
[c1-l013 >] *ou milhorar lhe o eſtado. ¶* Fitouo tori
[c1-l013 note] The gloss continues with improving someone’s station. Hito wo tori- begins the next example, whose final verb continues on the following row.
[c1-l014 >] tatçuru. *Armar a alguem, ou fazelo homẽ.*
[c1-l014 note] Tatçuru completes hito wo toritatsuru. Armar a alguem and fazelo homẽ describe establishing or making someone into a person of standing; the abbreviated homẽ is retained.
[c1-l015] Toritate. *O edificar. ¶ Item, O fazer a*
[c1-l015 note] The noun toritate denotes the act of building and then the act of establishing another person. It is a separate nominal entry following the verb.
[c1-l016 >] *alguem homem, ou pollo com eſtado. ¶* A-
[c1-l016 note] The gloss explains making someone a man of standing or setting him up in an estate or position. A- starts ano in the Japanese example below.
[c1-l017 >] no fitoua vaga toritategia. *Aquelle he fei-*
[c1-l017 note] Ano hito wa waga toritate ja means that the person owes his establishment to the speaker. Aquelle he fei- begins the Portuguese equivalent across the physical line break.
[c1-l018 >] *tura minha. i, Eu o fiz homem.*
[c1-l018 note] Tura completes feitura: that person is the speaker’s creation or protégé. Eu o fiz homem explicitly explains the sense as having established him.
[c1-l019] Toritçucami, u, ǒda. *Afferrar de algũa*
[c1-l019 note] Toritsukami means grasping or seizing something. The scan has two r types in Afferrar; the principal part ǒda retains its caron and historical contracted inflection.
[c1-l020 >] *couſa.*
[c1-l020 note] Couſa completes the object of afferrar de algũa, grasping something. It is the genuine short closing row of this entry.
[c1-l021] Toritçucuroi, ô, ôta. *Concertar, & apare-*
[c1-l021 note] Toritsukuroi means arranging or preparing something properly. The printed ô and ôta principal parts use circumflexes, unlike the caron in the preceding entry.
[c1-l022 >] *lhar bem, & polidamente quer ſeja vestido,*
[c1-l022 note] The explanation adds that the arrangement is neat and polished, whether the object is clothing or the room mentioned next. The inherited vestido reading is retained without globally changing the difficult italic st class.
[c1-l023 >] *quer Zaxiqu, &c. ¶* Toritçucurôta fu-
[c1-l023 note] Zaxiqu denotes zashiki, a room or reception space, within the Portuguese explanation and remains italic as printed. Enlargement shows no independent final i after the u before the comma; retain this shortened or damaged form with a nonblocking caveat. The new Japanese example begins toritsukurōta furumai, a carefully arranged banquet, across the break.
[c1-l024 >] rumai. *Conuite bem concertado, & apa-*
[c1-l024 note] Rumai completes furumai. The Portuguese Conuite describes a banquet or entertainment that has been properly arranged and prepared; the scan supports n rather than the OCR’s m.
[c1-l025 >] *relhado.*
[c1-l025 note] Relhado completes aparelhado, prepared or furnished. This finishes the banquet example rather than starting a new sense.
[c1-l026] Toritçugui, gu, ida. *Ser medianeiro, ou dar*
[c1-l026 note] Toritsugi as a verb means mediating or passing on a message. The next line specifies that the message or information is delivered to a lord.
[c1-l027 >] *recado, ou parte a algum ſenhor do que lhe tra-*
[c1-l027 note] The intermediary gives a lord notice of what people bring or send to him. The final tra- continues as trazem on the next row.
[c1-l028 >] *zem, ou mandão dizer. ¶ Item, Alicu-*
[c1-l028 note] Zem completes trazem, and mandão dizer means sending word. The scan supports dizer rather than OCR drzer; Alicu- begins the restricted-local-use label alicubi.
[c1-l029 >] *bi, Socorrer com mantimẽto, ou prata. Meliùs,*
[c1-l029 note] Bi completes alicubi, introducing a locally used sense of supplying someone with provisions or silver. Meliùs then introduces the preferred Japanese expression on the next line.
[c1-l030 >] Tori çuzzuquru.
[c1-l030 note] The cross-reference denotes continuing support, corresponding to toritsuzukuru. The print reads Tori çuzzuquru, with a space and no securely visible t before ç; retain this anomalous form instead of silently supplying the expected letter.
[c1-l031] Toritçugui. *Medianeiro, ou o que falla por*
[c1-l031 note] The noun toritsugi denotes an intermediary or spokesman. The relative clause continues with when and before whom he speaks.
[c1-l032 >] *alguem a ſeus tẽpos diante do ſenhor. ¶* To-
[c1-l032 note] The intermediary speaks for someone at the appropriate times before the lord. To- begins the illustrative toritsugi wo suru phrase.
[c1-l033 >] ritçuguiuo ſuru. *Ser medianeiro, ou in-*
[c1-l033 note] Ritçuguiuo ſuru completes toritsugi wo suru, acting as an intermediary. The Portuguese repeats that meaning and begins intercessor across the line break.
[c1-l034 >] *terceßor.*
[c1-l034 note] Terceßor completes interceßor, an intercessor or mediator. The visible ß-shaped type is retained in this short continuation.
[c1-l035] Toritçume, uru, eta. *Por em aperto. ¶* Xi-
[c1-l035 note] Toritsume is explained as putting something under pressure or in a strait. Xi- begins shiro, the fortress in the example below.
[c1-l036 >] rouo toritçumuru. *Por em aperto a for-*
[c1-l036 note] Shiro wo toritsumuru means pressing or closely besieging a fortress. The Portuguese for- begins fortaleza on the next physical row.
[c1-l037 >] *taleza.*
[c1-l037 note] Taleza completes fortaleza, the object placed under pressure. The full short continuation belongs to the siege example.
[c1-l038] Toritçuqe, uru, eta. *Tomando pegar, ou acre-*
[c1-l038 note] Toritsuke here means taking hold and attaching or adding something. Tomando is part of the italic Portuguese gloss; its capital is not a separate Japanese item.
[c1-l039 >] *centar. ¶* Toritçuqe, fittçuqe monouo
[c1-l039 note] The example combines toritsuke and hittsuke with mono wo yuu, describing speech that adds things together. The final verb is continued on the following row.
[c1-l040 >] yǔ. *Falar acrecentando de cà, & de là.*
[c1-l040 note] Yǔ completes the Japanese example. The Portuguese explains adding material from here and there while speaking, retaining the printed grave accents in cà and là.
[c1-l041] Toritçuqi, u, uita. *Afferrar, ou apegarſe*
[c1-l041 note] Toritsuki means clinging or taking firm hold. The uita principal part is retained as the dictionary’s historical notation, rather than expanded or modernized.
[c1-l042 >] *dalgũa couſa. Vt,* Fitono teni toritçuqu.
[c1-l042 note] Hito no te ni toritsuku illustrates grasping someone’s hand. Vt introduces the example and remains italic; the Japanese phrase is upright.
[c1-l043 >] *Pegar, ou afferrar da mão dalguem. ¶* Tẽ-
[c1-l043 note] The Portuguese translates the hand-grasping example. Tẽ- begins tengu with an abbreviation tilde, and belongs wholly to the upright Japanese phrase, not partly to the italic gloss.
[c1-l044 >] guga fitoni toritçuqu. *Entrar o diabo em*
[c1-l044 note] Tenguga hito ni toritsuku describes a tengu attaching itself to or possessing a person. The dictionary renders this as the devil entering someone; the continuation supplies the final object.
[c1-l045 >] *alguem.*
[c1-l045 note] Alguem completes the Portuguese explanation of possession. It is a short body-text continuation, not stray furniture.
[c1-l046] Toritçutçumi, u, unda. *Cercar com gente,*
[c1-l046 note] Toritsutsumi means surrounding with people or troops. The final line lists the military or civic targets that can be encircled.
[c1-l047 >] *como fortaleza, exercito, cidade, &c.*
[c1-l047 note] The examples are a fortress, an army, and a city. Enlarged inspection supports fortaleza and exercito, correcting the OCR’s ſortaleza and excreio without modernizing the spelling.

[c2-l009] Toritome, uru, eta. *Deter a alguem pegando*

[c2-l012] Tori tori. *O que caça, ou toma paßaros.*

[c2-l016 note] Tôriǔ denotes a stay or delay, and Tôriǔ suru means remaining or delaying. The scan supports r, correcting OCR Tôniǔ; the circumflex over o contrasts with the following headword’s caron.
