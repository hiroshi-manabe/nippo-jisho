[c1-l001 >] *peçonha dentro do mel: diz ſe de hum homem*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding entry with the image of poison inside honey, applied to a person. The scan separates mel from diz ſe with a colon; the OCR reading mele dix obscured both the boundary and the verb.
[c1-l002 >] *que de baixo de brandas palauras tem coração*
[c1-l002 note] The relative clause describes someone who conceals a malicious heart beneath gentle words. Brandas palauras explains the honey metaphor on the preceding row.
[c1-l003 >] *danado de matar, ou fazer outro mal.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes the metaphor: the heart is bent on killing or doing other harm. This is the end of the carried-over explanation, not part of the following secret-communication entry.
[c1-l004] Mitçǔ. Fiſocani tçǔzuru. *Communicarſe*
[c1-l004 note] Mittsuu is explained by hisoka ni tsuuzuru, communicating secretly. Both long-u marks are downward carons in this impression; the entry begins at the column margin rather than at continuation indentation.
[c1-l005 >] *ſecretamente por recados, ou por cartas, ou*
[c1-l005 note] The Portuguese specifies secret communication through messages or letters. Its final ou introduces the additional possibility on the next row.
[c1-l006 >] *de outra maneira.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes the means of secret communication: in some other manner. The short continuation belongs to Mitçǔ and is a complete physical row.
[c1-l007] Mittei. Fiſocani ſadamuru. *O determinar*
[c1-l007 note] Mittei is deciding or determining secretly, with hisoka ni sadamuru providing the Japanese explanation. The Portuguese infinitive phrase continues below.
[c1-l008 >] *algũa couſa ſecretamente.* S.
[c1-l008 note] Completes the act of determining something in secret. The final upright S. is the dictionary's written-usage label, not part of the italic Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l009] Mitzǒ. Fiſocani cacuſu. *Couſa que hum tem*
[c1-l009 note] Mitzoo is glossed by hisoka ni kakusu, hiding something privately. The Portuguese shifts to the valued object kept secret; the printed spelling and caron are retained.
[c1-l010 >] *ſecreta em muita estima como liuro, ou carta-*
[c1-l010 note] The object is secretly kept in high esteem, with a book or notebook as examples. Carta- continues into cartapacio on the following physical row.
[c1-l011 >] *pacio, ou couſas curioſas, &c.* S.
[c1-l011 note] Pacio completes cartapacio; curiosities or treasured objects extend the examples. The closing S. is upright and marks written usage.
[c1-l012] Mivaqe, uru, eta. *Vendo diſcernir. Vt,* Iẽ-
[c1-l012 note] Miwake means distinguishing by sight. The example starts Iẽ-, representing zen with consonantal I and a nasal abbreviation; the following row supplies to aku, the contrast between good and evil. The small tilde is retained rather than the OCR's Iã.
[c1-l013 >] to acutono futatçuuo aqiracani mivaqe
[c1-l013 note] Continues Iẽ-to aku to no futatsu wo akiraka ni miwake: clearly distinguishing the two, good and evil. The attached particles and the printed futatçuuo are preserved rather than respaced as modern Japanese.
[c1-l014 >] ſaxerareta. Mon. *Claramente diſcernio o*
[c1-l014 note] Saxerareta completes the Japanese verb phrase; the Portuguese renders it as having clearly distinguished. Mon. is an upright citation label, followed by the italic translation.
[c1-l015 >] *bem, & o mal.*
[c1-l015 note] The objects of discernment are good and evil, completing both the Portuguese sentence and the Japanese contrast begun three rows above.
[c1-l016] Mivaſure, uru, eta. *Eſquecerſe do que vio.*
[c1-l016 note] Mi-wasure is forgetting what one has seen, directly matched by esquecer-se do que vio. The printed long s in the Japanese headword is retained.
[c1-l017] Mivataxi. *Vista ao longe ou ao comprido.*
[c1-l017 note] Miwatashi here is the noun for a distant or extended view. The immediately following entry gives the corresponding verb rather than repeating this nominal definition.
[c1-l018] Mivataxi, ſu, aita. *Ver ao longe. Vt,* Faru-
[c1-l018 note] The principal forms describe looking into the distance; -aita is the supplied historical past ending. Faru- begins the illustrative expression across the physical line break.
[c1-l019 >] cano mucaiuo mivataxeba, &c. Mon.
[c1-l019 note] Farucano mucaiuo mivataxeba describes looking toward what lies far opposite. The printed Farucano is not normalized to Farucani; Mon. remains the upright citation label, including its terminal point.
[c1-l020 >] *Vẽdo o que eſtà de fronte muito ao lõge.*
[c1-l020 note] The translation says that one sees what is facing one a great distance away. The abbreviation tildes in Vẽdo and lõge and the grave in eſtà remain unexpanded.
[c1-l021] Miuchi. *Interior dos paços, ou caſas de peſ-*
[c1-l021 note] Miuchi first denotes the inner part of a noble residence or palace. The divided peſ- belongs to the Portuguese word peſſoas on the next line.
[c1-l022 >] *ſoas nobres. ¶ Item, aliquando, a ſenhora da*
[c1-l022 note] The residence belongs to noble people; a second, occasional sense identifies the lady of the house. Aliquando is explanatory Latin within the italic apparatus.
[c1-l023 >] *caſa, ou molher dalgum ſenhor.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes that second sense as the household's lady or a lord's wife. The historical Portuguese spelling molher is not modernized.
[c1-l024] Miuchixu. *Gente, ou criados falando com*
[c1-l024 note] Miuchixu refers respectfully to a household's people or retainers. The gloss begins to specify the honorific manner of referring to them.
[c1-l025 >] *honra, & reuerencia.*
[c1-l025 note] Honra and reuerencia complete the indication that this designation of retainers is respectful. The row does not begin a new entry.
[c1-l026] Miuo. *Canal, q̃ eſtà nos rios, ou enſeadas poron-*
[c1-l026 note] Miuo is a navigable channel in a river or inlet. The abbreviation q̃ stands unexpanded, and poron- continues into poronde on the next physical row.
[c1-l027 >] *de as Funes vão deſuiandoſe dos baxos.*
[c1-l027 note] Boats follow the channel while avoiding shoals. Funes is a Japanese boat term embedded, with a Portuguese plural ending, in the Portuguese explanation; its printed italic treatment is retained.
[c1-l028] Miuogui. *Paos, ou eſtacas q̃ metem pera co-*
[c1-l028 note] Miuogui denotes poles or stakes installed to identify the channel. The divided co- belongs to conhecer below, not to a Japanese example.
[c1-l029] *nhecer o canal.*
[c1-l029 note] Nhecer o canal completes the purpose of the stakes: recognizing the channel. Although a continuation, this physical row begins close to the column margin, as in the baseline.
[c1-l030] Mivocuri, u, utta. *A companhar com a viſta*
[c1-l030 note] Miwokuri means following a departing person with one's eyes. The printed principal parts u, utta and the visible spacing A companhar are kept rather than regularized.
[c1-l031 >] *ao que ſe vai.*
[c1-l031 note] Ao que ſe vai supplies the person who is leaving, completing the visual-accompaniment definition. This is not a general escorting-on-foot gloss.
[c1-l032] Mivoqi, u, oita. *Deixar, ou ter viſto bem.*
[c1-l032 note] Miwoki is leaving something seen or having observed it well. The following example emphasizes experience retained from earlier events.
[c1-l033 >] *¶* Mucaxino cotouo mivoita fito. *Homẽ q̃*
[c1-l033 note] Mukashi no koto wo miwoita hito describes a person who has seen things of former times. The Portuguese begins Homem que, abbreviated as Homẽ q̃ in the print.
[c1-l034 >] *tem viſto, ou paſſado por couſas antiguas, &*
[c1-l034 note] Such a person has witnessed or lived through old events. Paſſado por supplies experience in addition to merely having seen something.
[c1-l035 >] *de muitos annos.*
[c1-l035 note] De muitos annos completes the reference to events many years old. The separate short line remains attached to the example under Mivoqi.
[c1-l036] Mivoroxi, ſu, oita. *Ver de lugar alto pera baixo.*
[c1-l036 note] Miworoshi, with its supplied principal forms, means looking down from a high place. The next rows extend this spatial sense figuratively to contempt.
[c1-l037 >] *¶ Item, permet. Olhar a alguem com deſprezo.*
[c1-l037 note] The figurative extension is looking at someone with contempt. The compressed printed permet. is retained rather than expanded into an editorial explanation in the transcription.
[c1-l038 >] *¶* Fitouo choccato mivoroſu. *Idem.*
[c1-l038 note] Hito wo choccato miworosu illustrates looking down on someone; Idem refers to the contemptuous sense immediately above. The printed adverb is retained without imposing a modern equivalent.
[c1-l039] Mivôru. i, mivouaru, l, mimacaru. *Morrer,*
[c1-l039 note] Mivooru is equated with miwowaru and mimakaru and glossed as dying. The explicit death gloss keeps this entry distinct from a verb meaning simply to finish looking.
[c1-l040 >] *ou acabarſe. Vt,* Mivôrumade vaſururucoto
[c1-l040 note] The gloss adds coming to an end, then begins mivooru made wasururu koto. The following prohibition means not forgetting until life's end; this row is visibly indented.
[c1-l041 >] nacare. *Não vos eſqueçais tè ofim da vida.*
[c1-l041 note] Nakare completes the negative injunction: do not forget until the end of life. The printed tè and the joined ofim are preserved in the Portuguese rendering.
[c1-l042] Mivotoxi, u, oita. *Notar com os olhos algũa*
[c1-l042 note] Miotoshi first means noticing a fault with the eyes, not only the modern familiar sense of overlooking something. The scan prints the abbreviated principal part u, without a secure preceding s; that anomaly is retained.
[c1-l043 >] *falta, ou deſordẽ dalguem. ¶ Item, Ver*
[c1-l043 note] The noticed object is someone's fault or disorder. A second sense begins with Ver and continues the idea of judging someone less favorably.
[c1-l044 >] *com algum abatimento, ou menos conceito da*
[c1-l044 note] This second sense involves a lowering of regard or a lesser opinion. The object of that changed judgment follows on the next row.
[c1-l045 >] *peßoa do que tinha dantes. ¶ Item, Deixar*
[c1-l045 note] The person is regarded less highly than before. The next Item opens the separate sense of failing to see something, so the apparently opposing meanings are genuinely distinguished by the dictionary.
[c1-l046 >] *de ver por erro algũa couſa que deuera aduer-*
[c1-l046 note] The third sense is overlooking by mistake something that should have been noticed. The visible aduer- continues into aduertir on the following row.
[c1-l047 >] *tir, como algum bom lanço do jogo que hum*
[c1-l047 note] Tir completes aduertir, and a good move in a game supplies the example of a missed opportunity. This is still the third sense of Mivotoxi.
[c1-l048 >] *deixou paßar, &c.*
[c1-l048 note] The player has let that opportunity pass. This completes the body text of the first column; no body line or catchword follows beneath it.

[c2-l001] Mivôxe, uru, eta. *Acabar do ver per-*
[c2-l001 note] Mivooxe is finishing seeing something completely. The scan prints Acabar do ver, including do rather than expected de; that grammatical irregularity is preserved.
[c2-l002 >] *feitamente.*
[c2-l002 note] Feitamente completes perfeitamente, explaining that the seeing is brought fully to completion. The short continuation is a separate physical row.
[c2-l003] Mivoye. *Aruore nacida de ſemẽte. ¶ Per-*
[c2-l003 note] Mivoye denotes a tree grown from seed. The headword's v is clear on enlargement, correcting Miroye; the figurative extension begins at the line end.
[c2-l004 >] *met.* Cono monoua vaga iyeno mivoye-
[c2-l004 note] Per-met. introduces the example kono mono wa waga ie no mioe, comparing a person with something grown in the household. The final copula is deferred to the next row.
[c2-l005 >] gia. *Eſte moço naceo em caſa.*
[c2-l005 note] Gia completes the Japanese predication; the Portuguese explains that this young man was born in the household. It is the figurative application of the seed-grown tree, not another botanical sense.
[c2-l006] Mivoyobi, u, ôda. *Ver, ou chegar a ver.*
[c2-l006 note] Mivoyobi means seeing or managing to see. Enlargement gives Mivoyobi, without the spurious extra ij in the OCR; the past ending ôda carries a circumflex.
[c2-l007] Mivxinai, ǒ, ǒta. *Perder de viſta.*
[c2-l007 note] Mivxinai is miushinai, losing sight of something; v before x represents the vowel u. The scan supports v rather than the OCR's y, and both supplied long-o marks are carons.
[c2-l008] Mivye. *O plantar de ſemente, ou carouço.*
[c2-l008 note] Mivye denotes planting from a seed or fruit stone. This is related to, but distinct from, the earlier entry for a tree that has grown from seed.
[c2-l009 >] *Vt,* Mivyeuo ſuru. *Plantar de ſemente,*
[c2-l009 note] Mivyeuo suru illustrates performing that planting. The Portuguese repeats the seed-based method and continues with another word for a seed below.
[c2-l010 >] *ou depeuide, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] The alternative depeuide refers to planting from a pip or seed. Its joined spelling is retained, as is the historical u in peuide.
[c2-l011] Mixe, ſuru, eta. *Moſtrar.*
[c2-l011 note] Mixe is showing something. The historical principal form ſuru is actually printed and is not silently converted to uru or a modern miseru paradigm.
[c2-l012] Mixe. *Tenda, partileiro, ou taboa em que ſe-*
[c2-l012 note] Mixe as a noun is a stall, shelf, or board on which goods are displayed for sale. Tenda belongs wholly to the italic Portuguese explanation; the isolated roman T was an OCR styling error.
[c2-l013 >] *poẽ varias couſas pera vender. ¶* Mixe-
[c2-l013 note] Goods are placed on the stall or display board for sale. The Japanese example begins Mixe- and continues with the object particle on the next physical row.
[c2-l014 >] uo daſu. *Por tenda, ou partileiro. ¶* Mi-
[c2-l014 note] Mixeuo dasu means setting up a stall or display shelf. The scan repeats partileiro with r, not the OCR's partileito; Mi- begins the contrasting removal example.
[c2-l015 >] xeuo torivoqu. l, fazzuſu. *Tirar, ou deſ-*
[c2-l015 note] Mixewo toriwoku, alternatively fazzusu, describes taking the stall away. The Japanese alternatives are examples, not embedded terms in the Portuguese prose.
[c2-l016 >] *manchar a tenda.*
[c2-l016 note] Manchar completes desmanchar: dismantling the stall. It closes the removal example under the noun Mixe.
[c2-l017] Mixeburacaxi, ſu, aita. *Moſtrar algũa cou-*
[c2-l017 note] Miseburakashi means displaying something and then concealing it. The principal part aita is retained as the historical inflection provided in the entry.
[c2-l018 >] *ſa, & eſcondela, como fazem a os mininos.*
[c2-l018 note] The thing is shown and hidden as people do when teasing small children. The historical mininos and the separately printed a os are preserved.
[c2-l019] Mixedana. *Tenda, ou partileiro, onde poem*
[c2-l019 note] Misedana is a stall or shelf used for displaying merchandise. Unlike Mixe above, this entry explicitly names the display furniture as a compound.
[c2-l020 >] *couſas de venda.*
[c2-l020 note] Couſas de venda supplies the goods put on that shelf. This is a short continuation of Mixedana rather than an independent entry.
[c2-l021] Mixegatana. *Catana que ſe mostra ao que ma-*
[c2-l021 note] Misegatana is a sword displayed to a condemned person. The explanation continues across ma- and the following row, rather than defining an ordinary decorative sword.
[c2-l022 >] *tão por juſtiça, pera que o cortem à vontade*
[c2-l022 note] The person is being executed judicially; the displayed sword enables the actual blow to be delivered conveniently. The location of that blow follows below.
[c2-l023 >] *por de tras cõ outra. ¶* Mixegatanauo ſuru.
[c2-l023 note] The executioner strikes from behind with another sword. Mixegatanauo suru then illustrates performing this threatening display.
[c2-l024 >] *Fazer este medo, & moſtra cõ a Catana.*
[c2-l024 note] The translation describes making this frightening display with the sword. Catana is embedded in the Portuguese sentence and retains its observed italic treatment.
[c2-l025] Mixin. *Restante do tributo, rendimento, ou*
[c2-l025 note] Mishin denotes the outstanding balance of tribute, revenue, or obligatory service. The unpaid or unperformed nature of that balance is explained on the following rows.
[c2-l026 >] *outro ſeruiço obrigatorio que fica por fazer, ou*
[c2-l026 note] The balance can be another obligatory service still to be performed or paid. Obrigatorio is a single printed word; the OCR's internal split obligator io is removed.
[c2-l027 >] *pagar. ¶* Mixinuo naſu, l, ſumaſu. *Cõ*
[c2-l027 note] Mishin wo nasu, alternatively sumasu, means discharging the outstanding obligation. The Portuguese Cõ begins cumprir across the line boundary without a supplied hyphen.
[c2-l028 >] *prir com esta diuida, ou resto que ficou.*
[c2-l028 note] Prir completes Cõprir, followed by the debt or balance that remains. The continuation is retained without expanding the abbreviation or changing diuida to modern spelling.
[c2-l029 >] *¶* Mixinga aru, l, nocoru. *Ficar, ou*
[c2-l029 note] Mishin ga aru or nokoru means that an obligation remains outstanding. These two Japanese alternatives share the following Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l030 >] *reſtar algum ſeruiço, &c. por fazer. ¶* O-
[c2-l030 note] The remaining work is still to be done. The next example starts with upright O-, the first syllable of Oracio on the next row; the OCR had wrongly absorbed it into italic prose and read its hyphen as a point.
[c2-l031 >] raciono mixin. *Resto que fica dalgũas o-*
[c2-l031 note] Oracio no mishin applies the same outstanding-balance expression to prayers. Oracio is part of the Japanese example here, not an isolated Portuguese explanation; the translation begins Resto.
[c2-l032 >] *raçoẽs que hum cuſtuma a rezar.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes the reference to prayers that a person customarily recites but has not yet finished. The printed raçoẽs preserves the tilde on e.
[c2-l033] Mixiri, u, itta. *Conhecer. ¶* Miuo mi-
[c2-l033 note] Mishiri is knowing or recognizing; the damaged final i of Mixiri is visible, unlike the shortened OCR Mixir. Mi wo mi- begins the example about knowing oneself.
[c2-l034 >] xiru. *Conhecerſe aſi meſmo.*
[c2-l034 note] Xiru completes mi wo mishiru, glossed as knowing oneself. The Portuguese aſi meſmo retains the printed joined aſi rather than modern a si.
[c2-l035] Mixǒ. Imada vmarezu. *Antes de nacer.*
[c2-l035 note] Mishoo here means not yet born, explained by imada umarezu. Its caron distinguishes it graphically from the following entry with a circumflex.
[c2-l036 >] *Vt,* Mixǒ ijen. *Antes de nacer.*
[c2-l036 note] Mishoo ijen means before birth. The example repeats the caron of the headword and should not inherit the different accent in Mixô below.
[c2-l037] Mixô. Sucoxi varǒ. *Surrirſe.* S.
[c2-l037 note] Mixô is smiling or laughing a little, explained by sukoshi waroo. The headword has a circumflex while varǒ has a caron; upright S. marks written usage.
[c2-l038] Miya. *Ermida dos Camis.*
[c2-l038 note] Miya denotes a shrine of the kami. The Portuguese uses ermida as its explanatory category and the pluralized Japanese Camis within the gloss.
[c2-l039] Miya. *Filho do Rey. ¶ Itẽ, Seus paços.*
[c2-l039 note] A separate Miya entry denotes a royal son and, by extension, his palace. The second sense is explicitly introduced by the abbreviated Item.
[c2-l040] Miyabara. *Molher de quem o Rey ouue al-*
[c2-l040 note] Miyabara refers to a woman by whom the ruler has had a child. The qualifying exclusion that defines her status continues below.
[c2-l041 >] *gum filho, que não he Rainha.*
[c2-l041 note] The woman is not the queen. This completes the status distinction in Miyabara rather than excluding the child from the definition.
[c2-l042] Miyabito. i, Cuguetachi. *Nobres, ou fi-*
[c2-l042 note] Miyabito is equated with kugetachi, court nobles. The Portuguese divides fidalgos after fi- across the physical line break.
[c2-l043 >] *dalgos que ſeruem ao Dairi no paço.*
[c2-l043 note] Those nobles serve the Dairi at the palace. Dairi occurs inside the Portuguese explanation, whereas Cuguetachi above is a Japanese synonym and is not a typeface-toggle candidate.
[c2-l044] Miyaco. *Cidade onde està a corte do Rey.*
[c2-l044 note] Miyako is the city in which the ruler's court is located. The definition is functional and does not insert a specific city name absent from the print.
[c2-l045] Miyama. *Meliùs,* Miyamajitoto. *Paßari-*
[c2-l045 note] This Miyama is a bird name, with Miyamajitoto given as the preferred expression. Meliùs is italic editorial Latin; the Japanese alternative remains upright.
[c2-l046 >] *nho aßi chamado.*
[c2-l046 note] Nho completes Paßarinho, a small bird so called. The gloss does not itself provide a secure modern species identification.
[c2-l047] Miyama. Fucai yama. *Mõte de aruoredo mui-*
[c2-l047 note] The next Miyama is explained by fukai yama, a deep or densely wooded mountain. The Portuguese mui- continues into muito below.
[c2-l048 >] *to eſpeſſo.*
[c2-l048 note] To completes muito: the mountain's woodland is very thick. The separate far-right Miya- anticipates Miyamagacure on f0325 and has been moved to a catchword zone, not discarded or included in the running definition.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword
