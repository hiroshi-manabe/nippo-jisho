[c1-l001] Tǒji. Imano toqi. *Agora, ou esta hora*
[c1-l001 note] Tǒji is explained by ima no toki, the present time. The scan has a single initial T, not the OCR’s Tr; the Portuguese phrase continues with preſente below.
[c1-l002 >] *preſente.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes esta hora preſente, making explicit that this first Tǒji entry concerns the present moment.
[c1-l003] Tǒji. *Eſte mosteiro, ou varela.*
[c1-l003 note] This homophonous Tǒji means this monastery or temple. The Portuguese contrasts its institutional sense with the preceding temporal entry; the historical word varela is retained.
[c1-l004] Tôjimi. *Miolo de junco, que ſerue de torcida.*
[c1-l004 note] Tôjimi denotes the pith of rush used as a lamp wick. The abraded initial retains the top and stem of T, matching the surrounding headwords, rather than OCR F.
[c1-l005] Tǒjin. Carano fito. *Homem da China.*
[c1-l005 note] Tǒjin is paraphrased as Kara no hito, a person from China. The enlarged mark is a caron like that of the next entry, correcting the OCR’s circumflex; the Portuguese gives the same national designation.
[c1-l006] Tǒjin. i, Nuſubito. *Ladrão.*
[c1-l006 note] This Tǒjin is equated with nusubito and glossed as a thief. The visible capital N and following u correct the malformed OCR Xuſubito.
[c1-l007] Tojiqimi. l, xiqij. *Pao que atraueſa no por-*
[c1-l007 note] Tojiqimi and the alternative shikii designate a threshold. The damaged first vowel is read as o; the Portuguese begins a description of the timber crossing the doorway.
[c1-l008 >] *tal da porta em baixo.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes portal da porta em baixo: the threshold timber lies across the bottom of the door opening.
[c1-l009] Tôjit. Fuyuno fi. *Dia de inuerno.*
[c1-l009 note] Tôjit is explained by fuyu no hi, a winter day. Fuyuno has one initial F followed directly by u; the OCR’s extra i is unsupported.
[c1-l010] Tôjǒ. Tatacai araſô. i, Qenqua. *Arroi-*
[c1-l010 note] The Japanese explanation invokes fighting and contention, with kenka as a synonym. The scan supports Tatacai, not Tatatai; the Portuguese arroi- continues into arroido below.
[c1-l011 >] *do, ou briga.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes arroido and adds briga, a quarrel or fight. This is the continuation of the contention entry rather than a separate item.
[c1-l012] Tǒjǒ. *Esta fortaleza.*
[c1-l012 note] This Tǒjǒ denotes this fortress. It is a separate entry from the preceding word for contention and the following act of ascending to a castle.
[c1-l013] Tôjǒ. Xironi noboru. *O ſubir à fortaleza.*
[c1-l013 note] Shiro ni noboru explains ascending or going up to a fortress. The first accent is badly abraded, but its surviving roof-shaped trace supports ô rather than the preceding entry’s ǒ; the supplied NINJAL index also distinguishes these two headwords. This faint mark remains a nonblocking reading caveat.
[c1-l014] Tojǒ. *Aderradeira cerca, ou castello, & ca-*
[c1-l014 note] Tojǒ denotes the innermost enclosure or castle, with houses mentioned across the break. The missing e in OCR castllo is visible in castello; the unspaced Aderradeira is retained.
[c1-l015 >] *ſas que eſtão dentro da fortaleza.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes caſas and places those houses within the fortress. The row remains part of the enclosure definition.
[c1-l016] Tǒjǔ. Cotaye, mucǔ. *Repoſta. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l016 note] Tǒjǔ is explained by answering or responding, with Portuguese repoſta. The headword ends in marked u without the OCR’s extra i; the next sense concerns correspondence.
[c1-l017 >] *Palaura q̃ ſe poem nos ſobreſcritos das cartas*
[c1-l017 note] The second sense concerns a word placed in the superscriptions of letters. The abbreviation q̃ is preserved without expansion; its relative clause continues below.
[c1-l018 >] *que vão em repoſta de outras. S.*
[c1-l018 note] The letters are replies to earlier letters, explaining the correspondence use of Tǒjǔ. The damaged final letter of vão is a rounded o, not OCR y; S. is the upright written-register label.
[c1-l019] Tǒjǔ. Budǒno xiru. *Vinho de vuas. S.*
[c1-l019 note] This separate Tǒjǔ is grape wine, paraphrased as budō no shiru. The abraded u of Budǒno is supported by the surviving strokes and grape-wine gloss; S. marks written usage.
[c1-l020] Toma. *Esteira que ſe vſa pera emparo da chu-*
[c1-l020 note] Toma is a mat used for shelter from rain. The scan supports contiguous Esteira rather than OCR Este ra; the gloss’s rain word is divided chu- / ua.
[c1-l021 >] *ua nas embarcações, ou noutras partes.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes chuua and specifies use on vessels or elsewhere. This is the continuation of the rain-covering mat definition.
[c1-l022 >] *¶* Tomauo fuqu. *Cobrir a embarcação*
[c1-l022 note] Toma wo fuku means covering or roofing with mats, here applied to a boat. The paragraph mark before the Japanese example is visible and was omitted by OCR.
[c1-l023 >] *deſtas esteiras.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes the description of covering a vessel with these mats. The scan reads esteiras, not the OCR’s estriras; the source’s st form is retained.
[c1-l024] Tomabuqi. *Vt,* Tomabuqini ſuru. *Cobrir*
[c1-l024 note] Tomabuki denotes covering with toma mats; the example uses tomabuki ni suru. The damaged headword b and example n are read from the remaining strokes and the repeated form, correcting Tonabuqi and Tomabuqiri. The example’s n is abraded, so Tomabuqini is retained as a chosen but qualified reading.
[c1-l025 >] *aßi de empreſtado algũa caſa, ou outra couſa*
[c1-l025 note] The Portuguese describes a makeshift covering for a house or another object. Outra includes the visible t omitted in OCR oura; the tilde remains over u in algũa.
[c1-l026 >] *com estas eſteiras de palha.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes the temporary-roofing definition with these straw mats. The two words estas and eſteiras retain their supplied allographic distinction.
[c1-l027] Tǒmachicu-i. Ine, aſa, taqe, axi. *He pa*
[c1-l027 note] Tǒmachicu-i names rice, hemp, bamboo and reeds, as the four Japanese glosses show. The hyphen and final i belong to the headword; Ine is the rice word, not OCR Ite. The Portuguese palavra is physically divided without a securely visible end hyphen.
[c1-l028 >] *laura pera declarar que vem, ou estão muitos*
[c1-l028 note] Completes palavra and explains that this expression describes many people arriving or standing together. The scan supports laura and vem, not the malformed OCR liura and vemn.
[c1-l029 >] *homẽs juntos, particularmente quando eſtão com*
[c1-l029 note] Specifies men gathered together, particularly when bearing weapons. Homẽs has an initial h, not OCR b, and retains its abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l030 >] *as armas, & lanças aleuantadas. ¶* Tǒma-
[c1-l030 note] The men have their weapons and spears raised. Aleuantadas is one word in the scan, replacing OCR ales aita ſas; the next Japanese example begins with the divided headword.
[c1-l031 >] chicu-ino gotoqu yari, nagui natauo ta-
[c1-l031 note] Completes Tǒmachicu-i no gotoku and compares upright spears and naginata with densely growing plants. The scan supports an i after nagu; its visible gap before natauo is retained instead of joining the form editorially.
[c1-l032 >] terareta. *Tinhão aleuãtado as lanças, & Na*
[c1-l032 note] Terareta completes the Japanese verb for erecting the weapons. The Portuguese says they had raised the spears and naginata; Tinhão is wholly Portuguese italic text, and the divided Na begins the Japanese weapon name within that explanation.
[c1-l033 >] *guinatas à maneira de ſementeira de arroz eſ-*
[c1-l033 note] Guinatas completes the divided weapon name Naguinatas. The explanation compares the raised weapons with a field of rice; the final eſ- belongs to eſpigado on the next row.
[c1-l034 >] *pigado, linho canemo, bambus, & caniços*
[c1-l034 note] Completes eſpigado and lists flax/hemp, bamboo and reeds as the plants evoked by the dense array of weapons. The printed canemo is retained without supplying a modern accent.
[c1-l035 >] *crecidos.*
[c1-l035 note] Crecidos completes the description of the tall or grown plants. This short line closes the Tǒmachicu-i entry.
[c1-l036] Tǒmame. *Fauas.*
[c1-l036 note] Tǒmame is glossed as broad beans. The initial T is abraded but its remaining vertical stroke is visible; it must not be dropped as in the OCR.
[c1-l037] Tomari. *Detença, ou porto pera ſurgirem as*
[c1-l037 note] Tomari denotes a stopping place or harbour where vessels anchor. The Portuguese verb surgir has its nautical sense here, completed by embarcações below.
[c1-l038 >] *embarcações.*
[c1-l038 note] The vessels are the subject of ſurgirem in the preceding row, completing the harbour definition.
[c1-l039] Tomari, ru, atta. *Deterſe, pouſar, ou ficar.*
[c1-l039 note] The principal forms tomari, tomaru, tomatta describe stopping, resting or remaining. The initial T matches the other headwords and corrects OCR Fomari.
[c1-l040 >] *¶* Toriga qino yedani tomaru. *Pouſar, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Tori ga ki no eda ni tomaru describes a bird perching on a tree branch. The initial t of tomaru is damaged but agrees with the headword and its visible type; comaru would be a different verb.
[c1-l041 >] *deterſe o paßaro no ramo daruore. ¶* Dangui
[c1-l041 note] Completes the bird-perching translation, then starts a new example with dangui, a sermon or discourse. The historical daruore spelling remains unchanged.
[c1-l042 >] uo qiqedomo nanimo mimini tomaranu.
[c1-l042 note] The object particle continues Dangui: although hearing a sermon, nothing stays in the ears. Tomaranu is the negative of the same verb, used figuratively for retention.
[c1-l043 >] *Ainda q̃ ouço pregação não me fica nada nos*
[c1-l043 note] Begins the Portuguese translation: although I hear preaching, nothing remains in my ears. The opening vowel of ouço is o, not OCR c, while q̃ remains abbreviated.
[c1-l044 >] *ouuidos. ¶ Item, Ceßar a couſa. ¶* Co-
[c1-l044 note] Completes the ears phrase and introduces the further sense of an activity ceasing. Co- starts the next example’s kono across the physical break.
[c1-l045 >] no cuni ranno vchinareba, ranbuto, cha-
[c1-l045 note] Kono kuni ran no uchi nareba sets the example amid turmoil in the country. The scan supports vchinareba rather than the OCR’s vehinarebi; ranbu and the divided chanoyu expression name the activities that stop.
[c1-l046 >] noyuzuqi fattato tomatta. Mon. *Aue-*
[c1-l046 note] Completes chanoyu-zuki and says that these pursuits stopped abruptly. Mon. is a roman citation label; the Portuguese translation begins Aue- / do. An abbreviation tilde over e is not securely established in this worn impression, so the supplied unmarked Aue- is retained without supplying the expected n.
[c1-l047 >] *do neste reino reuoltas, & perturbações ceßa-*
[c1-l047 note] The translation describes upheavals and disturbances in the kingdom, after which the activities ceased. The scan has do and perturbações, correcting OCR de and perurbações; ceßa- continues as rão at the next column’s top.

[c2-l001 >] *rão derepente os bailos, tangeres, & cantos,*
[c2-l001 note] Rão completes ceßa- at the foot of the first column. The translation lists dances, instrumental music and singing as activities that ceased; the visible b corrects OCR hailos.
[c2-l002 >] *& exercicio do chanoyu.*
[c2-l002 note] Adds the practice of chanoyu to the suspended activities. Enlargement shows the initial c at the lowercase body height; chanoyu is a Japanese cultural term within the Portuguese explanation and is identified for whole-word typeface review.
[c2-l003] Tǒmaru. *Gallinha, ou gallo da China, que he*
[c2-l003 note] Tǒmaru denotes a Chinese hen or cock. The definition’s comparison with Japanese fowl continues on the next line.
[c2-l004 >] *mayor, & mais forte que o de Iapão.*
[c2-l004 note] Completes the poultry description as larger and stronger than the Japanese kind. The historical Portuguese mayor is preserved.
[c2-l005] Tǒmat. Ima, ſuye. *Tempo preſente, & fu-*
[c2-l005 note] Tǒmat contrasts ima and sue, present and future. The terminal fu- begins futuro, with a visible f rather than OCR long s; the final t of the Japanese headword is retained.
[c2-l006 >] *turo, ou agora, & depois. ¶* Tǒmatno
[c2-l006 note] Completes futuro and paraphrases the contrast as now and later. Tǒmatno begins the Japanese example, with the attached genitive particle preserved.
[c2-l007 >] xianga iru. *He neceßario conſiderar o pre-*
[c2-l007 note] Shian ga iru means that consideration is necessary, here of present and future circumstances. The scan supports conſiderar, restoring the i omitted by OCR.
[c2-l008 >] *ſente, & o futuro.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes preſente and the paired future, closing the example’s Portuguese translation.
[c2-l009] Tomaya. Tomano iye. *Caſa cuberta de cer-*
[c2-l009 note] Tomaya is explained as a toma house, covered with straw mats. The Portuguese certas is divided across this row and the next.
[c2-l010 >] *tas esteiras de palha, ou pobre, & baixa.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes certas esteiras de palha and adds that the house is humble and low. This is a description of the same dwelling, not a separate headword.
[c2-l011] Tome, uru, eta. *Deter a alguem, ou al.*
[c2-l011 note] The principal forms tome, tomuru, tometa mean stopping or detaining a person or thing. The end mark following al is retained provisionally as the supplied point; the word continues below.
[c2-l012 >] *gũa couſa.*
[c2-l012 note] Completes algũa couſa, extending the transitive stopping verb to an object as well as a person. The tilde stays on u as printed.
[c2-l013] Tomebari. *Agulha que ſe mete em certa par-*
[c2-l013 note] Tomebari denotes a needle inserted into a particular part of the body. The Portuguese explanation describes a therapeutic use, continued on the next row.
[c2-l014 >] *te do corpo pera impedir as camaras. ¶* To-
[c2-l014 note] The stated purpose is to stop diarrhoeal evacuations, the historical sense of camaras here. The next example repeats the needle term across the line break.
[c2-l015 >] mebariuo tatçuru. *Dar esta agulhada.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes tomebari wo tatsuru, setting or administering this needle. The Portuguese repeats the action as giving the needle puncture.
[c2-l016 >] *¶ Item, O arrematar a costura. Vt,* To-
[c2-l016 note] A second sense concerns fastening off a seam. The scan supports costura, with damaged o rather than OCR a; To- begins the sewing example below.
[c2-l017 >] mebariuo ſuru.
[c2-l017 note] Completes tomebari wo suru, performing the fastening-off stitch. This Japanese example belongs to the second, sewing-related sense rather than the preceding therapeutic needle.
[c2-l018] Tomedocoro. *Lugar, ou modo de ter, ou fa-*
[c2-l018 note] Tomedokoro is a place or means of stopping or restraining. The Portuguese fazer is divided at the line end and continues below.
[c2-l019 >] *zer ceßar. ¶* Tomedocoromo nai fito.
[c2-l019 note] Completes fazer ceßar, then gives a person with no stopping point or means of restraint. The following translation develops the unrestrained-person example.
[c2-l020 >] *Homem que não ha podelo ter, nem fazer ceſ-*
[c2-l020 note] The person cannot be restrained or made to stop. The Portuguese infinitive ceſſar is divided between the two long-s forms across this physical line boundary.
[c2-l021 >] *ſar na pratica, ou noutra couſa que vai conti-*
[c2-l021 note] Completes ceſſar and applies the lack of restraint to conversation or another continued activity. Pratica has its speech/conversation sense here.
[c2-l022 >] *nuando.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes continuando and closes the explanation of someone who goes on without stopping.
[c2-l023] Tomeyama. *Mato, ou monte vedado, como*
[c2-l023 note] Tomeyama is a reserved or closed woodland or mountain. The Portuguese comparison with a protected preserve continues on the next row.
[c2-l024 >] *coutada.*
[c2-l024 note] Coutada completes the reserved-land comparison, clarifying that access or use is restricted.
[c2-l025] Tomi, mu. *Verbo defect. Ser rico, & pro-*
[c2-l025 note] Tomi, tomu means being rich or prospering. The source labels it Verbo defect.; that grammatical qualification is preserved without inventing omitted principal parts.
[c2-l026 >] *ſpero, ou proſperar.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes proſpero and adds the verb proſperar. The paired gloss describes both the state of wealth and becoming prosperous.
[c2-l027] Tômiǒ. i, Miyacaxi. *Candea, ou alampa-*
[c2-l027 note] Tômiǒ denotes a devotional lamp, with miyakashi as its Japanese synonym. The scan shows a capital M, correcting OCR Xiyacaxi; the lamp word alampada is divided across rows.
[c2-l028 >] *da que ſe poem diante do Fotoque, ou Camis.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes alampada and places the lamp before the Buddha or kami. Fotoque and Camis are Japanese terms embedded in the Portuguese explanation and are identified for whole-word typeface review.
[c2-l029 >] *As vezes ſe toma pello azeite, ou cera que*
[c2-l029 note] The term may also designate the oil or wax itself. This metonymic extension of the lamp name continues into the burning-fuel clause below.
[c2-l030 >] *arde diante do Idolo.*
[c2-l030 note] The oil or wax burns before the image. Idolo is the Portuguese gloss’s religious terminology and is retained as part of the historical source.
[c2-l031] Tomiſacaye, uru, eta. *Proſperar, & flore-*
[c2-l031 note] Tomisakaye denotes prospering or flourishing in wealth. The Japanese principal forms and Portuguese flourishing verb both continue the semantic field of tomi.
[c2-l032 >] *cer em riquezas, &c.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes florecer em riquezas, specifying material prosperity rather than literal flowering.
[c2-l033] Tomo. *Companhia, ou os que a companhão.*
[c2-l033 note] Tomo denotes company or the people accompanying someone. The source’s separation a companhão is retained, without modernizing it to a single Portuguese word.
[c2-l034 >] *¶* Votomoni mairǒ. *Irei com voſco, ou*
[c2-l034 note] Otomo ni mairō is the deferential offer to accompany someone. The Portuguese begins I shall go with you, continuing with a more respectful form of address below.
[c2-l035 >] *com voßa mercè. ¶ Item, Moços, ou cri-*
[c2-l035 note] Completes the respectful address voßa mercè, correcting OCR voßia. A further sense describes boys or servants, with criados divided across the line break.
[c2-l036 >] *ados que acompanhão aſeu amo. Vt,* To-
[c2-l036 note] Completes criados and specifies servants accompanying their master. To- begins the verbal example tomo suru on the next physical line.
[c2-l037 >] mo ſuru. *A companhar o moço aſeu amo.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes tomo suru, a servant accompanying his master. The Portuguese initial A companhar preserves the printed spacing rather than silently joining it.
[c2-l038 >] *¶* Tomono xǔ. *Companheiros, ou gente*
[c2-l038 note] Tomo no shū denotes companions or an accompanying group. The damaged capital T is visible, replacing the OCR’s long-s initial in ſomono.
[c2-l039 >] *que a companha.*
[c2-l039 note] Completes the group description: people who accompany someone. The visible a companha spacing is preserved.
[c2-l040] Tomo. *Popa do nauio, ou embarcação.*
[c2-l040 note] This separate tomo means the stern of a vessel. It is a nautical noun, unrelated to the preceding companion sense in this entry sequence.
[c2-l041] Tomo. *Ainda que, doulhe que. Vt,* Fito-
[c2-l041 note] This tomo is concessive, glossed as even if or granted that. Fito- begins the example with hito on the following line.
[c2-l042 >] naritomo. *Ainda que ſeja homem.*
[c2-l042 note] Completes hito naritomo, even if he is a man. The Portuguese explicitly supplies the concessive sense of the preceding entry.
[c2-l043] Tomocacumo. *Como quer que ſeja, ou de qual*
[c2-l043 note] Tomokakumo means however it may be or in whatever manner. The scan has distinct word gaps in quer que ſeja and the final l of qual; no terminal hyphen is supplied before quer below.
[c2-l044 >] *quer maneira que ſeja.*
[c2-l044 note] Quer completes qualquer across the physical break, followed by any manner whatsoever. It is part of the preceding gloss, not a new word entry.
[c2-l045] Tomo cǒmo. *Idem.*
[c2-l045 note] Tomo kōmo receives the same meaning as tomokakumo. The initial is a capital T with a damaged top, not OCR r; the caron on cǒmo is retained.
[c2-l046] Tomo cǒmo zzuqin. *Barrete aberto em cima*
[c2-l046 note] This compound names a hood or cap open at the top. The doubled z in zzuqin is retained as the source’s Japanese spelling, and the gloss explains the unusual construction below.
[c2-l047 >] *de maneira que ſe pode por na cabeça por am-*
[c2-l047 note] The cap can be put onto the head through either opening. The last am- begins ambas on the following row.
[c2-l048 >] *bas as aberturas.*
[c2-l048 note] Completes ambas as aberturas and closes the cap definition. The separate Tomo- at the far right is a catchword anticipating Tomodachi on the next page, not part of this body line.
