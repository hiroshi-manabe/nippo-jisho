[c1-l001] Inyen. *Principio, ou cauſa. Vide,* Innen.
[c1-l001 note] Inyen is a principle or cause. The scan reads Principio, not Pencipio, and refers the reader to Innen with doubled n; this entry is anticipated by the preceding page's catchword.
[c1-l002] Inyen. i, Sacamori. *Beberetes.*
[c1-l002 note] This separate Inyen is equated with sakamori, a drinking gathering or entertainment. Beberetes distinguishes it from the preceding causal term.
[c1-l003] Inyǒ. *Palaura de liuros que ſe toma por noi-*
[c1-l003 note] Inyo is introduced as a bookish term covering complementary opposites. Noi- begins noite, the first member of the night-and-day pair on the next row.
[c1-l004 >] *te, & dia, ceo, & terra, molher, & homẽ,*
[c1-l004 note] Completes night and day, followed by heaven and earth, woman and man. These are the dictionary's illustrative pairs for inyo, not separate headwords.
[c1-l005 >] *&c. ¶* Inyǒ qeôquai. *Ajuntamento*
[c1-l005 note] Inyo qeookwai names a conjunction or union. The caron in Inyǒ and circumflex in qeôquai are distinct printed marks; the Portuguese specifies the partners below.
[c1-l006 >] *do homem, & molher. ¶ Item, Ajunta-*
[c1-l006 note] The first conjunction is that of man and woman. Item then opens a broader explanation, with Ajunta- continued on the next physical row.
[c1-l007 >] *mento de couſa como materia, & forma de q̃*
[c1-l007 note] Completes ajuntamento and uses matter and form as a philosophical analogy. The singular couſa and the abbreviated q̃ are retained as printed rather than regularized.
[c1-l008 >] *ſe compoem as couſas ſenſiueis. ¶* Inyǒ
[c1-l008 note] Matter and form are said to compose perceptible things. Inyǒ begins the next Japanese expression, completed by zuijun on the following row.
[c1-l009 >] zuijun. *Compoſição, & combinação destas*
[c1-l009 note] Zuijun completes the inyo expression, glossed as the composition or combination of these two principles. The demonstrative destas refers forward to duas couſas below.
[c1-l010 >] *duas couſas. ſ.* In, yǒ. *Que he materia, &*
[c1-l010 note] The two principles are explicitly named In and yǒ within the Portuguese explanation. Lowercase italic ſ. introduces the specification here; it is not the upright capital S usage label. The gloss starts another matter-and-form analogy.
[c1-l011 >] *forma, ou humidade, & quentura, &c.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes matter and form and adds moisture and heat as another explanatory pair. The historical spellings humidade and quentura are retained.
[c1-l012] Inyocu. *Concupiſcencia carnal, ou deſejos da*
[c1-l012 note] Inyoku is defined as carnal desire. The phrase 'desires of the flesh' is divided after da and completed in the next short row.
[c1-l013 >] *carne.*
[c1-l013 note] Carne completes the desire-of-the-flesh definition. Its r-n sequence was misread as m in the draft's came; the entire word is printed in italic.
[c1-l014] Inyu. i, Innen. *Principio, ou cauſa.*
[c1-l014 note] Inyu is another term identified with innen and glossed as a principle or cause. The doubled n in Innen agrees with the cross-reference at the top of the page.
[c1-l015] Inzan. Innogoxoye mairu. *Ir ao paço do*
[c1-l015 note] Inzan means visiting the court of the retired sovereign. The Japanese paraphrase has doubled n and the visible spelling Innogoxeye, rather than the draft's Inogoxoye; the Portuguese identifies the sovereign below.
[c1-l016 >] *Rey que ja renunciou o gouerno.*
[c1-l016 note] The king in question has already relinquished government. This completes the definition of attendance on a retired sovereign, rather than an ordinary royal audience.
[c1-l017] Inzot. *Leuar em companhia. S.*
[c1-l017 note] Inzot means taking someone along in company. The final t is retained as part of the historical spelling, and the final upright S marks written usage.

[c1-l018 initial=2] IPpa. Fitotçuno monpa. *Hũa religião, ou*
[c1-l018 note] Ippa is explained as one religious group or sect. The section-opening I is an enlarged initial spanning this and the next row; the following capital P belongs to the same headword and was not an italic standalone letter.

[c1-l020] Ippa. Fitotçuno nami. *Hũa onda. S.*
[c1-l020 note] This separate Ippa means one wave, explicitly paraphrased by hitotsu no nami. The upright S is the written-usage label.
[c1-l021] Ippai. *Adu. Modo de encher de todo, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Ippai is introduced as an adverb describing complete filling or fullness. The alternative state, being full, continues below.
[c1-l022 >] *eſtar cheo. Vt,* Chǒdo ippai iruru. *En-*
[c1-l022 note] Choodo ippai iruru illustrates filling something right up. En- is wholly italic Portuguese and begins encher, not a mixed roman-and-italic Japanese fragment.
[c1-l023 >] *cher de todo botando em algũa vaſilha. ¶* I-
[c1-l023 note] Completes encher and explains filling a container completely. I- starts Igrejani in the next example; the line's algũa keeps its observed tilde carrier.
[c1-l024 >] grejani fitoga ippai maitta. *Eſtaa a Igre-*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the example that people have come to fill the church. The Portuguese prints Eſtaa with doubled a before the separate article a; this historical form is not shortened to the draft's Eſta.
[c1-l025 >] *ja chea, & abarrotada de gente que veo.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes Igreja and describes the church as full and crowded with those who came. The historical singular veo follows the collective gente as printed.
[c1-l026 >] *¶ Item, Permet.* Yumiuo ippai fijte fa-
[c1-l026 note] The figurative extension of ippai concerns drawing a bow fully before releasing an arrow. Fa- begins hanatsu on the next row; fijte is retained in the printed historical spelling.
[c1-l027 >] natçu. *Tirar com frecha embebendõ o ar-*
[c1-l027 note] Completes hanatsu, to release or shoot. The Portuguese explains shooting an arrow with the bow fully drawn; the tilde in embebendõ is retained and ar- begins arco below.
[c1-l028 >] *co todo.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes arco todo, specifying the full draw of the bow. This short continuation closes the example rather than starting a new sense.
[c1-l029] Ippan. *Hum Goqui de arroz.*
[c1-l029 note] Ippan is one bowl of rice. Goqui names the bowl within the Portuguese definition and is printed in italic here; the initial I of the headword replaces the draft's lowercase l.
[c1-l030] Ippan. Fitotçuno fo. *Hũa vela de ma-*
[c1-l030 note] The second Ippan means one sail, as hitotsu no ho makes explicit. The maritime qualification is divided after ma-.
[c1-l031 >] *rear. S.*
[c1-l031 note] Rear completes marear, identifying a sailing sail rather than a candle. The upright S is the written-usage label.
[c1-l032] Ippei. *Hum bule, ou pichel de vinho.*
[c1-l032 note] Ippei is a vessel's measure of wine or sake, described as a pot or pitcher. The gloss's two vessel names are Portuguese alternatives, not separate Japanese forms.
[c1-l033] Ippen, l, ippenni. *De todo coração, ou ſoo*
[c1-l033 note] Ippen or ippenni means wholeheartedly or exclusively. Soo continues into mente below; the absence of a visible hyphen is retained.
[c1-l034 >] *mente. Vt,* Goxǒuo ippẽni negǒ. *De-*
[c1-l034 note] Completes somente and gives an example of single-mindedly desiring the next-life goal. The Portuguese renders this as desiring salvation, beginning with De-; the tilded ippẽni is not expanded.
[c1-l035 >] *ſejar de coração, & ſem miſtura de outra cou*
[c1-l035 note] Completes desejar and emphasizes heartfelt desire unmixed with anything else. Cou continues into couſa on the next row without an added hyphen.
[c1-l036 >] *ſa, a ſaluação.*
[c1-l036 note] Completes couſa and supplies salvation as the object of desire. This closes the religious example of wholehearted aspiration.
[c1-l037] Ippiô. Fitotçuno fiſago. *Hũa cabaça. S.*
[c1-l037 note] The Japanese hisago identifies a gourd, but the Portuguese visibly reads cabeça rather than the expected cabaça. This apparent printed anomaly is retained. The final upright S marks written usage.
[c1-l038] Ippiôxi. *Hum tom, ou hum compaſo. ¶* Ip-
[c1-l038 note] Ippyoshi denotes one tone or beat. The initial is capital I, not long s; the Japanese example starts with Ip-, outside the italic Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l039 >] piôxini vodoru. *Bailar, ou dançar em hũ*
[c1-l039 note] Completes ippyooshi ni odoru, dancing to a single rhythm or beat. Hũ begins the phrase specifying one and the same rhythm below.
[c1-l040 >] *meſmo tom, ou compaſo.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes the explanation as dancing in the same tone or measure. The source's single-s spelling compaſo is retained.
[c1-l041] Ippit. Fitofude. *Hũa carta. ¶ Itẽ, Hũ*
[c1-l041 note] Ippit is explained by hitofude and glossed as a letter, then as a short piece of writing. The missing initial I is restored from the scan; the final t remains unexpanded.
[c1-l042 >] *eſcrito, ou carta breue.*
[c1-l042 note] The second sense is a written note or brief letter. This completes the preceding Itẽ phrase.
[c1-l043] Ippǒ. Fitotçuno nori. *Hũa ley.*
[c1-l043 note] Ippo means one law or rule, as hitotsu no nori explains. The caron in the headword is retained distinctly from the circumflexes in Ippiô and Ippiôxi.
[c1-l044] Ippon. *Hũa certa dignidade.*
[c1-l044 note] Ippon names a particular rank or dignity. The gloss itself does not further specify the institution or rank system; no more specific identification is inserted into the transcription.
[c1-l045] Ippucu. Fitotçuno fara. *Hum ventre. Vt,*
[c1-l045 note] Ippuku literally names one belly or womb. The example continues at the top of the next column, where it describes siblings of the same womb.

[c2-l010 >] coroſazu xite iquru. *Fazer viuer o paßa-*

[c2-l014 >] *pera que dure. ¶* Fanauo iquru. *Pòr os*
[c2-l014 note] Completes the reason for banking the fire: so that it lasts. Hana wo ikuru begins a new example about keeping flowers fresh, whose explanation starts with putting their stems in water.

[c2-l016 note] Completes the flower-freshness explanation and begins an example about preserving chestnuts, mandarins, and similar produce. The visible tilde in micã represents the nasal ending missing from the OCR mica; no hyphen is supplied after iqu.
[c2-l017 >] ru. *Guardar castanhas, laranjas, & outra*

[c2-l019 >] *durem.*

[c2-l021 >] *guerra. Vt,* Fitouo iqedoru, l, iqedori

[c2-l023 note] Ikemizu is water from a pond or tank. The printed doubled zz is retained rather than normalized to a modern romanization.
[c2-l024] Iqi. *Folego. ¶* Iqiuo tçuqu. *Reſpirar*
[c2-l024 note] Iki is breath. Iki wo tsuku begins a collocation explained as breathing out, with the force of the exhalation specified on the next row.
[c2-l025 >] *botando o folego com força. ¶* Iqiuo tomu-
[c2-l025 note] Completes the forceful-exhalation gloss and starts iki wo tomuru, holding one's breath. The division tomu-/ru is kept at the physical line ending.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *mãos*
