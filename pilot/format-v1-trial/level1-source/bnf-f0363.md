[c1-l001 >] *Tomar poße da rẽda, ou terras a primeira vez.*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding entry: taking possession of a revenue or estate for the first time. The initial verb is Tomar, and the damaged contracted noun is read as rẽda, not OCR rẽla; the printed abbreviation is not expanded.
[c1-l002] Nhǔca. *O entrar, ou misturarſe hũa couſa cõ*
[c1-l002 note] Nyuuka denotes one thing entering or becoming mixed with another. The entry's territorial example below supports this sense, rather than the OCR's malformed initial Xh; the Portuguese verb is misturarſe.
[c1-l003 >] *outra: particularmente ſe diz de terras de hum*
[c1-l003 note] Outra completes the reciprocal relation of two things. The gloss then narrows the application to one lord's lands, continuing into the next row.
[c1-l004 >] *ſenhor que entrão pollas do outro. Vt,* Nhǔ-
[c1-l004 note] The lands of one lord extend into those of another. Vt introduces nyuuka suru, divided after Nhǔ- at the visible line-end mark.
[c1-l005 >] ca ſuru.
[c1-l005 note] Completes nyuuka suru, the verbal expression illustrating the preceding entry. This short Japanese continuation is a separate physical row, with no additional Portuguese translation.
[c1-l006] Nhǔcǒ. *Incenſo.*
[c1-l006 note] Nyuukoo is glossed simply as incense. Both the u and o carry downward-pointing marks in this impression; the spelling is preserved independently of modern vowel notation.
[c1-l007] Nhǔcocu. Cunini iru. *Entrar a primeira vez*
[c1-l007 note] Nyuukoku is explained by kuni ni iru, entering a country or domain. The Portuguese specifies the first entry, whose circumstances continue below.
[c1-l008 >] *no reino depois de tomado, ou conquiſtado,*
[c1-l008 note] The first entry follows taking or conquering the realm. This is a continuation of nyuukoku, not a separate military headword; the scan supports the contiguous depois.
[c1-l009 >] *ou dado po lo ſenhor da Tenca, &c.*
[c1-l009 note] Alternatively the domain has been granted by the ruler of the realm. Tenca is Japanese embedded in the italic Portuguese explanation. At po lo a weak intervening trace could be a damaged additional l; the aligned draft's spacing and single l are retained instead of confidently supplying pollo.
[c1-l010] Nhǔdǒ. Dǒni iru. *Rapado. ¶* Nhǔ-
[c1-l010 note] Nyuudoo, explained as doo ni iru, is glossed as a shaven person, that is, one who has taken the tonsure. The headword's second letter is h, not OCR n; the example begins at the right edge.
[c1-l011 >] dǒ ſuru. *Fazerſe rapado.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes nyuudoo suru, taking the tonsure or becoming shaven. The Portuguese reflexive phrase belongs to this example, not to the next entry with the same headword spelling.
[c1-l012] Nhǔdǒ. Dǒye iru. *Entrar na Tera pera fa-*
[c1-l012 note] This separate nyuudoo entry means entering a temple hall, explained by doo ye iru. The Portuguese has the Japanese temple word Tera within its sentence; fa- continues as fazer below.
[c1-l013 >] *zer oração.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes fazer oração: the temple is entered in order to pray. The two-word continuation belongs to the second nyuudoo entry.
[c1-l014] Nhǔdǒgumo. *Certo genero de aranhas gran-*
[c1-l014 note] Nyuudoogumo names a kind of large spider. The scan supplies a mark over u missing from the draft; no modern species identification is imposed on the description.
[c1-l015 >] *des, que tem o corpo redondo.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes grandes and specifies that these spiders have a round body. This physical description closes the preceding entry.
[c1-l016] Nhǔgiǒ. Giǒni iru. *Entrar no fogo, ou de*
[c1-l016 note] Nyuujoo is explained by joo ni iru. The dictionary's gloss concerns voluntarily entering fire or another place to die; the initial N and the verb iru correct OCR X and uru.
[c1-l017 >] *baixo da terra, ou nagoa tomando a morte por*
[c1-l017 note] Continues with going beneath the earth or into water and voluntarily accepting death. De above joins baixo here syntactically; the printed nagoa is retained without expansion.
[c1-l018 >] *ſi como fazem algũs Bonzos gentios.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes por si and attributes the practice to certain non-Christian Buddhist monks. This is the dictionary's historical explanation, including its religious vocabulary, rather than a modern definition of all uses of nyuujoo.
[c1-l019] Nhǔmet. *Morte de Fotoque.*
[c1-l019 note] Nyuumet, with a checked final t, is defined as the death of a Buddha. Fotoque is Japanese embedded within the Portuguese gloss; it is not a second headword.
[c1-l020] Nhǔmi. Chino agiuai. i, Amaqi agiuai.
[c1-l020 note] Nyuumi is paraphrased as chi no ajiwai and amaki ajiwai, the taste of milk and a sweet taste. The Portuguese gloss on the next row identifies milk; the mark over the headword's u is read as a caron.
[c1-l021 >] *Leite. ¶* Nhǔmiuo nomu. *Beber leite.*
[c1-l021 note] Leite gives the milk sense explicitly. Nyuumi wo nomu means drinking milk, exactly matching beber leite.
[c1-l022] Nhǔnan. Yauaraca. *Brandura, ou manſidão.*
[c1-l022 note] Nyuunan is identified with yawaraka, softness or gentleness. The Portuguese gives both mildness and meekness, rather than the modern technical sense of flexibility alone.
[c1-l023] Nhǔnanna. *Couſa de manſidão. ¶* Nhǔ-
[c1-l023 note] Nyuunanna is the adjectival form associated with gentleness. Its weakened u and mark are supported by the repeated form in the example; the draft's Nhinanna would misidentify the word.
[c1-l024 >] nanna fito. *Homem manſo, & brando.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes nyuunanna hito, a meek and gentle person. The divided headword is not an independent nanna entry.
[c1-l025] Nhǔtçu. Minatoye iru. *Entrar no porto. Vt,*
[c1-l025 note] Nyuutsu is explained by minato ye iru, entering a port. Vt introduces the formal verbal example on the next row.
[c1-l026 >] Nhùtçu tçucamatçuru.
[c1-l026 note] Nyuutsu tsukamatsuru is a deferential way of saying that one enters a port. The weak mark over u is retained as the draft's grave; damage prevents securely excluding a caron, so it is not harmonized with the preceding headword.
[c1-l027] Nhǔua. Yauaraca yauaraca. *Manſidão.*
[c1-l027 note] Nyuuwa denotes gentleness. The Japanese paraphrase repeats yawaraka; the first capital is Y, not OCR T, and the headword begins N rather than R.
[c1-l028 >] *¶* Nhǔua ninnicuno fito. *Homem mã-*
[c1-l028 note] Nyuuwa ninniku no hito describes a gentle, patient person. Ninniku here is forbearance, as the Portuguese continuation makes clear, not the unrelated garlic word.
[c1-l029 >] *ſo, & paciente.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes mã-ſo and supplies paciente, closing the explanation of the gentle and forbearing person.
[c1-l030] Nhǔuana. *Manſo, ou brando de condição.*
[c1-l030 note] Nyuuwa na is adjectival, describing someone meek or mild in temperament. The damaged initial is N; the Portuguese concerns disposition rather than material softness.
[c1-l031] Nhǔua ninnicu. *Manſidão, ou brandura com*
[c1-l031 note] Nyuuwa ninniku combines gentleness with patient forbearance. The visible space between the two Japanese elements is retained; the final Portuguese com governs paciencia below.
[c1-l032 >] *paciencia.*
[c1-l032 note] Paciencia completes the compound's explanation as gentleness with patience. This final row precedes the new alphabetical subsection, rather than belonging to its heading.

[c1-l033 initial=2] NI. *Particula de Datiuo.*
[c1-l033 note] Ni is identified as a dative particle. The opening N is an enlarged initial spanning two physical rows, followed by capital I; the OCR pilcrow is not printed text. Datiuo, not Daiuo, names the grammatical case.
[c1-l034 >] Ni. *Propoſição de Ablatiuo.*
[c1-l034 note] A second use of ni is described as an ablative preposition. Preserve the printed Propoſição rather than replacing it with an expected modern grammatical term; the line is indented beside the enlarged N above.
[c1-l035] Ni. *Carga, ou fato. ¶* Niuo tçumu. *Ar-*
[c1-l035 note] The noun ni means a load or baggage. Ni wo tsumu illustrates loading it; Portuguese Ar- begins arrumar across the physical line break.
[c1-l036 >] *rumar o fato na embarcação. ¶* Niuo no-
[c1-l036 note] Completes arranging the baggage aboard a vessel. Ni wo no- begins the next loading expression, nosuru, which continues below.
[c1-l037 >] ſuru. *Embarcar fato. ¶* Niuo voroſu.
[c1-l037 note] Nosuru means putting baggage aboard. Ni wo orosu then introduces unloading; initial v in voroſu belongs to the historical romanization and is not a separate syllable.
[c1-l038 >] *Deſembarcar o fato, ou deſcarregar a besta.*
[c1-l038 note] Translates ni wo orosu as taking baggage off a vessel or unloading a pack animal. Both alternatives belong to the same example.
[c1-l039 >] *¶* Niuo vôſuru. *Carregar besta, ou boy.*
[c1-l039 note] Ni wo oosuru is illustrated by loading a beast or an ox. The circumflex belongs to the Japanese verb, and the historical spelling boy remains unchanged.
[c1-l040 >] *¶* Niuo vô, l, cataguru. *Leuar fato às*
[c1-l040 note] Ni wo oo and the alternative kataguru mean carrying baggage on one's shoulders. Latin l marks the alternative; the final Portuguese word is às, continuing with costas below, not the draft's à plus a full stop.
[c1-l041 >] *coſtas. ¶* Nijiruxi. *Sinal, ou marca q̃*
[c1-l041 note] Costas completes the preceding carrying expression. Nijirushi is then a mark identifying baggage; q̃ is the printed contraction introducing the following relative clause.
[c1-l042 >] *poem no fato. ¶* Nini motanu. *Não*
[c1-l042 note] The identifying mark is placed on the baggage. Ni ni motanu introduces the figurative expression explained below as taking no notice or concern.
[c1-l043 >] *fazer caſo, ou não ſe lhe dar nada.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes the negative translation of ni ni motanu: not to care or make anything of it. This idiomatic gloss is distinct from physically failing to carry a load.
[c1-l044] Ni, iru, ita. *Cozer ao fogo.*
[c1-l044 note] Ni, niru, nita is the verb for cooking or boiling over a fire. The abbreviated principal parts are preserved rather than expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l045] Ni, iru, ita. *Ser ſemelhante, ou aßemelharſe.*
[c1-l045 note] This homographic verb means resembling or being similar, not cooking. The scan supports Ni and the full past ending ita, and recovers the Portuguese resemblance gloss from the severely garbled OCR; its example continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *¶* Voyani nita co. *Filho que ſe parece cõ*

[c2-l002 >] *ſeu pay, ou mãy.*
[c2-l002 note] The parent may be the child's father or mother. This continuation completes the resemblance example rather than starting the following plant entry.
[c2-l003] Niai. *Erua como anil de tingir dazul.*
[c2-l003 note] Niai names an herb compared with indigo and used for dyeing blue. The gloss provides the dyeing function but does not by itself establish a precise modern botanical identification.
[c2-l004] Niai, ǒ, yǒta. *Quadrar, ou ſer conueniente.*
[c2-l004 note] Niai and its principal forms mean fitting, suiting, or being appropriate. This verbal entry is distinct from the identically spelled plant name above.
[c2-l005] Niaino. *Couſa que quadra, ou he conueniente.*
[c2-l005 note] Niai no describes something that fits or is suitable. The Portuguese restates the adjectival use of the preceding verb.
[c2-l006] Nibe. *Peixe coruina.*
[c2-l006 note] Nibe is a fish compared with corvina. The historical Portuguese u/v spelling is retained, without claiming that the comparison determines a modern species.
[c2-l007] Nibe. *Certo grude forte com que pegão as ca-*
[c2-l007 note] A separate nibe entry denotes a strong adhesive. Its use in fastening the bow's cane components continues onto the next row.
[c2-l008 >] *nas do arco.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes canas do arco, the cane or bamboo parts of the bow. The short continuation explains the adhesive's application.
[c2-l009] Nibuguiǒ. *Homem que tem cuidado do fato.*
[c2-l009 note] Nibugyoo is the person responsible for baggage. The gloss expresses an assigned responsibility, not merely someone carrying an individual load.
[c2-l010] Nibui. *Couſa vagaroſa, ou tardia. ¶* Ni-
[c2-l010 note] Nibui describes slowness or sluggishness. The first example starts with the divided adjective Ni-bui and applies it to a horse.
[c2-l011 >] bui vma. *Caualo vagaroſo. ¶* Nibui
[c2-l011 note] Nibui uma is a slow horse. The following repetition of Nibui begins the example concerning a person on the next row.
[c2-l012 >] fito. *Homem vagaroſo, ou homem rude, &*
[c2-l012 note] Nibui hito is a slow person, also explained as someone intellectually dull. The second Portuguese characterization continues below.
[c2-l013 >] *tardo de engenho.*
[c2-l013 note] Tardo de engenho specifies slowness of understanding. It completes the human example and is not a further Japanese form.
[c2-l014 >] Nibǔ. Nibuſa.
[c2-l014 note] Nibuu and nibusa supply the adverbial and nominal forms associated with nibui. They are printed without a fresh Portuguese definition.
[c2-l015] Nicai. *Sobrado. ¶* Nicaizzucurino iye.
[c2-l015 note] Nikai is an upper storey. Nikaizukuri no iye illustrates a house built with an upper floor; the doubled z sequence is retained.
[c2-l016 >] *Caſa feita de ſobrado.*
[c2-l016 note] Translates the preceding house example as a house constructed with an upper storey. This is the building sense of sobrado.
[c2-l017] Nicaua. *Colla que ſe faz de pelle de vaca co-*
[c2-l017 note] Nikawa is glue made from cowhide. The adjective describing the hide is divided co-zida across the line break.
[c2-l018 >] *zida.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes cozida: the cowhide is boiled to make the glue. This one-word continuation remains a physical body line.
[c2-l019] Nicchǔ. *Ao meo dia.*
[c2-l019 note] Nicchuu means at midday. The Portuguese retains the historical meo spelling, and the Japanese u bears a downward mark.
[c2-l020] Nicco. l, niccoto. *Adu. Modo de ſe ſurrir.*
[c2-l020 note] Nikko and nikkoto denote a manner of smiling. The scan shows two c types in Nicco, unlike OCR Nico; Latin l introduces the alternative adverbial form.
[c2-l021] Niccorito. *Idem.*
[c2-l021 note] Nikkorito is another smiling expression. Idem refers back to the adverbial description in the preceding entry.
[c2-l022] Nichiguat. Fi, tçuqi. *Sol, & lũa.*
[c2-l022 note] Nichigwat is paraphrased by hi and tsuki, sun and moon. The final t is the historical checked ending, not an omitted modern syllable to restore.
[c2-l023] Nichiguen. Fino caguiri. *O determinar dia*
[c2-l023 note] Nichigen, explained as hi no kagiri, concerns fixing a day or time limit. The Portuguese construction continues with certo below.
[c2-l024 >] *certo. ¶* Nichiguenuo ſadamuru, l, ſa-
[c2-l024 note] Nichigen wo sadamuru, alternatively sasu, means setting a definite day. The second verb is visibly divided at the right edge.
[c2-l025 >] ſu. *Determinar, ou apontar algũ dia certo.*
[c2-l025 note] Completes sasu and translates both alternatives as determining or appointing a particular day. The contracted algũ is not expanded.
[c2-l026] Nichi nichi. i, Fibini. *Cada dia.*
[c2-l026 note] Nichi nichi is equated with hibi ni, every day. The scan's separate Nichi nichi is preserved rather than joined as a normalized compound.
[c2-l027] Nichirin. *Sol.*
[c2-l027 note] Nichirin denotes the sun, literally the solar disc in the Japanese compound. The printed gloss itself is only Sol.
[c2-l028] Nichiya. *Dia, & noite. Vt,* Nichiyano
[c2-l028 note] Nichiya means day and night. Nichiya no begins a quotation about continuous vigilance on the following row.
[c2-l029 >] yôjin fima nacariqereba, &c. Taif. *Lib.*
[c2-l029 note] Yoojin hima nakarikereba describes there being no respite from vigilance by day and night. Taif. is the upright citation label; Lib. begins the italic book reference continued below.
[c2-l030 >] *29. Não fazião ſe não vigiar de di, a &*
[c2-l030 note] Book 29 is followed by a translation saying that they did nothing but keep watch. The intrusive comma in printed di, a is retained as an apparent typographical anomaly within the intended word dia, not silently moved to modern punctuation.
[c2-l031 >] *de noite.*
[c2-l031 note] De noite completes the day-and-night vigilance translation. This short continuation closes the quotation.
[c2-l032] Nichiyô. *Influencia do ſol.*
[c2-l032 note] Nichiyoo is here defined as the influence of the sun. The circumflex distinguishes the printed mark from the carons in nearby entries; this is not the preceding nichiya word.
[c2-l033] Nichǔ. Mimino vchi. *Dentro nos ouuidos.*
[c2-l033 note] Nichuu is paraphrased by mimi no uchi, inside the ears. The Japanese explanation and Portuguese gloss agree on location within the ear, despite possible modern expectations about the compound's reading.
[c2-l034] Nicô. *Molher rapada.*
[c2-l034 note] Nikoo is a shaven woman, in the religious context a tonsured woman. The gloss uses the historical Molher spelling and does not supply a separate institutional title.
[c2-l035] Nicobu. *Certos limos, ou folhas largas do mar*
[c2-l035 note] Nikobu denotes a preparation of broad seaweed fronds. The final word cozidas on the next row establishes that they are cooked, rather than naming a raw plant alone.
[c2-l036 >] *cozidas.*
[c2-l036 note] Cozidas completes the seaweed definition: the broad marine leaves are boiled or cooked. It is a genuine one-word body continuation.
[c2-l037] Nicobu. *Callo, ou polmão q̃ ſe faz ao derredor*
[c2-l037 note] This separate nikobu is a callus or swelling. The historical Portuguese polmão is retained as printed; the anatomical location and cause follow below.
[c2-l038 >] *do peſcoço aos que carretão fato.*
[c2-l038 note] The swelling forms around the neck of people who carry baggage. This identifies a load-bearing callus, not the cooked-seaweed homograph above.
[c2-l039] Nicon. Mimino ne. *Potencia de ouuir, ou ou-*
[c2-l039 note] Nikon is explained by mimi no ne and denotes the faculty of hearing or the ears themselves. The final syllable of ouuidos is divided across the next row; the Japanese compound is not emended from its damaged n shapes.
[c2-l040 >] *uidos. S.*
[c2-l040 note] Completes ouuidos, ears. The closing S. is a separate upright written-register label, not part of the italic Portuguese noun. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l041] Niconicoto. *Adu. Modo deſe ſurrir. Vt,*
[c2-l041 note] Nikonikoto is an adverb describing smiling. The Portuguese gives the manner and introduces a verbal example below; the close-set deſe is retained.
[c2-l042 >] Niconicoto varǒ.
[c2-l042 note] Nikonikoto waroo means smiling or laughing with a cheerful expression. The final Japanese vowel has a caron in the scan.
[c2-l043] Niconicoto xite. *Idem. Vt,* Niconicoto
[c2-l043 note] Nikonikoto shite is another construction with the same smiling sense. The example begins here and supplies the following verb on the next row.
[c2-l044 >] xite iru. *Estarſe ſurrindo.*
[c2-l044 note] Nikonikoto shite iru describes being or remaining smiling. Portuguese estar-se sorrindo translates that continuing state; the historical ſurrindo spelling is preserved.
[c2-l045] Nicoyacana. *Couſa alegre. Vt,* Nicoyaca-
[c2-l045 note] Nikoyakana describes something cheerful. The example repeats the adjective across a visible line-end division before hito below.
[c2-l046 >] na fito. *Homem alegre, ou riſonho.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes nikoyakana hito, a cheerful or smiling person. The explanatory Portuguese riſonho is retained with its visible long s.
[c2-l047] Nicoyacani. *Adu.*
[c2-l047 note] Nikoyakani is the corresponding adverbial form. Adu. supplies only its grammatical classification, relying on the preceding cheerful-person entry for meaning.
