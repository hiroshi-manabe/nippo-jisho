[c1-l004 note] The saying applies to people, whose standing depends on how others value them. Fa- continues as fazem below; the abbreviation tildes in homẽs and quãto remain unexpanded.
[c1-l005 >] *zem nelles. ¶ Item,* Mochijru. *Co-*

[c1-l008] Mochino qi. *Aruore de cuja caſca piſada, fa-*
[c1-l008 note] Mochi no ki is a tree whose crushed bark supplies an adhesive. The description continues below; it does not itself justify imposing a modern botanical species name.
[c1-l009 >] *zem grude, ou viſco.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes fazem and identifies the product as glue or birdlime. This connects the tree-name above with its practical use.
[c1-l010] Mochiron. *Couſa clara, & aueriguada.*

[c1-l011] Mochitçuqe, uru, eta. *Ter por cuſtume, ou ha*
[c1-l011 note] Mochitsuke means having something as a habit or customary practice. The divided Portuguese habito has no clearly printed terminal hyphen here, so the baseline's bare ha is retained.
[c1-l012 >] *bito. Vt,* Iẽninye mochitçuqetaru fucai-
[c1-l012 note] Completes habito and begins an example of the deep devotion one has customarily held toward the saints. The scan supports fucai-, corresponding to fukai, rather than OCR ſucai-; Iẽninye is retained as printed, with consonantal initial I.
[c1-l013 >] xinjinniua vocotarazu. Mon. *Na de-*
[c1-l013 note] Shinjin ni wa okotarazu completes the instruction not to neglect devotion. Mon. supplies the citation; de- begins the Portuguese noun continued on the next physical line.
[c1-l014 >] *uação grande, que cuſtumastes ter aos ſanctos*
[c1-l014 note] The Portuguese specifies the great devotion customarily held toward the saints. The printed de-/uação spelling and cuſtumastes are preserved rather than modernized.
[c1-l015 >] *não faltando.*
[c1-l015 note] Not failing in that devotion completes the Portuguese construction and explains Japanese vocotarazu. This short continuation remains a separate physical row.
[c1-l016] Mochitçure, ruru, eta. *Leuarem muitos em*
[c1-l016 note] Mochitsure describes several people carrying things together. The principal form is visibly ruru, not OCR turu, matching the verb's inflection.
[c1-l017 >] *companhia algũa couſa. ¶* Cono fitobito-
[c1-l017 note] Completes the definition of carrying something in company, then begins these people in the Japanese example. The final hyphen belongs to the physical division of fitobitoga.
[c1-l018 >] ga vchigatana, maſacariuo mochitçurete
[c1-l018 note] These people carry uchigatana swords and masakari axes together. The case particle ga continues the preceding line, and the sentence proceeds to their disorderly entry below.
[c1-l019 >] midare iru. Voi. *Eſtes homẽs leuando Ca-*
[c1-l019 note] Midare iru describes entering in a disorderly crowd. Voi. is the upright citation label; the Portuguese begins the same account and divides Catanas across the line boundary.
[c1-l020 >] *tanas, & machados entrarão de tropel.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes Catanas and says that the sword- and axe-bearing men entered in a throng. The printed entrarão is retained in this past narrative context.
[c1-l021] Mochitçutaye, uru, eta. *Ter algũa couſa*
[c1-l021 note] Mochitsutae refers to holding or transmitting something over time. The following rows clarify inheritance through successive generations rather than a single act of carrying.
[c1-l022 >] *por tempos, ou dedução de idades. ¶* Mo-
[c1-l022 note] The possession extends through a succession of ages or generations. Mo- starts the Japanese example of an inherited household object on the following line.
[c1-l023 >] chitçutayeta vaga iyeno giǔdai, &c. *Cou*
[c1-l023 note] Completes mochitsutayeta and refers to the ancestral possession of one's house. Giǔdai identifies the heirloom or hereditary possession; the printed gi spelling and unhyphenated Portuguese Cou are retained.
[c1-l024 >] *ſa, ou peça que de muito tempo, & idades per*
[c1-l024 note] Completes cousa and describes an object held over a long period through successive generations. The scan reads per at the line end, not OCR por.
[c1-l025 >] *dedução hum tem em ſua caſa.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes the explanation of an object preserved in one's household through a succession of generations. Dedução here concerns transmission, not a logical deduction.
[c1-l026] Mochizzuqi. *Lũa chea.*
[c1-l026 note] Mochizuki is the full moon. The scan contains two successive z letters, Mochizzuqi, which the OCR reduced to one; the printed nasal abbreviation in Lũa is preserved.
[c1-l027] Mochiya. *Caſa onde ſe vend,em ou fazem*
[c1-l027 note] Mochiya is a shop where mochi are sold or made. The anomalous comma inside vend,em is visible in the print and is retained rather than moved to produce modern vendem, ou.
[c1-l028 >] *Mochis.*
[c1-l028 note] Mochis completes the Portuguese definition of the shop, using the Japanese food-name with a Portuguese plural ending. Its italic setting is retained; it is an embedded term, not a new headword.
[c1-l029] Mochiyori, u, otta. *Trazẽdo chegarſe. Vt,*
[c1-l029 note] Mochiyori means approaching while bringing something. The principal forms anticipate mochiyotta in the example; the Portuguese Trazẽdo keeps its printed abbreviation.
[c1-l030 >] Xujuno ſaqe, ſacanauo mochiyotta. Tac.
[c1-l030 note] The example describes arriving with various kinds of sake and food. Enlargement supports mochiyotta with two t letters, consistent with otta above, rather than OCR mochiyota; Tac. is the citation.
[c1-l031 >] *Trazendo muito vinho, & varios comeres*
[c1-l031 note] The Portuguese translates the goods brought as much wine and various foods. Comeres is the historical substantive plural for things to eat.
[c1-l032 >] *chegouſe.*
[c1-l032 note] Arrived or approached completes the translation of mochiyotta. This short verb is a genuine continuation of the preceding row.
[c1-l033] Mǒco. Taqeqi tora. *Tigre feroz, ou cru-*
[c1-l033 note] Mōko is a fierce tiger, paraphrased in Japanese as takeki tora. The caron over o differs from the circumflex in Môcot below and is preserved independently.
[c1-l034 >] *el.* S.
[c1-l034 note] Completes cruel, the second description of the fierce tiger. The upright S. marks written usage and is not part of the italic Portuguese adjective.
[c1-l035] Môcot. Qe, fone. *Cabelos, & oſſos.* S.
[c1-l035 note] Mōkotsu denotes hair and bones, restated as ke and hone. The final t represents the checked Sino-Japanese ending; the circumflex and upright written-register label are retained.
[c1-l036] Mocqe. *Couſa de deſgraça, ou que ſocedeo mal,*
[c1-l036 note] Mokke describes something unfortunate or ill-fated in this gloss. The historical form ſocedeo is retained; the following adjective adds that it is hard to endure.
[c1-l037 >] *inſofriuel, &c.*
[c1-l037 note] Insufferable completes the preceding description of something unfortunate. The old spelling inſofriuel remains literal rather than being normalized to a modern Portuguese form.
[c1-l038] Mocqena. *Idem.*
[c1-l038 note] Mokkena gives an adjectival form with the same sense as Mocqe. Idem refers to the immediately preceding definition, not to an omitted gloss.
[c1-l039] Mocqua. *Certa mezinha.*
[c1-l039 note] Mokkwa is identified only as a particular medicinal substance. The brief definition is retained without supplying an unprinted recipe or botanical identification.
[c1-l040] Mocquai. *O duuidar, ou ſoſpeitar dalguẽ.* S.
[c1-l040 note] Mokkai means doubting or suspecting someone in this written-register entry. The tilde in dalguẽ is an unexpanded abbreviation; S. is upright.
[c1-l041] Mocu. *Silencio. Vt,* Mocuſuru. *Calarſe.*
[c1-l041 note] Moku is silence; moku suru is to remain or become silent. The example stays in roman type between the italic Portuguese glosses.
[c1-l042] Mocu. Qino me. *Veas do pao, ou madeira.*
[c1-l042 note] This separate moku entry means the grain or veins of wood, paraphrased as ki no me. It is unrelated to the preceding silence sense despite identical romanization.
[c1-l043] Mocuba. Qino vma. *Caualo de pao.*
[c1-l043 note] Mokuba is a wooden horse, literally ki no uma. Initial v in vma represents the printed positional spelling of the vowel, not a consonantal modern v.
[c1-l044] Mocubut. Qibotoqe. *Idolo de pao.*
[c1-l044 note] Mokubutsu is a wooden Buddhist image, paraphrased as kibotoke. The scan supports Qibotoqe with t, correcting OCR Qiboroqe; the Portuguese calls it an idol of wood.
[c1-l045] Mocudai. i, Mexiro. *Official q̃ tem per officio*
[c1-l045 note] Mokudai, equated with meshiro, is an official charged with supervision. The comma after i and printed per are retained; q̃ abbreviates the relative word without expansion.
[c1-l046 >] *olhar, & vigiar pello q̃ lhe encarregão, como*
[c1-l046 note] The official must watch over what is entrusted to him. The scan includes lhe, not OCR he, and the plural verbal form encarregão is preserved.
[c1-l047 >] *por algũ lugar, pouoação, &c. ¶ Item, Nas*
[c1-l047 note] A place or settlement is an example of the official's charge. Nas begins a further explanation continued at the top of column 2; OCR's fragmented type runs and Iiem are corrected to the continuous italic Item, Nas.
