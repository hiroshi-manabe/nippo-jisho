[c1-l001] Rogan. *Pato brauo que eſta ẽtre os caniços.* S.
[c1-l001 note] Rogan is a wild duck living among reeds, marked as written usage by upright S. This headword confirms that the preceding page's bottom Rogan is a catchword, not extra body text.
[c1-l002] Rǒgan. Voitaru cauobaxe. *Roſto de ve-*
[c1-l002 note] This separate Rǒgan means an aged face, explicitly explained by Voitaru cauobaxe. The Portuguese velho is divided after ve-; it is not the duck-name definition above.
[c1-l003 >] *lho.* S.
[c1-l003 note] Completes the old-face gloss and supplies the written-register label S. The label is upright, unlike the Portuguese adjective fragment.
[c1-l004] Rǒguet. i, Xiuaſu. *Derradeiro mes, ou lũa*
[c1-l004 note] Rǒguet denotes the last month of the Japanese year, equated with Xiuaſu. The second pass resolves the small vowel mark as a caron, unlike the circumflex of the following hazy-moon entry; the gloss reads mes, not the OCR mer.
[c1-l005 >] *do anno de Iapão.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes the description of the final month of the Japanese year. Iapão is Portuguese typography, not a Japanese term for whole-word toggling.
[c1-l006] Rôguet. Voborozzuqi. *Lũa dantre nuuens*
[c1-l006 note] This homophonous entry concerns a hazy moon among clouds. The Japanese reads Voborozzuqi, with r and doubled z, and the Portuguese reads Lũa dantre nuuens rather than the garbled OCR sequence.
[c1-l007 >] *que eſcaſamente parece.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes the hazy-moon definition: it is only faintly visible. The clause explains the obscuration by clouds in the previous row.
[c1-l008] Rǒguet. Fogaracana tçuqi. *Lũa clara, &*
[c1-l008 note] Rǒguet is a bright moon, paraphrased by Fogaracana tçuqi. The visible caron distinguishes it from the immediately preceding hazy-moon entry, which has a circumflex.
[c1-l009 >] *reſplandecente.*
[c1-l009 note] Adds resplendent to the preceding bright-moon gloss. The whole word is a physical continuation and is kept on its own row.
[c1-l010] Rôgui. Qera, ari. *Bicho chamado* Q era, *&*
[c1-l010 note] Rôgui names the creatures Qera and ari; the Portuguese identifies the former by name and continues with ant below. The conspicuous gap in embedded Q era is retained rather than silently closing the supplied reading.
[c1-l011 >] *formiga.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes the pair with ant, translating ari. This short row belongs to the preceding creature-name entry.
[c1-l012] Roguio. Suzuqino vuo. *Peixe aßi chamado.*
[c1-l012 note] Roguio is the fish described in Japanese as Suzuqino vuo. The Portuguese merely says a fish so called; no additional species identification is imposed.
[c1-l013] Roguiǔ. Roba, vxi. *Aſno, & boy.*
[c1-l013 note] Roguiǔ combines donkey and ox, explicitly listed as Roba and vxi. The initial o is unmarked, unlike the old-cattle entry below.

[c1-l015] Rǒgo. Voite nochi. *Depois de ſer velho.*
[c1-l015 note] Rǒgo refers to the period after becoming old, as Voite nochi explains. The separate words de ſer are restored from the OCR's fused form.

[c1-l041] Rôju. i, Voiqi. *Aruore velha.*

[c1-l042] Rǒjǔ. i, Rǒdô. *Criados de caſa dalgi ſenhor.*
[c1-l042 note] Rǒjǔ is equated with Rǒdô and glossed as household servants of a lord. [c1-l044] Romei. Tçuyuno inochi. *Vida breue.*
[c1-l044 note] Romei is a brief life, figuratively explained as the life of dew by Tçuyuno inochi. The headword is upright throughout.
[c1-l045] Rǒmô. Voi foruru. *Velho deſtempado, ou*

[c1-l047 >] mô ſuru.
[c1-l047 note] Completes the example begun as Rǒ on the preceding row: becoming senile or decrepit. The opening mô is a continuation, not a new headword; the preceding dot-like mark is preserved without normalizing the divided form.
