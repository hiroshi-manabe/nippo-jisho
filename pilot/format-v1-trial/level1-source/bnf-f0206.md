[c1-l001] Focqet. Dairino coto. *Paços reais, que es-*
[c1-l001 note] Focqet is hokket-, a checked-syllable form associated with the northern palace; Dairino coto explicitly identifies the imperial palace. The scan supports the missing i in Dairino, while Paços reais begins the Portuguese explanation of the royal residence.
[c1-l002 >] *tão da parte do Norte.*
[c1-l002 note] This completes estão and locates the royal buildings on the northern side. The physical continuation is kept separate from the next sect-name entry.
[c1-l003] Focqexǔ. *Os daſeita de Focque.*
[c1-l003 note] Focqexǔ is Hokke-shū, the Lotus sect; the definition names its adherents. Focque is an embedded Japanese name, but enlargement supports the OCR's italic styling here rather than an automatic conversion to upright type.
[c1-l004] Focqi. i, Cocorouo vocoſu. *Excitarſe, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Focqi is hokki, arousing an intention; cocoro o okosu is the explanatory Japanese phrase. The sense and the stem require vocoſu, not OCR vocofu, and the scan supports the long s rather than f.
[c1-l005 >] *perſuadirſe deſi a fazer algũa couſa. ¶* Foc-
[c1-l005 note] Persuadir-se de si a fazer means moving oneself to do something. The scan separates deſi from a; Foc- begins the next example across the physical line break.
[c1-l006 >] qi ſuru. *Idem. ¶* Cocoroga focqi
[c1-l006 note] Qi completes Focqi suru, to arouse an intention. Cocoroga focqi then begins an example in which the heart awakens to a religious aspiration.
[c1-l007 >] xite goxǒuo negǒ. *Eſpertarſe em deua-*
[c1-l007 note] Xite goxǒuo negǒ is shite goshō o negō, aspiring to one's afterlife or salvation. The Portuguese starts the devotional explanation; deua- is retained as printed rather than normalized to a modern spelling.
[c1-l008 >] *ção, ou deſejo da ſaluação porſi, & de ſua*
[c1-l008 note] The gloss specifies devotion or desire for salvation, arising by oneself and of one's own will. Porſi is not silently respaced to modern por si in this diplomatic text.
[c1-l009 >] *vontade.*
[c1-l009 note] Vontade closes the explanation of voluntary religious aspiration. This short continuation belongs to the example above, not the following rank-name.
[c1-l010] Focqiô. Nori faxi. *Certa dignidade.*
[c1-l010 note] Focqiô is hokkyō, a rank glossed literally as nori hashi, the bridge of the law. Its circumflex is visibly distinct from the following entry's caron; NINJAL independently lists the same two differently accented headwords.
[c1-l011] Focqiǒ. *Lei.*
[c1-l011 note] Focqiǒ is a separate law-related entry, simply glossed Lei. Its printed caron is retained; it should not inherit the circumflex of the rank immediately above.
[c1-l012] Focumen. *O eſtar virado perà parte do Norte*
[c1-l012 note] Focumen is hokumen, facing north. Portuguese o estar virado describes an orientation or posture, with the following lines explaining its court context.
[c1-l013 >] *como coſtumão eſtar os que eſtão diante do Rei,*
[c1-l013 note] The north-facing people stand before the king. The clause describes customary court arrangement rather than a geographic group called Hokumen.
[c1-l014 >] *ficando elle virado pera aparte do Sul, que he,*
[c1-l014 note] The king himself faces south, contrasting with those facing him. The printed spacing of aparte is retained rather than regularized from Portuguese grammar alone.
[c1-l015 >] *Nanmen. ¶* Focumenno ſaburai. *Fi-*
[c1-l015 note] Nanmen is nanmen, facing south, and closes the preceding explanation. Focumenno saburai is hokumen no samurai, the attendants facing north. Enlargement on the second pass supports italic Nanmen, unlike the upright Japanese example following it.
[c1-l016 >] *dalgos que eſtão deſta maneira virados pera o*
[c1-l016 note] Fi- from the preceding line completes Fidalgos. The definition identifies the people standing in this orientation, not another sense of the verb to face.
[c1-l017 >] *Norte diante do Rei.*
[c1-l017 note] Norte diante do Rei closes the description: facing north before the king. The short line remains a physical continuation.
[c1-l018] Focumon. Qitano mon. *Porta da rua pera*
[c1-l018 note] Focumon is hokumon, explained as kita no mon, the north gate. Portuguese Porta da rua identifies a gate or outer doorway facing north.
[c1-l019 >] *o Norte.*
[c1-l019 note] O Norte completes the direction of the gate. There is no separate headword in this short continuation.
[c1-l020] Focunan. i, Qita, minami. *Norte, Sul.*
[c1-l020 note] Focunan is hokunan, north and south; kita and minami are its native Japanese equivalents. The Portuguese mirrors the same pair of directions.
[c1-l021] Focuro, *Sinal preto do roſto.*
[c1-l021 note] Focuro is hokuro, a mole or dark facial mark. Sinal preto do rosto gives the physical sense, not a figurative sign.
[c1-l022] Focurocu. *Caminho por terra pera aparte do*
[c1-l022 note] Focurocu names a land route toward the north. The following line supplies the reference point, so the gloss should not be read as a universal compass definition alone.
[c1-l023 >] *Norte em reſpeito de Miaco.*
[c1-l023 note] Miaco is the capital, the reference point for this northern land route. Enlargement on the second pass supports italic type here; being a Japanese name does not itself require upright type.
[c1-l024] Focurocudǒ. *Idem.*
[c1-l024 note] Focurocudǒ is Hokurikudō, referred back to the same northern route by Idem. The historical romanization and long-vowel mark remain unmodernized.
[c1-l025] Focuſan. Qitano yama. *Montes da parte*
[c1-l025 note] Focuſan is hokusan, northern mountains, explicitly kita no yama. The Portuguese plural Montes matches that geographic description; the headword's long s is not normalized from NINJAL's plain s.
[c1-l026 >] *do Norte.*
[c1-l026 note] Do Norte completes the definition of northern mountains. This short line does not add a separate lexical sense.
[c1-l027] Focuto. Qitano foxi. *Eſtrelado Norte.*
[c1-l027 note] Focuto is hokuto, with kita no hoshi as its gloss. The dictionary calls it the northern star; that wording is preserved even though modern Hokuto often evokes the Northern Dipper.
[c1-l028] Focuxei. Qitano foxi. *Idem.*
[c1-l028 note] Focuxei is hokusei, again glossed kita no hoshi. Idem refers to the preceding northern-star explanation rather than to the mountain entry above it.
[c1-l029] Focuxin. Qitanofoxi. *Idem.*
[c1-l029 note] Focuxin is hokushin, the northern celestial pole or star. Qitanofoxi is printed joined here; its lexical identity does not justify inserting a space absent from this line.
[c1-l030] Foda. *Pee, ou perna. B.*
[c1-l030 note] Foda denotes a foot or leg in this register, according to Pee, ou perna. The B. label is retained without guessing a more specific social or textual expansion here.
[c1-l031] Fôdai. *Vt,* Sonata fôdai. *Como quiſer-*
[c1-l031 note] Sonata fôdai means as you please or according to your wishes. The gloss begins Como quiserdes; the circumflex forms are retained independently of the different headword below.
[c1-l032 >] *des, ou for voſſa vontade.*
[c1-l032 note] Des completes quiserdes, followed by ou for vossa vontade, or as is your will. This closes the example of freedom to choose.
[c1-l033] Fǒdai. *Baixeza em couſas de maa criação, ou*
[c1-l033 note] Fǒdai here concerns low manners or bad upbringing. The printed caron distinguishes this heading from the preceding fôdai; no attempt is made to homogenize the two entries.

[c1-l034 note] Pouca policia refers to poor refinement or civility. The example Fôdai xigocuna mono means a thoroughly ill-mannered person; its circumflex is visibly printed even though the heading above has a caron.
[c1-l035 >] mono. *Homem muito baixo, & vil, ou*
[c1-l035 note] Mono completes the Japanese noun phrase. Homem muito baixo, e vil explains a base or ill-mannered person, not physical shortness alone.
[c1-l036 >] *mal criado.*
[c1-l036 note] Mal criado closes the alternatives describing bad upbringing. It belongs to the same example, not to the next adjectival heading.
[c1-l037] Fǒdaina. *Peſſoa vil nos cuſtumes, & policia.*
[c1-l037 note] Fǒdaina is the adjectival form, glossed a person base in customs and civility. OCR Peſca is a damaged reading of Peſſoa. Enlargement shows two tall s forms, not the usual beta-like ligature, followed by a worn o and a.
[c1-l038] Fôdan. *Pregação da lei. ¶* Fôdanuo
[c1-l038 note] Fôdan is hōdan, preaching the religious law. Fôdanuo starts the object-plus-suru example continued below.
[c1-l039 >] ſuru. *Prègar a lei.* Apoſtolo tachiua
[c1-l039 note] Suru completes hōdan o suru, to preach. Apoſtolo tachiua begins a Japanese sentence about the apostles; the whole loanword is upright, including the l incorrectly isolated as italic by OCR.
[c1-l040 >] govoqiteuo gofôdan n. ſareta. *Os A-*
[c1-l040 note] Govoqiteuo is go-okite o, the religious ordinance as object; gofôdan introduces honorific preaching. The small printed n. is retained as an abbreviated form rather than silently expanded to ni, and ſareta supplies the honorific verb.
[c1-l041 >] *poſtolos prègarão alei de Deos.*
[c1-l041 note] The Portuguese says that the apostles preached God's law. The surviving text reads the joined alei, and Deos retains its historical spelling.
[c1-l042] Fǒdate. *Colunna, ou eſteo de pao que ſerue*
[c1-l042 note] Fǒdate is hōdate, a supporting post at a doorway. Portuguese esteo means a prop or support; the scan shows eſteo, not the OCR double-s ligature eßeo.
[c1-l043 >] *como de hũa das ombreiras da porta.*
[c1-l043 note] The post serves like one of the door jambs. Ombreiras da porta confirms the architectural sense and completes the previous line.
[c1-l044] Fodaſare, uru, eta. *Eſtar amarrado, ou pre*
[c1-l044 note] Fodaſare is hodasare-, to be bound or restrained. The Portuguese word preso crosses the line without a visible hyphen; its first part is pre, not OCR ore.
[c1-l045 >] *ſo. ¶* Miǒrini fodaſaruru. *Eſtar preſo*
[c1-l045 note] Miǒrini fodaſaruru is myōri ni hodasaruru, being bound by reputation and gain. The damaged first vowel of fodaſaruru and the missing i in Miǒri were OCR errors; the following Portuguese gloss confirms the expression.
[c1-l046 >] *de honra, & cubiça.*
[c1-l046 note] De honra, e cubiça explains the bonds of honour and covetousness. This is the figurative sense of hodasaruru, not literal fetters.
[c1-l047] Fodaxi. Axigutçu. *Guilhão, ou braga de*
[c1-l047 note] Fodaxi is hodashi, a fetter; the enlarged synonym reads Axigutçu, ashigutsu, rather than OCR Axigurçu. The damaged t precedes çu, forming tsu; it is not a long s. Guilhão and braga de pao identify a shackle, with the definition continuing into column 2.
