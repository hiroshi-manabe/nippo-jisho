[c1-l001 >] *¶* Fonegumino tçuyoi fito gia. *He ho-*
[c1-l001 note] Honegumi no tsuyoi hito ja: a person of strong build. This continues the bone-frame entry from f208; initial f represents Japanese h, not an error to modernize.
[c1-l002 >] *mem forte, & rijo. ¶* Fonegumino ta-
[c1-l002 note] Forte e rijo describes strength and toughness. The second Japanese example starts takumashii across ta-/cumaxij, so the physical break is not a lexical boundary.
[c1-l003 >] cumaxij fito. *Homem forte, & mẽbrudo.*
[c1-l003 note] Takumashii hito, a robust or muscular person, agrees with membrudo. The final ij belongs to the historical spelling of the adjective.
[c1-l004] Fônen. Yutacana toxi. *Anno fertil, & a-*
[c1-l004 note] Hōnen, a plentiful harvest year, is glossed yutakana toshi. The Portuguese abundant adjective begins with a- here and continues on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] *bundante. Vt,* Cotoxiua fônengia. *Eſ-*
[c1-l005 note] Kotoshi wa hōnen ja says that this is a fruitful year. Read bundante to complete abundante, not the OCR's bandante; the next definition begins Es-/te.
[c1-l006 >] *te anno he proſpero, & de nouidade.*
[c1-l006 note] This year is prosperous and productive. The scan supports prospero rather than prostero; nouidade here concerns produce or the harvest, not simply a modern novelty.
[c1-l007] Fonenami. *Feição dos oßos. Vt,* Xixiai
[c1-l007 note] Honenami concerns the arrangement of bones; shishiai introduces flesh or bodily conformation in the horse example. The definition is anatomical rather than a reference to literal waves.
[c1-l008 >] fonenami, yomeno fuxiua tçucuri tçuqe-
[c1-l008 note] The example continues the description of a horse's conformation. I retain the printed yomeno fuxi rather than guessing a modern anatomical expression; its exact lexical interpretation remains uncertain.
[c1-l009 >] taru gotocu nari. *He caualo bẽ proporciona-*
[c1-l009 note] Tsukuri-tsuketaru gotoku nari compares the horse to something perfectly fashioned. Portuguese proporcionado crosses the line; the internal OCR space in proporciona- is unsupported.
[c1-l010 >] *do, & de boas feiçoẽs como ſe o fizerão ao tor*
[c1-l010 note] As if made on a lathe: como se o fizerão ao torno. The magnified scan supports se and fizerão; retain the historical verb ending rather than modernizing its syntax.
[c1-l011 >] *no como hum deſeja.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes torno and the comparison with the shape one desires. There is no reason to create a Japanese word from this short Portuguese continuation.
[c1-l012] Fone tçugai. *Iunturas dos oßos.*
[c1-l012 note] Hone-tsugai, joints of the bones. The plural masculine ossos fits both the scan and juntas; oßas was an OCR vowel error.
[c1-l013] Fongacu Bup. *Ser, ou ſuſtancia do primeiro*
[c1-l013 note] Hongaku, Buddhist original enlightenment, is represented here through a missionary description of primordial substance. Bup. is an upright Buddhist vocabulary label, not part of the headword.
[c1-l014 >] *principio, ou Fotoque ãtes deſe miſturar no cor-*
[c1-l014 note] The gloss describes a Fotoque before entering bodily life. Principio, Fotoque and ãtes are visible; misturar contains a t, not an ss ligature. These are transcription repairs, not an endorsement of the theological description.
[c1-l015 >] *po, & viuer, &c.*
[c1-l015 note] Cor-/po completes corpo, followed by living. The physical line belongs to the preceding Buddhist definition.
[c1-l016] Fongi. Bup. *Primeira ſubſtancia, ou Fotoque*
[c1-l016 note] Honji, the original ground or substance, is another Buddhist term. The damaged label is Bup., not Buo.; Fotoque remains italic as printed in this explanation.
[c1-l017 >] *antes de ſe fazer Cami & ſer venerado nalgũa*
[c1-l017 note] The original being precedes its manifestation as a kami and its veneration in a shrine. Preserve the close printed sefazer rather than silently regularizing Portuguese spacing.
[c1-l018 >] *ermida. ¶* Fongi ſuixacu. *Idem.*
[c1-l018 note] Honji suijaku, 本地垂迹, identifies the doctrine behind the preceding explanation. Suixacu is a historically intelligible spelling; Idem refers back to that gloss.
[c1-l019] Fongin. Fonno gin. *Proprio exercito, onde*
[c1-l019 note] Honjin, the principal army or headquarters, is analyzed as hon no jin. Unlike the preceding honji entry, this is a military rather than Buddhist definition.
[c1-l020 >] *eſta o principal capitaõ, ou general.*
[c1-l020 note] The chief captain or general is stationed there. Principal replaces prencipal; the printed accent is retained on capitão after comparison with the visible word.
[c1-l021] Fongo. *Texto, ou autoridade de algum texto.*
[c1-l021 note] Hongo here denotes an authoritative text or quotation. This sense must be kept distinct from the homograph in the next entry.
[c1-l022 >] *Vt,* Fongouo fiqu. *Alegar texto, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Hongo wo hiku means citing a text. Fiqu corresponds to hiku, and alegar is adducing authority rather than merely making an allegation.
[c1-l023] Fongo. *Vide* Fôgu *: porque aindaque ſe eſcreue*
[c1-l023 note] The second Fongo points to Fôgu, scrap paper. The magnified cross-reference reads Fôgu, not Fiǒgu; the Portuguese explicitly introduces a spelling/pronunciation distinction.
[c1-l024 >] *aßi pronunciaſe* Fôgu*, papeis borrados, &c.*
[c1-l024 note] Although written fongo, it is pronounced fōgu. The whole repeated Japanese word is upright, and the scan shows aßi and pronunciaſe. Keep the printed papeisborrados spacing.
[c1-l025] Fongocu. Motono cuni. *Proprio Reino*
[c1-l025 note] Hongoku, one's original country, is explained by moto no kuni. The Portuguese kingdom is one's own or native realm in this context.
[c1-l026 >] *onde hum naceo.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes the native-country definition: where someone was born. Naceo is retained as printed rather than updated to modern nasceu.
[c1-l027] Fonguai. *Comprazerſe, & alegrarſe. S.*
[c1-l027 note] Hongwai, 本懐, satisfaction or the fulfilment of a wish. The historical labialized spelling is meaningful; italic S. belongs to the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l028 >] *Vt,* Fonguaini zonji ſoro. *Alegro me,*
[c1-l028 note] Hongwai ni zonji sōrō expresses pleasure or satisfaction. The scan supports Fonguaini, not Fonguami; ni is the particle required by the construction.
[c1-l029 >] *& folgo muito. ¶ Item, Deſejo, ou von-*
[c1-l029 note] The speaker rejoices greatly. Item then introduces the related nominal sense of desire or wish, with vontade divided across rows.
[c1-l030 >] *tade. Vt,* Fonguaini canǒ. *Comprir os*
[c1-l030 note] Hongwai ni kanō, to have one's wishes fulfilled. Canǒ is the historical contracted form corresponding to kanau; the Portuguese explicitly confirms fulfilment.
[c1-l031 >] *deſejos.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the Portuguese object, desires. This is a continuation, not a new entry or a heading.
[c1-l032] Fonguan. *Proprio voto como o de Amida.*
[c1-l032 note] Hongwan, 本願, the original vow, exemplified by Amida's. Amida is part of the italic Portuguese explanation here.
[c1-l033 >] *¶ Item, Aliquando, O que edifica, ou*
[c1-l033 note] Aliquando introduces another occasional sense: the person who builds or founds something. It is Latin explanatory vocabulary, not a Japanese reading.
[c1-l034 >] *funda algũa Tera à ſua cuſta principalmente,*
[c1-l034 note] Someone founds a tera at their own expense. Read funda and sua, not fanda and sus; the borrowed temple word stays in the printed italic setting.
[c1-l035 >] *posto que a propria palaura he,* Fonguannin.
[c1-l035 note] The writer qualifies this usage: the more proper term is hongwannin, 本願人. This is why the same longer headword receives a cross-reference below.
[c1-l036 >] *¶ Item,* Fonguan. *Superior primeiro que*
[c1-l036 note] A further sense denotes the first superior of a temple. Fonguan is repeated as the word under discussion, not a new independent heading.
[c1-l037 >] *edificou algũa Tera, ou algum ſeu ſocceſor q̃*
[c1-l037 note] The founding superior or a successor who governs the temple. Preserve the historical soccesor-type spelling rather than substituting modern sucessor.
[c1-l038 >] *gouerna em ſeu lugar.*
[c1-l038 note] The successor governs in the founder's place. The short continuation completes the preceding relative clause.
[c1-l039] Fonguannin. *Vide,* Fonguan.
[c1-l039 note] Hongwannin refers back to hongwan. The nn spelling is compatible with gw an plus nin; it is not an accidental duplicated consonant to remove.
[c1-l040] Fonguen. Moto, minamoto. *Principio.*
[c1-l040 note] Honguen, 本源, source or origin, is explained by moto and minamoto. These Japanese equivalents agree directly with principio.
[c1-l041] Fongueqi. *Traição. ¶* Fongueqiuo vo-
[c1-l041 note] Hongeki denotes rebellion or treachery. The example ends in vokosu on the next row. The connector in Fongueqino is worn and retained after enlargement; an expected accusative -uo is not enough by itself to replace it.
[c1-l042 >] coſu. *Armar traição.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes vokosu and the Portuguese armar traição, plotting treachery. The scan shows a cedilla in traição; this is not merely modern spelling normalization.
[c1-l043] Fongui. i, Fonno coto. *Propria, ou legi-*
[c1-l043 note] Hongui, 本義, is an original or legitimate matter, explained as hon no koto. The i marker introduces an explanation rather than contributing Japanese syllables.
[c1-l044 >] *tima couſa. ¶ Item, Couſa antigua, & q̃*
[c1-l044 note] The second sense is something ancient or original. Antigua is retained as historical Portuguese; the following Latin phrase continues the explanation.
[c1-l045 >] *ab initio aßi auia de ſer.*
[c1-l045 note] Ab initio, from the beginning: it had originally been so. Read initio aßi auia, recovering the obscured letters rather than the OCR's intio aß auta.
[c1-l046] Fonguiacu, l, fongueqi. Somuqu ſacaxima.
[c1-l046 note] Hongyaku, 反逆, is equated with hongeki and glossed somuku, sakashima. The l is an alternative marker, not part of a Japanese word.
[c1-l047 >] *Traição.*
[c1-l047 note] Traição completes the rebellion entry. The library stamp overlaps this short row, but the word and its descender remain readable in the crop.

[c2-l001] Fonguiǒ. *Proprio texto, ou liuro da ley eßẽ-*
[c2-l001 note] Hongyō, a principal scriptural text. Liuro is visibly the intended Portuguese noun, not liaro; the open-o diacritic on the Japanese heading remains as read by OCR.
[c2-l002 >] *cial, &c.*
[c2-l002 note] Completes essencial, qualifying the religious text. No omitted new heading is implied by this short continuation.
[c2-l003] Fon-i. Fonno cocoro. *Proprio ſentido, ou*
[c2-l003 note] Hon-i, 本意, one's true intention or meaning, is explained as hon no kokoro. The internal hyphen distinguishes the vowel following n and is retained.
[c2-l004 >] *propria razão. ¶* Fon-iuo ſomuqu. *Deſ*
[c2-l004 note] Hon-i wo somuku means going against the proper intention or reason. The Portuguese discrepancy verb begins Des here, without an added line-end hyphen.
[c2-l005 >] *crepar do que he razão, & corteſia. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l005 note] Completes discrepar/descrepar: departing from reason and courtesy. Keep the historical printed vowel and division rather than substituting the modern verb.
[c2-l006 >] *Proprio deſejo. Vt,* Fon-ini naru. *Com*
[c2-l006 note] Hon-i ni naru, to attain one's desire. The Portuguese Com continues as comprir on the next row; do not manufacture a missing hyphen.
[c2-l007 >] *prir ſeus deſejos, ou vontade.*
[c2-l007 note] Completes the gloss: to fulfil one's wishes or will. Comprir is a historical spelling and is not an OCR error solely because cumprir is familiar today.
[c2-l008] Fonja. Fonno yaxiro. *Principal ermida, ou*
[c2-l008 note] Honja, 本社, the main shrine, is explained as hon no yashiro. The Portuguese ermida is the missionaries' comparison for the shrine building.
[c2-l009 >] *caſa do Cami em reſpeito doutras inferiores.*
[c2-l009 note] The principal house of the kami relative to lesser shrines. The magnified word is Cami, not cumi, and respeito contains the lost e; Cami is italic in this explanation.
[c2-l010] Fonjei. *Voto, ou juramento como o que fez*
[c2-l010 note] Honzei, 本誓, a vow or oath. Je represents the historical voiced sibilant here; it should not be read with a modern English j sound.
[c2-l011 >] *Amida, ou outros Fotoques.*
[c2-l011 note] Amida and other Fotoques are the examples of beings making such vows. Both borrowed names appear in the italic explanatory text.
[c2-l012] Fonjen. *Meſa principal com comer. i, A q̃*
[c2-l012 note] Honzen, 本膳, the principal meal tray. Portuguese mesa com comer is a table or tray with food, not an abstract main meal alone.
[c2-l013 >] *traz o arroz, & Xiru, &c.*
[c2-l013 note] The tray bears rice and xiru, soup. The magnified scan places the whole Xiru in italic; a roman X alone was an OCR style-boundary artifact.
[c2-l014] Fonjet. Fonno xet. *Verdadeira doctrina.*
[c2-l014 note] Honzet, 本説, authentic teaching, explained as hon no setsu. The final t is the period's word-final notation and should not be expanded in the diplomatic text.
[c2-l015 >] *¶ Item, Verdadeiras nouas. Na pratica*
[c2-l015 note] Another sense is true news. Na pratica introduces a usage note about the spoken or ordinary form, continued below.
[c2-l016 >] *ſe vſa,* Fonxet. Fixet, *he o contrario.*
[c2-l016 note] Fonxet is the practical form; Fixet is its opposite, false or unfounded news. These contrasted Japanese forms are upright within the Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l017] Fonji. *Principal Igreja, ou matriz.*
[c2-l017 note] Honji, 本寺, the principal temple, compared with a mother church. This is not the preceding Buddhist 本地 despite the similar modern pronunciation.
[c2-l018] Fonjin. Conbonno xin. *Primeiro principio de*
[c2-l018 note] Honjin, 本心, fundamental mind or one's true senses, explained by konbon no shin. The scan's Conbonuo is retained as the period's linked spelling.
[c2-l019 >] *q̃ ſe trata no Buppô. ¶ Item, Proprio ſer,*
[c2-l019 note] The first sense is discussed in Buddhist doctrine, Buppô. Read q̃ ſe trata: the faint long s was mistaken for d and joined to q̃. Buppô is wholly italic here.
[c2-l020 >] *& juizo. ¶* Fonjinuo vxinǒ. *Per-*
[c2-l020 note] Honjin wo ushinō, losing one's senses. The final marked o belongs to the historical verb form corresponding to ushinau, not to a Portuguese word.
[c2-l021 >] *der o juizo, ou eſtar fora de ſi cõ bebedice, &c.*
[c2-l021 note] The example means losing one's judgement or being beside oneself through drunkenness. Bebedice is not to be replaced by a modern synonym.
[c2-l022] Fonjo. *Original.*
[c2-l022 note] Honjo is an original document. Keep this short homograph separate from the longer place/landholding entry below.
[c2-l023] Fonjo. Motono tocoro. *Primeiro, ou anti-*
[c2-l023 note] Honjo, 本所, begins with the original or former place, moto no tokoro. The Portuguese anti-/guo is divided across the physical rows.
[c2-l024 >] *guo lugar. ¶* Fonjoye cayeru. *Tornar*
[c2-l024 note] Honjo ye kaeru means returning to one's former place. Ye is the directional particle and cayeru is the historical romanization of kaeru.
[c2-l025 >] *a ſeu primeiro, ou antigo lugar. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l025 note] Completes returning to the former place. The scan here reads antigo rather than the OCR's split a ntiguo; retain variation from anti-/guo immediately above.
[c2-l026 >] *Nome de Cigues ordinarios: vſaſe ſempre*
[c2-l026 note] Another sense is a designation for ordinary Cigues. This court-related borrowed term is kept as printed; a precise rank equivalence should not be inferred solely from ordinarios.
[c2-l027 >] *com* Go. *Vt,* Gofonjo. *¶* Gofonjo
[c2-l027 note] The title is used with honorific go, hence gohonjo. A new example begins with gohonjo no kura, continuing below.
[c2-l028 >] no cura. *Sella que fez hum official inſigne*
[c2-l028 note] Gohonjo no kura is a saddle attributed to a renowned craftsman. The scan supports hum official insigne; the missing g was badly faded, not a genuine space inside the adjective.
[c2-l029 >] *por nome* Ixedono *que a qualquer caualo qua*
[c2-l029 note] The craftsman is called Ixedono, Ise-dono; the saddle suits any horse. Keep the personal designation upright as printed and do not modernize its xe spelling.
[c2-l030 >] *dra. ¶* I*tem, No Cami,* Fonjo. *Se-*
[c2-l030 note] A Kami-region usage of Fonjo then means the landlord. No Cami belongs wholly to the italic Portuguese note; Fonjo is upright, rather than the OCR's split Ca ni, Fonji.
[c2-l031 >] *nhorio aquem pagam o foro dalgum chão,*
[c2-l031 note] The landlord receives the foro, a due or ground rent, for land. This is a social/legal gloss, not a second physical place example.
[c2-l032 >] *ou caſas.*
[c2-l032 note] Or houses: the short row finishes the rental-property definition. It remains body text rather than page furniture.
[c2-l033] Fonjo. Moto, fajime. *Primeiro principio.*
[c2-l033 note] A further Honjo means the first beginning, with moto and hajime as equivalents. The different homographs are not merged by their identical spelling.
[c2-l034] Fonmaru. *Fortaleza, ou castello principal, &*
[c2-l034 note] Honmaru, 本丸, the principal castle enclosure. Castello is retained with the OCR's short s; meaning supplies no reason to change that allograph.
[c2-l035 >] *mais interior onde mora o ſenhor, ou capitão em*
[c2-l035 note] The innermost enclosure houses the lord or captain. This narrows the previous general fortress gloss to the main residential/defensive enclosure.
[c2-l036 >] *reſpeito de outras cercas, & caſtellos menos*
[c2-l036 note] It is contrasted with other outer works and lesser castles. Cercas here denotes enclosures, not merely generic fences in isolation.
[c2-l037 >] *principaes que eſtão dentro da meſma fortaleza*
[c2-l037 note] Those secondary enclosures are within the same overall fortress. Principaes and fortaleza retain their historical spellings.
[c2-l038 >] *vniuerſal.*
[c2-l038 note] Vniuersal completes the sense of the fortress as a whole. It is a Portuguese continuation, not a separate universal concept entry.
[c2-l039] Fonmat. Moto, ſuye. *Principio, & fim.*
[c2-l039 note] Honmat, 本末, beginning and end, glossed moto and sue. The initial damaged capital is F, not ſ; the Japanese meaning independently supports the headword identification.
[c2-l040 >] *¶* Fiǒxino fonmatuo totonoyete, &c.
[c2-l040 note] Hyōshi no honmatsu wo totonoete: arranging the beginning and end of the rhythm or performance. The final t in the headword expands before the following particle here.
[c2-l041 >] *Temperando, & concertando de maneira os*
[c2-l041 note] The Portuguese describes tuning or coordinating instruments. Concertando includes the faint t, omitted by OCR; it does not refer to a different verb concerar.
[c2-l042 >] *inſtrumentos que concorde principio com fim.*
[c2-l042 note] The instruments should agree from beginning to end. Read instrumentos rather than instramentos; preserve the printed singular concorde rather than silently correcting grammar.
[c2-l043] Fonmiacu. *Pulſo natural, & ordinario.*
[c2-l043 note] Honmyaku, 本脈, the normal pulse. Ordinario begins with o, not c; the Japanese and Portuguese meanings coincide straightforwardly.
[c2-l044] Fonmǒ. *Deſejo. Vt,* Fonmǒuo toguru,
[c2-l044 note] Honmō, 本望, a wish; honmō wo toguru means fulfilling it. The caron is retained rather than replacing it from a modern long-vowel convention.
[c2-l045 >] l, taſſuru. *Cumprir ſeus deſejos.*
[c2-l045 note] Tassuru is an alternative verb for attaining one's desire. Both long s characters are independently visible in this upright Japanese sequence.
[c2-l046] Fonmon. *Proprio texto.*
[c2-l046 note] Honmon, 本文, the proper or original text. Proprio replaces the OCR's Progrio, confirmed by the printed p and the definition.
[c2-l047] Fonnin. Moto fito. *Homem que he prin-*
[c2-l047 note] Honnin, 本人, the principal person, glossed moto hito. Read prin- rather than prim,; f210 begins with cipal and completes principal. The repeated cipal at this page's foot is a catchword.
