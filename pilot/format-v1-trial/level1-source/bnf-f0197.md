[c1-l001 >>] *dirſe dambas as partes no jugar das armas, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Dirſe completes diuidirſe from f196; its repetition there is a catchword. The two sides separate in fencing, hence jugar rather than lugar.
[c1-l002 >] *na peleja.*
[c1-l002 note] Na peleja extends the separation sense to fighting generally. This closes hirakiwakareru from the preceding page.
[c1-l003] Firaricururito. *Adu. Modo de menear a*
[c1-l003 note] Hirari kururi to describes a quick whirling movement. The adverb is illustrated with a polearm rather than merely translated as speed.
[c1-l004 >] *alabarda, &c. Vt,* Naguinatauo firari cu-
[c1-l004 note] Naginata wo hirari cu-rurito begins the example of swinging the polearm. The physical break falls inside the mimetic expression.
[c1-l005 >] rurito mauaite vomoteuo furazu cacaru.
[c1-l005 note] The fighter swings it and attacks without turning his face away. Vomoteuo restores omote wo from the garbled spacing; mauaite is retained as printed rather than normalized to mawashite.
[c1-l006 >] Toſ. *Arremete ſem temor meneando a ala-*
[c1-l006 note] The source label Toſ. precedes charging fearlessly while moving the halberd. Preserve the label's visible spelling rather than guessing a full title.
[c1-l007 >] *barda, & dando voltas com ella.*
[c1-l007 note] Barda completes alabarda, and voltas describes turns made with it. The damaged t explains OCR's volras.
[c1-l008] Firarifirarito. *Adu. Modo de ſe ver, ou deſapa-*
[c1-l008 note] Hirari hirari to describes something appearing or disappearing quickly. The repetition belongs to one adverbial expression.
[c1-l009 >] *recer a couſa de preſſa como abano que ſe arre-*
[c1-l009 note] The comparison is a thrown fan moving out of sight. De preſſa characterizes the rapid appearance or disappearance.
[c1-l010 >] *meſſa, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] Meſſa completes arremeſſa, the throwing verb. The line is the end of the fan simile, not a new entry.
[c1-l011] Firata, l, firada. *Embarcação larga, & bai*
[c1-l011 note] Hirata and hirada are alternative names for the broad low boat already listed on f196. The reversed order is intentional cross-listing.
[c1-l012 >] *xa como Fune do rio.*
[c1-l012 note] A river boat illustrates the low vessel. Fune is a Japanese noun embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l013] Firataqe. *Cugumelos que nacem aos pès das*
[c1-l013 note] Hiratake denotes mushrooms growing at trees' feet. The headword's t and Portuguese Cugumelos are recovered from the damaged print, with the mushroom gloss supporting the reading.
[c1-l014 >] *aruores.*
[c1-l014 note] Aruores completes the habitat phrase, at the bases of trees. The missing r was OCR damage, not a word boundary.
[c1-l015] Firauchi. *O tecer algũa couſa larga como fitas,*
[c1-l015 note] Hirauchi here is weaving something broad, such as a ribbon. It is separate from the sword-striking sense introduced shortly afterward.
[c1-l016 >] *&c. Vt,* Firauchino vobi. *Fita, ou cingi*
[c1-l016 note] Hirauchi no obi is a broad woven sash or ribbon. Cingi is the beginning of the Portuguese belt word below.
[c1-l017 >] *douro largo. ¶ Item, O dar de pancha cõ*
[c1-l017 note] Douro completes cingidouro. Item introduces striking with the flat, with the sword supplied on the following line.
[c1-l018 >] *a Catana.*
[c1-l018 note] Catana completes the flat-of-the-sword example. It belongs to Portuguese syntax despite being a Japanese loanword.
[c1-l019] Firazaya. *Bainha larga.*
[c1-l019 note] Hirazaya is a broad scabbard. Bainha supplies the implement meaning without requiring a separate Japanese-script restoration.
[c1-l020] Firazzuqe. *O chegar a embarcação atraueſſa-*
[c1-l020 note] Hirazuke means bringing a vessel alongside broadside. Atraueſſada refers to its orientation rather than damage to the vessel.
[c1-l021 >] *da. Vt,* Firazzuqeni xeyo. *Chegai*
[c1-l021 note] Hirazuke ni seyo is an imperative to bring it alongside. The Portuguese Chegai likewise addresses the person manoeuvring it.
[c1-l022 >] *perto a Fune de ilharga.*
[c1-l022 note] The boat is brought near by its side. Fune remains an embedded Japanese word eligible for typeface correction.
[c1-l023] Firazzuqeno vobi. *Cinto, ou cingidouro de*
[c1-l023 note] Hirazuke no obi denotes a woman's broad belt. Cinto, not Vinto, matches the following synonym cingidouro and the visible initial.
[c1-l024 >] *molher muito largo.*
[c1-l024 note] Molher identifies the belt's wearer, while muito largo describes the belt. Retain the historical o spelling in molher.
[c1-l025] Firazzutçumi. *Maneira de lençol de linho ca*
[c1-l025 note] Hirazutsumi is a wrapping sheet. Lençol repairs the OCR letter error; ca begins the divided hemp term.
[c1-l026 >] *nemo groſſo pera emburilhar as camas, &c.*
[c1-l026 note] Canemo is completed and qualified as coarse, used to wrap bedding. Preserve emburilhar as printed rather than modernizing its internal order.
[c1-l027] Firefuxi, ſu, uita. *Deitarſe debruços.*
[c1-l027 note] Hirefusu is lying prostrate. Deitarſe debruços confirms the posture and restores the missing i in Deitarſe.
[c1-l028] Firei. Reini arazu. *Sem deuida ceremonia,*
[c1-l028 note] Hirei is explained as rei ni arazu, lacking proper ceremony. The r/e reading in Reini is supported by that explicit Japanese etymological gloss.
[c1-l029 >] *ou corteſia. Vt,* Xinua fireiuo vqezu.
[c1-l029 note] Shin wa hirei wo ukezu says the deity does not accept impropriety. Corteſia completes the Portuguese pair ceremony and courtesy.
[c1-l030 >] *O Cami não aceita offertas ſem as deuidas ce-*
[c1-l030 note] The Portuguese makes the implication explicit: a kami does not accept offerings without due rites. Cami is embedded terminology, not Portuguese cami.
[c1-l031 >] *remonias.*
[c1-l031 note] Remonias completes ceremonias and closes the ritual example. It should not be joined across physical lines in Level 1.
[c1-l032] Firei. Tamexini arazu. *Couſa que não he or*
[c1-l032 note] The second hirei is tameshi ni arazu, outside precedent or ordinary practice. It is a homonym of the preceding ritual impropriety entry.
[c1-l033 >] *dinaria, & cuſtumada.*
[c1-l033 note] Ordinaria and cuſtumada describe the practice that this thing is not. The apparent internal gap in the OCR is not retained as a lexical division.
[c1-l034] Fireô. l, firiô. Tobu tatçu. *Lagarto que*
[c1-l034 note] Hireō or hiryō is tobu tatsu, a flying dragon. The Portuguese calls it a flying lizard; preserve that historical gloss rather than replace it with a modern zoological term.
[c1-l035 >] *voa.*
[c1-l035 note] Voa completes the flying-lizard definition. It is the predicate of lagarto above.
[c1-l036] Firet. Iyaxiqu votoru. *Couſa baixa, & ruim.*
[c1-l036 note] Hiretsu is low or inferior, explained as iyashiku otoru. Bare final t is the checked coda, not a missing tsu to add to Level 1.
[c1-l037] Firetna. *Idem. ¶* Firetna fito. *Ho-*
[c1-l037 note] Firetna supplies the adjectival form, hiretsu na hito. Idem links it to the preceding low or inferior quality.
[c1-l038 >] *mem baixo, ou de eſpiritus baixos.*
[c1-l038 note] The person is lowly or of low spirit. Preserve eſpiritus as printed rather than regularizing its vowel to modern Portuguese.
[c1-l039] Firô. Firaqi arauaſu. i, Xujinno mayeni mo
[c1-l039 note] Hirō is making something known, hiraqi arawasu. Shujin no mae ni begins the explanation of speaking before one's lord; Xujinno repairs the incoherent OCR cluster.
[c1-l040 >] nouo môſu. *Falar, ou propor ao ſenhor, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Mono wo mōsu completes speaking or proposing something to a lord. The marked o in môſu is visible and expresses the long vowel.
[c1-l041 >] *apeſſoa nobre. ¶* Firôſuru. *Idem.*
[c1-l041 note] The addressee may also be a noble person. Firôſuru is the suru verb form, not the OCR's turu.
[c1-l042] Firǒ. Tçucare, itazzugauaxij. *Canſaço.*
[c1-l042 note] The next hirō means fatigue, explained by tsukare and itazugawashii. Its marked o differs from the preceding announcement sense and is preserved independently.
[c1-l043 >] *¶* Firǒxita. *Eſtou canſado, & desfeito.*
[c1-l043 note] Hirō shita means being worn out or exhausted. The example restores the headword's damaged F and vowel mark rather than reading modern romanization hiro.
[c1-l044 >] *¶. Item, Permet. Se diz de hum homem que*
[c1-l044 note] Per met. signals a figurative extension: the term can describe a man depleted of resources. It is editorial Portuguese, not Japanese.
[c1-l045 >] *eſtà pobre, & desfeito do fato, & riquezas*
[c1-l045 note] Poverty and loss of possessions explain that figurative exhaustion. Desfeito is one printed word here, unlike OCR's split form.
[c1-l046 >] *que tinha.*
[c1-l046 note] Que tinha refers to the possessions and wealth the person formerly had. It closes the depleted-resources sense.
[c1-l047] Firoba. *Lugar largo. ¶ Item, Lugar pu-*
[c1-l047 note] Hiroba is a broad place and, in the continuation, a public place. The damaged initial word is confirmed by the next column's Hirobaye example.

[c2-l001 >>] *blico, ou de muita gente. ¶* Firobaye de-
[c2-l001 note] Blico completes publico across the column break. Hiroba ye de-tsuketa introduces a person accustomed to appearing in public.
[c2-l002 >] tçuqeta fito. *Homem cuſtumado a ſair em*
[c2-l002 note] Tsuketa completes de-tsuketa. The gloss describes familiarity with going out before others, not merely arriving once.
[c2-l003 >] *publico, ou ajuntamento de gente.*
[c2-l003 note] Public appearances or gatherings complete the description of that accustomed person. Preserve the Portuguese ajuntamento form.
[c2-l004] Firobiroto. *Adu. Muito largo. ¶* Fi-
[c2-l004 note] Hirobiro to means very spacious or broadly. The repeated example begins at the line's right edge.
[c2-l005 >] robiroto xita coto. *Couſa muito larga, &*
[c2-l005 note] Hirobiro to shita koto describes a broad or extended thing. The unusual koto is preserved rather than replaced by expected mono.
[c2-l006 >] *eſtendida.*
[c2-l006 note] Eſtendida completes the two qualities, broad and spread out. This is the end of the adverbial example.
[c2-l007] Firobuta. *Cubertura larga como taboleiro em*
[c2-l007 note] Hirobuta is a broad cover or tray. The headword's o and Cubertura's b are visible despite damage; the next line explains its use.
[c2-l008 >] *qu poem veſtidos, ou outras couſas.*
[c2-l008 note] Clothes or other things are placed on it. The apparent qu is left as printed rather than expanded to que without evidence.
[c2-l009] Firocu. Cacuxi, xiruſu. i, Daijino rocu.
[c2-l009 note] Hiroku is a secret record: kakushi shirusu, with daiji no roku as a paraphrase. The punctuation separates explanatory Japanese components.
[c2-l010 >] *Liuro de grandes ſegredos, ou miſterios.*
[c2-l010 note] A book of great secrets or mysteries confirms the hi-roku analysis. The translation is about contents, not the book's physical size.
[c2-l011] Firogari, ru, atta. *Alargarſe, l, eſtẽderſe.*
[c2-l011 note] Hirogaru is becoming broader or spreading. The past ending atta is restored from the damaged type, not left as impossible acta.
[c2-l012 >] *¶ Permet.* Cuchiga firogaru, l, ſuboru.
[c2-l012 note] Kuchi ga hirogaru or suboru describes opening versus narrowing the mouth. The next example explicitly illustrates vowel pronunciation.
[c2-l013 >] *Abrirſe a boca, ou fecharſe na pronunciaçaõ*
[c2-l013 note] The gloss concerns opening or closing the mouth in pronunciation. Fecharſe repairs the OCR j without changing the reflexive construction.
[c2-l014 >] *dalgũas palauras. Vt,* Mǒ to yǔcotoua firo-
[c2-l014 note] Mǒ to yū koto wa hirogaru illustrates an open long o: the mouth opens in pronouncing Mǒ. The second enlarged pass confirms the caron on yǔ as well as the caron on Mǒ; neither is a nasal tilde.
[c2-l015 >] garu, Mô to yǔcotoua ſuboru.
[c2-l015 note] Mô to yū koto wa suboru gives the contrasting closed long o, with the mouth narrowed. Preserve the contrast Mǒ/Mô across these two physical lines; yǔ has a caron in both examples.
[c2-l016] Firogue, uru, eta. *Alargar, l, eſtender.*
[c2-l016 note] Hirogeru is the transitive counterpart, widening or extending something. Its ending uru is recorded separately from the stem.
[c2-l017] Firoi. *Couſa larga, ou eſpaçoſa. ¶* Firoi
[c2-l017 note] Hiroi means broad or spacious. Hiroi at the end begins a figurative example of a generous disposition.
[c2-l018 >] cocoro ,l, qi. *Condiçao liberal.*
[c2-l018 note] Hiroi kokoro or ki denotes generosity. Qi is ki, not qu; condição liberal confirms the character trait rather than physical area.
[c2-l019 >] Firoſa.
[c2-l019 note] Firoſa is the derived noun, breadth. The damaged o belongs to hiroi and should not yield hiresa.
[c2-l020 >] Firô.
[c2-l020 note] Firô is the contracted adverbial derivative of hiroi. The scan shows a circumflex; preserve it independently of the picking-up verb below.
[c2-l021] Firoi, ô, ôta. *Apanhar algũa couſa do chão.*
[c2-l021 note] Hirō here means picking something up from the ground. Couſa restores the damaged vowel in the Portuguese object.
[c2-l022 >] *¶* Taqiguiuo firô. *Apanhar lenha.*
[c2-l022 note] Takigi wo hirō means gathering firewood. The scan's circumflex is retained in the repeated verb.
[c2-l023 >] *¶* Conomiuo firô. *Apanhar fruita do*
[c2-l023 note] Konomi wo hirō means picking fruit or nuts from the ground. The following chão completes the source-location phrase.
[c2-l024 >] *chao. ¶ Item, Achar a caſo algũa couſa.*
[c2-l024 note] A further sense is finding something by chance. This extends the picking-up verb, not the broadness adjective.
[c2-l025 >] *Vt,* Canenadouo firô. *Achar a caſo pra*
[c2-l025 note] Kane nado wo hirō is finding money or similar things. The Portuguese silver example continues across pra-ta.
[c2-l026 >] *ta, ou outra couſa.*
[c2-l026 note] Ta completes prata, followed by other things one might find. The physical division is preserved.
[c2-l027] Firoiatçume, uru, eta. *Ajuntar apanhando*
[c2-l027 note] Hiroiatsumeru means gathering by picking things up. The compound's first element is hiroi, not the adjective hiroi in this context.
[c2-l028 >] *polo chão.*
[c2-l028 note] Polo chão specifies gathering from the ground. It completes the compound verb's definition.
[c2-l029] Firoigo. *Enjeitado que ſe acha em algũa parte.*
[c2-l029 note] Hiroigo is a foundling, a child found abandoned somewhere. Enjeitado gives the social rather than literal picked-up-object sense.
[c2-l030] Firoi mono. *Couſa que hum acha a caſo, &*
[c2-l030 note] Hiroimono is a thing found by chance. Hum is the generic finder, not the OCR's ham.
[c2-l031 >] *toma.*
[c2-l031 note] Toma completes finding and taking the object. It is part of the definition, not a new command.
[c2-l032] Firǒjin. *Pobre, & deſfeito de fato, alfa-*
[c2-l032 note] Firǒjin is a person depleted of possessions. It relates to the exhaustion or poverty sense of hirō in column 1, not to hiroi-mono.
[c2-l033 >] *yas, &c.*
[c2-l033 note] Yas completes alfayas, household goods or possessions. The continuation closes the poverty description.
[c2-l034] Firôjǒ. i, Atejǒ, l, tçuqejǒ. *Carta que*
[c2-l034 note] Hirōjō is a letter presented through an intermediary, equated with atejō or tsukejō. The first o has a circumflex and jō a caron in the scan.
[c2-l035 >] *vai encaminhada a peſſoa que hade falar, apreſẽ*
[c2-l035 note] The letter goes to someone who will speak and present it. Apreſẽ begins the divided presentation verb.
[c2-l036 >] *tar eſta carta, &c. posto q̃ por reuerencia não*
[c2-l036 note] The intermediary presents the letter because respect discourages direct address. Posto que introduces that qualification.
[c2-l037 >] *vai direitamente dirigida ao ſenhor, ou a peſ-*
[c2-l037 note] It is not directly addressed to the lord or principal person. Preserve direitamente as the printed adverb.
[c2-l038 >] *ſoa principal.*
[c2-l038 note] Soa completes peſsoa principal. This closes the account of the intermediary letter.
[c2-l039] Firoitori, u, otta. *Tomar o que eſtà no chão.*
[c2-l039 note] Hiroitoru is taking what lies on the ground. Its toru component makes the compound distinct from simply finding something.
[c2-l040] Firoma. *Sala grande.*
[c2-l040 note] Hiroma is a large hall or room. The damaged F is supported by the scan and the room gloss.
[c2-l041] Firomari, u, atta. *Alargarſe, ou estenderſe.*
[c2-l041 note] Hiromaru is becoming broad or extended. The missing final i in the citation form Firomari is restored.
[c2-l042 >] *¶ Item, Diuulgarſe como ley, ſeita, &c.*
[c2-l042 note] A law or sect can also spread. This figurative extension links physical expansion to dissemination.
[c2-l043] Firomari vatari, u, atta. *Diuulgarſe em mui*
[c2-l043 note] Hiromariwataru means spreading widely through many places. Diuulgarſe restores the dropped u in the Portuguese verb.
[c2-l044 >] *tas partes.*
[c2-l044 note] Tas completes muitas partes, specifying the wide geographical spread. It belongs to the compound entry above.
[c2-l045] Firome. *Dilatação da ley, &c.*
[c2-l045 note] Hirome is the dissemination of a law or teaching. It is the noun preceding the related transitive verb.
[c2-l046] Firome, uru, eta. *Alargar, ou eſtender.*
[c2-l046 note] Hiromeru means making something broader or extending it. The initial vowel is i, not OCR's a.
[c2-l047 >] *¶ Item, Promulgar. ¶* Govoqiteuo fi-
[c2-l047 note] The additional sense is promulgating: go-okite wo hi-romuru concerns proclaiming a regulation. Both Promulgar and Govoqiteuo are restored from the scan.
