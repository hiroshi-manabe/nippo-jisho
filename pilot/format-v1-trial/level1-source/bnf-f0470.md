[c1-l001 >] *uerno da Tenca. ¶* Yauo tamotçu. *Ter,*
[c1-l001 note] Uerno completes go-uerno from f469, the government of the realm, Tenca. A new example, ya wo tamotsu, concerns holding an arrow; Tenca is a Japanese term embedded in Portuguese exposition.
[c1-l002 >] *ou eſperar com a frecha no arco pera tirar.*
[c1-l002 note] The arrow is kept on the bow while waiting to shoot. This completes the physical holding sense of tamotsu rather than the preceding government sense.
[c1-l003 >] *¶* Gouoqiteuo tamotçu. *Guardar a ley*
[c1-l003 note] Go-okite wo tamotsu means observing the sacred law. The honorific Japanese formulation remains roman, while the Portuguese translation continues below.
[c1-l004 >] *ſancta. ¶* Inochiuotamotçu. *Viuer, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Sancta completes the sacred-law gloss; inochi wo tamotsu then introduces preserving one's life. The close-set Inochiuotamotçu is retained as printed.
[c1-l005 >] *eſtar com vida.*
[c1-l005 note] The life example is translated as living or remaining alive. The short continuation closes the series of tamotsu examples.
[c1-l006] Tamochitodoqe, uru, eta. *Perſeuerar na*
[c1-l006 note] Tamochitodoke means persevering in keeping or observing something. The worn Portuguese verb is Perſeuerar, not the OCR's interrupted Perſeu rar.
[c1-l007 >] *guarda, ou obſeruancia de algũa couſa.*
[c1-l007 note] The perseverance concerns guarding or observing something. Obſeruancia has an i rather than the OCR's t in obſeruancta; the historical u/v spelling is preserved.
[c1-l008] Tamon. *Outros que não ſão parentes, nem da*
[c1-l008 note] Tamon denotes outsiders who are not relatives. The t in Outros is visible despite weak impression and corrects OCR Ouros.
[c1-l009 >] *meſma gèração. ¶ Item, Outra ſeita, ou*
[c1-l009 note] The outsiders are not of the same lineage; a further sense concerns another sect. The scan supports ſeita, not OCR ſeuta, while the printed accent in gèração is retained.
[c1-l010 >] *os de outra ſeita, ou inſtituto.*
[c1-l010 note] The further sense includes people of another sect or institution. The word inſtituto contains the t omitted by OCR.
[c1-l011] Tamǒri, u, ǒtta. *Meliùs,* Tamauaru *Dar*
[c1-l011 note] Tamoori is a variant for which the dictionary prefers tamawaru. The italic Meliùs states that preference, and Dar starts the explanation about giving by an honored person. Fine traces above the past-form o support retaining ǒtta, but that caron is substantially worn.
[c1-l012 >] *peßoa honrada. Poſto que* Tamauaru *mais ſer-*
[c1-l012 note] An honored person gives; the following qualification contrasts the register of tamawaru. Honrada and its following stop replace OCR honcada; the embedded Japanese verb remains roman.
[c1-l013 >] *ue pera eſcritura, & pratica graue.*
[c1-l013 note] Ve completes ſerue: tamawaru is more suited to writing and formal speech. Pera, not OCR peta, introduces those settings.
[c1-l014] Tamot. i, Tano mono. *Couſa alhea.*
[c1-l014 note] Tamot is something belonging to another, explained as ta no mono. The checked final t is retained rather than expanded into an extra syllable.
[c1-l015] Tamoto. *Bolſo da manga. ¶* Tamotouo
[c1-l015 note] Tamoto is the pouch or hanging part of a sleeve. The Portuguese noun reads Bolſo, not OCR Balſo; the example begins tamoto wo.
[c1-l016 >] nuraſu. *P. Chorar.* Tamotono cauaqu
[c1-l016 note] Tamoto wo nurasu, wetting the sleeves, is glossed poetically as crying. The next expression begins 'the sleeves drying' and continues below; the local italic P. precedes its explanatory gloss.
[c1-l017 >] fimamonaxi. *Chorar muito. ¶* Tamo-
[c1-l017 note] Hima mo nashi completes the image of sleeves never having time to dry, meaning much weeping. A separate sleeve-grasping example begins after the paragraph mark.
[c1-l018 >] toni toritçuqu. l, ſugaru. *Pegarſe, ou*
[c1-l018 note] Tamoto ni toritsuku or sugaru means clinging to someone's sleeves. The alternative marker l is not part of the Japanese verbs.
[c1-l019 >] *afferrarſe das mangas. ¶* Tamotouo fi-
[c1-l019 note] The Portuguese confirms grasping the sleeves, with das rather than OCR dos. Tamoto wo fi- begins another example of stopping someone by the sleeve.
[c1-l020 >] cayuru. l, fiqitodomuru. *Deter a alguem*
[c1-l020 note] Cayuru completes ficayuru, alternatively fiqitodomuru, holding someone back. The source's historical verb forms are not replaced by their modern equivalents.
[c1-l021 >] *puxandolhe pollas mangas.*
[c1-l021 note] The person is detained by pulling on the sleeves. This completes the last tamoto example before the offering entry.
[c1-l022] Tamuqe. *O que ſe offrece ao idolo, offerta,*

[c1-l025 note] The offering is placed before an idol or a deceased person. Defuncto, not the OCR's deſtancto, is supported by the visible f and context; the example lists tea offerings and flowers.

[c1-l027 note] Que completes Fotoque, followed by offerings before the deceased: tea, foliage or flowers, and powdered wood. The worn plant word is read provisionally as fulhas, not OCR filhas; the ampersand after cha is visibly present.

[c1-l029 >] *& vai ardendo pouco a pouco. ¶ Item, Offe-*
[c1-l029 note] The incense burns gradually. Ardendo restores the missing d in OCR arendo; a further sense of offering begins with Offe- at the right edge.
[c1-l030 >] *recer, ou fazerſacrificio a Deos.*
[c1-l030 note] Recer completes offerecer, followed by making a sacrifice to God. The close-set fazerſacrificio is retained as printed.
[c1-l031] Tamura. Bechino mura. *Outro lugar, ou*
[c1-l031 note] Tamura means another settlement, explained by bechi no mura. The r in mura is worn but supported by its remaining stroke and the settlement gloss, correcting OCR muta.
[c1-l032 >] *pouoação.*
[c1-l032 note] Pouoação completes the alternative rendering as a settlement or village. The historical u/v spelling is retained.
[c1-l033] Tamuxi. *Empingem. ¶* Tamuxiga dequru.
[c1-l033 note] Tamushi is a skin eruption, glossed empingem. The example tamushi ga dekuru describes its appearance; no modern diagnostic claim is imposed on the historical description.
[c1-l034 >] *Nacer ẽpingẽ. ¶* Tamuxiga firogaru. *Estẽ-*
[c1-l034 note] The Portuguese first describes the eruption appearing, then tamushi ga hirogaru introduces its spreading. The nasal abbreviation marks and divided Estẽ- are retained.
[c1-l035 >] *derſe, ou ir lastrando a empingẽ. ¶* Tamuxiga
[c1-l035 note] Derſe completes the spreading gloss. The worn middle of lastrando is restored from surviving traces and the sense of spreading, rather than retaining the OCR's la trando; a new example follows.
[c1-l036 >] cuimauaru. *Ir a empingẽ comẽdo, ou laurãdo.*
[c1-l036 note] Tamushi ga kuimawaru describes the eruption spreading as though eating its way around. The Portuguese comẽdo and laurãdo preserve the same progressive image and their nasal abbreviations.
[c1-l037] Tan. *Tinta vermelha clara em pò, que ſerue*
[c1-l037 note] Tan is a light-red powdered pigment. The source identifies its material form but does not establish a modern chemical composition.
[c1-l038 >] *tambem pera mezinha.*
[c1-l038 note] The pigment is also said to serve as medicine. This records the historical dictionary's use statement, not present-day treatment advice.
[c1-l039] Tan. *Freima. ¶* Tanga zzuru. *Sairem*
[c1-l039 note] The homograph tan means phlegm; tan ga zzuru describes its coming out. A visible gap separates Tanga and zzuru, and the Portuguese begins the matching action.
[c1-l040 >] *freimas polla boca.*
[c1-l040 note] Phlegm comes out through the mouth, completing the example. The old Portuguese spelling freimas is retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l041] Tana. *Almario, ou parteleiro.*
[c1-l041 note] Tana is a cupboard or shelving structure. The source's Almario and parteleiro are preserved as historical spellings rather than corrected to modern Portuguese forms.
[c1-l042] Tanabata. *Duas estrellas aßi chamadas que e-*
[c1-l042 note] Tanabata is first defined through two named stars. Duas, not OCR Deus, gives the number; e- begins the verb continued on the next line.
[c1-l043 >] *ſtão na via lactea de fronte hũa da outra.*
[c1-l043 note] The two stars are described as facing one another on the Milky Way. The scan supports ſtão and lactea, correcting ſtio and ladea while retaining the source's phrasing.
[c1-l044 >] *¶ Item, O ſeptimo dia da ſeptima lua em que*
[c1-l044 note] Tanabata also denotes the seventh day of the seventh lunar month. The worn phrase is ſeptima lua, not OCR ſeptimala, and ſeptimo has t rather than u.
[c1-l045 >] *ſe aſoalha, ou tira fora fato, & roupa, &c.*
[c1-l045 note] On that day, household goods and clothes are aired or brought outside. The historical aſoalha is retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l046 >] *& ſe offerece a aquellas eſtrellas. ¶* Tana-
[c1-l046 note] Offerings are made to those stars. Tana- then begins the expression naming the festival day, continued below.
[c1-l047 >] batano fi. *Dia em que fazem esta ceremo-*
[c1-l047 note] Batano fi completes Tanabata no hi, the day when the ceremony is performed. Fazem corrects OCR ſezem, and the small visible terminal hyphen preserves the division of ceremonia into column 2.
