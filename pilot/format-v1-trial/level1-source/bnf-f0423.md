[c1-l004 >] *altas que ſe hião às nuuẽs.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the architectural image: the houses were so high that they reached the clouds. The historical hião and abbreviated nuuẽs are not modernized.
[c1-l005] Rocai. *Remos, & pangayos.*
[c1-l005 note] Rocai denotes rowing implements, glossed as oars and paddles. The unusual Portuguese pangayos is visible and does not need replacement by a modern term.
[c1-l006] Rôcutaxi. *Deixar morrer alguem tendoo mui-*
[c1-l006 note] Rôcutaxi denotes letting someone die through prolonged imprisonment. The scan restores the initial R and the x of Deixar; mui- continues as muito below.
[c1-l007 >] *to tempo no carcere.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes muito tempo no carcere: keeping the person in prison for a long time. This is the continuation of Rôcutaxi, not a separate definition.
[c1-l008] Roccacu. *Couſa de ſeis quinas, ou cantos.*
[c1-l008 note] Roccacu is a six-cornered or six-angled object. The doubled consonant belongs to the Japanese form; quinas and cantos provide mutually reinforcing Portuguese glosses.
[c1-l009] Roccacuna. *Idem.*
[c1-l009 note] The adjectival form Roccacuna has the same six-cornered meaning. Its initial R is upright with a decorative tail, not an isolated italic letter.
[c1-l010] Roccacuni. *Adu.*
[c1-l010 note] Roccacuni is the corresponding adverbial form, identified by italic Adu. The Japanese headword remains entirely upright.
[c1-l011] Roccon. *Seis ſentidos conforme aos Iapoẽs.*
[c1-l011 note] Roccon names six faculties in the Japanese classification. The Portuguese says six senses, not a compound word Seisſentidos; the explanation continues with the five external senses and a common faculty.
[c1-l012 >] *ſ, os cinco exteriores, & o ſentido comum. Vt,*
[c1-l012 note] Specifies five external senses and the common sense or mental faculty. The printed abbreviation ſ is preserved, and Vt introduces the following enumeration.
[c1-l013 >] *Guen, ni, bi, jet,* x*in,* I.
[c1-l013 note] Enumerates the six faculties through their Japanese names: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. The final upright I names the mental faculty, not the alternative marker l. The locally mixed type of xin is retained rather than regularized; its precise font boundary remains a nonblocking typography caveat.
[c1-l014] Rôchô. Cagono tori. *Paßaro que eſtà na*
[c1-l014 note] Rôchô is a bird in a cage, explicitly paraphrased by Cagono tori. The Portuguese bird-word is restored from the damaged OCR and continues with gayola on the next row.
[c1-l015 >] *gayola.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes the cage definition. Gayola is the printed historical Portuguese spelling; the short row remains a separate physical line.
[c1-l016] Rôchǔ. Yagurano vchi. *Dentro no balluar-*
[c1-l016 note] The first Rôchǔ is the interior of a tower or bastion, as Yagurano vchi makes clear. The Portuguese word balluarte is divided across the line end; it is not a full stop after balluar.
[c1-l017 >] *te, ou torrinha.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes balluarte and adds a small tower as an alternative. The diminutive torrinha clarifies the architectural, rather than prison, sense of this entry.
[c1-l018] Rôchǔ. Rôno vchi. *Dentro no tronco, ou*
[c1-l018 note] The next Rôchǔ concerns being inside a prison, paraphrased by Rôno vchi. This is a separate homophonous entry from the tower-interior sense above.
[c1-l019 >] *carcere.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes the prison definition with carcere. The one-word row belongs to the preceding Rôchǔ entry.
[c1-l020] Rochǔ. Michi naca. *Meyo do caminho.*
[c1-l020 note] Rochǔ is the middle of the road or journey, explained by Michi naca. The absence of a long-vowel mark on the first o distinguishes the printed form from the preceding entries.
[c1-l021] Rǒcô. *Fruito, ou merecimento do trabalho.*
[c1-l021 note] Rǒcô means the fruit or merit of labor. The middle consonant is c, not OCR e, and the two o marks differ in the printed headword.
[c1-l022 >] *¶* Rǒcôuo tçumu. *Ajuntar ſeruiços*
[c1-l022 note] Rǒcôuo tçumu means accumulating meritorious service. The object ending is uo, not OCR un; a visible paragraph sign introduces the example and the Portuguese has no comma after Ajuntar.
[c1-l023 >] *& merecimentos com trabalho.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes the translation as gathering services and merits through labor. It carries on the same example rather than introducing another Japanese expression.
[c1-l024] Rôcǒ. Chimata. i, Michitçuji. *Encru-*
[c1-l024 note] Rôcǒ denotes a crossroads or road junction, explained by Chimata and Michitçuji. The initial R is restored; the faint first vowel mark is provisionally read ô, with secondary support from the supplied NINJAL index, entry 212a12. Its worn direction remains uncertain; the final caron and Encru- are clear.
[c1-l025 >] *zilhada, ou caminho por onde todos paßão.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes encruzilhada and describes a route along which everyone passes. The historical ß form in paßão is retained; caminho begins with a lowercase c in the scan.
[c1-l026] Rôcocu. i, Toqei. *Relogio como darea,*
[c1-l026 note] Rôcocu is a timepiece, with Toqei as its Japanese equivalent. The Portuguese gives a sand clock as one example; the initial vowel mark and contracted darea are retained as printed.
[c1-l027 >] *agoa, &c.*
[c1-l027 note] Adds water clocks and other kinds to the preceding description of timepieces. Agoa is a continuation of the Portuguese list, not an independent entry.
[c1-l028] Rocu. Tamamono. i, Tacara. *Riquezas.*
[c1-l028 note] Rocu is wealth or a bestowed treasure, paraphrased with Tamamono and Tacara. The scan supports Riquezas, correcting the OCR's misplaced j.
[c1-l029 >] *¶* Rocuuo tçumu, l, tacuuayuru. *Ajun-*
[c1-l029 note] The two Japanese verbs describe accumulating or storing wealth. The alternative marker l is preserved, and Ajun- continues as Ajuntar below.
[c1-l030 >] *tar muitas riquezas pera dar.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the explanation: gathering much wealth in order to give it. The separation pera dar is visible and should not be collapsed into one word.
[c1-l031] Rǒcu. Xinrǒ curuximi. *Trabalho, pena,*
[c1-l031 note] This Rǒcu is toil and suffering, paraphrased as Xinrǒ curuximi. The scan supplies the mark on Xinrǒ; the Portuguese list continues with fatigue.
[c1-l032 >] *& canſaço.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes the labor-and-suffering definition by adding fatigue. The short Portuguese continuation remains on its own physical row.
[c1-l033] Rǒcu. Voino curuximi. *Trabalho, ou males*
[c1-l033 note] A separate Rǒcu concerns the pains of old age. Voino curuximi means the suffering of aging; the OCR's Vonuo cunuximi obscured both the possessive construction and the word for suffering.
[c1-l034 >] *da velhice.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the description of troubles or afflictions arising from old age. This limits the preceding Rǒcu more specifically than the general labor sense.
[c1-l035] Rocuchicu. *Seis animais.* i, Vxi, fitçuji, inu,
[c1-l035 note] Rocuchicu introduces a six-animal classification, beginning with cattle, sheep, and dogs. The Japanese enumeration follows i and is not an ordinary Portuguese sentence containing italicized Japanese words.
[c1-l036 >] vma, niuatori, inoco. *Boy, ou vaca, o-*
[c1-l036 note] Completes the Japanese list with horse, domestic fowl, and young boar. The Portuguese translation begins with ox or cow; its final o- belongs to ovelha on the following row.
[c1-l037 >] *uelha, cão, caualo, gallo, filho de porco mõtes.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes the animal list as sheep, dog, horse, cock, and offspring of a wild boar. The source's specific boar wording is retained rather than generalized silently to pig.
[c1-l038] Rocudai. i, Giſui, qua, fǔ, cǔ, xiqi. *Ter-*
[c1-l038 note] Rocudai names six elements. Giſui visibly runs earth and water together before fire, wind, space, and consciousness; no editorial comma is inserted inside that printed token. The marks over fǔ and cǔ are carons.
[c1-l039 >] *ra, agoa, fogo, vento, & mais duas ſuſtã-*
[c1-l039 note] Completes Terra and lists earth, water, fire, and wind, then introduces two further substances. The abbreviated and divided ſuſtã- continues below without expansion.
[c1-l040 >] *cias inuiſiueis.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes the six-element gloss with two invisible substances. The Portuguese characterization is preserved as the dictionary's explanation, not replaced by modern technical terminology.
[c1-l041] Rocudǒ. Mutçuno michi. i, Gigocu, gaqi,
[c1-l041 note] Rocudǒ means six paths or realms; Mutçuno michi supplies that Japanese explanation. The first realm-names are Gigocu and gaqi, and the initial M corrects OCR Nutçuno.
[c1-l042 >] chicuxǒ, xura, nin, den. *Seis infernos*
[c1-l042 note] Continues the realm list with animals, asuras, humans, and celestial beings. The visible den is retained rather than regularized to expected ten; the Portuguese calls all six infernos.
[c1-l043 >] *aßi chamados que os gẽtios imaginão auer neſta*
[c1-l043 note] Continues the dictionary's account of realms that non-Christians believe to exist. The religious framing belongs to the historical gloss; both ß and the abbreviated gẽtios are preserved.
[c1-l044 >] *vida preſente.*
[c1-l044 note] Completes the clause by locating those realms in the present life. The unusual scope of the Portuguese explanation is copied, not doctrinally corrected.
[c1-l045] Rocufaramit. *Seis qualidades, ou dotes dal-*
[c1-l045 note] Rocufaramit names six perfections, here explained as qualities or gifts of the soul. The scan restores qualidades; the final t is the printed Japanese checked ending, not an omitted syllable.
[c1-l046 >] *ma ſegundo ſe diz no* Buppò.
[c1-l046 note] Completes dalma and attributes the six qualities to Buddhist teaching. Buppò is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese explanation and is set upright in this impression.
[c1-l047] Rocugu. *Armas de todo o corpo. ¶* Ro-
[c1-l047 note] Rocugu denotes armor covering the whole body. The final Ro- begins an example continued at the top of the next column; it is body text, not a catchword.
