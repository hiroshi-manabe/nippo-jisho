[c1-l001] Vmigoxi. l, Vmigoxini. *Da outra banda*
[c1-l001 note] Umigoshi and umigoshini mean across, or on the other side of, the sea. The headword has mi, matching the repeated form; OCR Vnigoxi is corrected from the visible m.
[c1-l002 >] *do mar, ou paßando o mar. ¶* Vmigoxi-
[c1-l002 note] The Portuguese permits both the far side of the sea and crossing it. The paragraph sign introduces the Japanese example, whose umigoshi is physically divided after Vmigoxi-.
[c1-l003 >] no miyenu yamaxiru fino irite. *Posto que*
[c1-l003 note] The example says that one knows a mountain is there, although it cannot be seen across the sea, because the sun has set behind it. Posto que begins the concessive Portuguese translation below.
[c1-l004 >] *não parece monte da outra parte do mar, pare-*
[c1-l004 note] The concessive clause says no mountain appears on the other side of the sea. The visible pare- is the first part of parece and remains divided at the printed row ending.
[c1-l005 >] *ce que o auera pois ſe pos o ſol.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes the inference that there must be a mountain because the sun has set. Historical auera and unaccented pos are retained as printed.
[c1-l006] Vmiguiua. *Borda do mar.*
[c1-l006 note] Umigiwa is the edge or shore of the sea, directly glossed borda do mar.
[c1-l007] Vmiſuzume. *Hum paßarinho do mar.*
[c1-l007 note] Umisuzume is identified simply as a small seabird. The gloss does not justify assigning a more precise modern species.
[c1-l008] Vmizzura. *Superficie do mar.*
[c1-l008 note] Umizura denotes the surface of the sea; the doubled z spelling is retained without imposing a modern romanization.
[c1-l009] Vmore, ruru, eta. *Enterrarſe, ou cubrirſe*
[c1-l009 note] Umore, umoruru, umoreta describes becoming buried or covered. The Portuguese objects and covering materials are specified on the next row.
[c1-l010 >] *a erua, ou paos com a neue, terra, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] Grass or wood becomes covered by snow, earth, and similar material. The scan separates a erua; it is the Portuguese article plus noun, not the OCR compound aerua.
[c1-l011 >] *¶* Vôyuqi fureba, no, yamano cuſa,
[c1-l011 note] The paragraph sign, omitted by OCR, begins the example: when heavy snow falls, the plants of fields and mountains are buried. Vôyuqi is retained with the visible circumflex.
[c1-l012 >] qi mina vmoruru, l, vzzumoruru. *Cho-*
[c1-l012 note] Trees, too, become buried; vzzumoruru supplies an alternative verb. Cho- begins the Portuguese chovendo translation across the line break.
[c1-l013 >] *uendo muita neue ficão as eruas, & aruores*
[c1-l013 note] Completes Chouendo and describes abundant snow leaving grasses and trees covered. The clause continues with enterradas below.
[c1-l014 >] *enterradas de baixo. ¶ Item, Entulharſe,*
[c1-l014 note] The vegetation is buried underneath the snow. A new item then extends the verb to silting or filling up.
[c1-l015 >] *ou irſe entulhando acaua com terra, ou ciſco,*
[c1-l015 note] The filling-up sense concerns a ditch becoming clogged with earth or debris. The printed acaua is retained rather than normalized to a modern spelling or spacing.
[c1-l016 >] *que cae dentro.*
[c1-l016 note] The earth or debris falls into the ditch. This short continuation completes the preceding definition.
[c1-l017] Vmoregui. *Pao enterrado, ou cuberto de ter-*
[c1-l017 note] Umoregi is buried wood or timber covered by soil. The Portuguese terra is divided after ter- at the right rule.
[c1-l018 >] *ra como os que ficão caidos nos valles, & cu-*
[c1-l018 note] The example is wood that falls in valleys and becomes covered with earth. Cu- continues as bertos on the next row.
[c1-l019 >] *bertos ja de terra, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] Bertos completes cubertos: the fallen wood has already become covered with earth. The physical word division is retained.
[c1-l020] Vmorei. *Poço de que ſe não tira ja agoa, ou*
[c1-l020 note] Umorei is a well from which water is no longer drawn. The following alternative explains the well as nearly filled with soil.
[c1-l021 >] *que eſtà ja quaſi entulhado com a terra.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes the definition of an abandoned or filled-in well. The printed eſtà and ja are retained without modernizing their accents.
[c1-l022] Vmu. Ari naxi. *Auer, ou não auer: ſi, ou*
[c1-l022 note] Umu is explained by ari nashi, existence and nonexistence, and by Portuguese yes or no. These alternatives underlie the idioms that follow.
[c1-l023 >] *não. ¶* Vmunimo cacauarazu, l, vmu-
[c1-l023 note] Umu ni mo kakawarazu introduces indifference or failure to engage; the alternative expression begins umu- and continues below.
[c1-l024 >] no fenjimo xezu. *Não dando repoſta de ſi,*
[c1-l024 note] Umu no henji mo sezu means making no yes-or-no reply. The Portuguese explicitly describes not giving either answer.
[c1-l025 >] *ou de não, ou não tendo deuer com o que lhe di-*
[c1-l025 note] The Portuguese adds the sense of paying no regard to what someone says. Di- continues into zem on the next physical row.
[c1-l026 >] *zem. ¶* Vtomo mutomo vaqimayezu,
[c1-l026 note] After completing dizem, the next example describes not distinguishing yes from no. Wakimayezu is a negative verb form, not a malformed modern spelling.
[c1-l027 >] l, xirazu. *Diz ſe de hum homem neſcio que*
[c1-l027 note] Shirazu is an alternative to wakimayezu. The Portuguese applies the idiom to a foolish person and continues its explanation below.
[c1-l028 >] *não entende, nem percebe as couſas. ¶* V-
[c1-l028 note] The foolish person neither understands nor perceives things. V- starts another example with umu; the terminal hyphen belongs to that Japanese fragment.
[c1-l029 >] muno fedateuo xirazu. *Idem.*
[c1-l029 note] Umu no hedate wo shirazu means not knowing the distinction between existence and nonexistence or yes and no. Idem refers to the preceding foolish-person gloss.

[c1-l030] Vn. *Vitoria. Vt,* Vnuo firaqu, l, yeru.
[c1-l030 note] Un is first glossed victory, illustrated by un wo hiraku or eru, obtaining success. This begins the N subdivision after the internal heading.
[c1-l031 >] *Alcançar vitoria. ¶* Vnua tenni ari, aye-
[c1-l031 note] The first example means attaining victory. The next says fortune is in heaven; aye- begins ayete on the following physical row.
[c1-l032 >] te xirizoqu coto nacare. *Aventura, ou*
[c1-l032 note] The maxim continues with a prohibition on retreating. The Portuguese begins its rendering as fortune or victory, continuing below.
[c1-l033 >] *vitoria està no ceo, guardai vos não fujais, ou*
[c1-l033 note] The Portuguese places victory in heaven and warns the listener not to flee. The visible grave accent in està is restored without changing the provisional st allograph.
[c1-l034 >] *não vos afaſteis. ¶ Item, Fortuna, ou vẽ-*
[c1-l034 note] The warning continues: do not withdraw. A second item then glosses un as fortune or luck, with ventura divided between rows.
[c1-l035 >] *tura. Vt,* Vnni macaſuru. *Auenturar-*
[c1-l035 note] Un ni makasuru means entrusting oneself to fortune or taking a chance. Auenturar- is visibly continued by ſe below.
[c1-l036 >] *ſe. ¶* Vnga qiuamaru. *Acabarſe a*
[c1-l036 note] After completing aventurar-se, un ga kiwamaru describes good fortune reaching its end. The Portuguese noun phrase continues on the next line.
[c1-l037 >] *boa dita, ou ventura de alguem. ¶* Vn-
[c1-l037 note] The gloss completes the ending of a person's good luck. Vn- starts the next expression, un ga katamuku, without merging the physical rows.
[c1-l038 >] ga catamuqu. *Irſe acabando a boa dita.*
[c1-l038 note] Un ga katamuku means good fortune declining. The Portuguese describes a gradual ending rather than the completed loss in the preceding expression.
[c1-l039 >] *¶* Vnga tçuquru. *Acabarſe a ventura.*
[c1-l039 note] Un ga tsukuru is another expression for fortune coming to an end. The paragraph sign separates this example from the preceding one.
[c1-l040] Vnagaxi, ſu. l, fure vnagaſu. *Apregoar,*
[c1-l040 note] Unagasu, also fure unagasu, means proclaiming or giving an alarm or notice. The alternate form is Japanese, introduced by Latin vel.
[c1-l041 >] *ou dar rebate, & auiſo.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*chamar, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] The main text completes the sense of giving an alarm or warning. The displaced far-right chamar, &c. belongs in meaning to the crier definition on the next row; its opening mark and physical placement are preserved rather than reordered.
[c1-l042] Vnagaxi. *O q̃ tem este officio de apregoar, ou*
[c1-l042 note] Unagaxi here names the person whose office is proclaiming or calling out. The sentence is completed by the displaced chamar, &c. on the preceding physical row.
[c1-l043] Vnagui. *Inguia.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*dez, ou 12.* || P.
[c1-l043 note] Unagi is an eel, glossed Inguia in italic Portuguese. The far-right dez, ou 12. P. is displaced completion of the age range under Vnaico below; it is kept on this physical row, with the separate opening mark and upright poetic-use label.
[c1-l044] Vnaico. *Minino de dous, & tres annos atè*
[c1-l044 note] Unaiko denotes a child, with the Portuguese range beginning at two or three years and continuing to ten or twelve in the displaced material above. The visible initial V corrects OCR Nnaico.
[c1-l045] Vnaidori. i, Fototoguiſu. *Hum certo paſ-*
[c1-l045 note] Unaidori is equated with hototogisu. The Portuguese begins a bird description that continues at the top of the second column; the omitted initial V is restored from the scan.

[c2-l013] Vnbin. *Certa laya de Mochis, ou bolinhos fei-*

[c2-l015] Vnbu. Cumo, qiri. *Nuuem, & neuoa. S.*

[c2-l018] Vnca. Cumo, caſumi. *Nuuens, & neuoa,*

[c2-l024] Vncacu. Cumono marebito. *Cũgues, ou no-*

[c2-l028] Vnda. *Carta. S.*

[c2-l029] Vndei. Cumo, doro. *Nuuem, & lama. To-*
[c2-l029 note] Undei is explained literally as cloud and mud. The Portuguese develops this into the contrast between heaven and earth.
