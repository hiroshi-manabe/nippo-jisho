## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Fauoto. *Soido, ou eſtrondo das aſas do paſſaro.*
[c1-l001 note] Haoto is the sound of wings, explicitly described as a bird's wing-noise. The uo sequence belongs to the older spelling, not to the following hase verbs.
[c1-l002] Faxe, ſuru, eta, i. Faxiri, u. *Correr: ajun-*
[c1-l002 note] Hase is glossed by hashiri, running. The inflectional endings and explanatory i. are separate from the Portuguese sentence, whose ajun- continues below.
[c1-l003 >] *taſe eſta raiz a muitos verbos como ſe vera a-*
[c1-l003 note] The dictionary explains that this root attaches to many verbs. Both ajunta-se and abaixo cross physical row boundaries; neither fragment is a new entry.
[c1-l004 >] *baixo, & da algũa força ao verbo ſignificando*
[c1-l004 note] The prefixed root adds force to the verb. Significando leads into presteza, specifying speed rather than a separate lexical meaning on this row.
[c1-l005 >] *presteza. ¶ Item, Fazer correr. Vt,*
[c1-l005 note] Presteza completes the explanation of haste. Item adds a causative sense, making something run, illustrated with a horse on the next line.
[c1-l006 >] Vmauo faſuru. *Fazer correr o caualo.*
[c1-l006 note] Uma wo hasuru means making a horse run. The printed fasuru is retained rather than expanded to an expected hasesuru; the Portuguese confirms the causative interpretation.
[c1-l007] Faxeai, uǒ, uota. *Encontraremſe algũs cõ*
[c1-l007 note] Haseai describes people meeting in haste. The contracted inflection is supported by the NINJAL headword, but its accent is not silently supplied where the local print is worn.
[c1-l008 >] *preſſa.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes com pressa, the manner of meeting in the preceding entry. It is not a modifier of the following haseatsumari headword.
[c1-l009] Faxeatçumari, u, atta. *Ajuntarſe de preſſa.*
[c1-l009 note] Haseatsumari is gathering quickly. Atçumari represents intransitive atsumari, with -ru and -tta inflections, not the transitive atsume stem.
[c1-l010] Faxeague, uru, eta. *Dar a Fune ſobre algũa*
[c1-l010 note] Haseage here concerns running a vessel aground, not simply raising something. Fune is a Japanese boat-word embedded in Portuguese; its slant here differs from the clearly Roman occurrence two rows below, so this occurrence remains italic.
[c1-l011 >] *pedra, ou em algum baixo, &c. Vt,* Fune
[c1-l011 note] A rock or shoal is the obstacle the boat strikes. The final Fune begins the Japanese example and must join uo on the following row.
[c1-l012 >] uo xeni faxeagueta. *Deu a* Fune *em hũa*
[c1-l012 note] Fune wo se ni haseageta describes grounding a boat on a shoal. The second Fune is again embedded in Portuguese and printed Roman, unlike its italic surroundings.
[c1-l013 >] *restinga.*
[c1-l013 note] Restinga completes the shoal or sandbank description. Its short standalone row is part of the boat example, not page furniture.
[c1-l014] Faxearuqi, u, uita. *Correr de cà, pera là.*
[c1-l014 note] Hasearuki is running to and fro, literally combining haste with walking about. The final grave accent is visible in là, matching the de cà / pera là opposition.
[c1-l015] Faxeauaxe, ſuru, eta. *Fazer encontrar.*
[c1-l015 note] Haseawase is causing a meeting or encounter. Its transitive force contrasts with haseai above and is expressed by Portuguese fazer encontrar.
[c1-l016 >] *Vt,* Areni faxeauaxe, coreni ſaxiauaxe
[c1-l016 note] Are ni haseawase, kore ni sashiawase gives successive encounters in combat. Sashiawase is a plausible distinct verb, not automatically changed to another hase compound.
[c1-l017 >] tatacǒta. Feiq. *Lib. 4. Pelejaua en*
[c1-l017 note] Tatakōta completes the fighting example. Feiq. is the Roman Heike citation label; the following italic Portuguese identifies book four and begins the translation.
[c1-l018 >] *contrandoſe ora com aquelle, ora com eſte.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes encontrando-se: he fought, encountering now that opponent and now this one. This translation supports the alternating are/kore construction above.
[c1-l019] Faxecayeri, u, etta. *Tornarſe de preſſa.*
[c1-l019 note] Hasekaeri means returning quickly. The cayeri spelling represents kaeri and does not require a modernized kana-based spelling in Level 1.
[c1-l020] Faxetçudoi, ô, ôta. *Vir muita gente con-*
[c1-l020 note] Hasetsudoi describes many people assembling or coming successively. Its position before hasechigai is printed here and is not reordered to improve alphabetization.
[c1-l021 >] *tinuando.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes continuando, describing the continuing arrival of a crowd. The fragment belongs to hasetsudoi despite the intervening physical break.
[c1-l022] Faxechigai, ǒ, ǒta. *Correndo deſencõtrarſe.*
[c1-l022 note] Hasechigai means missing or passing one another while running. The scan contracts the nasal in desencontrar-se as õ, rather than printing a separate n after o.
[c1-l023] Faxechiri, u, itta. *Eſpalharſe muita gente*
[c1-l023 note] Hasechiri is scattering in haste. Chiri is the intransitive scattering verb; the Portuguese subject is a multitude of people.
[c1-l024 >] *com preſſa.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the manner of scattering, com pressa. Its placement is distinct from the next entry's downward movement.
[c1-l025] Faxecudari, u, atta. *Decer de preſſa.*
[c1-l025 note] Hasekudari means descending quickly. Decer is the printed historical Portuguese spelling corresponding to descer, not an error to modernize.
[c1-l026] Faxecuuauari, u, atta. *Correndo meterſe ẽ-*
[c1-l026 note] Hasekuwawari means running to join others. The repeated u letters encode kuwawari; the Portuguese ẽ- begins entre and retains its contraction.
[c1-l027 >] *tre outros.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes entre outros, entering among other people. This establishes the joining sense of kuwawaru rather than merely passing them.
[c1-l028] Faxefuſagari, u, atta. *Tapar, ou impedir al-*
[c1-l028 note] Hasefusagari is blocking a place by running into position. Fusagari supplies the obstruction meaning reflected by tapar and impedir.
[c1-l029 >] *gum lugar correndo.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes algum lugar correndo: some place is blocked while running. The split al-gum is Portuguese and not part of the next Japanese headword.
[c1-l030] Faxei. Namino coye. *Som das ondas.*
[c1-l030 note] Hasei is wave-sound, clarified by nami no koe. This is a Sino-Japanese noun, plausibly 波声, not another compound of hase 'run'.
[c1-l031] Faxeiri, iru, itta. *Entrar depreſſa.*
[c1-l031 note] Haseiri is entering quickly. The explicit iru ending agrees with the entering verb and distinguishes it from neighboring forms with ordinary -ru endings.
[c1-l032] Faxemairi, iru, itta. *Ir, ou vir de preſſa.*
[c1-l032 note] Hasemairi is going or coming in haste. Mairi has the directional/humble verb sense; the worn middle letters agree with the NINJAL headword.
[c1-l033] Faxemauari, u, atta. *Rodear cõ preſſa.*
[c1-l033 note] Hasemawari is going around quickly. Mauari represents mawari, and the Portuguese reinforces circular movement rather than arrival.
[c1-l034] Faxemeguri, u, utta. *Rodear indo de cà*
[c1-l034 note] Hasemeguri likewise describes going around. The compressed utta ending is retained as printed and also appears in the NINJAL headword, rather than being expanded to megutta.
[c1-l035 >] *pera là correndo.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes going from here to there while running. The paired directional adverbs explain the range of the circling movement.
[c1-l036] Faxemucai, ǒ, ǒta. *Encontrarſe de preſſa.*
[c1-l036 note] Hasemukai is running to meet or face someone. The meeting definition is consistent with mukau and the contracted open-o inflections.
[c1-l037] Faxen. Yabureta fune. *Embarcaçaõ quebrada,*
[c1-l037 note] Hasen means a damaged vessel, clarified as yabureta fune. This noun, plausibly 破船, is unrelated to the adjacent running compounds despite its similar beginning.
[c1-l038 >] *ou deſfeita.*
[c1-l038 note] Adds 'or broken up' to the vessel definition. The physical fragment remains separate from hasenarabi below; no modern spelling is substituted.
[c1-l039] Faxenarabi, u, ǒda. *Irem iguaes com preſſa*
[c1-l039 note] Hasenarabi means proceeding abreast in haste. The contracted -ōda form is historically possible and supported by NINJAL, so it is not normalized to naranda.
[c1-l040 >] *como funes, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] Boats are given as the example of moving abreast. Funes is a Portuguese plural built on Japanese fune and is italic in this occurrence.
[c1-l041] Faxenobori, u, otta. *Subir de preſſa.*
[c1-l041 note] Hasenobori is ascending quickly. The ending otta combines with the preceding stem convention to represent nobotta, not a separate word.
[c1-l042] Faxenobi, u, onda, l, bita. *Correndo paſſar*
[c1-l042 note] Hasenobi means running ahead. The two listed past endings reflect alternative conjugational treatment, also preserved in the NINJAL headword; they are not collapsed to one modern form.
[c1-l043 >] *diante.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes passar diante, passing ahead. Although only one word long, this is the end of the hasenobi definition and is correctly associated with its own physical row.
[c1-l044] Faxenuqe, uru, eta. *Paſſar diante de outro*
[c1-l044 note] Hasenuke is overtaking another person. Nuke gives the passing-through or getting-ahead sense specified by diante de outro.
[c1-l045 >] *correndo.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes the overtaking definition by specifying running. The row belongs to hasenuke, not the arrival entry below.
[c1-l046] Faxeſanji, uru, ita. *Vir correndo, ou com-*
[c1-l046 note] Hasesanji means coming in haste, a compound with sanjiru/sanzuru. The final printed division mark separates com from pressa on the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *preſſa.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes com pressa at the foot of the column. It is body text rather than a catchword; the next column begins a new hase compound.

[c2-l001] Faxeſugui, uru, ita. *Paſſar auãte de preſſa.*
[c2-l001 note] Hasesugi means passing ahead quickly. Sugiru supplies the passing-beyond sense, consistent with Portuguese avante.
[c2-l002] Faxeſuſumi, u, unda. *Ir a diante.*
[c2-l002 note] Hasesusumi is advancing or pressing forward. Its -sumi/-sunda inflection is coherent and distinct from the preceding -sugiru verb.
[c2-l003] Faxetauoxi, ſu, oita. *Derrubar correndo.*
[c2-l003 note] Hasetaoshi means driving something down while running. The oita ending is retained as printed, not normalized to a modern taoshita form.
[c2-l004 >] *Vt,* Tacaguixi yori vmauo faxetauoxite, &c.
[c2-l004 note] Takagishi yori uma wo hasetaoshite describes driving a horse down from a high bank. The Japanese object and source phrase agree with the following Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l005 >] *Derrubou, ou deitou em baxo o caualo corren-*
[c2-l005 note] The translation describes bringing the horse down while running. Baxo is the historical printed form, and corren- continues into correndo below.
[c2-l006 >] *do de cima de hũa rocha, ou lugar ingreme.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes the descent from a rock or steep place. This confirms takagishi as the elevated bank or cliff rather than a personal name.
[c2-l007] Faxetçuqi, u, uita. *Chegar correndo.*
[c2-l007 note] Hasetsuki is arriving at a run. Tsuku supplies arrival, with the listed past ending representing tsuita.
[c2-l008] Faxetouori, u, otta. *Paſſar correndo.*
[c2-l008 note] Hasetōri is running past or through. The touori spelling preserves the older vowel sequence rather than replacing it with an accented vowel.
[c2-l009] Faxeuocure, uru, eta. *Ficar a tras corrẽdo.*
[c2-l009 note] Haseokure is falling behind while running. The printed wo spelling and nasal contraction in corrẽdo are retained independently of modern orthography.
[c2-l010] Faxeyoxe, ſuru, eta. *Chegar algũa couſa*
[c2-l010 note] Haseyose is bringing something close quickly. Chegar is used transitively here; the following row specifies the manner.
[c2-l011 >] *aſſi com preſſa.*
[c2-l011 note] Completes the hastened bringing-near definition. The demonstrative manner-word aſſi belongs to Portuguese, not Japanese ashi.
[c2-l012] Faxi. *Dous paoſinhos com que comẽ os Iapoẽs.*
[c2-l012 note] Hashi here means chopsticks, described as two little sticks used for eating. The following identical headwords represent different senses, not duplicate OCR lines.
[c2-l013] Faxi. *Bico de paſſaro.*
[c2-l013 note] This hashi is a bird's beak. Portuguese bico distinguishes it from chopsticks above and an edge below.
[c2-l014] Faxi. *Borda, ou cabo de algũa couſa.*
[c2-l014 note] Hashi is an edge or end. Cabo, with a worn b visible in the scan, fits the definition; calo 'callus' would not explain this paired gloss.
[c2-l015] Faxi. *Eſcada.*
[c2-l015 note] Hashi is here a ladder or stair, an older lexical sense. It is not silently expanded to modern hashigo.
[c2-l016] Faxi. *Ponte. ¶* Faxiuo vataſu. *Fazer,*
[c2-l016 note] Hashi now means a bridge. Hashi wo watasu is constructing or laying a bridge across, not the intransitive crossing example below.
[c2-l017 >] *ou atraueſar a ponte no rio. ¶* Faxiuo fi-
[c2-l017 note] The translation completes spanning the river. The next Japanese example begins hashi wo hi-, which continues as hiku on the following row.
[c2-l018 >] qu. *Tirar a ponte. ¶* Faxiuo vataru.
[c2-l018 note] Hi-qu gives hiku, removing the bridge. The next example hashi wo wataru switches to crossing it, preserving the transitive/intransitive contrast.
[c2-l019 >] *Paſſar a ponte. ¶* Faxiuo caquru. *Ar-*
[c2-l019 note] Crossing the bridge translates wataru. Hashi wo kakuru then introduces setting up a bridge, using the historical kakuru form rather than modern kakeru.
[c2-l020 >] *mar, ou por ponte.*
[c2-l020 note] Completes armar, ou por ponte, erecting or placing a bridge. This short continuation is not an independent Japanese item.
[c2-l021] Faxi, ſuru. *Deſmãchar as cõtas feitas. Vide,* Faſã-
[c2-l021 note] Hashi/suru here concerns undoing completed accounts, not a bridge. The reference to hasan points back to the accounting entry; the terminal sign is retained without assuming modern spelling.
[c2-l022] Faxibami. *Hũa certa fruita.*
[c2-l022 note] Hashibami is a kind of fruit or nut, plausibly hazel. The dictionary itself gives only this broad identification, distinct from the woodworking homonym below.
[c2-l023] Faxibami. *Taboinhas que ſe metem, & en-*
[c2-l023 note] The woodworking hashibami consists of small boards inserted into others. En- begins encaixão, the fitting or jointing action continued below.
[c2-l024 >] *caxão em outras nas bordas como em meſas,*
[c2-l024 note] The boards fit into the edges of other boards, as in tables. This describes an edge joint or batten, not the nut of the previous entry.
[c2-l025 >] *&c. pera não empenarem.*
[c2-l025 note] The purpose is to prevent warping. Empenar has its woodworking sense here; the negative is essential to the explanation.
[c2-l026] Faxibaxi. *Bordas, ou cabos dalgũa couſa.*
[c2-l026 note] Hashibashi is the reduplicated edges or ends of something. The voiced second member bashi is reflected by b rather than a repeated faxi spelling.
[c2-l027 >] ¶ Zaixono faxibaxi. *Arrabaldes do lu-*
[c2-l027 note] Zaisho no hashibashi denotes the outskirts of a settlement. Arrabaldes supports the peripheral-place sense rather than literal objects' ends.
[c2-l028 >] *gar. ¶ Item, Pouco. Vt,* Faxibaxiuo
[c2-l028 note] Lugar completes the settlement gloss. Item adds a small-amount sense; hashibashi wo begins the hearing example below.
[c2-l029 >] qijta, l, vqecamauatta. *Ouui leuemente,*
[c2-l029 note] The intended Japanese is kiita or uketamawatta, having heard only portions. The scan's damaged t was misread as i; qijta retains the visibly descending second vowel letter rather than silently normalizing it to qiita.
[c2-l030 >] *ou pouco do que paſſou*
[c2-l030 note] Completes hearing lightly or only a little of what happened. This translation makes the hearing-verb interpretation above much stronger than a literal reading of the original OCR qijia.
[c2-l031] Faxibune. *Embarcação pequena como man-*
[c2-l031 note] Hashibune is a small boat. The Portuguese compares it with a manchua and a little ship's boat, continuing across the row break.
[c2-l032 >] *chua, ou batelinho.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes manchua, followed by batelinho. Both are vessel comparisons and do not belong to the measles entry below.
[c2-l033] Faxica. *Sarampão. ¶* Faxicauo ſuru.
[c2-l033 note] Hashika means measles; hashika wo suru is having the illness. The scan reads Sarampão with a, matching the repeated disease name on the next row.
[c2-l034 >] *Ter ſarampão.*
[c2-l034 note] Translates the Japanese illness expression as having measles. Its lowercase initial is independent of the capitalized noun on the preceding row.
[c2-l035] Faxica, l, nogui. *Pragana de trigo, ou arroz*
[c2-l035 note] A separate hashika, also nogi, is an awn of wheat or rice. The Portuguese botanical gloss prevents confusing it with the preceding disease homonym.
[c2-l036] Faxicai. *Couſa aſpera, & deſabrida como veſ-*
[c2-l036 note] Hashikai describes something rough or harsh. The first illustration is coarse clothing against bare skin, with vestido split across rows.
[c2-l037 >] *tido groſſeiro ſobre a carne nua, &c. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l037 note] Completes vestido grosseiro, coarse clothing on bare flesh. The scan preserves a faint initial g and the long-s pair, not OCR rolleiro; Itẽ begins the additional brittle-material sense.
[c2-l038 >] *Couſa facil de quebrar como ferro fraco, &*
[c2-l038 note] Adds something easily broken, such as weak iron. This extends hashikai from surface harshness to brittleness rather than introducing a new headword.
[c2-l039 >] *quebradiço. ¶ Per met.* Faxicai fito.
[c2-l039 note] Quebradiço completes brittle iron. Per met. marks a metaphorical human application: hashikai hito, a sharp or harsh person.
[c2-l040 >] *Homem que da facilmente no coração de outro*
[c2-l040 note] The person easily strikes another's heart or feelings. The following explanation specifies blunt speech, not a literal physical blow.
[c2-l041 >] *falando logo o que ſente ſem reſguardo, nem*
[c2-l041 note] Such a person immediately says what they think without restraint. Nem connects to brandura below, completing the absence of gentleness.
[c2-l042 >] *brandura. ¶ Item, Homem agudo, & ve-*
[c2-l042 note] Brandura completes the harsh-speaking sense. Item then adds an acute, quick person, a potentially more positive intellectual sense of the adjective.
[c2-l043 >] *loz no entender, & fallar leuemente.*
[c2-l043 note] Completes veloz: quick in understanding and speaking readily. This belongs to the perceptive-person sense rather than the earlier brittle-material definition.
[c2-l044] Faxicai qi. *Pao que quebra facilmente.*
[c2-l044 note] Hashikai ki is wood that breaks easily. Ki is the Japanese noun for wood; Portuguese pao is not normalized to modern pão.
[c2-l045] Faxigacari. *Lugar no theatro que eſta de bai-*
[c2-l045 note] Hashigakari is the theatre passage or approach. The definition begins with its position below an arch; the physical bai- break belongs to debaixo.
[c2-l046 >] *xo de hum arco por onde ſaem as figuras a pu-*
[c2-l046 note] Performers emerge to public view through this arch or passage. Figuras refers to stage characters, not printed illustrations.
[c2-l047 >] *blico.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes publico in the theatre definition. The short row is body text and remains separate from the signature and catchword beneath it.
