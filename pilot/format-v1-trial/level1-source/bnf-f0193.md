## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Fiqichiraxi, u, aita. *Eſpalhar, ou por al-*
[c1-l001 note] Hikichirasu is scattering or spreading out. The headword starts this leaf and confirms that the preceding Fiqi is a catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *gũa couſa eſtendida como em prato, bande-*
[c1-l002 note] The thing is spread across a plate or tray. Both algũa and bandeja are divided across physical lines without changing the sentence structure.
[c1-l003 >] *ja, &c.*
[c1-l003 note] Ja finishes bandeja, tray; this short fragment belongs to the gloss rather than Japanese ja.
[c1-l004] Fiqicomori, ru, otta. *Recolherſe, ou es-*
[c1-l004 note] Hikikomoru is withdrawing or remaining shut in. Recolher-se gives the first equivalent and es- begins estar.
[c1-l005 >] *tar encerrado.*
[c1-l005 note] Estar encerrado completes being enclosed or secluded. It is the second Portuguese equivalent of hikikomoru.
[c1-l006] Fiqicoto. *Couſa que ſe alega como exemplo,*
[c1-l006 note] Hikikoto is something adduced as an example, connecting hiku's citation sense with koto.
[c1-l007 >] *hiſtoria, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] A story is another example of what may be cited. Historia remains within the same gloss.
[c1-l008] Fiqicoxi, ſu, oita. *Puxando por algum pao*
[c1-l008 note] Hikikosu is pulling timber over an obstruction. Its oita past is preserved rather than regularized from modern morphology.
[c1-l009 >] *paſſar monte, ou outro lugar alto, &c. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l009 note] The obstruction can be a mountain or high place. The second tall-looking type in paſſar is not an l forming palſar.
[c1-l010 >] *Deſaparecerem as aues quando as ſeguem por*
[c1-l010 note] An additional sense concerns birds disappearing while pursued. Por is split visually at the edge with a lowered r, not a comma after po.
[c1-l011 >] *cauſa dalgum monte, ou ſerra que eſtà dian-*
[c1-l011 note] A mountain or ridge in front accounts for the disappearance. Diante continues below, and its small division mark is displaced downward.
[c1-l012 >] *te principalmente ſe diz das gallinhas do*
[c1-l012 note] The usage particularly concerns gallinhas do mato, wild birds; retain this description instead of imposing a precise modern species.
[c1-l013 >] *mato. Item, No Ximo ſe diz de gente que*
[c1-l013 note] The regional note extends the expression in Ximo to people. Ximo is a Japanese regional label inside Portuguese.
[c1-l014 >] *ſe encobre, ou deſaparece com algum mon-*
[c1-l014 note] Those people become hidden or disappear behind a mountain; this is the regional extension of the preceding bird sense.
[c1-l015 >] *te, &c.*
[c1-l015 note] Te completes monte, ending the terrain qualification. It is not a Japanese hand noun here.
[c1-l016] Fiqicudaxi, u. aita. *Abaixar algũa couſa*
[c1-l016 note] Hikikudasu is drawing something downward. The following phrase states how the lowering is done.
[c1-l017 >] *puxando por ella.*
[c1-l017 note] Puxando porella explains that one lowers the object by pulling it; the fused porella spelling is retained.
[c1-l018] Fiqida. *Meliùs*, Coxifiqi. *Coxo, ou manco.*
[c1-l018 note] Hikida is glossed as lame or limping, with koshihiki expressly preferred. The damaged F is still recoverable as an initial.
[c1-l019] Fiqidaxi, u, aita. *Tirar fora puxando.*
[c1-l019 note] Hikidasu is extracting something by pulling outward, a compact literal compound definition.
[c1-l020] Fiqidemono. *Premio, ou dadiua que dà peſ-*
[c1-l020 note] Hikidemono is a reward or gift. The social relation of giver and recipient follows below.
[c1-l021 >] *ſoa mais nobre, ou igual a outra.*
[c1-l021 note] The giver is superior or equal to the recipient, a restriction in this particular gift definition.
[c1-l022] Fiqifada. *Pelle curtida, delgada.*
[c1-l022 note] Hikihada is thin tanned hide. Curtida qualifies the prepared leather, not a Japanese inflection.
[c1-l023] Fiqifa. *Tempo, ou conjunção de aleuantar*
[c1-l023 note] Hikiha denotes the time or opportunity to break camp; conjunção here expresses occasion.
[c1-l024 >] *o arrayal.*
[c1-l024 note] O arrayal completes the camp-breaking gloss and identifies what is raised or abandoned.
[c1-l025] Fiqifagui, u, aida. *Eſfolar, ou deſpir.*
[c1-l025 note] Hikihagu means stripping skin or clothing away. The aida ending agrees with the voiced g-series of hagu.
[c1-l026] Fiqifanachi, u, aita. *Deſapegar, ou a-*
[c1-l026 note] Hikihanatsu is detaching or separating. The second Portuguese infinitive is divided apartar.
[c1-l027 >] *partar.*
[c1-l027 note] Partar completes apartar; this is not a new entry or omitted Japanese word.
[c1-l028] Fiqifanare, uru, eta. *Apartarſe.*
[c1-l028 note] Hikihanaruru is separating oneself, in contrast with the transitive hanatsu/hanasu entries nearby.
[c1-l029] Fiqifanaxi, u, aita. *Apartar, ou deſapegar.*
[c1-l029 note] Hikihanasu returns to the transitive meaning of separating or detaching something.
[c1-l030] Fiqifari, u, atta. *Puxando eſtirar, ou ente*
[c1-l030 note] Hikiharu means stretching taut by pulling, as confirmed by the bow example. An OCR t in the headword obscured haru.
[c1-l031 >] *ſar. Vt,* Yumiuo fiſaxiqu fiqifatte ireba,
[c1-l031 note] Yumi o hisashiku hikihatte ireba describes keeping a bow drawn or strung for a long time. The consequence comes in the next row.
[c1-l032 >] yunjei tçucuru. *Estando o arco muito tem-*
[c1-l032 note] Yunzei tsukuru means the bow's strength is exhausted. The Portuguese bow context and visible initial y support yunjei rather than xunjei.
[c1-l033 >] *po armado afroxaſe. ¶ Item,* Fiqifaru.
[c1-l033 note] A bow left strung loses tension. Item then introduces another hikiharu use rather than continuing the bow example.
[c1-l034 >] *Querer comprar de hũa parte, & de outra al*
[c1-l034 note] Two sides both want to buy the same thing, described figuratively as pulling from opposite directions.
[c1-l035 >] *gũa couſa como quem puxa por cada parte.*
[c1-l035 note] The comparison with each side pulling completes that competing-purchasers explanation.
[c1-l036] Fiqifataqe, uru, eta. *Abrir, & eſtender.*
[c1-l036 note] Hikihatakeru is opening or spreading something out. The examples below specify limbs and objects.
[c1-l037 >] *Vt,* Xuſocuuo fiqi fataquru. *Abrir, &*
[c1-l037 note] Shusoku o hiki hatakuru means opening out hands and feet, with shusoku as the paired limb noun.
[c1-l038 >] *alargar as mãos, & pès. ¶* Qino yeda,
[c1-l038 note] Hands and feet finish the first example. Ki no yeda starts the next, concerning branches.
[c1-l039 >] bunmauaxi, nadouo fiqifataquru. *Eſtẽ-*
[c1-l039 note] Branches and bunmawashi, a compass, are spread open. The infinitive ends quru, not the OCR quiu.
[c1-l040 >] *der, & a largar ramos de aruore, abrir o com*
[c1-l040 note] Spreading branches and opening a compass translate those two Japanese objects. Keep the visibly separated a largar.
[c1-l041 >] *paßo, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] Passo completes compasso, the drawing instrument, not a step in this context.
[c1-l042] Fiqifazzuxi, ſu, uita. *Deſencaixar a cou-*
[c1-l042 note] Hikihazusu means dislodging something from its fitted position. Its zzu spelling is preserved in the diplomatic text.
[c1-l043 >] *ſa de ſeu lugar como pee de banco, deſarmar pa-*
[c1-l043 note] A bench leg or dismantled hangings illustrates the removal. Pee is the visibly doubled-vowel word for foot.
[c1-l044 >] *nos darmar, &c. ¶ Item, Permet*
[c1-l044 note] Panos darmar completes the furnishings example. Permet introduces a figurative extension rather than a separate Japanese word.
[c1-l045 >] *Apartarſe, ou eſcapulirſe de algũa par-*
[c1-l045 note] Separating oneself or slipping away from a place supplies that figurative sense.
[c1-l046 >] *te, &c.*
[c1-l046 note] Te finishes parte, completing the departure-from-a-place gloss.
[c1-l047] Fiqifiraqi, u, aita. *Abrir com algũa força*
[c1-l047 note] Hikihiraku means opening with force or by pulling. Both the initial F and the internal fi need recovery from faint print.
[c1-l048 >] *ou puxando.*
[c1-l048 note] Ou puxando finishes the explanation of how the opening is accomplished; the bottom rule is visible beneath this row.

## column-2 [column] Column 2

[c2-l001] Fiqifirogue, uru, eta. *Abrir, ou eſtender.*
[c2-l001 note] Hikihirogeru is opening or extending something broadly, related to hiroi rather than the previous hiraku compound.
[c2-l002] Fiqi focorobacaxi, u, aita. *Puxando des*
[c2-l002 note] Hiki hokorobakasu describes pulling stitching apart. Des begins the Portuguese descoſer over the row break.
[c2-l003 >] *coſer veſtidos, &c.*
[c2-l003 note] Unsewing garments completes the preceding verb's definition. Coſer supplies the sewing component of descoſer.
[c2-l004] Fiqifuxe, uru, eta. *Derrubar algũa couſa*
[c2-l004 note] Hikifuseru is throwing or pulling something down. The destination follows below.
[c2-l005 >] *no chaõ.*
[c2-l005 note] No chaõ specifies the ground as the destination. Preserve the tilde carrier rather than normalizing to modern chão.
[c2-l006] Fiqigairu. i, Vacufiqi. *Manduco.*
[c2-l006 note] Hikigairu is given the synonym Vacufiqi and the unusual gloss Manduco. The animal-like headword is recognizable, but the local synonym and Portuguese label remain uncertain; preserve them rather than inventing an explanation.
[c2-l007] Fiqigui. *Pao com que fazem andar à roda,*
[c2-l007 note] Hikigi is a wooden handle used to rotate something, identified as a millstone in the continuation.
[c2-l008 >] *a mò de moer cha, farinha, &c.*
[c2-l008 note] A mò is the millstone for grinding tea or flour. The feminine article is visible and matches mò; cha is Japanese-derived material within the gloss.
[c2-l009] Fiqiguiri. *Hũa laya de verruma grande de que*
[c2-l009 note] Hikigiri is a large boring tool. The g in giri is important, distinguishing it from a cutting compound.
[c2-l010 >] *uſaõ os carpinteiros de nauios.*
[c2-l010 note] Ship carpenters use this large auger. The Portuguese relative clause completes the tool definition.
[c2-l011] Fiqiguxi, ſuru, ita. *Leuar conſigo gente.*
[c2-l011 note] Hikigusu is taking people along with oneself. The headword's suru/ita sequence gives the verb's inflectional information.
[c2-l012 >] *Vt,* Fitouo fiqiguſuru.
[c2-l012 note] Hito o hikigusuru explicitly illustrates bringing a person along; the whole phrase is Japanese after Vt.
[c2-l013] Fiqiidaxi, ſu, aita. *Puxando tirar fora.*
[c2-l013 note] Hikiidasu is pulling something out. The double i records the compound boundary and must not be collapsed to hikidasu merely because both entries exist.
[c2-l014] Fiqijei. *Gente que ſe vai retirando, ou aco-*
[c2-l014 note] Hikizei denotes retreating personnel or forces; zei is the group element rather than a Portuguese syllable.
[c2-l015 >] *lhendo.*
[c2-l015 note] Acolhendo completes the withdrawing sense applied to the people above.
[c2-l016] Fiqiire, uru, eta. *Meter dentro. ¶* Fito
[c2-l016 note] Hikiireru is drawing someone or something inside. The two adjacent i types correspond to hiki plus ire, repaired from OCR omission.
[c2-l017 >] uo acudǒni fiqi iruru. *Meter a alguem em*
[c2-l017 note] Hito o akudō ni hiki iruru is leading someone onto an evil path. The compound's segmentation is explicit in the example.
[c2-l018 >] *peccados, ou no caminho da maldade.*
[c2-l018 note] Sins or the path of wickedness explains akudō in this figurative application.
[c2-l019] Fiqi ire. *Meter dentro na fortaleza a alguem*
[c2-l019 note] Hiki ire is bringing someone inside a fortress, with a treacherous qualification on the next line.
[c2-l020 >] *por traição, ou na caſa pera furtar. ¶* Fi-
[c2-l020 note] The entry includes admitting someone by betrayal or admitting a thief into a house. Fi- starts its suru example.
[c2-l021 >] quireuo ſuru. *Meter alguẽ desta maneira.*
[c2-l021 note] The printed Fiquireuo ſuru is retained even though the surrounding entry favours hikiire; the line visibly lacks the expected extra i. The meaning is admission in this treacherous manner.
[c2-l022] Fiqiiro. *Moſtra de ſe querer retirar, ou a-*
[c2-l022 note] Hikiiro is an appearance or indication of retreat. The compound hiki plus iro explains the adjacent i types missed by OCR.
[c2-l023 >] *colher. ¶* Fiqiironi natta, l, fiqiironi
[c2-l023 note] Hikiiro ni natta or hikiiro ni miyeta means becoming or appearing ready to withdraw. Both examples require the same iro element.
[c2-l024 >] miyeta. *Mostrarem os inimigos geito de ſe*
[c2-l024 note] The enemies show signs of retreat; inimigos is the Portuguese noun, repaired from an m/n confusion.
[c2-l025 >] *retirarem.*
[c2-l025 note] Retirarem completes the appearance-of-retreat gloss, not a separate instruction.
[c2-l026] Fiqiiru. *Verb. defect. Vt,* Icuſauo fiqi
[c2-l026 note] Hikiiru is labelled a defective verb and illustrated with an army. Preserve this historical form rather than replacing it with modern hikiiru spelling conventions.
[c2-l027 >] iru. *Capitanear, ou dirigir, & menear o*
[c2-l027 note] Commanding, directing and managing explain the military leadership verb. Iru continues the Japanese example from above.
[c2-l028 >] *exercito.*
[c2-l028 note] O exercito is the object of those leadership verbs, completing the gloss.
[c2-l029] Fiqimadouaxi, ſu, aita. *Guiar mal, ou fa-*
[c2-l029 note] Hikimadowasu is leading astray. Madouaxi is a continuous compound element, not ma followed by a separate word.
[c2-l030 >] *zer errar o caminho. ¶ Item, Permet. Fa*
[c2-l030 note] Misleading someone's route is extended figuratively by Permet. The next fazer begins at the row end.
[c2-l031 >] *zer errar a alguem no caminho da ſaluação, ſci*
[c2-l031 note] One can lead another astray in salvation or learning. Sci- belongs to sciencias below.
[c2-l032 >] *encias, &c.*
[c2-l032 note] Sciencias completes the domains in which misleading guidance may occur.
[c2-l033] Fiqimaqi, u, aita. *Emburilhar, ou amarrar*
[c2-l033 note] Hikimaku is wrapping or tying. Emburilhar is preserved as printed, rather than silently reordered to modern embrulhar.
[c2-l034 >] *como cingindoſe, &c.*
[c2-l034 note] Girding oneself illustrates the wrapping action; cingindo-se clarifies how the object is wound around.
[c2-l035] Fiqimauari, u, atta. *Rodear puxando, ou*
[c2-l035 note] Hikimawaru is going round while pulling or leading. The visible m must not become ni in the kana reading.
[c2-l036 >] *leuando alguem.*
[c2-l036 note] Taking someone along completes the second manner of going round.
[c2-l037] Fiqimauaxi. *Certa laya de ſerra de carpinteiro.*
[c2-l037 note] Hikimawashi is a carpenter's saw in this entry. Serra is the tool noun; ferra was a meaning-changing long-s error.
[c2-l038] Fiqimauaxi. *Fauor que ſe faz a algum hoſpede*
[c2-l038 note] The next hikimawashi means assistance to a guest. Hospede is recognizable despite the OCR b/o errors.
[c2-l039 >] *encaminhandoo, inſtruindo, &c.*
[c2-l039 note] Guiding and instructing the guest explain the assistance sense, distinct from the saw noun.
[c2-l040] Fiqimauaxi, ſu, aita. *Puxando fazer rode-*
[c2-l040 note] Hikimawasu is making something turn or go round by pulling. The verb's literal example follows.
[c2-l041 >] *ar. Vt,* Vmauo fiqimauaſu. *Fazer ro-*
[c2-l041 note] Uma o hikimawasu is leading a horse round. Ro- starts rodear in the Portuguese repetition.
[c2-l042 >] *dear o caualo. ¶ Item, Fauorecer, ou en-*
[c2-l042 note] Rodear o cavalo completes that example. Item introduces helping or directing someone as a figurative extension.
[c2-l043 >] *caminhar, & inſtruir a alguem que não ſabe ẽ*
[c2-l043 note] The person lacks knowledge in some affairs, and receives direction or instruction. The final ẽ abbreviates the preposition.
[c2-l044 >] *negocios, &c.*
[c2-l044 note] Negocios completes the domain of assistance, meaning affairs or dealings rather than necessarily modern commerce.
[c2-l045] Fiqime. *Hũa certa laya de frechas a ma-*
[c2-l045 note] Hikime denotes a type of arrow. Its shape and use are explained in the next two lines.
[c2-l046 >] *neira de rocas, dentro das quais metem cartas q̃*
[c2-l046 note] The arrows are compared to distaffs and contain letters inside. Preserve the source's quais spelling and abbreviated q̃.
[c2-l047 >] *lanção à parte dos inimigos.*
[c2-l047 note] The letter-carrying arrows are shot toward the enemy side. The following Fiqi is separate page furniture.
