[c1-l001 >] Quanjǒjini itoma aqiarazu. *Não ſe pode*
[c1-l001 note] The example continues the preceding entry: the pen cannot exhaust the matter in writing. The visible aqiarazu is retained; its internal qia is not silently regularized to an expected Japanese expression.
[c1-l002 >] *acabar de eſcreuer, ou eſgotar com a pena.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the preceding negative construction: one cannot finish writing about it or exhaust it with the pen. Eſgotar is the Portuguese verb for exhausting a subject.
[c1-l003] Quanjǒſu. l. quanjǒji. i, Fude. *Pena de*
[c1-l003 note] Quanjǒſu and quanjǒji are alternative literary names for a writing brush, explicitly identified by Fude. The two carons and the long ſ in the first form are distinct printed details.
[c1-l004 >] *eſcreuer. S.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes pena de escrever, a writing implement. The upright S. is the written-register label, not part of the Portuguese noun phrase. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l005] Quanju. *Numero de vezes que hum Bonzo*
[c1-l005 note] Quanju here concerns the number of recitations undertaken by a Buddhist cleric. The definition develops across the next four physical lines.
[c1-l006 >] *lẽ, ou promete de ler o Foquequiǒ por algum de*
[c1-l006 note] The cleric reads, or promises to read, the Lotus Sutra for a deceased person. Foquequiǒ is the embedded Japanese title, here in italic type; its final mark is a caron. De continues into funto without a supplied hyphen.

[c1-l007 note] Funto completes defunto. The paper recording the recitations is offered tied to a branch; hũ abbreviates the indefinite article and is not expanded.
[c1-l008 >] *ramo à peßoa aquem quer moſtrar que faz esta*
[c1-l008 note] The branch is presented to the person whom the cleric wishes to inform of this pious act. The printed aquem remains joined.
[c1-l009 >] *obra pia.*
[c1-l009 note] Obra pia completes the account of the commemorative religious undertaking. This short continuation belongs to the preceding Quanju definition.
[c1-l010] Quanju. *Certo officio, ou cargo de Bonzos que*

[c1-l020] Quanqen. Quanji miru. *Conſideração, ou*
[c1-l020 note] Quanqen and its Japanese explanation Quanji miru concern contemplation or seeing reflectively. The artificial OCR space within Quanji is removed; the Portuguese alternative continues below.

[c1-l022 note] This different Quanqen means limited knowledge. The following image of looking at the sky through a narrow tube explains the restriction metaphorically. The final e of que sits low at the right edge but belongs to this word, not a separate body row.

[c1-l028] Quanqua. Yamome vonna, yamome voto-

[c1-l031 note] The Japanese list continues with a widowed man, an orphan, and a person alone. Viuua begins the Portuguese enumeration that follows.

[c1-l034 note] Quanquacu is glossed as a tomb or burial place. The short Portuguese definition is complete on this row.
[c1-l035] Quanquanto. *Adu. Deſcanſadamente. Vt,*
[c1-l035 note] Quanquanto is explicitly classified as an adverb, meaning at ease. Italic Vt, introduces the example of an untroubled way of life.
[c1-l036 >] Yǔyǔquanquanto xite curaſu. *Leuar boa*
[c1-l036 note] Yǔyǔquanquanto xite curaſu describes living at leisure or without anxiety. The repeated long-u marks are carons; the Portuguese Leuar boa continues below.

[c1-l038] Quanracu. Yorocobi, tanoximu. *Conten-*

[c1-l039 note] Tamentos completes contentamentos. Quanracuuo qiuamuru means enjoying pleasures to the fullest; the following Go- is Portuguese gozar, not Japanese go.
[c1-l040 >] *zar de muitos prazeres. S.*
[c1-l040 note] Zar completes gozar and the translation speaks of many pleasures. The upright S. marks written usage. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l041] Quanracu. i, Vazzurai. *Doença.*
[c1-l041 note] This Quanracu is identified with Vazzurai, illness, and is unrelated to the preceding pleasure sense. The double z is retained in the Japanese synonym.
[c1-l042] Quanrei. *Nome de certa dignidade.*
[c1-l042 note] Quanrei names a particular rank or office. Nome corrects OCR Nomo; the brief gloss does not itself specify the full institutional history.
[c1-l043] Quanri. Camuri, cutçu. *Coroa, & calça*
[c1-l043 note] Quanri is crown and footwear, explained by Camuri and cutçu. The damaged headword is supported by its repetition in the next row; calçado is divided without adding an unprinted hyphen.
[c1-l044 >] *do. Vt,* Quanri zǒuo vonajǔ xezu. *Co*

[c1-l045 >] *roa, & çapatos não ſe poem no meſmo gudão. i*
[c1-l045 note] Roa completes Coroa; crown and shoes are not placed in the same gudão. The final explanatory i leads into the figurative social interpretation below; the source has a stop after gudão and no supplied comma after i.
[c1-l046 >] *Os altos, & os baixos não tem o meſmo lugar*
[c1-l046 note] Explains the proverb: people of high and low rank do not occupy the same place. The absence of final punctuation in the baseline is consistent with the visible right edge.
[c1-l047] Quanſat. Mi ſaſſuru. *O conjeiutar, ou ve*
[c1-l047 note] Quanſat concerns considering or conjecturing, supported by Mi ſaſſuru. Enlargement favours the anomalous printed conjeiutar rather than silently restoring conjeiturar. The final ve likewise lacks a secure r of expected ver; both readings remain nonblocking caveats.
[c1-l048 >] *com a conſideração.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes the explanation as seeing through consideration. The OCR garble is replaced by the visible Portuguese phrase; this is indented body text above the bottom rule.
