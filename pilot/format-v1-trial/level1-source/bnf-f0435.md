[c1-l001] Sangiǔcǒ. Bup. *Tres graos ſupremos de que*
[c1-l001 note] Sangiuco is explained as three supreme ranks in Buddhism. The upright domain label visibly reads Bup., not the truncated OCR Bu.
[c1-l002 >] *ſe fala no Buppò.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the reference to ranks discussed in Buddhist teaching. Buppò is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese explanation, with the printed grave retained.
[c1-l003] Sangiǔin. *Liuro aßi chamado, como voca-*
[c1-l003 note] Sangiuin names a book described as a dictionary of written characters. The gloss begins vocabulario across the physical break.
[c1-l004 >] *bulario deletras.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes vocabulario deletras, specifying a character dictionary. The close printed deletras is retained rather than regularized to modern spacing.
[c1-l005] Sango. *Depois do parto. ¶* Sango, ſan-
[c1-l005 note] Sango means after childbirth; the paired expression begins contrasting the periods after and before delivery. The final san- belongs to sanjen below.
[c1-l006 >] jen. *Depois, & antes do parto.*
[c1-l006 note] Jen completes sanjen, before childbirth. The Portuguese explicitly gives the two periods in the same order as the Japanese pair.
[c1-l007] Sango. Tama tama. i, Meixuno na. *Pe-*
[c1-l007 note] This separate sango entry names a precious stone, rather than the postpartum period. The unusual printed Tama tama and Meixuno na are retained, not rewritten from the expected lexical identification.
[c1-l008 >] *dra precioſa aßi chamada.*
[c1-l008 note] Dra completes pedra: a precious stone bearing this name. The source gives a general precious-material gloss, without a more precise mineral description.
[c1-l009] Sangô. Mitçuno xiuaza. *Tres obras, ou*
[c1-l009 note] Sango is paraphrased as three acts or operations. The circumflex distinguishes this printed headword from the unaccented entries above.
[c1-l010 >] *operaçoens dos olhos, ouuidos, & lingua.*
[c1-l010 note] Specifies operations of the eyes, ears, and tongue. This is the dictionary's own explanation, not an invitation to substitute a different doctrinal list.
[c1-l011 >] *Vt,* Sangô xizzumete cono qiǒuo docu-
[c1-l011 note] The example calls for quieting these three activities and reciting this scripture. Docu- continues into ju; the repeated headword retains its circumflex.
[c1-l012 >] ju ſubexitote. Taif. *Lib. 20. Aquie*
[c1-l012 note] Ju completes dokuju, scriptural recitation. The upright Taif. citation is followed by italic Lib. 20.; Aquie continues into aquietando without a securely visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l013 >] *tando os olhos, ouuidos, & lingua. i, com*
[c1-l013 note] Completes aquietando and repeats the eyes, ears, and tongue, then paraphrases the action as reading attentively. The scan supports ouuidos, not OCR ouxidos.
[c1-l014 >] *atenção lereis eſte liuro.*
[c1-l014 note] Concludes the translation: you will read this book with attention. Liuro is the historical u/v spelling for book, not OCR ſiuro.
[c1-l015] Sangocu. Mitçuno cuni. i, Tǒdo, Tengicu,
[c1-l015 note] Sangoku means three countries; the following names enumerate them. The small explanatory i, after cuni is present in the scan and was lost in the draft.
[c1-l016 >] Vagachô. *Tresreinos. ſ. China, Sião, Ia-*
[c1-l016 note] Wagacho completes the Japanese enumeration. The Portuguese names China, Siam, and Japan; its Sião is preserved even though Tengicu conventionally points to India. Ia- continues below.
[c1-l017 >] *pão.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes Iapão, Japan, as the last of the three kingdoms. This short continuation remains a separate physical body row.
[c1-l018] Sangǔ. *Vt,* Ixe ſangǔ. *Ir a Tenxǒ daijin*
[c1-l018 note] Ise sangu is a pilgrimage to the deity named Tenxǒ daijin in the Portuguese gloss. The name's visible long-o caron replaces the OCR tilde.
[c1-l019 >] *que he hum Cami de grande romaria que eſta no*
[c1-l019 note] Describes this kami as the object of a major pilgrimage. Cami is a Japanese religious term within an otherwise Portuguese clause.
[c1-l020 >] *reino de Ixe.*
[c1-l020 note] Locates the pilgrimage in the province called Ixe, Ise. The place name is embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l021] Sangue. Fagi cuyamu. *O confeßar, & ma-*
[c1-l021 note] Sange is glossed through shame and repentance, then as confession or disclosure. Ma- continues into manifestar on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *nifeſtar. ¶* Zaixǒuo ſangue ſuru. *Con-*
[c1-l022 note] Completes manifestar and gives an example of confessing sins. The accent over Zaixǒ is a caron; Con- is entirely italic Portuguese, not a mixed-font Japanese fragment.
[c1-l023 >] *feßar, ou deſcobrir os peccados.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes confessar and explains revealing one's sins. The worn verb reads deſcobrir in the scan, rather than the OCR's deſcobrar.
[c1-l024] Sangue. Yamano xita. *Ao pee do monte.*
[c1-l024 note] This homographic sange means below the mountain, not confession. The Portuguese visibly has the historical double-vowel pee.
[c1-l025] Sangueô. Mitçuno voxiye. *Tres doctrinas.*
[c1-l025 note] Sangueo denotes three teachings. The initial S, omitted by OCR, is visible; the printed gueô spelling is retained rather than normalized to a modern romanization.
[c1-l026 >] *de tres meſtres, ou philoſophos antiguos. ſ.*
[c1-l026 note] The teachings are attributed to three ancient masters or philosophers. The abbreviation at the end introduces their names below.
[c1-l027 >] Xaca, Rǒxi, Côxi.
[c1-l027 note] Names Shaka, Laozi, and Confucius as the three teachers. The first name reads Xaca, not Xica; these are names completing a Portuguese enumeration.
[c1-l028] Sangui. *Paoſinhos, ou tentos das ſortes, ou cõtas.*
[c1-l028 note] Sangi are small rods or counters used for lots or calculations. The printed nasal abbreviation in cõtas is retained unexpanded.
[c1-l029 >] *Vide.* San.
[c1-l029 note] Directs the reader to San for related information about the counting or divination rods. San is a Japanese cross-reference within Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l030] Sanguiǒ. Yamano catachi. *Feição do mon-*
[c1-l030 note] Sangyo is the form or appearance of a mountain, supported by Yamano catachi. OCR Nanano and the spurious italic letter inside the headword are corrected from the scan.
[c1-l031 >] *te.* S.
[c1-l031 note] Te completes monte. The upright S. marks written usage; the additional OCR letters are show-through, not further text.
[c1-l032] Sangun. *Tres eſquadrões de gente militar que*
[c1-l032 note] Sangun denotes three military divisions. The following rows specify the strength of each division rather than defining another entry.
[c1-l033 >] *tem cada hum dous mil, & quinhentos ſol-*
[c1-l033 note] States that each of the three divisions contains two thousand five hundred soldiers. Sol- continues into soldados below.
[c1-l034 >] *dados.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes soldados and the numerical explanation of the military divisions. Nothing is added to the printed troop count.
[c1-l035] Sanje. *Tres tempos. ſ.* Quaco, guẽzai,
[c1-l035 note] Sanze denotes three times or ages. Quaco and guẽzai name past and present; the tilde in guẽzai remains unexpanded.
[c1-l036 >] mirai. *Paßado, preſente, & futuro.*
[c1-l036 note] Mirai completes the Japanese list with the future. The Portuguese repeats past, present, and future; preſente begins with the printed lowercase p.
[c1-l037] Sanjen. i, Sangacu. *O entrar a tomar os põtos*
[c1-l037 note] Sanzen, also given as sangaku, concerns receiving subjects for Zen meditation. The abbreviated põtos introduces the points themselves on the next line.
[c1-l038 >] *da meditação dosIenxùs, ou os meſmos pontos.*
[c1-l038 note] Completes the definition as the Zen practitioners' meditation topics, or those topics themselves. Ienxùs is the Japanese sect designation within Portuguese; pontos corrects OCR pantos.
[c1-l039] Sanjen. *Antes do parto.*
[c1-l039 note] This distinct sanjen means before childbirth, contrasting with sango earlier on the page. It is not part of the meditation entry.
[c1-l040] Sanjen. Cacayaqi, u. *Reſplandecer.* S.
[c1-l040 note] Another sanjen is glossed by the printed Cacayaqi, u, corresponding to kagayaku, to shine. The source spelling is retained; the initial is S, not OCR B, and the final upright S. marks written usage.
[c1-l041] Sanji, zuru, ita. Chiraxi, ſu. *Deſabafar.*
[c1-l041 note] The principal forms of sanzuru and the paraphrase chirasu introduce releasing or venting something. The Portuguese verb has f in Deſabafar, not the OCR long-s substitution.
[c1-l042 >] *¶* Qiuo ſanzuru. *Deſabafar o coração.*
[c1-l042 note] Ki wo sanzuru is explained as unburdening the heart. This supplies the emotional sense of the preceding verb entry.
[c1-l043 >] *¶* Iconuo ſanzuru. *Vingarſe. ¶* Mu-
[c1-l043 note] Ikon wo sanzuru is avenging a grievance. Mu- starts mune on the following row, introducing further objects of the same verb.
[c1-l044 >] ne, iqidouori. l, vppunuo ſanzuru. *Idẽ.*
[c1-l044 note] Mune, ikidowori, and uppun continue the expressions for venting resentment. The final i in iqidouori and italic Idẽ. replace incomplete and mixed-font OCR readings.
[c1-l045] Sanji, zuru, ita. *Ir, ou vir.*
[c1-l045 note] A separate sanzuru entry means to go or come. Ir is a Portuguese infinitive here, not OCR Vr or a Japanese fragment.
[c1-l046] Sanjin. *Vt,* Gǒcono ſanjin. *Gente, ou*
[c1-l046 note] The example goko no sanjin begins describing an itinerant person. The Portuguese definition continues over the final two rows of the column.
[c1-l047 >] *peßoa que anda por diuerſas partes, ſem ter lu-*
[c1-l047 note] Describes a person wandering through different places without a fixed location. The scan supports peßoa, diuerſas, and ſem; lu- begins lugar.
[c1-l048 >] *gar nem habitação certa.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes lugar and adds the absence of a settled dwelling. This is running body text above the bottom rule, not a catchword.
