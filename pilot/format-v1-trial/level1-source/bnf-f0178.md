## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Faxigaqi. *Couſa eſcrita na margem. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l001 note] Hashigaki denotes writing in a margin. The further explanation concerns a letter's added writing, not necessarily a modern book preface.
[c1-l002 >] *Præcipuè, o que ſe torna a eſcreuer no principio*
[c1-l002 note] Latin praecipue means especially. The definition singles out material written again at the beginning of a letter, continuing below.
[c1-l003 >] *da carta em letra mais miuda.*
[c1-l003 note] The added text uses smaller handwriting. This physical description is important: hashigaki here is not limited to a formal introductory essay.
[c1-l004] Faxigauaraqe. *Bacio, ou teſto concauo de*
[c1-l004 note] Hashigawarake is an earthenware dish associated with chopsticks. The concave basin or lid-like form is explicit in the Portuguese.
[c1-l005 >] *barro em que ſe leuão os dous paozinhos com*
[c1-l005 note] The two little sticks are carried in the earthenware vessel. The middle of paozinhos is worn, so the existing spelling is retained provisionally.
[c1-l006 >] *que comem metidos dentro.*
[c1-l006 note] The sticks used for eating are placed inside the dish. Metidos refers to the sticks, not to the people eating.
[c1-l007] Faxigueta. *Paos como frechais da ponte ſobre*
[c1-l007 note] Hashigeta denotes bridge timbers, compared with frechais or structural beams. The next row specifies what rests on them.
[c1-l008 >] *que aſſentão os balauſtes.*
[c1-l008 note] The balusters rest on these timbers. This closes the bridge construction definition, not the ladder entry below.
[c1-l009] Faxigo, l, faxinoco. *Paos que atraueſão na*
[c1-l009 note] Hashigo or hashinoko here names the transverse sticks of a portable ladder. The narrower rung sense is explicit, despite modern hashigo commonly denoting the whole ladder.
[c1-l010 >] *eſcada de mão.* X.
[c1-l010 note] Completes escada de mão, a portable ladder. The final label is visibly X., not OCR A.; it is a usage mark rather than Japanese a.
[c1-l011] Faxi-j. *O eſtar a hũa parte, ou canto da ca-*
[c1-l011 note] Hashii is sitting or remaining at a side or corner. The printed j represents the final i; the interior hyphen is not a line-break marker.
[c1-l012 >] *ſa, varanda, &c. P.*
[c1-l012 note] The possible location is a house or veranda. The final P. is visibly slanted and belongs to the usage notation, not the Japanese expression.
[c1-l013] Faxin. Fariuo toru. i, Monouo nù. *Coſer*
[c1-l013 note] Hashin, taking up the needle, is explained as sewing something. Monouo nù represents mono wo nuu; the unusual accent is retained, not converted from modern kana expectations.
[c1-l014 >] *com aguha. ¶* Faxinuo ſuru. *Coſer, ou*
[c1-l014 note] Hashin wo suru means sewing with a needle. Portuguese aguha is the printed historical form and is not expanded to agulha.
[c1-l015 >] *fazer cuſtura.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes fazer custura, doing needlework. Custura's vowel differs from modern costura but is retained diplomatically.
[c1-l016] Faxinja. *Alfayate, ou cuſtureira.*
[c1-l016 note] Hashinja denotes a tailor or seamstress. The -ja element supplies the person associated with the sewing activity defined above.
[c1-l017] Faxino co. *Traueſas da eſcada de mão.*
[c1-l017 note] Hashi no ko again denotes ladder rungs. This spaced phrase is preserved independently of the joined faxinoco spelling above.
[c1-l018] Faxiqin. i, Xencono tagui. *Certos piuetes*
[c1-l018 note] Hashikin is described as a kind of senkō, thin incense sticks. Tagui means a kind or class; the printed Xencono is not expanded to a guessed long-vowel spelling.
[c1-l019 >] *delgados.*
[c1-l019 note] Delgados specifies that the incense sticks are slender. It is a physical-line continuation, not an isolated gloss for hashira.
[c1-l020] Faxira. *Eſteo, ou colunna de pao.*
[c1-l020 note] Hashira means a wooden post or column. Esteo is a supporting post, agreeing with colunna rather than referring to a personal name.
[c1-l021] Faxiracaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer correr, ou fazer*
[c1-l021 note] Hashirakashi is causative, making someone run. The listed -su and -aita endings belong to the older conjugational notation.
[c1-l022 >] *ir de preſſa com recado, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] The running may be a rapid errand with a message. Recado provides a practical example of the causative action, not a separate lexical sense.
[c1-l023] Faxiradate. *O aleuantar as colunas de pao*
[c1-l023 note] Hashiradate is raising wooden building posts. The noun combines hashira and tate, with voiced d in the compound.
[c1-l024 >] *no lugar do edificio, ou caſas. Vt,* Faxira-
[c1-l024 note] The posts are raised on the building site. The final Faxira- begins the Japanese example continued below.
[c1-l025 >] dateuo ſuru.
[c1-l025 note] Completes hashiradate wo suru, performing the raising of posts. This short row belongs to the preceding example and is not a new headword.
[c1-l026] Faxiragacuxi. *Algũa guernição, ou pintura*
[c1-l026 note] Hashiragakushi is an ornament or painting used to conceal a post. Guernição is retained as the printed Portuguese variant.
[c1-l027 >] *que ſe dependura na colunna pera a encobrir,*
[c1-l027 note] The covering hangs on the column to conceal it. The Portuguese object refers back to the column, matching Japanese kakushi 'hiding'.
[c1-l028 >] *& ornar.*
[c1-l028 note] Adds the purpose of ornamentation as well as concealment. This is the end of the hashiragakushi definition.
[c1-l029] Faxirayoxe. *Pao que ſe prèga ao comprido na*
[c1-l029 note] Hashirayose is a strip of wood nailed lengthwise. Pregar here means fastening with nails, not preaching.
[c1-l030 >] *ombreira da porta peraque entre a porta, & a*
[c1-l030 note] The strip is on a door jamb so that the door and jamb meet without a gap. The printed peraque remains joined.
[c1-l031 >] *ombreira não fique eſpaço aberto pera poder*
[c1-l031 note] The purpose is to prevent an open space between jamb and door. The negative não is essential to the construction's function.
[c1-l032 >] *ver, ou meter os dẽdos. No Cami ſe diz.*
[c1-l032 note] No gap should permit looking or inserting fingers. Cami names the upper-region dialect; its local typeface is italic in this occurrence.
[c1-l033 >] Toyoxe.
[c1-l033 note] Toyose is the regional alternative just introduced. It is a continuation of the previous definition, not a new alphabetically placed headword.
[c1-l034] Faxiri. *Canas darremeſo. Vt,* Faxiriuo
[c1-l034 note] This hashiri is a throwing cane or dart. The Portuguese distinguishes it from the running verb and kitchen fixture below.
[c1-l035 >] tçuqi idaſu. *Arremeſar canas.*
[c1-l035 note] Hashiri wo tsukiidasu means throwing or thrusting out the canes. The Portuguese arremesar supports the projectile interpretation.
[c1-l036] Faxiri. *Lugar como taboa onde lauão a louça*
[c1-l036 note] A different hashiri is a board-like kitchen washing place. The definition concerns crockery, not an act of running.
[c1-l037 >] *na cozinha, & per onde eſcorre a agoa.*
[c1-l037 note] Water drains from this kitchen washing surface. The drainage explains the functional relationship to flowing or running water.
[c1-l038] Faxiri, u, itta. *Correr. ¶ Item, Fugir*
[c1-l038 note] Hashiri is the running verb; the added Fugir introduces fleeing. The scan reads Fugir, without the extra i inserted by OCR.
[c1-l039 >] *do ſeruiço do ſenhor, acolherſe da guerra, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] The fleeing is from a lord's service or from war. Acolher-se here participates in a withdrawal sense, not merely welcoming someone.
[c1-l040 >] *¶ Item, Ir a Fune à vela. Vt,* Funega
[c1-l040 note] Another sense concerns a boat travelling under sail. Fune is embedded Japanese within italic Portuguese; the final Funega begins the example.
[c1-l041 >] faxiru. *¶* Vmaga faxitta. *Morreo o*
[c1-l041 note] Fune ga hashiru completes the sailing example. Uma ga hashitta is then explicitly translated as the horse died, a special usage rather than literal running.
[c1-l042 >] *caualo. ¶ Item,* Faxiru. *Saltar, ou eſ-*
[c1-l042 note] Caualo completes the horse-death expression. Item introduces things jumping or bursting in fire, again an extended use of hashiru.
[c1-l043 >] *tourar algũà couſa no fogo. Vt,* Xiuo ga fa-
[c1-l043 note] Shio ga hashiru is the salt example, supported by Portuguese sal below. OCR Xiurga cannot parse; the worn final vowel of Xiuo and following ga are recoverable from the scan.
[c1-l044 >] xiru. *O ſal dentro do fogo ſalta. ¶* Cu-
[c1-l044 note] The salt jumps in the fire. The final Cu- begins kuri, chestnuts, on the next physical line.
[c1-l045 >] riga faxiru. *As caſtanhas eſtourão, ou ar-*
[c1-l045 note] Kuri ga hashiru describes chestnuts bursting. The Portuguese translation parallels the salt example rather than assigning hashiru a new unrelated headword.
[c1-l046 >] *rebentão no fogo.* X. *¶* Chiga faxiru.
[c1-l046 note] The chestnuts burst in fire, followed by usage label X. Chi ga hashiru then introduces blood spurting; the scan supports g rather than OCR j.
[c1-l047 >] *Eſguichar o ſangue. ¶* Catanaga faxiru.
[c1-l047 note] Blood spurts, then katana ga hashiru introduces a sword example continued in the other column. The damaged n letters caused OCR Cataliaga; the sword translation confirms Katana.

## column-2 [column] Column 2

[c2-l001 >] *Deſembainharſe a Catana por ſi.* X. *No Ca-*
[c2-l001 note] The sword comes out of its sheath by itself, completing katana ga hashiru from the previous column. Catana is italic here; X. is a Roman usage label.
[c2-l002 >] *mi ſe diz, ſayabaxiru. ¶* Chiyeno fa-
[c2-l002 note] Cami is split across rows and names the region using sayabashiru. The alternative verb appears italic here, while Chiye no begins a new Japanese example.
[c2-l003 >] xitta monogia. *He homem ſagaz, &*
[c2-l003 note] Chie no hashitta mono ja describes an astute person. Portuguese sagaz agrees with intellectual quickness, not the literal running of wisdom.
[c2-l004 >] *agudo. ¶* Faxiru vmani muchi. *Prou.*
[c2-l004 note] Hashiru uma ni muchi is the proverb about whipping a running horse. Prou. is an editorial label, not part of the Japanese sentence.
[c2-l005 >] *Ao que tem inclinação, & impeto pera*
[c2-l005 note] The proverb concerns someone already inclined and eager to act. This qualification is necessary to understand the horse comparison.
[c2-l006 >] *fazer algũa couſa exhortalo, he como dar*
[c2-l006 note] Exhorting such a person is like urging on the running horse. The scan reads exhortalo, not OCR exhertalo; the meaning and visible o agree.
[c2-l007 >] *com a vara no caualo, que vai correndo.*
[c2-l007 note] The comparison concludes with striking a horse that is already running. It is a proverb explanation, not a recommendation inserted by the transcriber.
[c2-l008] Faxiriagari, ru, atta. *Subir correndo.*
[c2-l008 note] Hashiriagari means running upward. The past ending is atta, supported by the scan and NINJAL, not OCR aeta.
[c2-l009] Faxiricacari, u, atta. *Arremeter correndo.*
[c2-l009 note] Hashirikakari means charging at a run. Correndo has a worn e but is a single Portuguese word, not the OCR gap corr ndo.
[c2-l010] Faxiricayeri, u, etta. *Tornarſe correndo.*
[c2-l010 note] Hashirikaeri means returning at a run. The cayeri spelling and etta ending correspond to kaeri/kaetta.
[c2-l011] Faxirichiri, u, itta. *Correr eſpalhandoſe.*
[c2-l011 note] Hashirichiri means running while scattering. Chiru supplies dispersal, consistent with espalhando-se.
[c2-l012] Faxiri cogura. *Vt,* Faxiri cogurauo ſuru.
[c2-l012 note] Hashiri kogura is illustrated with wo suru. NINJAL also records cogura, so it is not normalized to an anticipated kurabe; Vt is the italic example label.
[c2-l013 >] *Correrem dous a par, ou muitos juntos.*
[c2-l013 note] The explanation is two running abreast or many together. This supports a collective-running sense without requiring a modern race-word substitution.
[c2-l014] Faxiricorobi, u, ôda. *Correndo cair.*
[c2-l014 note] Hashirikorobi means falling while running. The contracted ôda ending is preserved rather than expanded to modern koronda.
[c2-l015] Faxiricoye, uru, eta. *Paſſar por cima dal-*
[c2-l015 note] Hashirikoye means running over something. Koye represents koe, crossing over, and the object continues across the Portuguese word break.
[c2-l016 >] *gũa couſa correndo.*
[c2-l016 note] Completes dalgũa couſa correndo, running over some thing. The contracted nasal remains within the physical continuation.
[c2-l017] Faxiri cudari, u, atta. *Decer correndo.*
[c2-l017 note] Hashiri kudari means running down. The printed internal space is retained, unlike neighboring joined compounds.
[c2-l018] Faxiricuſa. *Hũa maneira de eriſipula, ou*
[c2-l018 note] Hashirikusa denotes a spreading skin complaint in this historical definition. The contemporary disease comparison is recorded as the dictionary's description, not a modern diagnosis.
[c2-l019 >] *bretoeja que ſe eſpalha, & dilata pello corpo*
[c2-l019 note] The rash spreads over the body. The printed bretoeja is preserved rather than modernized to brotoeja.
[c2-l020 >] *muito perigoſa.*
[c2-l020 note] Muito perigosa completes the dictionary's characterization of the complaint as dangerous. It modifies the preceding illness description.
[c2-l021] Faxiri jde, uru, eta. *Sair correndo.*
[c2-l021 note] Hashiri ide is running out. The printed j in jde represents vowel i, supported by NINJAL, rather than a Japanese consonantal j.
[c2-l022] Faxiri gidameqi, u, eita. *Correr com grãde*
[c2-l022 note] Hashiri jidameki describes running with considerable bustle. The gi spelling represents ji and agrees with the agitation described below.
[c2-l023 >] *rebuliço, ou preſſa.*
[c2-l023 note] Rebuliço or pressa specifies commotion or haste. The short continuation completes the preceding verb's definition.
[c2-l024] Faxirimai. *Priuado de algum ſenhor grande*
[c2-l024 note] Hashirimai names a great lord's trusted attendant. Privado is a favored confidential servant here, not the adjective 'private'.
[c2-l025 >] *que tudo faz, & a tudo acode com preſteza.*
[c2-l025 note] The attendant does everything and responds promptly. This functional explanation accounts for the running-about component of the Japanese noun.
[c2-l026] Faxirimai, ǒ, ǒta. *Seruir com presteza.*
[c2-l026 note] The verb hashirimai means serving promptly. Its inflections distinguish it from the uninflected attendant noun above.
[c2-l027] Faxirimauari, u, atta. *Correndo rodear.*
[c2-l027 note] Hashirimawari is running around. Mawari supplies the circular movement independently of the running prefix.
[c2-l028] Faxirimeguri, u, utta. *Idem.*
[c2-l028 note] Hashirimeguri has the same meaning as hashirimawari, as Idem states. The unusual abbreviated utta ending is also in NINJAL and remains unchanged.
[c2-l029] Faxirimodori, u, otta. *Tornar correndo.*
[c2-l029 note] Hashirimodori means returning at a run. The damaged d is supported by the Portuguese tornar and the headword data.
[c2-l030] Faxirimono. *O que fugio do ſeruiço do ſenhor.*
[c2-l030 note] Hashirimono is someone who fled a lord's service. This noun uses the fugitive sense of hashiru established in the other column.
[c2-l031] Faxirimucai, ǒ, ǒta. *Correndo porſe de*
[c2-l031 note] Hashirimukai means running to face or confront someone. The Portuguese de fronte continues below.
[c2-l032 >] *fronte.*
[c2-l032 note] Fronte completes the facing position. It belongs to hashirimukai, not to the next running-ahead verb.
[c2-l033] Faxirinobi, u, ita. *Correndo ir diante, ou*
[c2-l033 note] Hashirinobi means running ahead. The listed ita ending is retained as printed and supported by NINJAL despite nearby alternative nobi conjugations.
[c2-l034 >] *paſſar a diante.*
[c2-l034 note] Completes passar a diante, passing ahead. Both Portuguese formulations explain the same Japanese compound.
[c2-l035] Faxiritobi. *Vt,* Faxiritobiuo ſuru. *Corrẽ-*
[c2-l035 note] Hashiritobi wo suru is making a running jump. Vt is italic, and the scan contracts the final Portuguese Corren- to Corrẽ-.
[c2-l036 >] *do ſaltar.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes correndo saltar, jumping with a run-up. This is the end of the noun-plus-suru example.
[c2-l037] Faxiritobi, u, ǒda. *Correndo ſaltar.*
[c2-l037 note] The separately conjugated hashiritobi verb has the same running-jump sense. Its ǒda past ending is not normalized to modern tonda.
[c2-l038] Faxiriyagura. *Baluarte, ou baileu deſcuberto*
[c2-l038 note] Hashiriyagura is an uncovered defensive platform or bastion. Yagura supplies the structural noun, rather than a verb of running.
[c2-l039 >] *ſem tellado nem toldo.*
[c2-l039 note] The structure lacks a roof or awning. Tellado is the retained printed spelling, not silently corrected to telhado.
[c2-l040] Faxiriyori, u, otta. *Chegarſe correndo.*
[c2-l040 note] Hashiriyori means approaching at a run. Yoru supplies approach, agreeing with chegar-se.
[c2-l041] Faxita. *Reſto que fica da conta, ou numero im-*
[c2-l041 note] Hashita denotes a remainder or incomplete quantity. Im- continues into imperfeito below; its terminal division mark is visible at the rule.
[c2-l042 >] *perfeito, & falto daquelle que he ordinario,*
[c2-l042 note] The amount falls short of the ordinary full quantity. This is a numerical sense, not the later social-status use of hashita.
[c2-l043 >] *como ſe faltaſſem 10. ou 20. folhas de pa-*
[c2-l043 note] The example is ten or twenty paper sheets short of a hundred. The printed figures are quantities and retain their following punctuation.
[c2-l044 >] *pel pera cento que tem hua mão de* Torinoco:
[c2-l044 note] A full unit of torinoko paper is described here as one hundred sheets. Torinoco is Roman within the Portuguese explanation, and the scan ends this row with a colon.
[c2-l045 >] *aquelle numero imperfeito, & falto ſe dirà*
[c2-l045 note] The incomplete quantity is said to be the following Japanese expression. The scan supports dirà rather than the OCR divided diri-.
[c2-l046 >] iſsocuno faxita.
[c2-l046 note] Issoku no hashita means the incomplete remainder of a bundle or counted unit. It completes the numerical example rather than beginning a new entry.
[c2-l047] Faxita. *Vide,* Faxit. mono.
[c2-l047 note] The cross-reference reads Faxit. mono, pointing toward the following hashitamono entry. OCR Eixit does not match the surviving capital and following letters.
