[c1-l001] Naigaxiro. *O naõ eſtimar. Vt,* Fitouo nai
[c1-l001 note] Naigashiro means treating something or someone as of no account. The example hito wo naigashiro ni suru begins here and continues without a printed terminal hyphen; it is anticipated by the catchword on f348.
[c1-l002 >] gaxironi ſuru. *Naõ fazer caſo dalguem.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the example as disregarding someone. Both Portuguese negatives retain the printed tilde over the final o, rather than being normalized to não.
[c1-l003] Naigue. Vchi, foca. *Dentro, & fora. Vt,*
[c1-l003 note] Naige is paraphrased as uchi and hoka, inside and outside. The following example applies that contrast to a person's conduct, not merely to physical location.
[c1-l004 >] Naigue tomoni jinjǒni gozaru. *No in-*
[c1-l004 note] The example says that a person is well-mannered both inwardly and outwardly. Jinjǒ is understood through the courteous-conduct gloss here, not restricted to its common modern sense 'ordinary'.
[c1-l005 >] *terior, & exterior he politico, & cortes.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes interior and explains the person as civil and courteous in both respects. Politico concerns cultivated conduct in this context, not political affiliation; the visible ampersand before cortes is retained.
[c1-l006] Naigueico. i, Naixôde qeicouo ſuru. *E-*
[c1-l006 note] Naigeiko is private practice, paraphrased as doing keiko in private. The divided Portuguese E- begins Exercitarſe on the next physical row.
[c1-l007 >] *xercitarſe, ou enſayarſe dentro de caſa antes*
[c1-l007 note] Continues the definition as practicing or rehearsing indoors beforehand. The Portuguese enſayarſe is retained in its historical spelling.
[c1-l008 >] *de ſair em publico.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the purpose of private practice: before appearing publicly. This closes the naigeiko entry rather than describing the next headword.
[c1-l009] Naigui. Vchino coto. *Couſa interior, ou de*
[c1-l009 note] Naigi is an internal or private matter, paraphrased as uchi no koto. The first gloss continues with 'inside' on the next row.
[c1-l010 >] *dentro. Vt* Naiguiuo mǒſu. *Falar a al-*
[c1-l010 note] Naigi wo moosu illustrates communicating a matter privately. No punctuation is clearly printed after the italic Vt here; al- begins alguem in the translation on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] *guem em ſecreto, ou de maneira que naõ ſeja*
[c1-l011 note] Completes the recipient and specifies speaking secretly or in a way that will not become public. The negative's final-o tilde is retained as printed.
[c1-l012 >] *publico, & patente. ¶ Item, Molher ca-*
[c1-l012 note] Closes the secrecy gloss with 'public and manifest', then introduces the distinct sense 'married woman'. Publico corrects OCR puolico; the printed ca- continues into caſada below.
[c1-l013 >] *ſada. i, Naifǒ.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes caſada and supplies naifoo as a synonym. The final vowel has a caron, not the OCR tilde. This synonym after i, is not included in the optional embedded-term mapping.
[c1-l014] Naiguifiǒgiǒ. *Conſultas ſecretas.*
[c1-l014 note] Naigi-hyoogioo denotes private consultations. Both visibly printed carons are preserved rather than regularized by the modern spelling of the compound.
[c1-l015] Naijin. *Interior do templo, ou ermida como*
[c1-l015 note] Naijin is the inner part of a temple or shrine-like sanctuary. The Portuguese compares the inner space to a chapel on the following row.
[c1-l016 >] *capella, &c.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes the architectural comparison with a chapel. It remains a physical continuation rather than a separate lexical gloss for capella.
[c1-l017] Nainai. *Interiormẽte, ou em ſecreto. ¶* Nai-
[c1-l017 note] Nainai means inwardly or privately. The example begins with the same adverb split across the physical line break; the Portuguese tilde abbreviates the ending of interiormẽte.
[c1-l018 >] nai mǒxi ireôto zonjita. *Cuidei, ou deſe-*
[c1-l018 note] Nainai mooshi ireoo to zonjita expresses having privately intended or wished to make an approach. The historical prospective ireô is retained; the Portuguese deſe- continues into the first-person past 'wished' below.
[c1-l019 >] *jei cõ migo de vos conuidar, &c. ¶* Nai-
[c1-l019 note] Completes deſejei and says that the speaker inwardly wished to invite the addressee. The left end is abraded, but jei cõ migo is supported by the surviving type and construction, replacing OCR yrici cõ nigo while retaining the printed separation cõ migo. A second nainai example starts at the right.
[c1-l020 >] naiuo vcagǒ. *Perguntar, ou inquirir o in-*
[c1-l020 note] Nainai wo ukagoo concerns inquiring into what is private or inward. The Portuguese in- begins interior on the next line, not a separate prefix in the Japanese example.
[c1-l021 >] *terior, ou o que paßa dentro. i, Antes de fa-*
[c1-l021 note] Explains the object of inquiry as what is going on inside. The further paraphrase concerns learning someone's disposition before speaking publicly, with falar divided at fa-.
[c1-l022 >] *lar, & tratar em publico, ſaber o interior,*
[c1-l022 note] Completes falar and states the contrast between public discussion and private knowledge. The interior here is a person's inward intention rather than a room or building.
[c1-l023 >] *& vontade dalgũa peßoa. ¶ Item, Por*
[c1-l023 note] Completes the explanation with someone's will or intention. Item then introduces an additional repeated or recurrent inward sense, continued below.
[c1-l024 >] *vezes, ou muitas vezes no interior.*
[c1-l024 note] The dictionary adds 'at times, or many times inwardly'. The visible interior corrects OCR intertor; the unusual wording is retained rather than rewritten into a more familiar modern definition.
[c1-l025] Nainet. *Febre, ou quentura interior. Vt,*
[c1-l025 note] Nainet denotes inward fever or heat. The final t is the dictionary's checked-coda notation, not a missing tçu to be expanded in the canonical text.
[c1-l026 >] Nainetga aru. *Terfebre intrinſeca.*
[c1-l026 note] Nainet ga aru means having internal fever. The close printed Terfebre is retained; this is a historical dictionary gloss, not a contemporary medical diagnosis.
[c1-l027] Nai necqi. *Febre intrinſeca.*
[c1-l027 note] Nai nekki gives a related expression for internal heat or fever. The visible space after Nai and the cq spelling are preserved rather than fused or modernized.
[c1-l028] Nai qeico. *Vide,* Naigueico.
[c1-l028 note] The visible gap after Nai is retained, paralleling Nai necqi above. Naikeiko redirects the reader to naigeiko, private practice, already defined in this column. The cross-reference is not a second full definition.
[c1-l029] Naiqen. i, Aqi tano yoxi, axiuo miru co-
[c1-l029 note] Naiken is explained as examining whether the autumn rice fields are good or bad. The phrase ends with the first part of koto, while the original spacing Aqi tano is retained.
[c1-l030 >] to. *Ver a nouidade do arroz antes de ſe co-*
[c1-l030 note] Completes koto and translates the inspection as viewing the prospective rice crop before harvesting. Nouidade means the new crop in this agricultural context, not novelty in the modern abstract sense.
[c1-l031 >] *lher no Outono, a quanto reſponderà, &c.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes colher and explains estimating what the autumn crop will yield. The future ending and grave accent in reſponderà are preserved.
[c1-l032 >] *Vt,* Naiqenuo ſuru.
[c1-l032 note] Naiken wo suru illustrates carrying out that inspection. The initial N is supported by the headword and surviving type, correcting the OCR X in Xaiqenuo.
[c1-l033] Nairan. Vchi miru. *Ver às eſcondidas, ou ſe-*
[c1-l033 note] Nairan is viewing privately or secretly, paraphrased as uchi miru. The Portuguese ſe- continues into ſecretamente on the following row.
[c1-l034 >] *cretamente.* S.
[c1-l034 note] Completes the adverb 'secretly'. The final S. is a visibly upright written-language usage label, not part of the italic gloss.
[c1-l035] Nairi. i, Inferno. *Vt,* Nairino ſoconi
[c1-l035 note] Nairi is equated with hell. The example begins nairi no soko ni, 'in the depths of hell'; the short gloss Inferno is retained in the roman type printed here.
[c1-l036 >] xizzumu. *Eſtar metido nas profundezas do*
[c1-l036 note] Shizumu completes the Japanese image of sinking into hell's depths. The Portuguese describes being confined in those depths, with its final do continuing below.
[c1-l037 >] *Inferno.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes the Portuguese reference to hell. Ink disturbance affects the initial letters, but the surviving word and the immediately preceding context establish Inferno.
[c1-l038] Nairiqi. *Riquezas que não aparecem de fora.*
[c1-l038 note] Nairiki denotes resources or wealth that are not apparent outwardly. This resembles naifuku on the preceding page but remains a distinct headword.
[c1-l039 >] *Vt,* Nairiqiga yoi. *Ter hum no interior,*
[c1-l039 note] Nairiki ga yoi illustrates possessing substantial private resources. The Portuguese no interior continues into an equivalent 'in secret' on the next row.
[c1-l040 >] *ou em ſecreto bom fato, ou riquezas. ¶* Nai-
[c1-l040 note] Fato here means goods or possessions, not a factual proposition. The example describes good resources held privately, then begins a contrasting nairiki ga warui example at the right edge.
[c1-l041 >] riqiga varui. *Ter no interior, ou em ſecre-*
[c1-l041 note] Completes nairiki ga warui, having poor private resources. The Portuguese again contrasts outward appearance with assets held inwardly or secretly.
[c1-l042 >] *to pouco fato, ou forças de bẽs temporaes.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes ſecreto and specifies few possessions or little strength in worldly goods. The nasal abbreviation bẽs and the historical temporaes are preserved.
[c1-l043] Naiſan. *Contar, ou fazer as contas interiormẽ-*
[c1-l043 note] Naisan means counting or calculating inwardly, matching the cross-reference under Naican on f348. The Portuguese adverb is physically divided interiormẽ- / te.
[c1-l044 >] *te conſigo.*
[c1-l044 note] Completes the phrase 'inwardly with oneself'. Conſigo is supported by the surviving letters; the OCR couſigo confuses the damaged n with u.
[c1-l045] Naiſô. *O fazer a ſaber, ou propor interiormẽ-*
[c1-l045 note] Naisoo is communicating or presenting something privately, with the recipient specified below. The headword's long ſ corrects OCR Naiiô; its identification is corroborated by the supplied NINJAL entry, while the scan supplies the circumflex.
[c1-l046 >] *te algũa couſa a peßoa nobre.*
[c1-l046 note] Completes the description as privately proposing a matter to a noble person. The ending of interiormẽte belongs to the preceding row's divided adverb.
[c1-l047] Naiſon. Vchi ſonzuru. *Corrupção interior,*
[c1-l047 note] Naison is internal damage or corruption, paraphrased as uchi sonzuru. The scan supports the missing i in Vchi and interior rather than OCR imerior; the Portuguese continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *ou das ẽtranhas por cauſa de muito vinho, &c.*

[c2-l002] Naitçǔ. *O deſcobrir, ou dar a entender a ou-*
[c2-l002 note] Naitsuu concerns disclosing or communicating concealed matters to others. The final ou- begins outros and the entry continues across several physical rows.
[c2-l003 >] *tros o que paßa ſecretamente: particularmẽte*
[c2-l003 note] Completes outros and specifies disclosure of what happens secretly. Particularmẽte is printed as one word without the OCR's terminal full stop; the colon after ſecretamente is retained.
[c2-l004 >] *ſe vſa entre inimigos, ou outras peßoas, que ſe*
[c2-l004 note] The dictionary says the expression is used especially between enemies or people who dislike one another. The visibly indented row continues the same definition; the comma after peßoas is preserved.
[c2-l005 >] *querem mal. Vt,* Naitçǔuo ſuru. *Deſ-*
[c2-l005 note] Completes the mutual-hostility clause and gives naitsuu wo suru. Deſ- begins the translated verb deſcobrir on the following row.
[c2-l006 >] *cobrir deſta maneira o que paßa, &c.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes the example as disclosing what happens in this manner. Its indentation has been restored because it is a continuation, not an independent unindented entry.
[c2-l007] Naitocu. *Proueitos, ou beneſes que hum tira*
[c2-l007 note] Naitoku denotes profits or benefits obtained from a position or activity. The last headword vowel is u, correcting OCR Naitoca; the following examples confirm the same stem.
[c2-l008 >] *dalgum officio, ou renda que grangea, &c.*
[c2-l008 note] Specifies benefits from an office or income that someone acquires. The historical verb grangea and the spelling officio are retained.
[c2-l009 >] *¶* Naitocuno fucai fito. *Homem que ti-*
[c2-l009 note] Naitoku no fukai hito describes someone deriving substantial advantage or profit. The translation's ti- continues into tira, not into another Japanese word.
[c2-l010 >] *ra muito proueito, ou ganho deſta maneira.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes the description as drawing much profit or gain in that way. This remains part of the example introduced on the preceding row.
[c2-l011 >] *¶* Naitocuuo toru. *Tirar proueito, ou intereſe.*
[c2-l011 note] Naitoku wo toru means obtaining benefit or advantage. The adjacent uu comes from the noun ending and the attached object particle uo and is not reduced.
[c2-l012] Naixa. i, Naixôno fito. *Peßoa familiar que*
[c2-l012 note] Naisha is a trusted intimate or household insider, paraphrased as naishoo no hito. The Portuguese explanation describes the person's privileged access rather than simply a blood relative.
[c2-l013 >] *em caſa dalguem trata os negocios liuremente*
[c2-l013 note] The intimate can transact business freely in someone's house. This liberty of dealing leads into access to the master on the next row.
[c2-l014 >] *com o ſenhor, & tem mais liure entrada*
[c2-l014 note] Continues that the person deals with the lord and has freer access to him. The comparison concerns access and familiarity, not a separate financial privilege.
[c2-l015 >] *com elle.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes the statement of personal access to the lord. The short row is retained as the close of the naisha definition.
[c2-l016] Naixacu. *Parentes da parte de pay. ¶* Gue-
[c2-l016 note] Naishaku denotes relatives on the father's side. The damaged a in Naixacu corrects OCR Nuixacu; the contrasting maternal term begins Gue- at the right edge.
[c2-l017 >] xacu. *Parentes da parte da mãy.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes Guexacu, the contrasting name for relatives on the mother's side. The initial fragment belongs to the preceding row's Gue-, not to an independent xacu entry.
[c2-l018] Naixidocoro. *Nome de hum rico eſpelho do*
[c2-l018 note] Naishidokoro is explained here as the name of a precious mirror belonging to the Dairi. The possessor is supplied by the next physical row.
[c2-l019 >] *Dairi que he hũa das tres peças ricas de Ia-*
[c2-l019 note] The mirror is described as one of Japan's three precious objects. The scan supports Dairi rather than OCR Daini and shows the cedilla in peças; Dairi is a Japanese title embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l020 >] *pão, que ſe chamão,* Sanjǔno jingui.
[c2-l020 note] Completes Iapão and supplies the collective Japanese name Sanjǔno jingui. Enlargement shows a damaged u with a caron, not the OCR i in Sanjino; the historical spelling is retained rather than replaced with a modern reading.
[c2-l021] Naixin. *Coração interior, ou ſecreto.*
[c2-l021 note] Naishin is the inward or secret heart. The faint i between a and x is visible, correcting Naxin; the supplied NINJAL Naixin entry supports this lexical identification without determining the type.
[c2-l022] Naixô. *Interior, ou vontade. ¶* Deosno
[c2-l022 note] Naishoo is glossed as the interior or one's will. The next example begins Deosno, making God's will the object under discussion; the headword has a circumflex.
[c2-l023 >] gonaixǒuo ſomuqu. *Ir contra a vontade,*
[c2-l023 note] The example means opposing God's will. Its gonaixǒ has a visible caron, unlike the circumflex in the headword; this printed difference is preserved rather than harmonized.
[c2-l024 >] *& beneplacito de Deos.*
[c2-l024 note] Completes the translation with God's good pleasure or approval. This is the second coordinated object of going against, not a separate theological entry.
[c2-l025] Naiyacu. Vchino cuſuri. *Mezinha que ſe*
[c2-l025 note] Naiyaku is paraphrased as internal medicine. The Portuguese identifies a remedy taken by drinking, completed by bebe on the next row.
[c2-l026 >] *bebe.*
[c2-l026 note] The single word completes the definition of a remedy that is drunk. It remains its own physical row despite its brevity.
[c2-l027] Naizon. *Cuidar, ou ter pera ſi.*
[c2-l027 note] Naizon is to think or privately suppose something. Ter pera ſi here concerns one's own judgment, not simply possession; the historical form pera is preserved.
[c2-l028] Najeni. *Porque.*
[c2-l028 note] Najeni is the interrogative 'why', represented by the historical one-word Portuguese Porque. Its spelling remains distinct from Najoni below.
[c2-l029] Najicaua. *Porque, ou porque cauſa?*
[c2-l029 note] Najikawa is glossed as asking why or for what cause. The final question mark confirms the interrogative force rather than the causal conjunction 'because'.
[c2-l030] Najimi, u, ûda. *Ser familiar dalguem, ou*
[c2-l030 note] Najimu concerns becoming familiar with someone or associating closely. The abbreviated inflection ûda has the printed circumflex; it is not changed to a caron from expectations about the paradigm.
[c2-l031 >] *tratalo familiarmente. Vt,* Tanen najimi
[c2-l031 note] Continues with dealing familiarly with that person and begins an example of a long-standing relationship. The scan reads familiarmente, correcting the OCR confusion famitarmente.
[c2-l032 >] mǒſaretaru xucunnite maximaſu. Xid.
[c2-l032 note] Completes the honorific example concerning a lord with whom someone has long been familiar, as translated below. Xid. is a citation label; its final period belongs to the same roman type as the abbreviation.
[c2-l033 >] *He ſenhor com quem tratou familiarmente mui*
[c2-l033 note] Begins the Portuguese rendering: this is a lord with whom the person has dealt familiarly for many years. The final mui continues into tos below, without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c2-l034 >] *tos annos.*
[c2-l034 note] Completes muitos annos, 'many years', closing the long-familiarity example. The physical division mui / tos is retained without supplying a hyphen.
[c2-l035] Najoni. *Porque.*
[c2-l035 note] Najoni supplies another interrogative form meaning 'why'. The final ni is legible and is not assimilated to the earlier Najeni.
[c2-l036] Nama. *Couſa crua. Este nome junto a raiz de*
[c2-l036 note] Nama is first glossed as something raw, then discussed as an element attached to verb roots. Este visibly has round s, which is retained in contrast to surrounding long-s types.
[c2-l037 >] *algũs verbos ſignifica fazer mal, ou imperfei-*
[c2-l037 note] Explains that nama can indicate doing an action poorly or incompletely. The damaged medial letters belong to ſignifica, correcting OCR ſigaifica; imperfeitamente is divided at the printed hyphen.
[c2-l038 >] *tamente o que o verbo ſignifica. Vt,* Teuo
[c2-l038 note] Completes the explanation of imperfectly performing the action denoted by the verb. Teuo opens a full Japanese example, not an isolated Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese prose.
[c2-l039 >] nama araini ſuru. *Lauar mal as mãos.*
[c2-l039 note] Te wo nama-arai ni suru illustrates inadequately washing the hands. The source's separate nama araini is preserved, without imposing modern compound boundaries.
[c2-l040 >] *¶* Teuo nama nogoini ſuru. *Alimpar*
[c2-l040 note] A second example uses nama with wiping or cleaning the hands. Its Portuguese translation begins Alimpar and continues on the next row; this is a standalone Japanese example.
[c2-l041 >] *mal as mãos. ¶ Vſaſe tambem em compo-*
[c2-l041 note] Completes 'clean the hands poorly' and adds that nama also appears in compounds. The statement introduces the subsequent series of nama- headwords.
[c2-l042 >] *ſição de muitos nomes como ſe vera a baixo.*
[c2-l042 note] Finishes the cross-reference to many nominal compounds that will be seen below. The printed a baixo remains two words and vera is not given a modern accent.
[c2-l043] Namabi. *O não estar ſeco, ou enxuto de todo.*
[c2-l043 note] Namabi denotes not being completely dry. The difficult italic s/t group in estar is retained as in the aligned draft rather than reclassified globally; the nasal spelling não is also preserved.
[c2-l044 >] *Vt,* Namabini gozaru.
[c2-l044 note] The example states that something remains incompletely dry. Namabini is the adverbial or predicative form used with gozaru, matching the related entries below.
[c2-l045] Namabina. *Couſa que não eſtà enxuta bem.*
[c2-l045 note] Namabina describes a thing that is not thoroughly dry. The attributive na contrasts with the following ni form; the Portuguese eſtà retains its printed accent.
[c2-l046] Namabini. *Adu.*
[c2-l046 note] Namabini is explicitly labeled an adverb. The short entry refers back to the incomplete dryness already defined and illustrated.
[c2-l047] Namabuqi. *Ruim fundição, & imperfeita.*
[c2-l047 note] Namabuki is poor or imperfect casting/smelting. The swash R and weak following u produce the misleading OCR Raim; surviving strokes support Ruim, though the u is locally abraded.
