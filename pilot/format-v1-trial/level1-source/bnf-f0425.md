[c1-l001] Rǒrô. *Vt,* Rǒrôiuru. *Eſtar deſterrado,*
[c1-l001 note] Rǒrô introduces an example of living banished or withdrawn. The scan restores the complete headword, the example Rǒrôiuru, and Eſtar deſterrado from the severely garbled OCR; the first and second vowel marks are respectively caron and circumflex.
[c1-l002 >] *& recolhido ſem ſair a publico, nem ter de-*
[c1-l002 note] Continues the withdrawn-life definition: remaining secluded, not appearing in public, and having no dealings with the world. The scan reads ſair, not the OCR ſamr; the printed de- leads into uer below.
[c1-l003 >] *uer com o mundo.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes ter deuer com o mundo, having dealings with the world. The archaic wording and physical split are retained rather than rewritten as modern Portuguese.
[c1-l004] Rôrôtoxite. *Adu. Modo de eſtar ſolitario,*
[c1-l004 note] Rôrôtoxite is an adverbial expression for being solitary. Both visible headword marks are circumflexes, unlike the first caron of the entry above; Adu. belongs to the italic grammatical explanation.
[c1-l005 >] *& recolhido ſẽ tratar com a gente como ve-*
[c1-l005 note] Expands the solitary manner as remaining secluded without dealing with people. The abbreviated ſẽ is preserved, and the comparison with old people continues after ve-.
[c1-l006 >] *lhos, ou doentes de malenconia, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes the comparison with old people and sufferers from melancholy. The Portuguese describes a manner of withdrawal, not a second Japanese lemma; the historical malenconia is retained.
[c1-l007] Rǒſai. *Hũa doença como etica, ou tiſica.*
[c1-l007 note] Rǒſai names a wasting illness, compared in the source to hectic disease or phthisis. This is a historical gloss rather than a modern diagnosis; the abbreviation Hũa and both long ſ forms are preserved.
[c1-l008] Roſai, ǒ, ǒta. *Pedir eſmola, comer, ou ſuſtẽtação.*
[c1-l008 note] The verb Roſai means asking for alms, food, or sustenance. The scan restores initial R, the carons on its abbreviated forms, and the words comer and ſuſtẽtação; no modern expansion is substituted for the abbreviation.
[c1-l009] Roſai. *Vt,* Roſaiuo itaſu. *Idem.*
[c1-l009 note] The noun Roſai appears in the construction Roſaiuo itaſu, with the same alms-seeking meaning as the preceding verb. Vt and Idem are italic apparatus, while the full Japanese example is upright.
[c1-l010] Roſainin. *Peßoa q̃ pede eſmola, ou ſuſtẽtação.*
[c1-l010 note] Roſainin denotes someone who asks for alms or sustenance. The Portuguese retains both the q̃ abbreviation and the abbreviated syllable in ſuſtẽtação, rather than silently expanding either.
[c1-l011] Rǒſo. Voitaru nezumi. *Rato velho.* S.
[c1-l011 note] Rǒſo means an old rat, explicitly paraphrased by Voitaru nezumi. The final S. is the upright written-register label, not part of the italic Portuguese definition.
[c1-l012] Roſǒ. i, Auoi yenogu. *Tinta muito azul*
[c1-l012 note] Roſǒ is a blue pigment, explained by Auoi yenogu. The scan restores initial R and muito; its final vowel has a caron, whereas the first o is unmarked.
[c1-l013 >] *de pintar.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the blue-pigment gloss by specifying its use in painting. This continuation belongs to Roſǒ and introduces no new Japanese text.
[c1-l014] Rǒſô. Voita fijiri. *Religioſo, ou Bõzo velho.*
[c1-l014 note] Rǒſô is an aged religious person or Buddhist monk, paraphrased by Voita fijiri. The two vowel marks differ: caron first, circumflex second; the abbreviation in Bõzo is retained.
[c1-l015] Rôſô. i, Sôno coromo. *Veſtido, ou habito*
[c1-l015 note] This separate Rôſô concerns a monk's clothing, as Sôno coromo explains. Both vowel marks are circumflexes. On the second pass the broken medial type is read as long ſ: its detached head lies to the right of the lower shaft, unlike an i dot. The supplied NINJAL entry 213a10 corroborates the lexical identification, not the typography.
[c1-l016 >] *de Bonzo.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes the definition as the dress or habit of a Buddhist monk. Bonzo is part of the Portuguese explanation, not a separate romanized Japanese citation.
[c1-l017] Rǒſocu. Voitaru axi. *Velho que ja não pode*
[c1-l017 note] Rǒſocu, explained by Voitaru axi, concerns the weakened legs of an old person. The Portuguese reads Velho, correcting the OCR Vello, and continues the person's limited walking ability below.
[c1-l018 >] *andar muito. ¶* Rǒſocude gozaruni-

[c1-l019 >] yotte, mairu coto canauanu. *Por quanto*

[c1-l033] Rǒuô. Voitaru voqina. *Velho.*

[c1-l034] Rǒuoxǒ. *Certa dignidade de Bonzos velhos*

[c1-l036] Rôxa. *Preſo, ou encarcerado.*

[c1-l037] Rǒxa. Voitaru mono. *Velho.*

[c1-l038] Roxen. i, Michino tçucai jeni. *Dinheiro, ou*

[c1-l040] Rôxẽ. *Embarcação que tẽ camara, ou varãda.*

[c1-l041] Roxet. i, Michino ſǒ. *Nouas de caminho.*

[c1-l042] Rôxet. i, Xiuaſuno yuqi. *Neue da vnde-*
[c1-l042 note] This Rôxet denotes winter-month snow, explained by Xiuaſuno yuqi. The Portuguese visibly starts vnde-cima, which is retained despite its discrepancy with the usual identification of Xiuaſu; the transcription does not silently correct the source's month number.
[c1-l043 >] *cima lũa, ou mes.* S.

[c1-l046] Rǒxi, ſuru, ita. *Enuelhecer.*

[c1-l047 note] Rǒxin is an old retainer or servant, paraphrased by Voitaru xinca. The scan restores R and its caron from the OCR Boxin, and the written-register label S. is upright.
