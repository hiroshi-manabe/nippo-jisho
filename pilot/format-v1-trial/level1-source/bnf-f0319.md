[c1-l008 note] Mimyoo is explained by kidoku naru koto, something miraculous or marvelous. The upward-pointing circumflex is visible on enlargement, correcting OCR Mimiǒ; the supplied NINJAL headword independently agrees with Mimiô. Milagrosa is divided at the physical line end.

[c1-l009 note] Grosa completes milagrosa; maravilhosa supplies the alternative 'marvelous'. This ends the mimyoo gloss before the separate word mimi, ear.
[c1-l010] Mimi. *Orelha. ¶* Mimiga acanu. *Não*
[c1-l010 note] Mimi is an ear. Mimi ga akanu begins an idiomatic explanation of not understanding or not being willing to listen, with the Portuguese negative carried into the next row.
[c1-l011 >] *entender, ou não querer ouuir por ſer rude, ou*
[c1-l011 note] The refusal or failure to listen is attributed to lack of understanding or stubbornness. The damaged vowel supports rude, not OCR rade; the second alternative is completed below.
[c1-l012 >] *teimoſo. ¶* Mimiga naru. *Zonirem as*
[c1-l012 note] Teimoso completes the previous alternative. Mimi ga naru describes ringing in the ears; preserve the historical Portuguese Zonirem rather than modernizing its vowel.
[c1-l013 >] *orelhas. ¶* Mimiga tçubururu. *Enſur-*
[c1-l013 note] Orelhas completes the ringing gloss. Mimi ga tsubururu means becoming deaf, with ensurdecer divided across the next physical break.
[c1-l014 >] *decer. ¶ Permet. Não entender por rude,*
[c1-l014 note] Decer completes ensurdecer. The figurative extension is not understanding through lack of comprehension; the compressed Permet. label is retained unexpanded.
[c1-l015 >] *ou por outra couſa. ¶* Mimi tçubure. l,
[c1-l015 note] The figurative failure to understand may have another cause. Mimi tsubure then begins a noun phrase for a deaf person, followed by an alternative form.
[c1-l016 >] mimino tçubureta fito. *Surdo, ou homẽ*
[c1-l016 note] Mimi no tsubureta hito is a person whose hearing has failed. The Portuguese gives both deafness and the extended sense of a person unable to grasp things.
[c1-l017 >] *que não percebe as couſas por rudeza, &c.*
[c1-l017 note] The figurative person does not perceive matters through dullness. This completes the preceding alternative rather than introducing a new hearing verb.
[c1-l018 >] *¶* Mimini ſaſayaqu. *Falar à puridade.*
[c1-l018 note] Mimi ni sasayaku is whispering into someone's ear, glossed as speaking privately. The Japanese sense and the long-s types support ſaſayaqu rather than OCR faſayaqu; the omitted opening pilcrow is restored from the scan.
[c1-l019 >] *¶* Mimiuo ſumaſu. *Ouuir com atenção.*
[c1-l019 note] Mimi wo sumasu means listening attentively. The Portuguese directly supplies the attentive-listening sense, not a literal cleaning of the ear.
[c1-l020 >] *¶* Mimiuo tozzuru. l, fuſagu. *Fechar*
[c1-l020 note] Mimi wo tozuru or fusagu gives two ways of closing or blocking one's ears. The lower-case l introduces the alternative, and Fechar continues into its object below.
[c1-l021 >] *as orelhas, ou não querer ouuir. ¶* Mimi-
[c1-l021 note] The idiom also means refusing to listen. The following Mimi- starts another example and must remain physically divided from uo below.
[c1-l022 >] uo vodorocaſu. *Eſpantarſe ouuindo algũa*
[c1-l022 note] Mimi wo odorokasu is being startled by something heard. The printed initial v belongs to the historical spelling and is not normalized to o.
[c1-l023 >] *couſa. ¶* Mimiuo coyaſu. *Fartar as*
[c1-l023 note] Cousa completes the startling-hearing gloss. Mimi wo koyasu begins the image of satisfying or enriching the ears with pleasant things heard.
[c1-l024 >] *orelhas ouuindo boas couſas, & apraziueis.*
[c1-l024 note] The ears are satisfied by hearing good and pleasing things. This completes the idiomatic explanation of koyasu rather than adding another independent definition of mimi.
[c1-l025 >] *¶* Mimini iru. *Entrar algũa couſa nas*
[c1-l025 note] Mimi ni iru is something entering the ears or becoming audible or intelligible. The Portuguese phrase continues with the body-part object below.
[c1-l026 >] *orelhas, ou deixarſe entender. ¶* Mimiuo
[c1-l026 note] The alternative gloss is making oneself understood. Mimi wo starts the following ear-refreshing idiom and remains on this physical row.
[c1-l027 >] ſuſugu. *Refreſcar os ouuidos ouuindo boas*
[c1-l027 note] Mimi wo susugu is explained as refreshing the hearing through good things heard. Preserve the printed susugu form and the continuation into the objects below.
[c1-l028 >] *couſas, & goſtoſas. ¶* Mimiuo caqu.

[c1-l029 >] *Eſgarauatar as orelhas.*

[c1-l030] Mimiaca. l, mimino aca. *Sujidade das*
[c1-l030 note] Mimiaka and mimi no aka are two expressions for dirt or wax in the ears. The lowercase l is the alternative marker, not part of the headword.
[c1-l031 >] *orelhas.*

[c1-l032] Mimicaqi. *Eſgarauatador das orelhas.*
[c1-l032 note] Mimikaki is the implement for picking or cleaning the ears. The Portuguese agent/instrument noun refers to the tool in this context.
[c1-l033] Mimichicai. *Couſa que ſe ent ende bem, ou*
[c1-l033 note] Mimichikai describes something readily understood. The tight but visible internal spacing in ent ende is retained from the aligned draft, not silently regularized to modern word spacing.

[c1-l035 note] Mimidare concerns throbbing ears or discharge from them. The Portuguese mana(r) construction begins at ma- and continues below.

[c1-l042] Mimigauaraqe. *Certa feição de teſto pequeno,*
[c1-l042 note] Mimigawarake is a small earthenware lid or dish of a particular shape. The raised, rounded terminal mark is read as a comma rather than the OCR hyphen; its impression is weak.

[c1-l044] Mimigaximaxij. *Auer traquinada que ſe*

[c1-l045 >] *ſente nos ouuidos, ou moleſta aos que ouuem.*
[c1-l045 note] The noise is felt in the ears or troubles those hearing it. This gives the unpleasant auditory force of the preceding adjective.
[c1-l046] Mimigicai. *Couſa que ſe deixa facilmente*
[c1-l046 note] Mimijikai is again something easily understood. The voiced spelling gicai is printed and should not be assimilated to the earlier chicai headword.
[c1-l047 >] *entender.*
[c1-l047 note] Entender completes the easy-understanding gloss. The related mimijikana form follows at the head of the next column.

[c2-l010] Miminofa. *Orelhas.* X.

[c2-l011 note] The gloss denotes the root or attachment of the ears. Mimi no ne is lexically expected and is the supplied NINJAL reading, but this impression has an abraded, u-like type with no secure upper n bridge. The baseline ue is therefore retained as a nonblocking glyph uncertainty, not interpreted as proof of an ear-upper-part meaning. The printed arreigadadas spacing is also preserved.
[c2-l012] Mimiqiqi. *Peßoa que ouue bem. ¶ Item,*

[c2-l013 >] *O que percebe logo arezão, &c.*

[c2-l014] Mimitçucu. *Moucho aue nocturna. No Cami*

[c2-l015 >] *ſe diz* Mimizzucu.
[c2-l015 note] The source says the form used in Cami is mimizuku. Printed diz corrects OCR dix; Mimizzucu is an upright Japanese word embedded in this Portuguese statement and is eligible for whole-word typeface review.
[c2-l016] Mimitoi. *Vt,* Mimitoi fito. *Homem que*
[c2-l016 note] Mimitoi hito is a person quick to hear or apprehend. The Portuguese subject phrase is continued by the hearing and perception verbs below.
[c2-l017 >] *ouue, ou percebe depreſſa. ¶ Item, permet.*
[c2-l017 note] The person hears or perceives quickly. Item, permet. then marks a figurative extension to intellectual understanding rather than merely auditory acuity.
[c2-l018 >] *Homem que entende depreſſa, & penetra as*
[c2-l018 note] The figurative sense is someone who understands quickly and penetrates matters. The final article as belongs to cousas on the next row.
[c2-l019 >] *couſas.*
[c2-l019 note] Cousas completes the description of a perceptive person. This one-word continuation remains body text, not a heading or label.
[c2-l020] Mimitoxi. *O ouuir bem, ou perceber de preſſa*
[c2-l020 note] Mimitoshi denotes hearing well or perceiving quickly. The historical toshi form and the printed separation de preſſa are retained.
[c2-l021 >] *algũa couſa.* S.
[c2-l021 note] The object is some thing perceived quickly. The final upright S. identifies written usage and is distinguished from the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l022] Mimixij, ijte. l, ijta. *Enſurdecer. Verb.*
[c2-l022 note] Mimishii is becoming deaf. The printed abbreviated forms ijte and ijta are retained, and Verb. introduces the grammatical qualification on the next row.

[c2-l045] Minacami. *Fonte. Vt,* Minacami nigot-
