[c1-l001 >] *continuarſe de idade em idade como algũa pe-*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding page's tsutawari definition: something is transmitted from age to age. The divided pe- leads into peça, an object or piece, on the next row.
[c1-l002 >] *ça, ou doutrina, arte, &c.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes peça and adds teaching and art as things handed down through generations; this is the continuation of tsutawari, not a separate entry.
[c1-l003] Tçutaye, uru, eta. *Deixar por tradição, ou*
[c1-l003 note] Tsutaye, tsutayuru, tsutayeta is the transitive verb for handing something down. The Portuguese begins with leaving something through tradition and continues with instruction below.
[c1-l004 >] *enſinar como doutrina, arte, &c.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the teaching sense with doctrine and art as examples. Doutrina is a continuous printed word; the draft's do utrina split does not correspond to the visible type.
[c1-l005] Tçutayeqiqi, u, jta. *Ouuir por tradição, ou*
[c1-l005 note] Tsutaekiki means hearing something passed on by others. The final principal part is visibly printed jta with a descending initial type; it is retained rather than regularized to an expected ita.
[c1-l006 >] *de boca em boca.*
[c1-l006 note] De boca em boca specifies transmission by word of mouth, completing the explanation of hearing through tradition.
[c1-l007] Tçutayeqitari, u, atta. *Vir por deſcendẽcia,*
[c1-l007 note] Tsutaekitari is coming down through a succession. The damaged initial of por is read with the surviving stroke and the Portuguese construction, replacing OCR oor; the nasal abbreviation in deſcendẽcia remains unexpanded.
[c1-l008 >] *ou tradição dos que vem enſinãdo.*
[c1-l008 note] The tradition comes from those who have been teaching it. The weak r in tradição is present, and the tilde in enſinãdo preserves the printed abbreviation.
[c1-l009] Tçutayezzutaye. *De mão em mão, ou por*
[c1-l009 note] Tsutaezutae describes transmission from one person to another, matching de mão em mão. The headword's second t belongs to tsutaye; the repeated form on the following row confirms the correction of OCR Tçucayezzutaye.
[c1-l010 >] *tradiçio. ¶* Tçutayezzutayeni yǔ. *Di-*
[c1-l010 note] Tsutaezutae ni yuu means telling something along a chain of transmission. The narrow, damaged vowel in tradiçio is retained from the aligned draft; tradição is intended, but no regularization is imposed. Di- begins dizer across the line break.
[c1-l011 >] *zer hum a outro por tradição, ou deſcendencia.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes dizer: one person tells another by tradition or succession. Deſcendencia describes the passing-on process, not necessarily biological descent alone.
[c1-l012] Tçutçu. *Canudo de bambu.*
[c1-l012 note] Tsutsu is a bamboo tube. The final u, though weak, fits the visible type and is not the draft's a; the following compounds repeat the same stem.
[c1-l013] Tçutçudori. *Hum certo paſſaro.*
[c1-l013 note] Tsutsudori is identified simply as a particular bird. Both internal tç and the vowel in paſſaro are visible; the gloss does not itself establish a modern species name.
[c1-l014] Tçutçuga. *Vt,* Tçutçuganǒ maitta. *Veo*
[c1-l014 note] Tsutsuga is illustrated by tsutsuganou maitta, arriving without mishap. The visibly caroned o is preserved, and Veo starts the Portuguese past-tense translation.
[c1-l015 >] *ſem perigo.*
[c1-l015 note] Sem perigo completes Veo: the person came without danger. It explains the negative adverbial expression tsutsuganou rather than assigning safety as an isolated literal gloss of tsutsuga.
[c1-l016] Tçutçuguiri. *Cortar algũa couſa direito, ou em*
[c1-l016 note] Tsutsugiri refers to cutting something straight across or into rounds. The Portuguese alternative em continues into redondo on the next row.
[c1-l017 >] *redondo.*
[c1-l017 note] Redondo completes em redondo, cutting across the rounded object. This short continuation belongs to tsutsugiri.
[c1-l018] Tçutçuji. *Flor aßi chamada que vem no San-*
[c1-l018 note] Tsutsuji is a flower said to appear in the third month, Sanguachi, divided over this and the following line. The capital F in Flor is visible despite the draft's Tlor; the botanical name is not modernized.
[c1-l019 >] *guachi. Item, A meſma aruorezinha que dà*
[c1-l019 note] Guachi completes the Japanese month-name Sanguachi within the Portuguese gloss. The entry also denotes the small tree or shrub that produces the flower; the visibly italic type is retained.
[c1-l020 >] *eſta flor.*
[c1-l020 note] Esta flor completes the description of the shrub producing the named blossom, not a separate botanical entry.
[c1-l021] Tçutçujin. *Canudozinho de metal pera meter*
[c1-l021 note] Tsutsujin denotes a small metal tube to be inserted into a swollen or injured part. The faint o in Canudozinho is present between d and z and is retained.
[c1-l022 >] *nos inchaços, ou noutras partes leſas pera por*
[c1-l022 note] The metal tube is inserted into swellings or other injured parts. Pera por leads into the purpose of drainage on the next row.
[c1-l023 >] *elle ſayr algu humor, ou materia.*
[c1-l023 note] The purpose is to let fluid or matter flow out through the tube. Humor is abraded but recoverable from its surviving h, m and r and the medical gloss; no tilde is supplied in the weak algu without clear visible support.
[c1-l024] Tçutçumi. *Vallos, ou marachões que ſeruem*
[c1-l024 note] Tsutsumi here means embankments or dikes. Portuguese vallos and marachões identify structures that restrain the water, with their purpose completed below.
[c1-l025 >] *pera ter mãonos rios, &c. ¶* Tçutçumi-
[c1-l025 note] The embankments keep rivers under control; ter mão conveys restraint. The close printed mãonos is retained. Tçutçumi- begins an example continued with the object particle on the next row.
[c1-l026 >] uo tçuqu. *Fazer eſtes vallos.*
[c1-l026 note] Tsutsumi wo tsuku means constructing such embankments. The surviving letters support Fazer, replacing OCR Fazor, and the divided Japanese phrase remains on its original rows.
[c1-l027] Tçutçumi. *Saco de canga, ou ſeda, &c.*
[c1-l027 note] A second tsutsumi entry denotes a bag made of canga cloth or silk. The headword contains m, not the OCR sequence nr, and is distinct in meaning from the preceding embankment entry.
[c1-l028 >] *¶ Item,* Tçutçumi. *Fardo de linho, ou*
[c1-l028 note] The entry also covers a bale of linen or a bundle. The tall initial l of linho corrects OCR tinho; the explanatory alternative continues below.
[c1-l029 >] *trouxa. Mas não ſe vſa ſe não com algũa com-*
[c1-l029 note] Trouxa supplies the alternative bundle gloss. The dictionary says this sense is used only in a compound, with composição divided at the physical line end.
[c1-l030 >] *poſição. Vt,* Fitotçu çumi, futatçu-
[c1-l030 note] The examples mean one bundle and two bundles. Enlargement supports Fitotçu rather than OCR Fico, but the next expected t in the first compound is not established in the abraded gap; the visible Fitotçu çumi is retained with that caveat.
[c1-l031 >] tçumi.
[c1-l031 note] Completes futatçu-tçumi, the two-bundle example. This is an indented continuation of the preceding row, not a new headword; the original stable ID is retained.
[c1-l032] Tçu çumi, u, unda. *Enuoluer, ou enfarde-*
[c1-l032 note] The verb is tsutsumi, wrapping or bundling, with principal parts in -u and -unda. The headword's internal t is too abraded to establish; the visible gap in Tçu çumi is retained. Enfarde- begins enfardelar, packing into a bundle.
[c1-l033 >] *lar, &c. Vt,* Cuſuriuo tçutçumu. *En-*
[c1-l033 note] Kusuri wo tsutsumu illustrates wrapping medicine. The scan supports Cuſuriuo, with a long s and a weak u after C, instead of the draft's malformed Crſuriuo; En- starts the translation enuoluer.
[c1-l034 >] *uoluer, ou emburulhar algũa mezinha com pa-*
[c1-l034 note] The medicine is wrapped or bundled in paper, with papel divided across the row boundary. The visible vowel sequence supports emburulhar, preserving its historical form.
[c1-l035 >] *pel. ¶* Xirouo tçutçumu. i, Cacoi ma-
[c1-l035 note] After completing papel, shiro wo tsutsumu introduces surrounding a fortress, paraphrased by kakoi mawasu. The explanatory Japanese phrase continues on the next line and is not an embedded Portuguese loan term.
[c1-l036 >] uaſu. *Cercar a fortaleza. ¶* Teqiuo
[c1-l036 note] Uasu completes mawasu, and cercar a fortaleza gives the enclosing sense. Teki wo begins the next example with an enemy as its object.
[c1-l037 >] toritçutçumu. *Tomar ao imigo no meo,*
[c1-l037 note] Toritsutsumu completes the enemy-encirclement example. The Portuguese describes taking the enemy in the middle; imigo and meo are retained historical forms.
[c1-l038 >] *ou cercalo. ¶* Tçutçumazu monouo yǔ.
[c1-l038 note] Cercalo completes the encirclement gloss. Tsutsumazu mono wo yuu then uses the negative form figuratively, speaking without concealment.
[c1-l039 >] *Fallar ſem encobrir nada. ¶* Fitomeuo
[c1-l039 note] The Portuguese explicitly says to speak while hiding nothing. Hitome wo begins a contrasting example about concealing oneself from other people's sight.
[c1-l040 >] tçutçumu. *Encobrirſe, ou diſfraçarſe pe-*
[c1-l040 note] Hitome wo tsutsumu is explained as concealing or disguising oneself. The first tall letter of diſfraçarſe is long s, followed by f; the source's historical metathesized form is retained rather than modernized to disfarçar.
[c1-l041 >] *ra não ſer viſto, & conhecido.*
[c1-l041 note] Completes pera: the purpose of concealment is not to be seen or recognized. This closes the hitome wo tsutsumu example.
[c1-l042] Tçutçumiguſocu. *Armas cubertas com al.*
[c1-l042 note] Tsutsumi-gusoku denotes armor whose metal is covered with another material. The terminal mark after al is retained as the dot-like mark in the draft, rather than silently regularized to a line-end hyphen.
[c1-l043 >] *gũa couſa por cima do ferro com o cubertas de*
[c1-l043 note] Completes alguma and explains that the covering lies over the iron, with leather coverings as the example. The draft's close com o spacing is retained in this abraded passage, interpreted as the comparative como.
[c1-l044 >] *couro, &c.*
[c1-l044 note] Couro completes the example of a leather covering over armor. The short row closes the entry and remains separate from the following verb.
[c1-l045] Tçutçumimauaxi, ſu, aita. *Emburulhar à roda.*
[c1-l045 note] Tsutsumimawashi means wrapping something all around. The printed -aita principal part is historical sa-row i-onbin, not a defective modern -ashita; the headword's tç corrects OCR eç.
[c1-l046] Tçutçumimono. *Fardo, ou couſa enfardela-*
[c1-l046 note] Tsutsumimono is a bale or something bundled up. The abraded enfardela- is continuous, with a visible terminal hyphen and its final syllable on the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *da, ou emburulhada.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes enfardelada and adds emburulhada, wrapped up. The weak opening letter is read d in agreement with the surviving ascender and the divided participle, rather than OCR c.

[c2-l001] Tçutçumitate, uru, eta. *Acabar de em buru-*

[c2-l003] Tçutçuqi, u, uita. *Picar o paſſaro cõ o bico.*
[c2-l003 note] Tsutsuki denotes a bird pecking with its beak. The abbreviated principal forms are preserved as printed; cõ is the nasal abbreviation of the Portuguese preposition.
[c2-l004 >] *¶ Item, No Ximo, Arder, ou latejar cõdor.*
[c2-l004 note] The regional label Ximo introduces a second sense, burning or throbbing with pain. Ximo is a Japanese region-name embedded in the Portuguese explanation. Enlarged second-pass comparison confirms its italic type, which is preserved.
[c2-l005] Tçutçuqinuqi, u, uita. *Tirar o paſſaro algũa*
[c2-l005 note] Tsutsukinuki describes a bird pecking something out. The object and the means, its beak, are specified in the continuation below.
[c2-l006 >] *couſa com o bico, como olhos dalgum animal.*
[c2-l006 note] A bird removes something with its beak, such as an animal's eyes. The scan supports olhos rather than the draft's olhes.
[c2-l007] Tçutçuximi, u, ǔda. *Humilharſe, ou ter*
[c2-l007 note] Tsutsushimi is humbling oneself or exercising caution and respect. The weak but visibly marked vowel in the final principal part is retained as ǔda, rather than expanded to an expected unda.
[c2-l008 >] *tento, ou reſpeito. ¶* Miuo tçutçuximu.
[c2-l008 note] Completes the gloss with attentiveness or respect. Mi wo tsutsushimu exemplifies restraining or composing oneself.
[c2-l009 >] *Ter tento, & precato ſobre ſi compondoſe, &*
[c2-l009 note] The example means keeping watch and precaution over oneself, composing oneself. The opening capital is T in Ter, not OCR P; the coordination continues below.
[c2-l010 >] *temperandoſe.*
[c2-l010 note] Temperandoſe completes the explanation as moderating oneself. The printed word is continuous, without the draft's artificial division before ſe.
[c2-l011] Tçute. *Portador, ou ocaſião de mandar carta,*
[c2-l011 note] Tsute is a carrier or an opportunity to send a letter. The gloss concerns an available means of communication, not only a physical messenger.
[c2-l012 >] *&c. ¶* Tçuteuo qijte fumiuo yarǒzu.
[c2-l012 note] Tsute wo kiite fumi wo yaroozu means that, on hearing of a means of sending it, the speaker will send a letter. The prospective -oozu form and the printed spelling qijte are retained.
[c2-l013 >] *Ouuindo que ha portador eſcreuerei.*
[c2-l013 note] The Portuguese translation says that hearing there is a carrier, the speaker will write. The damaged vowel in portador is a, not OCR u.
[c2-l014] Tçuto. *Preſente de inaca emburulhado em pa-*
[c2-l014 note] Tsuto is a country gift wrapped in straw. Inaca is the Japanese word for the countryside embedded within the Portuguese explanation; emburulhado preserves the visible historical vowel sequence.
[c2-l015 >] *lha. Vt,* Inacazzuto. l, cuſazzuto. *Idẽ.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes palha, straw, and gives inakazuto and kusazuto as examples with the same meaning. Idẽ retains the nasal abbreviation, and the two Japanese forms are examples rather than terms embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c2-l016] Tçuto. l, tçutoni. *Polamenhaã cedo.*
[c2-l016 note] Tsuto or tsutoni means early in the morning. The closely set Polamenhaã and the tilde on the second a are retained instead of modernizing the Portuguese phrase.
[c2-l017] Tçutome. *Obra deler liuros, ou orar, ou que*
[c2-l017 note] Tsutome here denotes devotional work, such as reading books or praying. The printed close spacing in deler is retained; the religious qualification continues below.
[c2-l018 >] *pertence a ſaluação. ¶* Tçutomeuo naiu.
[c2-l018 note] The work pertains to salvation. In the example the expected Japanese would be tsutome wo nasu, but enlargement shows a dotted, i-like type in naiu; the anomalous supplied reading is retained instead of silently replacing it with naſu.
[c2-l019 >] *Fazer ſemelhantes obras.*
[c2-l019 note] The Portuguese defines the example as performing such works. This supports its sense while leaving the preceding anomalous naiu spelling unchanged.
[c2-l020] Tçutome, uru, eta. *Comprir com ſua obri-*
[c2-l020 note] Tsutome, tsutomuru, tsutometa means fulfilling one's duty. The Portuguese spelling Comprir is historical and is preserved; obrigação is divided at the physical line break.
[c2-l021 >] *gação, ou fazer boas obras, &c. ¶* Yacuuo
[c2-l021 note] Completes obrigação and adds doing good works. Yaku wo begins an example about fulfilling an office or duty.
[c2-l022 >] tçutomuru. *Fazer ſeu officio. ¶* Ienuo-
[c2-l022 note] Tsutomuru completes yaku wo tsutomuru, doing one's duty. The next example begins Ien wo, with consonantal I representing zen, virtue; the terminal hyphen is retained as printed.
[c2-l023 >] tçutomuru. *Fazer obras virtuoſas.*
[c2-l023 note] Ien wo tsutomuru is performing virtuous works. Both occurrences of tçutomuru contain t, not the OCR's c, and the explanation makes the moral sense of Ien clear.
[c2-l024] Tçutome faguemaxi, ſu, ita. *Comprir cõ di-*
[c2-l024 note] Tsutome hagemashi denotes applying oneself diligently or with all one's strength. The printed principal part ita is retained; di- leads into diligencia below.
[c2-l025 >] *ligencia, ou fazer com todas as forças. Vt,*
[c2-l025 note] Completes diligencia and gives the alternative of doing something with all one's strength. Vt introduces the following moral example and is visibly italic apparatus.
[c2-l026 >] Ienuo tçutome faguemaſu. *Fazer obras*
[c2-l026 note] Zen wo tsutome hagemasu means diligently applying oneself to virtuous conduct. Consonantal I in Ien and the printed long s in faguemaſu are preserved.
[c2-l027 >] *de virtude com diligencia.*
[c2-l027 note] Completes the Portuguese translation as doing works of virtue diligently. It qualifies the manner of the preceding example rather than opening a new entry.
[c2-l028] Tçuttachi, tçu, atta. *Eſtar em pè, ou ale-*
[c2-l028 note] Tsuttachi describes standing or rising to one's feet. The doubled t is printed, and the Portuguese ale- continues into aleuantarſe on the next row.
[c2-l029 >] *uantarſe.*
[c2-l029 note] Completes aleuantarſe, to get up. The short row belongs to the standing verb and is retained as a separate physical line.
[c2-l030] Tçuttachiagari, ru, atta. *Idem.*
[c2-l030 note] Tsuttachiagari is referred to the same standing-up meaning. The scan shows two t types in the compound, correcting the draft's single-t Tçutachiagari without changing its principal-part format.
[c2-l031] Tçutto. *Muito, ou perto, fundo, &c. ¶* Tçut-
[c2-l031 note] Tsutto is glossed as an intensifier or manner adverb with senses such as much, close, or deeply. The repeated example begins Tçut- with a visible hyphen, not the draft's final a.
[c2-l032 >] to coreye vogiare. *Chegar bem pera cà.*
[c2-l032 note] Tsutto kore ye ojare invites someone to come right over here. The visible final mark in cà is a grave, not the draft's tilde, and the historical spelling vogiare is retained.
[c2-l033] Tçuua. *Erua aßichamada que ſe dà nas prayas.*
[c2-l033 note] Tsuwa is an herb said to grow on beaches. The initial is T, matching the adjacent headwords, rather than OCR F; the close aßichamada spacing and historical prayas are retained.
[c2-l034] Tçuua. *Cuſpo: palaura de molheres.*
[c2-l034 note] A separate tsuwa entry denotes saliva or spittle and is labeled a women's word. The usage attribution is the dictionary's, and the Portuguese spelling molheres is preserved.
[c2-l035] Tçuuamono. *Soldado, ou gente de guerra.*
[c2-l035 note] Tsuwamono means a soldier or fighting men. The gloss directly identifies the military sense rather than the related material sense of weapons.
[c2-l036] Tçuuari, ru, atta. *Eſtar doente de prenhicão.*
[c2-l036 note] Tsuwari, tsuwaru, tsuwatta denotes suffering the sickness associated with pregnancy. The initial is T rather than OCR F. Enlarged second-pass inspection does not establish a cedilla in prenhicão, so the supplied plain c is retained rather than normalized.
[c2-l037 >] *¶ Item, Brotarem as aruores com gomos, &*
[c2-l037 note] A second sense applies to trees putting out buds. The coordinated fresh leaves continue on the next row.
[c2-l038 >] *folhas nouas. Vt,* Ciga tçuuaru. *¶ Per-*

[c2-l039 >] *met.* Qedamonoga tçuuaru. *Andarem*
[c2-l039 note] The figurative example kedamono ga tsuwaru concerns animals entering heat. Met. completes the printed Per-met. label; the Portuguese infinitive continues below.
[c2-l040 >] *os animaes com o cio. Meliùs, Sacaru.*
[c2-l040 note] Completes the explanation as animals being in heat. Meliùs recommends the Japanese alternative Sacaru, sakaru; its visibly sloping type is retained as printed. This is an alternative-form citation rather than an ordinary headword.
[c2-l041] Tçuuari. *Doẽça de molher prenhe. Vt,* Tçu-
[c2-l041 note] Tsuwari as a noun is the sickness of a pregnant woman. The example repeats the noun across the physical line break; Doẽça preserves the printed nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l042 >] uariuo ſuru.
[c2-l042 note] Completes tsuwari wo suru, experiencing pregnancy sickness. The object particle remains attached in the printed spelling, and no Portuguese gloss has been omitted from this short row.
[c2-l043] Tçuxi. *Caniçada que ſe faz de bambus em alto*
[c2-l043 note] Tsushi is a raised bamboo platform or loft. The final headword letter is i rather than OCR u, and faz has f, not long s; bambus corrects the malformed bamrus. The subsequently consulted NINJAL entry 251b19 corroborates the lexical identification, not the local glyph choices.
[c2-l044 >] *como jũto do forro da caſa, & pera por algũas*
[c2-l044 note] The platform sits high, near the house's ceiling, and is used to put things on. The visible tilde in jũto is retained rather than omitting its nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l045 >] *couſas.*
[c2-l045 note] Couſas completes the storage function of the bamboo loft. The entry ends here before the bodily-pain term below.
[c2-l046] Tçuxin. *O doer o corpo.*
[c2-l046 note] Tsushin denotes aching of the body, directly explained by the Portuguese verbal noun phrase. The scan supports the supplied spelling without expanding the Japanese term.
[c2-l047] Tçuxxinde. *Com corteſia, & reuerencia.*
[c2-l047 note] Tsusshinde means respectfully or reverently. The headword has a doubled x rather than OCR ax, and reuerencia ends in a; its example continues on the next page, anticipated by the catchword.
