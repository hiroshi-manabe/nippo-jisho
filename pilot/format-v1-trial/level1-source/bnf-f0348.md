[c1-l001] Naguecomi,u, ôda. *Lançando meter. Vt,*
[c1-l001 note] Nagekomi/nagekomu means putting something in by throwing it. The circumflex in the abbreviated past principal part is retained as printed, not harmonized with other -comi entries.
[c1-l002 >] ſenninuo ſanzanni tçucamatçuri, yuqino
[c1-l002 note] The example describes treating a holy man harshly before throwing him into snow. Sennin is the object of the action; the final yuqino modifies the snow-filled hole on the following row.
[c1-l003 >] tçumoritaru anani naguecomi, &c. *1. p.*
[c1-l003 note] The hole is one in which snow has accumulated; naguecomi supplies the act of throwing the man into it. The following abbreviated part reference belongs with the citation label on the next row, not with the Japanese example.

[c1-l004 note] The roman citation label precedes the Portuguese translation, which calls the victim a saint. The final me begins meterão on the following physical line; no terminal hyphen is supplied where the scan shows none.
[c1-l005 >] *terão em hũa coua chea de neue.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes meterão: they put him into a pit full of snow. The printed tilde is over the u in hũa; chea and neue retain the historical spellings.

[c1-l007 note] Completes the object and specifies the trajectory: one thing is thrown over another. This short continuation remains a separate row rather than being attached to the next headword.
