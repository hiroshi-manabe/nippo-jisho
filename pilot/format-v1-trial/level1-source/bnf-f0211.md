[c1-l001] Fonxi. Fonno xixǒ. *Primeiro, & proprio*
[c1-l001 note] Honshi is the original teacher; honno shishō makes that relation explicit. Primeiro and proprio continue into mestre on the following row. No final full stop is visible after proprio.
[c1-l002 >] *meſtre. i, Xaca. Como cuidão os gentios.*
[c1-l002 note] The gloss identifies the teacher as Xaca, Shaka, and qualifies this as the belief of the gentios. Xaca is italic here, embedded in the Portuguese sentence rather than a new headword.
[c1-l003] Fonxin. *Meliùs,* Fonjin. Fonno cocoro.
[c1-l003 note] Honshin, one's original mind, is referred to the preferred form honjin with Meliùs. This is the dictionary's own pronunciation preference, not grounds to change the first headword.
[c1-l004 >] *Proprio ſentido, ou juizo. ¶* Fonxinni
[c1-l004 note] Sentido and juizo describe the proper senses or judgment. Fonxinni begins the locative phrase completed by motozzuqu on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] motozzuqu. *Tornar em ſeu acordo, ou juizo.*
[c1-l005 note] Honshin ni motozuku is explained as returning to one's senses or judgment. The zzu spelling represents the voiced dental series; it is not an accidental doubled z.
[c1-l006] Fonxiqi. *Singeleza, ou candura.*
[c1-l006 note] Honshiki denotes simplicity or candour in this gloss, not necessarily the modern dominant sense of formal or authentic style. Candura is retained as printed.
[c1-l007] Fonxiqina. *Singelo, & candido.* Fon-
[c1-l007 note] The adjectival honshiki-na is glossed as simple and candid. The final Fon- belongs to the following alternative, not to the Portuguese definition.
[c1-l008 >] xiqiùdo. *Idem.*
[c1-l008 note] This completes Fon-xiqiùdo, approximately honshikyūdo, a person of that character; Idem repeats the preceding sense. The visible u mark is retained rather than supplied from morphology.
[c1-l009] Fonxǒ. *Verdadeiro juizo. ¶* Fonxǒuo
[c1-l009 note] Honshō is one's true judgment or consciousness. The second occurrence takes object marker uo and leads into losing consciousness.
[c1-l010 >] vxinǒ. *Perder o juizo, ou eſmorecer.*
[c1-l010 note] Ushinō, lose, completes honshō o ushinō. Portuguese gives both loss of reason and fainting; the printed open-o mark is maintained.
[c1-l011 >] *¶* Fonxǒni naru. *Tornar em ſi. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l011 note] Honshō ni naru is explained as coming to oneself again. A clear initial pilcrow introduces this example; Item then opens a further Buddhist sense.
[c1-l012 >] *Suſtancia como eſpiritu de que ſe falla no*
[c1-l012 note] The additional sense is a substance likened to spirit in Buddhist teaching. The scan reads Suſtancia, not the garbled OCR buſtanũa; the internal consonant group is visible.
[c1-l013 >] *Buppô.*
[c1-l013 note] Buppô completes the reference to Buddhist doctrine. The word is italic in this location; its circumflex is visible and is not a grave accent.
[c1-l014] Fonxocu. *Primeiro offiçio, ou dignidade.*
[c1-l014 note] Honshoku is an original office or dignity. Offiçio retains the printed cedilla, independently of modern Portuguese spelling.
[c1-l015] Fonxu. *Proprio, ou verdadeiro ſenhor.*
[c1-l015 note] Honshu denotes the proper or true lord. The Portuguese establishes ownership or lordship, not the geographical name Honshū.
[c1-l016] Fonyacu. *Explicar algũa couſa por outra lin*
[c1-l016 note] Honyaku is explaining something in another language. The last lin continues lingua below; no division sign is inferred from that continuation alone.
[c1-l017 >] *gua, ou traduzir em outra lingua. Vt,* Fon-
[c1-l017 note] The alternative Portuguese explanation is translating into another language. Vt introduces the example split Fon-/yacu across the rows.
[c1-l018 >] yacu ſuru.
[c1-l018 note] Yacu ſuru completes honyaku suru, to translate. This short physical row is part of the example, not a separate lexical entry.
[c1-l019] Fonza. *Vide.* Fonzanoxu.
[c1-l019 note] Honza is referred to honza no shu. The following full entry explains the established or long-serving members of a household.
[c1-l020] Fonzan. *Serra, ou monte principal onde vão*
[c1-l020 note] Honzan is the principal mountain or religious centre. The pilgrimage clause continues below; the gloss should not be reduced to a purely topographical mountain.
[c1-l021 >] *os gentios em romaria.*
[c1-l021 note] Em romaria supplies the religious sense: the gentios go there on pilgrimage. This completes the preceding relative clause.
[c1-l022] Fonzanoxu, *l*, fonzaxu. *Gente, ou cria-*
[c1-l022 note] Honza no shu and honzashu name established household people or servants. The Portuguese cria- continues criados, not a new word on the next row.
[c1-l023 >] *dos antigos que ha muito tempo ſeruem a al-*
[c1-l023 note] These are old servants who have served a lord for a long time. Tempo is supported by both the visible p and the temporal construction, correcting temoo.
[c1-l024 >] *gum ſenhor. ¶* Xinzaxu. *Os que ha pouco*
[c1-l024 note] After completing algum senhor, the contrasting shinzashu introduces newer servants. Its shin- contrasts with the preceding established honza group.
[c1-l025 >] *tempo começarão a ſeruir a algum ſenhor.*
[c1-l025 note] The new servants began serving only recently. The temporal ha pouco tempo contrasts explicitly with ha muito tempo in the earlier definition.
[c1-l026] Fonzon. *Idolo, ou pagode.*
[c1-l026 note] Honzon is the principal object of worship. Idolo and pagode are the missionaries' Portuguese terms for the image, not a claim that the Japanese word means a building here.
[c1-l027] Foppôuô. *Vt,* Foppôuôno ſanjin. *Tres*
[c1-l027 note] The phrase Foppôuôno ſanjin concerns the three bodies or aspects of the Buddhist principle. The initial headword's marks are worn; the fuller repeated example supplies comparison, but its clearer marks do not justify inventing missing strokes.
[c1-l028 >] *maneiras de attributos, ou qualidades que ha*
[c1-l028 note] Tres maneiras de attributos describes three kinds of attributes or qualities. Enlarged type supports attributos, replacing the nonword atricutos.
[c1-l029 >] *naquelle primeiro principio de que ſe fala no*
[c1-l029 note] The attributes belong to that first principle discussed in Buddhist teaching. Primeiro principio is the Portuguese conceptual framing, not an additional Japanese expression.
[c1-l030 >] *Buppô.*
[c1-l030 note] Buppô completes the Buddhist reference. The whole word is italic, including B, and the mark is a circumflex rather than the OCR grave.
[c1-l031] Foqe. *Vapor, ou bafo que ſae da agua quen-*
[c1-l031 note] Hoke is vapour rising from hot water. Bafo means breath or steam; the f is visible and semantically fits, unlike baſo.
[c1-l032 >] *te. ¶* Foqega tatçu. *Aleuantarſe eſte*
[c1-l032 note] Hoke ga tatsu means the vapour rises. The definition repeats this in Portuguese with Aleuantarſe, while quen-/te completes the description of hot water.
[c1-l033 >] *vapor, ou bafo.*
[c1-l033 note] The vapour is again called bafo. The letters belong to one word; the OCR's internal space split the a from the f without a comparable word gap.
[c1-l034] Fôqe, quru, eta. *Ser paruo, ou emparuoe*
[c1-l034 note] Hōqe, quru, eta gives the inflections of becoming foolish or losing one's wits. The printed long vowel and the older verbal form are retained rather than modernized to bokeru.
[c1-l035 >] *cer. ¶* Fôqeta fito, l, fôqemono. *Par*
[c1-l035 note] Hōqeta fito and hōqemono both describe a foolish person. The Portuguese Par continues paruo below; a missing end mark is not supplied on morphological grounds.
[c1-l036 >] *uo, ou tolo.*
[c1-l036 note] Uo completes paruo, followed by synonymous tolo. This is a short continuation of the previous definition, not independent page furniture.
[c1-l037] Fǒqei. Cǒbaxij megumi. *Beneficios, ou mi*
[c1-l037 note] Hōkei is an honorific kindness or favour, glossed kōbashii megumi. Enlargement shows the caron in Cǒbaxij, omitted by OCR; megumi and Beneficios support the benefaction sense.
[c1-l038 >] *mos, & afabilidade que ſe mostra a alguem*
[c1-l038 note] Mimos and afabilidade explain favours and affability shown to someone. The visible ascender in the latter supports d rather than OCR c.
[c1-l039 >] *falando com corteſia. S.*
[c1-l039 note] Falando com corteſia specifies courteous speech; S marks written usage. It qualifies the preceding honorific expression, not the following sword entry.
[c1-l040] Fôqen. Tacaranoqen. *Eſpada de* Tenxǒdai-
[c1-l040 note] Hōken, takara no ken, is a precious sword. Tenxǒdai- begins Tenshō Daijin, named as the sword's owner; the Japanese name is upright within the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l041 >] jin, *que he hũa das tres peças prezadas que tẽ*
[c1-l041 note] The enlarged first types read jin, completing Tenxǒdai-jin, not ju. The sword is described as one of three treasured objects, connecting the explanation to the imperial regalia.
[c1-l042 >] *o* D*air*i. *¶ Item, Eſpada do Rei. i,*
[c1-l042 note] Dairi names the imperial holder of the treasures. Item adds the king's sword as a further sense, then introduces the synonym on the next row.
[c1-l043 >] Guioqen.
[c1-l043 note] Guioqen is gyoken, the imperial sword. It belongs to the preceding i, explanation and is not a fresh alphabetically placed heading.
[c1-l044] Foqet. Chiuo voguinǒ. *O temperar, ou re-*
[c1-l044 note] Hoket-, chi o oginō, means replenishing the blood. The checked final t is not an omitted vowel, and Portuguese temperar or refazer supplies the medical sense.
[c1-l045 >] *fazer o ſangue. S.*
[c1-l045 note] Fazer completes refazer o sangue, to restore the blood. The final S is the written-usage label.
[c1-l046] Fôqet. Dairi. *Paços reaes.*
[c1-l046 note] Hōket and Dairi denote the imperial palace. Its marked vowel distinguishes this entry from the immediately preceding blood-replenishing word.
[c1-l047] Foqi. *Lugar alto como precipicio. ¶* Foqi
[c1-l047 note] Hoki denotes a high place such as a precipice. Alto and precipicio are recoverable in the scan, unlike OCR lto and preipotio. The final Foqi begins the cross-column example.

[c2-l001 >>] cara votçuru. *Cair deste lugar alto, &*

[c2-l003] Foq*i*. Qiuo voguinǒ. *Tomar mezinhas pe*
[c2-l003 note] Hoki, ki o oginō, replenishes vital energy. Taking medicine supplies the practical explanation; this is distinct from the preceding geographical hoki.
[c2-l004 >] *ra o coração, ou pera refazer os eſpiritus vi-*
[c2-l004 note] The medicines are for the heart or restoration of the vital spirits. Vi- continues vitaes, the historical Portuguese physiological terminology.
[c2-l005 >] *taes. S.*

[c2-l006] Fôqi. Norino fata. *Sinal, ou bandeira da*
[c2-l006 note] Hōqi, nori no hata, is a banner of the doctrine or sect. Fata is the Japanese word hata, not a Portuguese noun.
[c2-l007 >] *ſeita. ¶* Fôqiuo aguru. *Aleuantar bã-*
[c2-l007 note] The sect's banner is raised: hōqi o aguru. Portuguese aleuantar explains the action; bã- continues bandeira, not an independent abbreviation.
[c2-l008 >] *deira dalgũa ſeita, ou lei diuulgandoa.*
[c2-l008 note] Deira completes bandeira: propagating a sect or law is likened to raising its flag. The scan has deira, not OCR detira; no extra t is present.
[c2-l009] Fôqi. Fachi vocoru. *Sair, ou ajuntarſe cõ*
[c2-l009 note] Hōki means an armed uprising, so the paraphrase is linguistically expected to be tachi okoru. Enlargement nevertheless shows Fachi, with an F-like middle arm; retain the printed reading and flag a possible printing error rather than silently supplying Tachi.
[c2-l010 >] *armas muita gente. Vt,* Zanto xoxoni fô-
[c2-l010 note] Zantō shosho ni hōki su describes the remnants rising in various places. The Portuguese gloss specifies many people assembling under arms; fô- continues the verb on the next row.
[c2-l011 >] qiſu. *O restante da gente aleuantouſe com*
[c2-l011 note] Qiſu completes hōki su. The remaining people rose up; the Portuguese rest- spelling is retained without treating its s shape as a linguistic correction.
[c2-l012 >] *guerra pellos lugares.*
[c2-l012 note] The uprising takes place with warfare throughout the localities. Pellos is the printed historical contraction, not modern pelos.
[c2-l013] Fôqi. Tacarano vtçuuamono. *Vaſos, ou*
[c2-l013 note] Hōki, takara no utsuwamono, means precious vessels. The Japanese gloss distinguishes this homophone from an uprising or a religious banner.
[c2-l014 >] *outras peças ricas.*
[c2-l014 note] Other rich objects are included alongside vessels. Peças refers to articles or objects here, not theatrical pieces.
[c2-l015] Fǒqi. *Vaſoura.*
[c2-l015 note] Hōki is a broom. The caron distinguishes its historical vowel from nearby circumflex headwords; Portuguese vaſoura supplies an unambiguous gloss.
[c2-l016] Fǒqiboxi. *Meliùs,* Qiacuxei. *Cometa.*
[c2-l016 note] Hōkiboshi is a comet, literally the broom star. The dictionary prefers Qiacuxei, kyakusei, rather than rejecting the vernacular compound.
[c2-l017] Foqifoqi. *Adu. Aquelle modo de ſoar a cou*
[c2-l017 note] Hokihoki is an adverbial sound description. The account begins the noise of something breaking, with cou continuing couſa across the row boundary.
[c2-l018 >] *ſa quando ſe quebra como pao ſeco, &c.*
[c2-l018 note] Dry wood is the example of the cracking sound. Pao ſeco is historical Portuguese and should not be modernized to pão seco in Level 1.
[c2-l019] Fǒqiguſa. *Certa erua de que ſe fazem vaſou-*
[c2-l019 note] Hōkigusa is a plant used to make brooms. The compound's guſa corresponds to kusa with voicing, not a Portuguese word embedded in the definition.
[c2-l020 >] *ras. No Cami ſe diz,* Fǒqigui. *Por que*
[c2-l020 note] In Cami the plant is called hōkigi. Por que introduces the reason for the tree-like name; the scan supports P rather than OCR F. Cami is italic here and remains so.
[c2-l021 >] *he como plantaſinha de pois de grande.*
[c2-l021 note] When grown, the plant resembles a small woody plant. De pois remains spaced as printed; it is not a reason to alter the botanical headword.
[c2-l022] Fôqiǒ. Tacarano faco. *Caixão, ou arca de*
[c2-l022 note] Hōkyō, takara no hako, is a treasure chest. The two accented vowels belong to separate moraic components, matching the Japanese box gloss.
[c2-l023 >] *riquezas, ou õde estão metidas couſas precioſas.*
[c2-l023 note] The chest holds wealth or precious things. Portuguese õde is an abbreviated onde, retained rather than expanded into the diplomatic text.
[c2-l024] Fǒqua. Fiuo fanatçu. *O queimar, particu-*
[c2-l024 note] Hōkwa, hi o hanatsu, means setting fire. The definition begins a restriction on its customary uses; particu- continues particularly.
[c2-l025 >] *lar mente ſe vſa quando queimão a caſa dalgum*
[c2-l025 note] The term is especially used for burning a house. The visible separation in lar mente is retained even though the Portuguese word continues particularmente.
[c2-l026 >] *malfeitor, ou quando poẽ fogo nas caſas, &*
[c2-l026 note] Burning a wrongdoer's house and burning enemy houses are the specified cases. Malfeitor is the historical Portuguese noun, not a defective Japanese gloss.
[c2-l027 >] *pouoaçoẽs dos inimigos. ¶* Iyeuo fǒqua
[c2-l027 note] Enemy settlements complete the second context. Iye o hōkwa introduces the Japanese example of setting fire to a house.
[c2-l028 >] ſuru. *Queimar a caſa.*
[c2-l028 note] Suru completes the preceding Japanese phrase; Portuguese queimar a caſa translates the whole example. It is not an independent suru entry.
[c2-l029] Fǒqua. *Fogos que ſe fazem na China em ſinal*
[c2-l029 note] This homophonous hōkwa is a Chinese beacon fire. Em ſinal means as a signal; the separate i and n are visible where OCR read ſmal.
[c2-l030 >] *de quando entrão os inimigos. ¶* Fǒquauo
[c2-l030 note] The beacon signals an enemy incursion. Hōkwa o begins the example of raising the warning fire, distinct from the preceding arson sense.
[c2-l031 >] aguru. *Aleuantar este fogo pera que a gen-*
[c2-l031 note] Aguru completes raising the beacon. The scan supports Aleuantar, not Aleu intar; the object is the signal fire and gen- continues gente.
[c2-l032 >] *te ſe ajunte, & ſe defenda dos inimigos q̃ vẽ.*
[c2-l032 note] The people gather and defend themselves against approaching enemies. Defenda has meaningful f, not ſ; the scan and Portuguese grammar agree on the correction.
[c2-l033] Fǒquan. Tacarano camuri. *Coroa rica, &*
[c2-l033 note] Hōkwan, takara no kamuri, is a rich crown. The second-pass enlargement clearly shows a caron, so the initial OCR reading is retained despite NINJAL's Fôquan. The external headword does not override the printed accent.
[c2-l034 >] *ornada. S.*
[c2-l034 note] The crown is ornamented. S is the written-register label attached to this Japanese entry, not a fragment of the next headword.
[c2-l035] Fôquan. *Certa maneira de frauta.*
[c2-l035 note] Another hōkwan denotes a kind of flute. Historical frauta is retained and should not be normalized to flauta merely because that is modern Portuguese.
[c2-l036] Fora. *Cauerna, ou lapa.*
[c2-l036 note] Hora means a cave or cavern. Dirt crosses ou, but the surviving strokes and the pairing cauerna/ou/lapa support ou rather than OCR ox.
[c2-l037] Fôracu. *Folguedo como auto, bailo, &c. que*
[c2-l037 note] Hōraku is a performance such as a play or dance. Auto is a theatrical piece in this context; the entry proceeds to its religious setting.
[c2-l038 >] *ſe faz diante dos Camis, & Fotoques, ou por*
[c2-l038 note] The performance is before Camis and Fotoques. Both borrowed Japanese religious terms appear italic in the scan; retain that typography and allow review toggling.
[c2-l039 >] *reſpeito dalgum defunto ja de muito tempo.*
[c2-l039 note] A performance may also commemorate someone long deceased. Defunto is supported by the scan and sense; OCR de ſunto split the word and confused f with ſ.
[c2-l040] Fôracu ſuru. *Fazer eſte folguedo.*
[c2-l040 note] Hōraku suru means performing this entertainment. The complete Portuguese infinitival gloss makes the verbal use clear.
[c2-l041] Foragai. i, Forano cai. *Buzio com que cha*
[c2-l041 note] Horagai, also hora no kai, is a conch used to summon people. The worn intervening i is retained from the print; cha continues chamão on the next row without an invented hyphen.
[c2-l042 >] *mão a gente.*
[c2-l042 note] Mão completes chamão, they call the people. The row is not the independent Portuguese noun mão, hand.
[c2-l043] Fôran. Cǒbaxij araragui. *Nome de hũa cer*
[c2-l043 note] Hōran is glossed kōbashii araragi, a fragrant plant. The description identifies scent but does not justify assigning a modern botanical species from this line alone.
[c2-l044 >] *ta erua cheiroſa.*
[c2-l044 note] Ta completes certa: a particular fragrant herb. The continuation belongs to hōran, not to the following conch entry.
[c2-l045] Forano cai. *Buzio com q̃ tangẽ como buzina.*
[c2-l045 note] Hora no kai is the conch blown like a horn. Tangẽ is the printed nasal abbreviation of the verb, retained rather than expanded.
[c2-l046] Fǒrat. Rachiuo fanatçu. *Deſenfreamento, ou*
[c2-l046 note] Hōrat, rachi o hanatsu, concerns unrestrained conduct. The worn initial F still has surviving strokes; restore it from OCR's ǒrat, supported by the headword sequence and NINJAL Fǒrat.
[c2-l047 >] *deſcomedimento, ou pouca policia nas acçoẽs,*
[c2-l047 note] The Portuguese gloss describes excess and lack of civility in actions. Policia here is civil order or comportment, not specifically a modern police force.
