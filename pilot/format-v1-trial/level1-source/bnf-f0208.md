[c1-l001] Fojiro. *Hum paßarinho aßi chamado.*
[c1-l001 note] Fojiro is the bird name hojiro (hoojiro, 頬白). The definition only says a small bird so called. Neither o visibly carries an accent here; the headword index explicitly notes its normalized Fôjiro differs from the apparent print, so no accent is supplied.
[c1-l002] Fǒjo. Cǒbaxij taſuqe. *Ajuda, ou fauor*
[c1-l002 note] Hōjo, help or favour, is paraphrased as kōbashii tasuke: the honorific value is made explicit by the following Portuguese line. The long s in taſuqe belongs to Japanese tasuke, not tafuke.
[c1-l003 >] *falando com reſpeito. Vt* Gofǒjoni azzuca-
[c1-l003 note] The example begins gohōjo ni azzuka-, continued by rubecu on the next physical line: a respectful request to receive assistance. Vt introduces the example and is italic; the Japanese remains upright.
[c1-l004 >] rubecu ſoro. *Peço vos queme ajudeis. S.*
[c1-l004 note] Together with the preceding line, azzukarubecu soro corresponds to azukarubeku sōrō. Peço vos que me ajudeis means 'I ask you to help me'; the visible joining queme is retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l005] Fôjǒ. Fôuǒno xiro. *Corte. l, cidade prin-*
[c1-l005 note] Hōjō is the royal court or capital, paraphrased as hōō no shiro, the king's castle. The two accented vowels in Fôuǒno are distinct printed components; their historical accent pattern is not flattened to a modern spelling.
[c1-l006 >] *cipal del Rei.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes principal del Rei: the king's principal city. This is the continuation of the court/capital definition, not an additional Japanese entry.
[c1-l007] Fǒjǒye. *Certa cerimonia gentilica que ſe faz*
[c1-l007 note] Hōjōe is the ceremony of releasing living creatures (放生会). The Portuguese calls it a gentile ceremony; that is the historical dictionary's religious framing, not an endorsement in this note.
[c1-l008 >] *a Fachiman.*
[c1-l008 note] The recipient is Fachiman, Hachiman (八幡). The embedded Japanese divine name appears italic with the Portuguese here and is preserved that way.
[c1-l009] Fôman. Norini manzuru. *Soberba, & arro*
[c1-l009 note] Hōman is religious pride, explained as nori ni manzuru. The Portuguese begins 'pride and arrogance'; arro continues as gãcia, with no new lexical item at the break.
[c1-l010 >] *gãcia dos que dilatão ou pregão algũa lei falſa*
[c1-l010 note] The definition describes those spreading or preaching a supposedly false law. Worn type caused dilatão, pregão and falſa to be misread; their letter traces and sentence sense support these readings. The polemical evaluation belongs to the source.
[c1-l011 >] *dizendo que naõ ha outra lei milhor no mundo,*
[c1-l011 note] The boast is that no other law is better in the world. Milhor is the printed historical form, not a reason to replace it with modern melhor; the tilde positions are retained.
[c1-l012 >] *ou ſaluação, &c.*
[c1-l012 note] The phrase ends 'or salvation, etc.', continuing the dictionary's account of exclusivist religious pride. The slightly awkward scope is not silently rewritten into modern Portuguese.
[c1-l013] Fomare. *Louuor. ¶* Fomareuo toru.
[c1-l013 note] Homare means praise or honour; homare wo toru is to obtain it. The worn e in Fomareuo was mistaken for c; both the scan and the transparent Japanese construction resolve it.
[c1-l014 >] *Alcançar honra, & louuor.*
[c1-l014 note] Alcançar honra, & louuor translates the preceding example: to attain honour and praise. The initial OCR Altanzar is not a plausible reading of the visible Portuguese word.
[c1-l015] Fome, uru, eta. *Louuar.*
[c1-l015 note] Home, uru, eta gives the inflectional forms of homeru, to praise. The compressed conjugation notation must not be expanded in the diplomatic text.
[c1-l016] Fomeague, uru, eta. *Louuando aleuantar*
[c1-l016 note] Homeage-, homeaguru, homeageta means to extol or praise someone highly. Aleuantar is the source's spelling for raising someone up through praise.
[c1-l017 >] *a alguẽ.*
[c1-l017 note] A alguẽ supplies the person being extolled. The abbreviation by nasal tilde and the physical short line are retained.
[c1-l018] Fomedocoro. *Couſa, & ocaſião de louuar*
[c1-l018 note] Homedokoro is an aspect or occasion for praise. Ocaſião, occasion, fits both the scan's i and the sense; the OCR t in ocaſtão was spurious.
[c1-l019 >] *algũa couſa.*
[c1-l019 note] Algũa couſa completes the phrase 'to praise something'. This does not begin a new sense or headword.
[c1-l020] Fǒmei. Cobaxij na. *Louuor, & honra. S*
[c1-l020 note] Hōmei is a favourable name or reputation, glossed kōbashii na and Portuguese praise and honour. Louuor has a worn first o, not the OCR c in Lcuuor. The initial o of Cobaxij is unaccented in this occurrence.
[c1-l021] Fǒmen. Cob. xij yuruxi, i, võ yuruxi. *Per*
[c1-l021 note] Hōmen is honourable permission or pardon, paraphrased kōbashii yurushi and on-yurushi. The internal gap/dot in Cob. xij is retained as visible rather than silently regularized; the linguistic reading still crosses it. The second pass recovered the final j descender, which OCR had treated as i.
[c1-l022 >] *dão de peßoa alta, & nobre.*
[c1-l022 note] The pardon comes from a high-ranking noble person. The β-like ligature in peßoa is retained as such, following the project's distinction from separately printed long s letters.
[c1-l023] Fomeqi, u, eita. *Fazer grande quentura.*
[c1-l023 note] Homeku is to become hot; the principal parts homeqi, u, eita agree with the Japanese verb. The Portuguese describes great heat, not praise despite its proximity to home- entries.
[c1-l024 >] *ou eſtar muito quente. ¶* Miga fomequ.
[c1-l024 note] Mi ga homequ means the body becomes hot. The scan supports fomequ, not femeçu; reading the Japanese and the neighbouring headword exposes both errors.
[c1-l025 >] *eſtar eſquentado, ou ter febre.*
[c1-l025 note] The Portuguese identifies the bodily heat as being hot or feverish. It translates the previous example rather than adding another conjugation.
[c1-l026] Fômi. Norino agiuai. *Goſto, & fruito da lei.*
[c1-l026 note] Fômi is hōmi (法味), the taste or benefit of the teaching, paraphrased nori no ajiwai. Enlargement resolves the heavily worn mi and Norino; OCR Fǒnn and Noriro were not linguistically coherent.
[c1-l027] Fomio. *Nome que ſe poẽ de pois de hum ſe*
[c1-l027 note] Fomio denotes hōmyō (法名), the name given upon entering religious life. The headword's accents are not visible, unlike the example below, so the unaccented printed reading remains. Nome, not None, is supported by the worn letter remnants.
[c1-l028 >] *fazer religioſo, ou rapar. ¶* Cano jen-
[c1-l028 note] The definition continues with becoming a religious or taking the tonsure. Cano jen- begins 'that Zen monk', continued as mon; the Japanese example is distinct from the preceding Portuguese.
[c1-l029 >] mon fômiǒua nanto? *Como ſe chama aquel*
[c1-l029 note] Cano jenmon fômiǒua nanto asks the religious name of that Zen monk. Enlargement shows both accents in the example and the word chama, 'is called'; the OCR fonuoua obscured the Japanese compound.
[c1-l030 >] *le rapado.*
[c1-l030 note] Rapado, literally a shaved person, completes the Portuguese reference to the tonsured monk. The damaged a is not o; no extra descriptive words are supplied.
[c1-l031] Fômon. Norino mondǒ. *Diſputa acerca das*
[c1-l031 note] Hōmon here is debate about doctrine, paraphrased nori no mondō. It is distinct from the homophonous 'gate of the teaching' in the following entry.
[c1-l032 >] *leis.*
[c1-l032 note] Leis completes the object of the doctrinal debate. The dictionary's lei encompasses religious law or teaching here, not only civil legislation.
[c1-l033] Fômon. Norino cado. *Seita, ou inſtituto*
[c1-l033 note] Hōmon, nori no kado, is a sect or religious institution (法門). A faint circumflex is visible over the headword's o; it should not be omitted merely because OCR missed it.
[c1-l034 >] *differente. S.*
[c1-l034 note] Differente completes the description of a distinct institution. The scan has the initial di and double f; S. is an italic register marker.
[c1-l035] Fǒmon. Cobaxij tô. *O perguntar, ou vi-*
[c1-l035 note] Hōmon in this entry concerns an honourable inquiry or visit (訪問), paraphrased kōbashii tō. The worn heading still supports mon rather than men; enlargement shows the end of Cobaxij. The headword's incomplete accent is read as caron with the lexical evidence.
[c1-l036 >] *ſitar por carta, honrando a peſſoa que viſitou,*
[c1-l036 note] The visit can be by letter, honouring the person who visited. Honrando replaces the broken OCR hom ando, and peſſoa replaces peſca; the two tall s forms are visible under enlargement.
[c1-l037 >] *ou eſcreueo. S.*
[c1-l037 note] Ou eſcreueo adds the correspondent who wrote rather than visited. The italic S. closes the usage note, not the Japanese example.
[c1-l038] Fômot. *Eſmola q̃ ſe dà por reſpeito de propagar*
[c1-l038 note] Hōmot is an offering devoted to religious purposes. The definition says alms given for propagating the law; reſpeito and propagar have recoverable worn letters, unlike the initial OCR forms.
[c1-l039 >] *a lei, aleuantar igrejas, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] Raising churches or temples is a further use of the offering. Igrejas is the Portuguese explanatory vocabulary even in this Buddhist context.
[c1-l040] Fômu. *Certo officio entre os Bõzos.*
[c1-l040 note] Fômu is hōmu, a Buddhist office, not Fônu. The m is damaged but the surviving strokes and the headword data agree; the definition does not itself identify a more precise institutional role.
[c1-l041] Fomura. *Chamas de fogo. ¶* Fomurauo
[c1-l041 note] Homura means flames. The example begins homura wo and is completed by moyasu, used figuratively for anger rather than an instruction to kindle an actual fire.
[c1-l042 >] moyaſu. *Encolerizarſe, ou acenderſe em*
[c1-l042 note] Homura wo moyasu is glossed as becoming angry or inflamed. The scan supports continuous moyaſu, Encolerizarſe and acenderſe; the OCR spaces and missing letters had disrupted both languages.
[c1-l043 >] *colera.*
[c1-l043 note] Colera completes 'to become inflamed with anger'. No accent is added from the modern Portuguese spelling cólera.
[c1-l044] Fǒmuri, uru, utta. *Enterrar corpos mortos.*
[c1-l044 note] Hōmuru is to bury the dead. The printed principal-parts notation uru, utta is retained, even though it is abbreviated differently from the full modern dictionary form.
[c1-l045 >] *Vt,* Xigaiuo fǒmuru.
[c1-l045 note] Shigai wo hōmuru means to bury a corpse. Vt is italic and the Japanese example upright; the phrase confirms the preceding verb's meaning.
[c1-l046] Fon. Moto. *Treslado, ou original. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l046 note] Hon is glossed moto, origin or original, and Portuguese a copy or original. The initial M of Moto is worn but cannot sensibly be Noto in this definition.
[c1-l047 >] *Couſa propria, verdadeira, & determinada.*
[c1-l047 note] A further sense is something proper, true and determined. Determinada is damaged but recoverable; the OCR deterninada was an m/n confusion.

[c2-l001] Fonami. *Eſpigas de trigo, ou arroz, &c. q̃*
[c2-l001 note] Honami refers to the arrangement of ears of grain, including wheat or rice. The final relative q̃ links to the next line and was mistaken by OCR for d.
[c2-l002 >] *estão todas juntas, & hum pouco inclinadas*
[c2-l002 note] The ears are together and slightly inclined. The final s of inclinadas is visible; it is not a line-ending hyphen. The source's short s in estão is retained.
[c2-l003 >] *¶* Fonamiga yoi. *Eſtar boa a ſeara, &*
[c2-l003 note] Honami ga yoi means the crop's ears are well formed or arranged. The Portuguese describes a good field of grain, continuing on the following line.
[c2-l004 >] *bem eſpigada.*
[c2-l004 note] Bem eſpigada completes the description of a well-eared crop. This agricultural sense distinguishes honami from the following hon- compounds.
[c2-l005] Fonbara. *Filho legitimo. Vt,* Are fonbara
[c2-l005 note] Honbara is a legitimate child, illustrated by are honbara no ko ja. The definition's legitimo has an ordinary damaged m, not the extra letters in OCR legitimro.
[c2-l006 >] no cogia, l, ano co fonbaragia. *Aquel-*
[c2-l006 note] The alternative ano ko honbara ja uses the same noun; fonbaragia corrects the OCR fonbatagia. Gia is the historical ja spelling, not gya.
[c2-l007 >] *le he filho legitimo.*
[c2-l007 note] The Portuguese completes 'that is a legitimate son'. The two Japanese word orders receive a single shared translation.
[c2-l008] Fonbiǒ. Motono yamai. *Doença propria, &*
[c2-l008 note] Honbyō, moto no yamai, is the original or primary disease. The contrast with secondary illnesses is completed below.
[c2-l009 >] *principal em comparação de outras accidentari*
[c2-l009 note] The comparison is with accidental or supervening illnesses. Accidentari continues into as; the damaged e should not be read as o in accidentari.
[c2-l010 >] *as. ¶ Item, Permet. Vicio, ou manha*
[c2-l010 note] Per met. introduces a metaphorical sense: an ingrained vice or habit. The printed joining Permet. is left intact rather than expanded to a full Latin phrase.
[c2-l011 >] *propria, ou primeira. ¶* Fonbiǒga vo-
[c2-l011 note] Fonbiǒga vo- begins honbyō ga okotta, a relapse into the original ailment or habit. Here the following Portuguese selects the metaphorical sense.
[c2-l012 >] cotta. *Tornou a ſeus vicios, ou maos*
[c2-l012 note] Completes okotta and translates it as returning to one's vices or bad customs. Maos has a final s, not the OCR hyphen; the following line provides cuſtumes.
[c2-l013 >] *cuſtumes.*
[c2-l013 note] Cuſtumes completes the relapse into bad habits. Its initial u spelling is preserved rather than replaced with modern costumes.
[c2-l014] Fonbô. Fonno nori. *Propria lei, ou doctri-*
[c2-l014 note] Honbō, hon no nori, is the original doctrine or teaching. The definition is explicitly religious rather than the unrelated modern homophone for a temple residence.
[c2-l015 >] *na.* Bup.
[c2-l015 note] Bup. labels the Buddhist vocabulary after the completed word doctrina. The whole label, including its punctuation, is upright.
[c2-l016] Fonbon. *Proprio, ou verdadeiro.*
[c2-l016 note] Honbon is glossed as proper or true. The following no and ni formations show how this base functions adjectivally and adverbially.
[c2-l017] Fonbonno. *Couſa propria, & legitima, ou*
[c2-l017 note] Honbon no qualifies something proper and legitimate. This is an attributive form, with the Portuguese continuation adding 'true'.
[c2-l018 >] *verdadeira.*
[c2-l018 note] Verdadeira completes the adjectival definition. It belongs to the preceding honbonno line.
[c2-l019 >] Fonbonni. *Adu.*
[c2-l019 note] Honbon ni is the adverbial counterpart. Adu. is an italic grammatical label, not a Japanese word to be converted to kana.
[c2-l020] Fonbucu. *Tornar como primeiro. ¶* Ya-
[c2-l020 note] Honbuku means returning to the former state, especially recovery. Ya- starts yamai/illness in the following example, but the source actually spells the continuation as ma ga.
[c2-l021 >] ma ga fonbucu ſuru. *Sàrar da infirmica-*
[c2-l021 note] Across the break, yama ga honbuku suru is translated as recovering from illness. The visible yama form is retained instead of supplying an unprinted i to make yamai.
[c2-l022 >] *de. ¶ Itẽ,* Fonbucu, l, *potiùs* fǒpucu ſuru.
[c2-l022 note] A second sense restores someone to their former position. Potiùs is the Latin 'rather', not Japanese potiùo; it introduces the preferable expression fǒpucu suru (hōfuku). The actual word is pucu, not OCR pocu.
[c2-l023 >] *Ser reſtituido à patria do desterro, ou a ſeu*
[c2-l023 note] The restoration may be to one's homeland from exile. Desterro, exile, resolves the OCR destemo; the original short s is preserved.
[c2-l024 >] *eſtado, & renda.*
[c2-l024 note] Or restoration to one's estate and income. Renda begins with lowercase r, not the OCR capital T.
[c2-l025] Fonbun. *Primeiro principio.* Bup.
[c2-l025 note] Honbun is the first principle, with the Buddhist label Bup. The middle letter is worn; the neighbouring repeated label and the scan support u rather than OCR a. The label is wholly upright.
[c2-l026] Fonbut. Fonno Fotoqe. *Primeiro, & pro*
[c2-l026 note] Honbut, hon no hotoke, denotes the original Buddha. The checked final t in the headword is intentional; Primeiro corrects an OCR omission rather than expanding the Japanese.
[c2-l027 >] *prio Fotoque.*
[c2-l027 note] Proprio Fotoque completes the definition. This embedded Japanese-derived name appears italic, with its source spelling que, which must not be mechanically changed to qi or qe.
[c2-l028] Fonchi. Fonno chiye. *Verdadeiro ſaber do*
[c2-l028 note] Honchi is true wisdom, paraphrased hon no chiye. Fonchi rather than Fenchi agrees with both the visible vowel and this explanation.
[c2-l029 >] *que ſe fala no* Buppô.
[c2-l029 note] The wisdom is that spoken of in Buppô, Buddhist doctrine. Fala is Portuguese 'speaks', not ſala; Buppô is upright and has a circumflex rather than a nasal tilde.
[c2-l030] Fonchi. Motono nǒchi. *Renda propria q̃*
[c2-l030 note] A separate honchi means one's original land or revenue, moto no nōchi. The two homophones should not be merged simply because their headwords match.
[c2-l031 >] *hum tinha dantes. ¶* Fonchiye cayeru.
[c2-l031 note] Fonchiye cayeru is honchi e kaeru, to return to one's original holding. The particle ye is visible; OCR yo would give the wrong Japanese construction.
[c2-l032 >] *Tornar a ſua propria renda.*
[c2-l032 note] Renda, income or holding, translates the return to honchi. The initial r was mistaken for c by OCR.
[c2-l033] Fondǒ. Motono michi. *Verdadeiro, ou pro*
[c2-l033 note] Hondō is the true or original path, paraphrased moto no michi. Michi is clearly the intended and visible word, not the OCR niichi.
[c2-l034 >] *prio caminho, ou eſtrada. ¶ Per met.* Fõ
[c2-l034 note] The literal path becomes a metaphor for an upright person. Fõ is Japanese and continues as dono below; the initial F must not remain inside the Portuguese italic run.
[c2-l035 >] dono. l, fondǒna fito. *Homem verda-*
[c2-l035 note] Hondō no or hondō na hito describes a true, upright person. The accent over the worn o is incompletely printed; the established headword and surviving trace support retaining the caron reading.
[c2-l036 >] *deiro, & recto.*
[c2-l036 note] Verdadeiro, & recto completes the moral description. Recto is the source spelling, not modern reto.
[c2-l037] Fondǒ. *Arte de medicina ſem mistura de çu*
[c2-l037 note] Hondō also denotes medicine as distinct from surgery. Arte corrects the OCR Atte; the surgery word visibly begins çu and continues as rugia, so the historical çurugia is not modernized.
[c2-l038 >] *rugia. ¶* Fondouo narǒ. *Aprender*
[c2-l038 note] Hondouo narǒ means to learn medicine. The unaccented o in Fondouo is retained where the scan lacks a secure mark; Aprender is one Portuguese word, not A prenaer.
[c2-l039 >] *a arte de medicina. ¶* Fondǒuo ſuru. *E-*
[c2-l039 note] The next example hondō wo suru means to practise medicine. Its o is visibly accented, unlike the previous occurrence; the Portuguese E- continues into xercitar.
[c2-l040 >] *xercitar a medicina.*
[c2-l040 note] Exercitar a medicina translates practising medicine. The line break inside the infinitive remains a physical feature, not a separate word boundary.
[c2-l041] Fondǒ. *Igreja matriz, ou principal em reſpeito*
[c2-l041 note] Hondō is also the principal temple building. Igreja matriz and principal describe its relation to subsidiary chapels; the worn tr and p do not support OCR maniz or princiral.
[c2-l042 >] *dalgũas ermidas, ou capelas vizinhas.*
[c2-l042 note] The smaller surrounding buildings are ermidas or nearby chapels. The apparent OCR spaces within ermidas and vizinhas are damaged letters, not word divisions.
[c2-l043] Fone. *Oßo. ¶* Foneuo voru. *Por mui-*
[c2-l043 note] Hone means bone; hone wo oru figuratively means to exert oneself. Oßo begins with a worn O, not C, and preserves the s ligature.
[c2-l044 >] *ta diligencia com trabalho.*
[c2-l044 note] The idiom is translated as applying much diligence with effort. The dictionary already records the figurative use familiar from modern 骨を折る.
[c2-l045] Fone. *Armação do abano, ſombreiro, Biǒbus,*
[c2-l045 note] Hone also means a framework: of a fan, umbrella or folding screen. Biǒbus is an embedded Japanese loanword with Portuguese plural s; the scan supports a caron over o rather than a tilde.
[c2-l046 >] *portas de papel, &c.*
[c2-l046 note] Paper doors are another object with such a framework. This line finishes the list; the next heading is a new compound.
[c2-l047] Fonegumi. *Oßada, ou armação dos oßos.*
[c2-l047 note] Honegumi is the skeleton or arrangement of bones. Oßada, armação and oßos are recoverable under the worn print; the OCR Opara and aſot obscured the entire gloss. The bottom-right Fone- is a catchword, not part of this definition.
