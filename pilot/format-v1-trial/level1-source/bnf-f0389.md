[c1-l001 >] *couſa ſe apreße, deſejo apreßarme ainda q̃ ſe-*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the previous page’s maxim about hastening a good event: the speaker wishes to hurry, even if only by a short time. Couſa repeats the f388 catchword; the whole row is an indented Portuguese continuation, not a new entry.

[c1-l002 note] Ja completes ſe-ja, followed by a quarter or half an hour. The printed mea is the historical form and is not regularized to meia.
[c1-l003] Qichinichi. *Bom dia, & ditoſo, ou bem eſ-*
[c1-l003 note] Qichinichi means an auspicious day. Bom dia, ditoſo and bem eſ- describe a good, fortunate, well-begun day, rather than merely a greeting.
[c1-l004 >] *treado.*
[c1-l004 note] Treado completes eſtreado, the final characterization of the auspicious day. This short continuation closes Qichinichi.
[c1-l005] Qichirei. Yoqi tamexi. *Bom custume, ou vſo.*
[c1-l005 note] Qichirei is a good precedent or usage, paraphrased by yoki tameshi. The scan joins Qichirei as one word; the Portuguese custume and its provisional italic st classification are preserved.
[c1-l006] Qichǒ. *Cortinas, ou panos ricos de armar que*
[c1-l006 note] Qichǒ denotes a curtained furnishing, described as curtains or rich hanging cloths. The caron over o is visible and the sentence continues with the supporting framework.
[c1-l007 >] *antigamente armauão ſobre hum madeiramento*
[c1-l007 note] Explains that these hangings were formerly arranged over a wooden frame. Armauão and madeiramento are retained as historical Portuguese, not modernized.
[c1-l008 >] *que ſe podia menear, ou mudar como Biǒbus.*
[c1-l008 note] The framework could be moved or shifted like folding screens. Enlargement reads Biǒbus with a caron, not OCR Bibhus; the Japanese-derived term is embedded in an otherwise italic Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l009] Qichǒ. i, Fidarui. *Fome, ou barriga fa-*
[c1-l009 note] This Qichǒ is hunger, paraphrased by fidarui, hungry. The Portuguese begins barriga fa-, a hungry belly, and does not refer to the furnishing above.
[c1-l010 >] *minta.* S.
[c1-l010 note] Minta completes faminta. The final S. is an upright usage label outside the italic Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l011] Qichô. *Tornar a ſeu reino. ¶* Qichôſuru.
[c1-l011 note] Qichô is returning to one’s country. The scan has Tornar, not Tomar, and the repeated verb Qichôſuru also bears the circumflex; its compact spelling is retained.
[c1-l012 >] *Idem. Como os que vão à China, ou aos Luçoẽs*
[c1-l012 note] The same meaning is illustrated by people who go to China or Luzon. Luçoẽs is the printed historical geographical name and retains its visible tilde carrier.
[c1-l013 >] *& tornam a Iapão.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the example: they return to Japan. The initial in Iapão is capital I, not a lowercase l; this return from abroad is contrasted with the later Qicocu entry.
[c1-l014] Qichǒmen. *Cantos rombos, ou rodondos q̃*
[c1-l014 note] Qichǒmen here is an architectural treatment of rounded or blunted corners, not the modern personality adjective. The scan reads Cantos, restoring the t lost by OCR, and preserves rodondos.
[c1-l015 >] *ſe fazem como ſeparados ẽ algũas Faxiras qua-*
[c1-l015 note] The corners are formed as distinct edges on certain square pillars. Faxiras, from hashira with a Portuguese plural ending, occurs inside the Portuguese explanation and is retained in the printed italic setting.
[c1-l016 >] *dradas, &c. ¶* Qichǒmenuo toru. *Di-*
[c1-l016 note] Dradas completes quadradas. Qichǒmenuo toru illustrates making the architectural corner treatment; the following Di- is the italic beginning of Portuguese Diuidir, not a Japanese fragment.

[c1-l017 note] Completes Diuidir: shaping or separating these corners and edges so they can be lacquered. Enlargement supports vruxarẽ, the Japanese-derived lacquer verb, rather than OCR vrucarẽ; the abbreviated ending remains unexpanded.
[c1-l018 >] *& ornarem.*
[c1-l018 note] Ornarem completes the purpose of the corner treatment, ornamenting the members. It belongs to the architectural Qichǒmen sense, not to the following honorific address.
[c1-l019] Qicô. Tattoqi qimi. *Senhor, voſſa merce,*
[c1-l019 note] Qicô is an honorific for the addressee, paraphrased by an honored lord. The scan has Qicô rather than Qacô and voſſa merce rather than the corrupted OCR vaſa meree; the paired long-s shapes were enlarged for inspection.
[c1-l020 >] *ſenhoria, &c.* S.
[c1-l020 note] Senhoria extends the list of honorific equivalents. The upright S. stands outside the italic gloss as the usage label.
[c1-l021] Qicocu. *Certa fruita que ſe dà em hũs eſpinhos*
[c1-l021 note] This Qicocu is a medicinal fruit produced on a thorny plant. The scan’s dà and hũs retain their visible accent and abbreviation, without adding a modern botanical identification.
[c1-l022 >] *que ſerue de mezinha.*
[c1-l022 note] The fruit is said to serve as a remedy. The printed mezinha has the historical medicinal sense; this short continuation closes the plant entry.

[c1-l023 note] The next Qicocu is returning to one’s own local country or domain, explicitly paraphrased cuniye cayeru. The Portuguese reino begins across rei-no and contrasts with Japan as a whole below.

[c1-l024 note] Completes reino particular and gives Qicocu ſuru as the verb phrase. Por que then introduces the explanation distinguishing local return from return from abroad.
[c1-l025 >] *tornar de outro reino eſtrãgeiro ao reino vniuer-*
[c1-l025 note] The distinction concerns returning from a foreign country to the larger realm of Japan. The hyphen in vniuer- is visible and retained before the continuation.

[c1-l026 note] Completes vniuerſal de Iapão, meaning Japan taken as the whole realm. The sentence says a different expression is more properly used; the name begins with capital I rather than OCR l.
[c1-l027 >] Qichô ſuru.
[c1-l027 note] Qichô ſuru supplies that preferred expression for returning to Japan from abroad. It is a standalone roman phrase continuing the Portuguese discussion, not a newly repeated headword.
[c1-l028] Qicodôjǒ. Voquru, iru, fataraqu xizzu-
[c1-l028 note] The compound groups rising, being or sitting, activity, and stillness. The Japanese paraphrase continues with xizzu-cani; two z letters are visible, so the OCR’s single z is not retained.

[c1-l030 note] Completes trabalhar and being still without moving. The printed compact ſemouer is retained, and the abbreviation at the end has a tilde, Itẽ, introducing the additional temporal sense.
[c1-l031 >] *Em todo tempo, ou ſempre. Vt,* Qicodô-
[c1-l031 note] The added sense is at all times or always. Qicodô- begins the example using the compound in a phrase for the entire span of one’s activities.

[c1-l033 note] Todo tempo completes remembering at all times. The final S. is an upright usage label, not part of the italic Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l034] Qicon. *Calete, ou compreiſão. ¶* Qicon
[c1-l034 note] Qicon refers to constitution or mettle, rendered by calete and compreiſão. The repeated Qicon begins an example of a person with a strong constitution; the OCR’s gap after Q is removed.

[c1-l035 note] No tçuyoi fito completes qicon no tsuyoi hito, a person strong in constitution or spirit. The Portuguese cõpreiſão retains its printed abbreviation rather than being modernized to compleição.

[c1-l036 note] Qiconja, alternatively qiconxa, names a person of good constitution or mettle. The two roman Japanese alternatives are separated by the printed l, abbreviation, not treated as terms embedded in Portuguese.

[c1-l038] Qicori. *Homem que corta lenha nos matos.*
[c1-l038 note] Qicori is a woodcutter, specifically a man cutting firewood in woodland. The initial Q belongs to the headword, and enlargement confirms the narrow printed space between corta and lenha.
[c1-l039] Qicot. Fadaye, fone. *Superficie da carne,*
[c1-l039 note] Qicot combines the surface of the flesh and bones, paraphrased by hadaye and hone. The checked final t is retained; the Portuguese supplies the first part of the anatomical pair.
[c1-l040 >] *& oßos.* S.
[c1-l040 note] Adds bones to the flesh surface above. The final S. is an upright usage label; oßos keeps the printed double-s representation.
[c1-l041] Qicoximeſare, uru, eta. *Ouuir, ou comer, &*
[c1-l041 note] Qicoximeſare is an honorific verb covering hearing and consuming food or drink. The scan reads Ouuir with two u letters; the social restriction and third verb continue below.

[c1-l043 note] Qicoximexi has the same honorific senses, with its own principal parts. The initial letter is roman Q, replacing the isolated italic O in the OCR.

[c1-l044 note] Qicoximexi ire means hearing and taking in something, said of a person of rank. The historical iruru principal part is retained, while the Portuguese nobility phrase continues across no-bre.

[c1-l047 note] The person of rank hears something or happens to hear it. This closes the honorific expression and the column, without an additional omitted example.
