[c1-l001] Souocu. Voroſocana iye. *Caſa mal feita,*
[c1-l001 note] Souocu is a poorly built house, as explained by Voroſocana iye and the Portuguese. The unmarked o distinguishes this entry from the following grass-thatched dwelling.
[c1-l002 >] *& pobre.*
[c1-l002 note] The continuation adds that the house is poor or humble; it completes the preceding definition rather than forming a separate entry.
[c1-l003] Sǒuocu. Cuſano iye. *Caſa feita de eruas. i,*
[c1-l003 note] Sǒuocu is a house made of grass or similar vegetation. Cuſano iye supplies the Japanese paraphrase; the caron belongs to this headword, unlike Souocu above.
[c1-l004 >] *Choupana.*
[c1-l004 note] Choupana identifies the grass-built dwelling as a hut. This short, indented physical line completes the preceding explanation.
[c1-l005] Sǒuon. *Não ſe vſa aßi ſò ſe não com algũa cõ-*
[c1-l005 note] Sǒuon is said not to be used by itself, only in a compound or expression. The Portuguese word for composition is divided after cõ- at the right edge.
[c1-l006 >] *poſição. Vt,* Sandai sǒuonno qimi. *Se-*
[c1-l006 note] Sandai sǒuonno qimi introduces a lord whose benefactions extend across three generations. The printed space after Sandai and the cedilla in poſição are retained; Se- begins senhor.
[c1-l007 >] *nhor de quem ſe tem recebidos muitos beneficios*
[c1-l007 note] The continuation identifies the lord as one from whom many benefits have been received. It carries the Portuguese explanation of the preceding Japanese expression.
[c1-l008 >] *deſde ſeus ante paßados.*
[c1-l008 note] The benefits extend back to the ancestors. The printed separation ante paßados is preserved rather than modernizing the Portuguese compound.
[c1-l009] Sôxa. i, Mǒxitçugui, l, toritçuguite. *Ho-*
[c1-l009 note] Sôxa denotes an intermediary who speaks or conveys messages for others. Mǒxitçugui and toritçuguite are alternative Japanese explanations, not terms embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c1-l010 >] *mem que tem por officio falar por outros, ou*
[c1-l010 note] The intermediary has the duty of speaking on behalf of others. The alternative duty introduced by ou continues onto the next physical line.
[c1-l011 >] *leuar recados ao ſenhor.*
[c1-l011 note] Carrying messages to the lord completes the duties of the sôxa. The final senhor belongs to this definition, not the following place-name.
[c1-l012] Sôxadocoro. *Lugar como portaria onde ſe to-*
[c1-l012 note] Sôxadocoro is the place where such messages are received, compared with a porter’s lodge or reception office. To- is the printed beginning of tomão.
[c1-l013 >] *mão os recados.*
[c1-l013 note] The continuation completes tomão os recados, receiving messages. The historical nasal ending is preserved as printed.
[c1-l014] Sǒxei. Youo fayǒ ſu. i, Youo fayǒ ſaru. *O*
[c1-l014 note] The entry concerns leaving life early: Youo fayǒ ſu is explained by Youo fayǒ ſaru. The damaged headword is read Sǒxei, supported by the supplied NINJAL index; its first vowel and accent are weak in this impression.
[c1-l015 >] *morrer de pouca idade, ou de preßa.*
[c1-l015 note] The Portuguese specifies dying young or quickly, confirming the early-death sense rather than resurrection in the next entry.
[c1-l016] Soxei. i, Yomigayeru. *Reſurreição. Vt,*
[c1-l016 note] Soxei is resurrection or revival, paraphrased by Yomigayeru. Its unmarked vowel is retained independently of the previous headword.
[c1-l017 >] Soxei xita fito. *Homem que reſucitou.*
[c1-l017 note] Soxei xita fito means a person who has come back to life. The Portuguese past-tense verb confirms that the example describes the revived person.
[c1-l018] Sǒxen. Auoi coqe. *Muſgos verdes.* S.
[c1-l018 note] Sǒxen, explained by Auoi coqe, denotes green moss. The initial is S, not OCR B; the terminal written-register label is a separate upright S., with no following lexical a.
[c1-l019] Soxet. Xirayuqi. *Neue.*
[c1-l019 note] Soxet is white snow, with Xirayuqi as its Japanese explanation. The final t is retained as the historical checked-syllable spelling.
[c1-l020] Sǒxet. Ximo, yuqi. *Geada, & neue.*
[c1-l020 note] Sǒxet combines frost and snow, explicitly paired as Ximo, yuqi. The caron distinguishes this compound from the preceding snow entry.
[c1-l021] Soxi. *Homẽs inſignes antiguos que fundarão*
[c1-l021 note] Soxi refers to eminent ancient founders. The Portuguese relative clause continues below to identify the religious traditions they founded.
[c1-l022 >] *algũa religião, ou ſeita. ¶* Soxi xento-
[c1-l022 note] The founders established a religion or sect. Soxi xento- continues as xentocu below; the damaged t is read from its surviving foot and the meaningful compound, not as OCR e.
[c1-l023 >] cu. *Idem.*
[c1-l023 note] Cu completes xentocu, paired with Soxi for ancestral founders and earlier worthies. Idem refers to the same definition and is not a new headword.
[c1-l024] Sǒxi. Narabu cami. *Liuros de verſos, ou hi-*
[c1-l024 note] Sǒxi names books of verse or narrative. Narabu cami explains their assembled sheets; hi- begins the Portuguese word for histories or stories.
[c1-l025 >] *ſtorias eſcritos em lingua branda de Iapão.*
[c1-l025 note] The books are written in the vernacular or plain language of Japan. The damaged country-name is read Iapão; its descender and the bilingual context exclude the OCR laſão.
[c1-l026] Sǒxi. *Tres, ou quatro.* Sǒxinin tçurete, tçu-
[c1-l026 note] Here Sǒxi means three or four. Sǒxinin tçurete begins an example about taking three or four companions; the initial Portuguese word is Tres, not OCR Pres.
[c1-l027 >] neyorimo fiqi tçucurôte iderareta. Feiq.
[c1-l027 note] The example continues with dressing more carefully than usual and going out. Feiq. is the upright citation label, separate from the Japanese sentence.
[c1-l028 >] *Lib. 10. Leuando conſigo em companhia tres,*
[c1-l028 note] The printed reference is Lib. 10. The Portuguese translation begins by describing the three or four accompanying people; the line ends with a comma.
[c1-l029 >] *ou quatro homens, ſahio milhor atauiado, &*
[c1-l029 note] The person went out better dressed than usual. ſahio, meaning went out, replaces OCR ſabio and agrees with iderareta in the Japanese example.
[c1-l030 >] *luſtroſo do que cuſtumaua. ¶* Cami sǒ xi-
[c1-l030 note] The first example concludes with being more splendidly dressed than usual. Cami sǒ xi- begins the second example, counting three or four sheets of paper.
[c1-l031 >] mai. *Tres, ou quatro folhas de papel.*
[c1-l031 note] Mai completes the paper-counter example, translated as three or four sheets. It is the continuation of xi-mai, not an independent entry.
[c1-l032] Soxi. *Filhos de pois do morgado, ou qualquer*
[c1-l032 note] Soxi denotes sons other than the primary heir. The initial S is damaged but identifiable; reading Foxi would disconnect the headword from this definition.
[c1-l033 >] *filho que não he morgado.*
[c1-l033 note] The continuation clarifies that any son who is not the heir is included. Morgado is the Portuguese inheritance term used by the dictionary.
[c1-l034] Sǒxi. *Por breue eſpaço. ¶* Sǒxi tenpai.
[c1-l034 note] Sǒxi here means a brief interval. Sǒxi tenpai supplies a synonymous expression, distinct from the preceding numerical and kinship entries.
[c1-l035 >] *O meſmo. i, em quanto hum leuanta a cabeça*
[c1-l035 note] The brief interval is explained as the time taken to raise one’s head. The explanatory clause continues on the next row.
[c1-l036 >] *pera cima, ou a abaixa.*
[c1-l036 note] Raising or lowering the head completes the illustration of a momentary interval. The printed alternatives are preserved without combining physical lines.
[c1-l037] Sôxi, ſuru, ita. *Falar ao Rey. ¶* Quan-
[c1-l037 note] Sôxi and its principal forms mean addressing the ruler. Quan- begins the following musical-performance example.
[c1-l038 >] guenuo sǒſuru. *Fazer armonia, ou deſ-*
[c1-l038 note] Quanguenuo sǒſuru means performing instrumental music before the ruler. The example visibly has a caron in sǒſuru; it is not normalized to the headword’s circumflex.
[c1-l039 >] *cante diante do Rey. ¶* Bugacu. l, ca-
[c1-l039 note] The musical gloss ends with performance before the ruler. Bugacu and the divided cagura introduce the next example of music and dance.
[c1-l040 >] gurauo ſôſuru. *Auer canto, & bailo di-*
[c1-l040 note] Gurauo completes cagurauo, followed by ſôſuru. The Portuguese describes singing and dancing; di- continues diante on the next row.
[c1-l041 >] *ante do Rey, ou do Cami.*
[c1-l041 note] The performance takes place before the ruler or a kami. Cami occurs inside the Portuguese explanation and is therefore recorded as a whole-word typeface target.
[c1-l042] Sǒxiguiri. *Furador de fechar cartas.*
[c1-l042 note] Sǒxiguiri is an awl used in closing letters. The Portuguese verb is fechar, with f, rather than the OCR’s ſechar.
[c1-l043] Soxin. Voroſocana cocoro. *Coração deſa-*
[c1-l043 note] Soxin is explained by Voroſocana cocoro, a neglectful or indifferent heart. The Japanese adjective’s ſo sequence replaces the malformed OCR bc.
[c1-l044 >] *moroſo, ou deſcuidado.* S.
[c1-l044 note] The heart is described as unloving or careless. The upright S. is the written-register label, not part of the italic definition.
[c1-l045] Sôxin. *O deſtruirſe, ou acabar. ¶* Sôxin
[c1-l045 note] Sôxin denotes being destroyed or brought to an end. The repeated Sôxin begins a longer expression explained through exhausting hardship.
[c1-l046 >] xumiǒ. *Ser mortò com trabalhos, & afli-*
[c1-l046 note] The expression begun with Sôxin is completed by xumiǒ, whose dotted i follows m on renewed inspection. It describes dying under hardships and afflictions; the accent area of mortò is stained, so the baseline grave is retained provisionally.
[c1-l047 >] *çoẽs, como os que por amor de Deos, ou da*
[c1-l047 note] Çoẽs completes afliçoẽs. The example concerns enduring hardship for love of God or another purpose, and continues at the top of the second column.

[c2-l012 note] The jointly composed verses are called Renga. This is a Japanese term embedded in Portuguese prose, and its upright type is distinguished from the surrounding gloss.
[c2-l013] Sôxocu. Cuſano iro. *Cor de eruas.* S.
[c2-l013 note] Sôxocu is the colour of grass or herbs, explained by Cuſano iro. The upright S. marks written usage.
[c2-l014] Soxocu. Voroſocana xocubut. *Comer groſ-*

[c2-l015 >] *ſeiro, & ruim.*
[c2-l015 note] The divided adjective finishes as groſſeiro, followed by ruim, poor or bad. This continues the food definition.
[c2-l016] Soxônin. *O que pede, ou propoem ao ſenhor*

[c2-l017 >] *algũa couſa.*

[c2-l018] Sǒxǔ. Fayaqi aqi. *Principio do Outono q̃*
