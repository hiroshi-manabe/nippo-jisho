[c1-l001] Gijin. *Camis da terra.*
[c1-l001 note] Gijin denotes deities of the earth or land. The surviving letters and the preceding page's catchword support Gijin, not the draft's Gin; the Portuguese is da terra, not da uera.
[c1-l002] Gijiqij. *Huns paos que atraueßão de columna*
[c1-l002 note] Gijiqij denotes wooden members running between columns. The initial G is abraded but still partly visible; the architectural gloss and following regional synonym support the recovered headword. Enlargement shows the right-hand s component of the ß ligature in atraueßão, rather than a lone ſ.
[c1-l003 >] *a columna nos Zaxiquis ao longo dos Tatamis.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes the architectural description: from column to column in rooms, along the tatami mats. Zaxiquis and Tatamis are Japanese terms embedded in the Portuguese explanation, distinct from the following regional usage label.
[c1-l004 >] X. *No Cami ſe diz,* Yoxejiqij.
[c1-l004 note] X. marks western regional usage, while the upper-region equivalent is Yoxejiqij. Cami is the regional name inside the Portuguese sentence; Yoxejiqij is the cited alternative form.
[c1-l005] Gijut. *Curar algũa doença vſando de muitos*
[c1-l005 note] Gijut concerns treating an illness by many methods. The checked final t is retained, and the Portuguese list of means continues below.
[c1-l006 >] *meos, & artes, ou mezinhas. ¶* Gijut
[c1-l006 note] Completes the list of methods, techniques, or medicines. A new example starts with the same Gijut, whose object particle appears on the next physical row.
[c1-l007 >] uo tçucuſu. *Vſar de todos os meos, & re-*
[c1-l007 note] Gijut uo tçucuſu means exhausting the available therapeutic means. The particle is uo, not the draft's uio, and re- begins remedios below.
[c1-l008 >] *medios pera curar algũa doença.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes remedios and the aim of curing an illness. This line closes the therapeutic-method entry.
[c1-l009] Gin. *Aguila. ¶* Ginuo taqu. *Por a-*
[c1-l009 note] Gin is the aromatic wood called aguila in this gloss, as the following incense-burning example makes clear. Ginuo taqu begins burning it for fragrance; the example's initial is G rather than C.
[c1-l010 >] *guila no fogo pera cheiro. ¶* Ginuo tomu-
[c1-l010 note] Completes placing aguila on the fire for its scent. The second example begins Ginuo tomu-, continued by ru on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] ru. *Perfumarſe com aguila, ou os veſtidos.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes tomuru and describes perfuming oneself or one's clothes with the aromatic wood. This confirms the fragrance sense of aguila in this entry.
[c1-l012] Gin. *Arrayal. ¶* Ginuo yaburu. *Rõ-*
[c1-l012 note] This separate Gin is a military camp. The final n of the headword is worn but supported by the repeated Ginuo; the example describes breaking or destroying the camp.
[c1-l013 >] *per, ou destruir o arrayal. ¶* Ginuo catô
[c1-l013 note] Completes Romper and begins another example for strengthening the camp. Catô has a circumflex, and its entire spelling belongs to the roman Japanese text, not partly to italic Portuguese.
[c1-l014 >] ſuru, l, catamuru. *Fortalecer, ou baſtecer*
[c1-l014 note] Completes catô suru and gives catamuru as an alternative. The Portuguese explains fortifying or provisioning the camp.
[c1-l015 >] *o arrayal. ¶* Ginuo toru, l, gindoriuo
[c1-l015 note] Completes the camp object and starts expressions for taking up a camp position. Ginuo replaces the draft's garbled Cmnuo; the alternative gindoriuo continues below.
[c1-l016 >] ſuru. *Aßẽtar o arrayal.* Ginuo fiqu. *Ale-*
[c1-l016 note] Completes gindoriuo suru, establishing a camp. Ginuo fiqu then introduces lifting or withdrawing the camp; Ale- begins its Portuguese translation.
[c1-l017 >] *uantar o arrayal, ou ceßar a guerra.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes Aleuantar and adds ceasing the war. The entry therefore distinguishes breaking a camp, fortifying it, establishing it, and withdrawing it.
[c1-l018] Gin-ai. Chirichiri. *Ciſco. ¶ Item, Im*
[c1-l018 note] Gin-ai denotes dust or small refuse, paraphrased by Chirichiri. The final Japanese syllable is ri, not the draft's ni; a further figurative sense begins with Im, without a secure line-end hyphen.
[c1-l019 >] *munditia interior.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes Immunditia interior, inward impurity. The line provides the extended sense of Gin-ai rather than another physical kind of dust.
[c1-l020] Ginbarai. *O queimar o lugar, ou campo onde*
[c1-l020 note] Ginbarai is defined here as burning the place or field occupied by an army. Queimar is the visible Portuguese verb; the definition is not replaced by a more familiar modern sense of the Japanese compound.
[c1-l021 >] *eſteue o exercito. Vt,* Ginbaraiuo ſuru.
[c1-l021 note] Completes the former army-camp location and gives Ginbaraiuo suru as the example. The spelling eſteue is retained as historical Portuguese.
[c1-l022] Ginbu. Ginni tçururu nimbu. *Os que a*
[c1-l022 note] Ginbu denotes labourers accompanying the army. The Portuguese begins a word divided a/carretão across the physical rows, without a secure hyphen.
[c1-l023 >] *carretão no exercito, ou leuão fato.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes acarretão: people who transport things in the army or carry baggage. Fato here belongs to the load-carrying description, not the modern English sense of 'fact'.
[c1-l024] Ginbure. *Pregão pera aguerra. ¶* Ginbu-
[c1-l024 note] Ginbure is a proclamation for war. Ginbu- begins the example Ginbureuo suru, and the printed aguerra spacing is retained.
[c1-l025 >] reuo ſuru. *Lançar pregão pera que ſe par-*
[c1-l025 note] Completes the Japanese example and explains issuing a proclamation that people should depart. The Portuguese verb partão is divided after par-.
[c1-l026 >] *tão pera aguerra.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes the call to depart for war. This is the end of the proclamation entry.
[c1-l027] Ginchǒqe. *Nome de hũa flor.*
[c1-l027 note] Ginchǒqe is identified only as the name of a flower. The caron and hũa are supported by the scan; no botanical species is supplied from outside the entry.
[c1-l028] Ginchǔ. Ginno vchi. *Dentro no exercito,*
[c1-l028 note] Ginchǔ means within the army or camp. The damaged final vowel carries a caron over u, not the draft's unmarked a, and Ginno vchi confirms the interior-location sense.
[c1-l029 >] *ou arrayal.*
[c1-l029 note] Adds 'or camp' to the location definition. The short row completes Ginchǔ, not the next aromatic-wood entry.
[c1-l030] Gincǒ. *Aguila.*
[c1-l030 note] Gincǒ is another name for the aromatic wood glossed aguila earlier in the column. The final caron is retained.
[c1-l031] Gindachi. *Oir, ou partirſe pera aguerra.*
[c1-l031 note] Gindachi is going or setting out for war. The beginning of the gloss is Oir, an O followed by the infinitive ir with close printed spacing, not the draft's Dir.
[c1-l032] Gindori. *Vt,* Gindoriuo ſuru. *Aſſentar*
[c1-l032 note] Gindoriuo suru means establishing a camp. The headword Gindori is supported by the surviving strokes and its clear repetition in the example, correcting Ciuidori.
[c1-l033 >] *o arrayal, ou alojar o exercito.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes establishing the camp or lodging the army. This is the full military-camping definition.
[c1-l034] Gingai. Chiri, acuta. *Ciſco, palhas, &c.*
[c1-l034 note] Gingai is dust and refuse, paraphrased by Chiri and acuta. The Portuguese adds straw and similar small litter.
[c1-l035 >] *¶ Item, permet. Immunditia.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*exercito.*
[c1-l035 note] The main text gives the figurative sense 'impurity'. At the far right, the parenthesis and exercito. are physically displaced completion material for Gingaye on the following row; they remain here in named far-right segments rather than being mistaken for part of the impurity gloss.
[c1-l036] Gingaye. *O mudar o arrayal, ou poſto do*
[c1-l036 note] Gingaye means changing the camp or army position. Its grammatical completion exercito. is printed at the far right of the preceding row and is preserved there, not silently moved into this line.
[c1-l037] Ginja. *Aguila, & almiſcre.*
[c1-l037 note] Ginja combines the aromatic wood and musk. The final vowel of almiſcre is e in the scan, correcting the draft's almiſcro while retaining the historical word.
[c1-l038] Ginſui. *Agua de cheiro.*
[c1-l038 note] Ginſui is scented water. The headword contains n followed by long s, not the draft's u, and the Portuguese confirms the fragrance-related grouping.
[c1-l039] Gintǒ. i, Coguchi. *Conflicto, ou principio da*
[c1-l039 note] Gintǒ, explained by Coguchi, concerns the opening or front of battle. The Portuguese begins with conflict or the beginning of the battle and continues below.
[c1-l040 >] *batalha. ¶ Item, Principio de hũ eſquadrão,*
[c1-l040 note] Completes batalha and adds the front or beginning of a squadron. The abbreviated hũ remains unexpanded.
[c1-l041 >] *ou manga de gente. ¶ Itẽ, Principo de hũa*
[c1-l041 note] Adds a body of troops and then begins another sense, the head of an ordered group. The printed Principo is retained without supplying an expected extra i; hũa is damaged but compatible with the surviving marks and the following feminine ordem.
[c1-l042 >] *ordem de gente como Cũgues que a companhão*
[c1-l042 note] The example is an ordered procession of court nobles accompanying the ruler. Cũgues is the embedded Japanese designation; the visible a companhão spacing is preserved.
[c1-l043 >] *ao* Dairi*, quando ſay fora, &c.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes the procession example: accompanying the Dairi when he goes out. The faint i in Dairi is retained from the surviving sequence; quando and fora correct the draft's false spacing and vowel.
[c1-l044] Ginxo. Ginuo totta tocoro. *Lugar onde*
[c1-l044 note] Ginxo is the place where a camp has been established. The Japanese paraphrase begins Ginuo, not Ganuo; the Portuguese location description continues below.
[c1-l045 >] *eſta alojado o exercito. ¶* Ginxouo ca-
[c1-l045 note] Completes the place where the army is lodged. The damaged Portuguese reads alojado and exercito; Ginxouo ca- begins an example about enclosing or fortifying the camp.
[c1-l046 >] mayuru. *Cercar, ou fortalecer o lugar do*
[c1-l046 note] Completes camayuru and explains surrounding or strengthening the camp site. The Portuguese verb is Cercar, not the draft's Cercur.
[c1-l047 >] *arreyal.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes the camp-site definition. The local arreyal vowel spelling is retained rather than harmonized with arrayal earlier in the column.
