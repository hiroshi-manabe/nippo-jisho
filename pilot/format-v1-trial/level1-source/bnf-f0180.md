## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Fayaxi. *Deſcante, ou muſica de inſtrumentos.*
[c1-l001 note] Hayashi here is musical accompaniment, not the grove sense at the end of f179. Deſcante and instrumental music define the new entry.
[c1-l002 >] *¶* Fayaxiuo ſuru. *Fazer eſte deſcante, ou*
[c1-l002 note] Hayashi wo suru means to perform this music. The wo marks hayashi as the object; the Portuguese translation continues on the next row.
[c1-l003 >] *muſica.*
[c1-l003 note] Muſica completes the explanation of making the accompaniment. Its separate row is a physical continuation, not a new item.
[c1-l004] Fayaxi, u, aita. *Descantar com inſtrumentos*
[c1-l004 note] Hayasu is the corresponding verb for musical performance. The printed abbreviated ending u is retained, although the full lexical form is hayasu.
[c1-l005 >] *muſicos, & vozes.* || {mark} *(* || {word} *uas &c.*
[c1-l005 note] Instruments and voices finish the musical definition. The separate right-hand '( uas &c.' completes er- in the following growth entry; it does not belong to the music.
[c1-l006] Fayaxi, u, aita. *Fazer crecer cabelos, er-*
[c1-l006 note] This hayasu means to cause hair or plants to grow. Its er- is completed by the displaced uas above; the musical homograph must not control this interpretation.
[c1-l007] Fayaxi, u, aita. *Cortar, ou trinchar. Pala-*
[c1-l007 note] This hayasu instead means to cut or carve. The register qualification begins Pala- here and is completed on the following row.
[c1-l008 >] *ura de molheres.*
[c1-l008 note] Palavra de molheres identifies the cutting sense as women's speech. This is the dictionary's historical usage classification, not a restriction imposed on the transcription.
[c1-l009] Fayaximono. *Bailo juntamente com canto de*
[c1-l009 note] Hayashimono is a dance accompanied by singing. The Portuguese subject continues into the group of people described on the next line.
[c1-l010 >] *muitos que vão andando jũta mẽte. ¶* Fayaxi
[c1-l010 note] Many people go along together while performing. The scan visibly abbreviates the latter expression as jũta mẽte; Fayaxi starts the Japanese example after the paragraph mark.
[c1-l011 >] monouo ſuru. *Fazer eſte bailo, ou canto*
[c1-l011 note] Mono wo suru completes hayashimono wo suru. The example concerns performing the dance or song described above, not an unrelated generic mono.
[c1-l012 >] *por feſta, ou alegria derepente.*
[c1-l012 note] The performance is for festivity or spontaneous joy. Derepente is retained as joined in the source rather than regularized to modern de repente.
[c1-l013] Faye. Yaburi coboſu. *Deſtruição. ¶* Faye-
[c1-l013 note] Hae here means destruction, explained by yaburi kobosu. It is a Sino-Japanese-looking destruction word, not the fish name in the next entry.
[c1-l014 >] ni voyobu. *deſtruirſe algũa couſa.*
[c1-l014 note] Hae ni oyobu means to come to destruction. The scan begins the Portuguese gloss with lowercase d; its sense confirms the cross-line Japanese phrase.
[c1-l015] Faye. *Hum peixe.*
[c1-l015 note] This separate hae names a fish, with no further identification. The generic gloss does not justify selecting a modern species.
[c1-l016] Faye, uru, eta. *Eſtender, ou eſtirar. Vt,*
[c1-l016 note] Haye/hae is a verb of stretching or spreading here. The supplied uru gives hayuru; it should not be replaced by the modern haeru paradigm in Level 1.
[c1-l017 >] Xiqimonouo fayuru. *Estender alcati-*
[c1-l017 note] Shikimono wo hayuru means to spread out a covering. Alcati- continues alcatifa 'carpet' below, giving a concrete example of the stretching/spreading sense.
[c1-l018 >] *fa, ou couſa ſemelhante. ¶* Itouo fayuru.
[c1-l018 note] The carpet or similar object completes the first example. Ito wo hayuru then introduces stretching thread, a second example of the same verb.
[c1-l019 >] *Eſtender, ou eſtirar algum fio. ¶ Item, Na-*
[c1-l019 note] The thread example is translated by extend or stretch. Item then opens the growth sense, whose Na- is continued below.
[c1-l020 >] *çerem cabelos, eruas, &c.*
[c1-l020 note] Na-çerem cabelos, eruas gives hair or plants growing. This is the intransitive counterpart of the growth sense of hayasu earlier on the page.
[c1-l021] Faza. *Eſpaço, ou tempo.* X. Ytçuno fazani
[c1-l021 note] Haza means an interval of space or time. Itsu no haza ni introduces a question about the time available for an action.
[c1-l022 >] coreuo xôca? *Quando, ou em q̃ tempo farei*
[c1-l022 note] Kore wo shō ka means 'shall I do this?' The interrogative follows the time expression above; xô retains the source's closed long-vowel mark.
[c1-l023 >] *iſto? No Cami ſe diz, Itçunomani.*
[c1-l023 note] Isto closes the Portuguese question. Cami and itsu no ma ni are embedded Japanese terms here; both are set within italic prose in this scan.
[c1-l024] Fazama. *Idem. ¶ Vt,* Vchinimo tçuua-
[c1-l024 note] Hazama is another interval word. The literary example begins uchi ni mo tsuwa-, continuing tsuwamono 'soldiers' on the next row.
[c1-l025 >] monodomo fima fazamamonǒ michimi-
[c1-l025 note] The soldiers fill the place hima hazama mo nō: without gaps. The printed fima supports hima; monǒ contains mo plus contracted 'without,' not an independent modern mono.
[c1-l026 >] chite itta. Feiq. *lib. 1. Entrarão os ſolda-*
[c1-l026 note] Chite completes michimichite, 'filling completely,' before itta. Feiq. identifies the source, and lowercase lib. begins its book reference.
[c1-l027 >] *dos dentro de maneira que eſtaua tudo cheo.*
[c1-l027 note] The Portuguese says soldiers entered until everything was full. It corroborates the no-empty-space interpretation of the Japanese compound and contraction above.
[c1-l028] Fazamari, u, atta. *Estar entalado, ou poſto*
[c1-l028 note] Hazamaru means to be wedged or placed between things. The voiced z is the historical headword spelling and is not changed to modern hasamaru.
[c1-l029 >] *entre duas couſas.* || {mark} *(* || {word} *couſas.*
[c1-l029 note] Entre duas couſas completes the intransitive definition. Only one additional displaced '( couſas.' is printed; it completes the following transitive entry, so the duplicate OCR copy is removed.
[c1-l030] Fazame, uru. *Entalar, ou meter entre duas*
[c1-l030 note] Hazame gives the transitive counterpart: insert or wedge between two things. Its final object couſas is the displaced fragment on the preceding row.
[c1-l031] Fazu. *Concerto. Vt,* Fazuuo auaſuru. *Com-*
[c1-l031 note] Hazu is an arrangement or agreement. Hazu wo awasuru illustrates fulfilling it; the Portuguese com- continues comprir below.
[c1-l032 >] *prir a promeſſa, & concerto. ¶* Fazuga
[c1-l032 note] Comprir a promeſſa e concerto means fulfilling the promise or agreement. Fazuga begins a contrasting example with hazu as subject.
[c1-l033 >] chigǒ. *Diſcrepar do concerto, ou promeſſa.*
[c1-l033 note] Hazu ga chigō means to differ from the arrangement or promise. The contracted chigō corresponds to chigau; no extra syllable should be supplied.
[c1-l034 >] *¶ Item,* Fazuuo auaſuru. *Fazer de peſ-*
[c1-l034 note] Another hazu wo awasuru sense concerns living up to expectations. The Portuguese fazer de peſ- must be read through the following row.
[c1-l035 >] *ſoa, ou como ſe eſpera delle na guerra, &c.*
[c1-l035 note] The explanation is to act as expected of a person, for example in war. This develops hazu as expected conduct rather than merely a negotiated promise.
[c1-l036 >] *¶* Fazuno auanu fito. *Homem que não*
[c1-l036 note] Hazu no awanu hito describes someone who fails to meet an agreement or expectation. No is the subject-linking particle in the relative expression.
[c1-l037 >] *cumpre com o que ficou, ou que não faz o que*
[c1-l037 note] The person does not fulfill what was agreed or do what is required. This continues the relative clause explaining the Japanese description.
[c1-l038 >] *deue conforme ao conceito que ſe tem de ſua*
[c1-l038 note] The duty is measured against the opinion held of that person's abilities or character. Conceito here supplies that expectation, not a new abstract headword.
[c1-l039 >] *peſſoa, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] Peſſoa closes the account of someone failing expectations. The following hazu entry changes to the physical archery sense.
[c1-l040] Fazu. *Mauça do arco. Vt,* Fazuuo tçu-
[c1-l040 note] Hazu now denotes the bow's nock or notch. The example hazu wo tsu-gu is divided at the row end and concerns making or attaching it.
[c1-l041 >] gu. *Fazer a mauça doutra cana, corno, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] Tsu-gu is completed before the explanation of making the notch from another cane or horn. Mauça is the source's technical Portuguese word and is preserved.
[c1-l042 >] *¶* Fazuuo ſaſu. *Fazer a mauça das fre-*
[c1-l042 note] Hazu wo sasu concerns making the arrow nocks. Fre- continues frechas; the example must not be confused with putting a whole arrow on the string.
[c1-l043 >] *chas com que as aſſentão na corda do arco.*
[c1-l043 note] The notches let arrows sit on the bowstring. This clause explains their function and completes the preceding arrow-making example.
[c1-l044 >] *¶* Fazumaqiuo ſuru. *Apertar com fio*
[c1-l044 note] Hazumaki wo suru describes binding with thread. The compound's maki element and the Portuguese apertar com fio agree on wrapping or fastening.
[c1-l045 >] *junto da mauça da frecha.*
[c1-l045 note] The binding is next to the arrow's nock. This locative phrase finishes hazumaki rather than introducing a separate action.
[c1-l046] Fazudacani. *Adu. Modo de por a aljaua*
[c1-l046 note] Hazudaka ni is an adverbial expression for placing the quiver high. The raised position is completed below and illustrated across the column break.
[c1-l047 >] *com frechas na cinta alta, & eminente.*
[c1-l047 note] The quiver with arrows is placed high at the belt. The Japanese example begins at the top of column 2 and should be read with this definition.

[c2-l001 >] *¶* Nijǔxi ſaita qirifuno yauo fazudaca-
[c2-l001 note] Nijǔxi is twenty-four, confirmed by the Portuguese numeral below; the scan has a marked u, not i. The decorated arrows are being placed hazudaka, with their nocks high.
[c2-l002 >] ni totte tçuqe, &c. Yax. *Tomando a*
[c2-l002 note] Ni totte tsuqe completes the example crossing from column 1. Yax. is a roman citation label, while Tomando begins the Portuguese translation.
[c2-l003 >] *aljaua em que tinha metidas 24. ſetas de pe-*
[c2-l003 note] The quiver contains twenty-four arrows, corroborating nijǔshi above. The final pe- continues penas, the feathers described in the next row.
[c2-l004 >] *nas com certas liſtras atraueſſadas, & pondoa*
[c2-l004 note] The feathers have transverse stripes. Pondoa refers to putting the quiver in place, continuing the action expressed by totte tsuqe.
[c2-l005 >] *na cinta de maneira q̃ ficaſe alta, & eminẽte.*
[c2-l005 note] The quiver is placed at the belt so it stands high. This completes the explanation of hazudaka ni begun in the other column.
[c2-l006] Fazumaqi. *Parte da ſeta junto da mauça que*
[c2-l006 note] Hazumaki is the portion of the arrow beside its nock. The definition corresponds to the thread-wrapping example in column 1.
[c2-l007 >] *cuſtumão apertar, & emburulhar cõ fio.*
[c2-l007 note] This part is tightened and wrapped with thread. The scan reads emburulhar, with an extra u compared with modern embrulhar; that historical form is retained.
[c2-l008] Fazuſaxi. *Certo caniuete com que abrem a mau-*
[c2-l008 note] Hazusashi is a small knife used to open the bow's notch. The scan's technical context identifies a tool, not a verb phrase hazu sasu.
[c2-l009 >] *ça do arco pera meter a corda. ¶ Itẽ, Qual-*
[c2-l009 note] Mau-ça is completed before the purpose of inserting the bowstring. Item then broadens the word beyond this specialized use.
[c2-l010 >] *quer caniuete, ou faca pequena de ponta aguda.*
[c2-l010 note] Any small knife with a sharp point falls under the extended sense. Qual-quer crosses the preceding line break and is one Portuguese word.
[c2-l011] Fazzu. *Mezinha pera fazer camaras.*
[c2-l011 note] Hazu names a purgative medicine in this entry. Fazer camaras refers to bowel evacuation in the dictionary's medical vocabulary, not constructing rooms.
[c2-l012] Fazzucaxigari, u, atta. *Ter pejo, ou ver-*
[c2-l012 note] Hazukashigaru is to feel or display embarrassment. Ter pejo and ver-gonha supply the paired Portuguese equivalents across this line break.
[c2-l013 >] *gonha.*
[c2-l013 note] Gonha completes vergonha, 'shame' or 'embarrassment.' It belongs to the verb definition, not to the adjective printed next.
[c2-l014] Fazzucaxij. *Couſa de que ſe tem pejo, ou ver*
[c2-l014 note] Hazukashii is something one feels shame about. A small mark above the first a may be an accent, also noted by NINJAL, but is too uncertain here to add to the diplomatic text.
[c2-l015 >] *gonha. ¶ Item, Ter pejo, ou vergonha.*
[c2-l015 note] The second adjective sense is simply to feel shame. This personal-predicate use explains why a Portuguese verbal gloss accompanies a Japanese adjective.
[c2-l016 >] Fazzucaxiſa.
[c2-l016 note] Hazukashisa is the abstract noun in -sa. The isolated derivative row inherits the embarrassment sense rather than needing a second gloss.
[c2-l017 >] Fazzucaxû. *¶* Fazzucaxǔ vomô.
[c2-l017 note] The contracted adverb appears with differently shaped u marks: Fazzucaxû, then Fazzucaxǔ vomô. The latter means to feel ashamed; its final vowel is not i.
[c2-l018 >] *Ter vergonha.* || {mark} *(* || {word} *tro.* || {usage} *S.*
[c2-l018 note] Ter vergonha translates the preceding example. The displaced '( tro. S.' instead completes ou- on the following line, giving 'shame another person.'
[c2-l019] Fazzucaxime, uru, eta. *Enuergonhar a ou-*
[c2-l019 note] Hazukashime is the verb for shaming someone else. Read ou- with the displaced tro above; the translation is not left with a missing object.
[c2-l020] Fazzumacaxi, ſu, aita. *Deter o folego. Vt,*
[c2-l020 note] Hazumakasu means to hold the breath in this entry. It is distinguished from the intransitive hazumu immediately below by the causative-looking -akas- form.
[c2-l021 >] Iqiuo fazzumacaſu. *Deter o folego, ou dei-*
[c2-l021 note] Iki wo hazumakasu repeats the breath-holding sense. The object marker wo and Portuguese deter agree, with dei- beginning the alternative explanation.
[c2-l022 >] *xar de reſpirar.*
[c2-l022 note] Dei-xar de respirar completes 'cease breathing.' This is a linguistic gloss of the example, not an instruction or modern medical description.
[c2-l023] Fazzumi, u, unda. *Deterſe o folego, ou não*
[c2-l023 note] Hazumu here concerns breath being stopped or impeded. The historical sense should not be replaced with only the modern ball-bouncing meaning.
[c2-l024 >] *poder reſfolegar, abafado, &c. Vt,* Iqiga
[c2-l024 note] The person cannot catch breath and is stifled. Iki ga begins the intransitive Japanese example, with its verb on the following short row.
[c2-l025 >] fazzumu.
[c2-l025 note] The scan reads fazzumu, completing iki ga hazumu. The OCR fazzuiu failed to recognize the m; the headword paradigm and breath gloss support the correction.
[c2-l026] Fazzumi, u, unda. *Pular a pela. Vt,* Ma-
[c2-l026 note] This separate hazumu entry describes a ball bouncing. Ma- begins mari, the ball that is subject of the example below.
[c2-l027 >] riga fazzumu. *¶ Item, Ir o caualo aluo-*
[c2-l027 note] Mari ga hazumu completes the bouncing-ball example. Item then introduces a horse moving excitedly, starting aluo- before the next row.
[c2-l028 >] *raçado, & como brincando. Vt,* Vmano
[c2-l028 note] Aluo-raçado and brincando explain the horse's lively movement. Uma no opens a longer literary example whose reins and movement are described below.
[c2-l029 >] axino voyobǒ fodoua tazzunauo curete
[c2-l029 note] Uma no ashi no oyobō hodo wa concerns how far the horse's feet reach. Tazuna wo kurete means giving or letting out the reins; the Portuguese below supports it.
[c2-l030 >] ayumaxei, fazzumaba tazzunauo cutte
[c2-l030 note] Ayumasei is an imperative causative 'make it walk'; hazumaba is conditional. Tazuna wo cutte contrasts drawing in the reins with letting them out.
[c2-l031 >] voyogaxei. Feiq. *Lib. 2. Fazei an-*
[c2-l031 note] Oyogasei is 'make it swim,' not an unrelated malformed walking verb. The Feiq. citation and Portuguese nadar below support that reading in this horse passage.
[c2-l032 >] *dar o caualo largando lhe as redeas quanto elle*
[c2-l032 note] The translation says to let the horse move while giving it rein as far as it can. The explicit horse object links this to the longer Japanese example.
[c2-l033 >] *poder, & ſe for muito apreſſado puxando lhe*
[c2-l033 note] If it becomes too hurried, the rider draws on the reins. This corresponds to the Japanese conditional hazumaba and tazuna wo cutte.
[c2-l034 >] *pellas redeas fazey o nadar.*
[c2-l034 note] The Portuguese explicitly ends 'make it swim.' The unusual action is therefore retained rather than normalizing oyogasei to a more expected walking command.
[c2-l035] Fazzuna. *Loro do cabreſto com que amarrão o*
[c2-l035 note] Hazuna is a halter strap for tying a horse. Initial f belongs to the historical ha-series transcription, not modern tazuna imposed from the earlier example.
[c2-l036 >] *cauallo na eſtrebaria.*
[c2-l036 note] The horse is tied in the stable. Cavallo is printed with doubled l here, unlike some nearby instances of caualo, and is retained.
[c2-l037] Fazzuna. *Duas amarras com que tem mão no*
[c2-l037 note] A separate hazuna entry concerns two mooring ropes holding a vessel. The nautical definition prevents treating it as another horse strap example.
[c2-l038 >] *nauio, hũa dellas eſta no mar com a fateixa, &*
[c2-l038 note] One rope is in the sea with the grapnel or anchor. The paired structure hũa ... outra continues on the following row.
[c2-l039 >] *outra na popa.*
[c2-l039 note] The other rope is at the stern. This short continuation completes the two-part nautical description.
[c2-l040] Fazzure. *Borde, ou arrabalde fora dalgũa po-*
[c2-l040 note] Hazure means an edge or outskirts beyond a settlement. Borde is retained as the visible Portuguese form rather than modernized to borda.
[c2-l041 >] *uoação. Vt,* Mura, l, machi fazzureni
[c2-l041 note] The example is mura or machi hazure ni, 'on the outskirts of a village or town.' Final ni is visible, not the OCR m; the next row's sumu confirms the locative construction.
[c2-l042 >] ſumu. *Morar fora, ou nos arrabaldes da vila,*
[c2-l042 note] Sumu completes 'live on the outskirts.' Morar fora and arrabaldes provide a direct bilingual check on hazure ni above.
[c2-l043 >] *ou pouoação.*
[c2-l043 note] Ou pouoação completes the reference to a village or settlement. This closes the noun entry before the related verb begins.
[c2-l044] Fazzure, uru, eta. *Diſcrepar. ¶* Mi-
[c2-l044 note] Hazururu means to deviate or be out of alignment. Mi- starts michi in the first example, not a separate short headword.
[c2-l045 >] chini fazzururu. *Errar o caminho. ¶* Fat-
[c2-l045 note] Michi ni hazururu is to stray from the road. The next example starts Fat-, completing hatto 'law' below.
[c2-l046 >] toni fazzururu. *Não guardar a lei.* Fito-
[c2-l046 note] Hatto ni hazururu means not to keep the law. Fito- begins the final example, which develops departure from ordinary human behavior.
[c2-l047 >] namini fazzururu. *Ser ſingular, ou não ir*
[c2-l047 note] Hitonami ni hazururu means to depart from the ordinary run of people. The Portuguese says to be singular or not go along with others; its last phrase continues below and onto f181.
[c2-l048 >>] *com*
[c2-l048 note] This right-aligned com continues 'não ir com' in the preceding gloss. It is retained as the supplied body continuation, with the rest of the sentence on the next page.
