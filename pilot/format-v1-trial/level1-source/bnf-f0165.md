## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Faguiximi. *O ranger os dentes apertandoos cõ*
[c1-l001 note] `Faguiximi` describes grinding the teeth in anger, not merely chewing. Portuguese `ranger os dentes` supplies the action; the cause continues below.
[c1-l002 >] *ira. Vt,* Faguiximiuo ſuru.
[c1-l002 note] `ira` completes “clenching them in anger.” `Faguiximiuo ſuru` makes the preceding noun into an action with `suru`; read across the physical break.
[c1-l003] Faguqi. *Linguas. ¶ Item, Penas de paſ-*
[c1-l003 note] `Faguqi` gives ハグキ. The first gloss is transcribed `Linguas`, which is lexically surprising for modern 歯茎; do not silently replace the Portuguese. The second sense concerns feathers.
[c1-l004 >] *ſaros.*
[c1-l004 note] This finishes `paſ- / ſaros`, “birds,” in the second sense of `Faguqi`. It is not a separate Japanese entry or a continuation of the next verb.
[c1-l005] Faguracaxi, ſu, aita. *Ir à mão, & repren-*
[c1-l005 note] `Faguracaxi` introduces a causative-looking stem. Here `ir à mão` means checking or restraining someone; the definition continues with rebuke, not literal walking.
[c1-l006 >] *der a alguem que eſta pera dizer, ou fazer al-*
[c1-l006 note] The person is about to say or do something unsuitable. This supplies the human object of the restraint described above.
[c1-l007 >] *gũa couſa que não conuem, com que fique como*
[c1-l007 note] `que não conuem` qualifies the proposed action as inappropriate. The result clause begins `com que fique`, continued by embarrassment below.
[c1-l008 >] *corrido, & enuergonhado. ¶* Vmauo fa-
[c1-l008 note] `corrido` here means abashed, paired with `enuergonhado`, not “having run.” `Vmauo fa-` begins a separate horse example.
[c1-l009 >] guracaſu. *Impedir o caualo que não faça, ou*
[c1-l009 note] Join `fa- / guracaſu`: ウマヲ ハグラカス. The Portuguese now describes preventing the horse from performing an undesirable movement.
[c1-l010 >] *ſaya com algũa manha, ou mouimento ruim*
[c1-l010 note] `manha` denotes a trick or bad habit in this horse context. The clause continues the prevention, not a new sense of the headword.
[c1-l011 >] *com lhe apertar o freo, &c.*
[c1-l011 note] Tightening the bridle is the means of restraint. `freo` is the historical Portuguese form corresponding to modern `freio`.
[c1-l012] Fagure, ruru, eta. *Ficar corrido, ou enuer-*
[c1-l012 note] `Fagure` is explained here as becoming abashed or embarrassed. Modern はぐれる alone would not capture the specific social sense given in Portuguese.
[c1-l013 >] *gonhado. Vt,* Mairi fagurete iyeni maira-
[c1-l013 note] `Mairi fagurete iyeni maira-` describes failing to make a visit through embarrassment; the final negative belongs to the next line.
[c1-l014 >] nu. *Não vir a viſitar a alguem com vergo-*
[c1-l014 note] `maira- / nu` is the negative verb, “does not visit.” The Portuguese explains the absence as shame, completed below.
[c1-l015 >] *nha por ter tardado, & não vir a tempo.*
[c1-l015 note] The embarrassment results from having delayed and missed the proper time. This causal detail matters to the preceding Japanese example.
[c1-l016] Fai. Vogamu. *Venerar, ou adorar: mas não*
[c1-l016 note] This `Fai` is 拝, with `Vogamu` オガム as its explanation. The Portuguese explicitly limits its grammatical use in the next line.
[c1-l017 >] *ſe vſa ſenão com, xi, ſuru. Vt,* Faiſu-
[c1-l017 note] `xi, ſuru` are Japanese verbal forms cited inside Portuguese prose. `Faiſu-` begins the actual example; the note is about inflection, not two Portuguese words.
[c1-l018 >] ru. *Venerar, ou adorar.*
[c1-l018 note] `Faiſu- / ru` yields 拝する, “venerate or worship.” The short line completes both the Japanese example and its Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l019] Fai. i, Faino zǒ. *Bofes.*
[c1-l019 note] `Faino zǒ` means the lung organ; Portuguese `Bofes` confirms 肺. This is a different homograph from the preceding 拝.
[c1-l020] Fai. *Raizes tenras de golfão.*
[c1-l020 note] Portuguese describes tender roots of a water-lily-like plant (`golfão`). Do not infer the exact plant or Japanese kanji solely from the spelling `Fai`.
[c1-l021] Fai. *Cinza.*
[c1-l021 note] `Cinza` means ash, identifying this `Fai` as 灰. It must remain separate from the neighboring homographs.
[c1-l022] Fai. *Moſcas.*
[c1-l022 note] `Moſcas` means flies. The reading ハイ represents a historical form for the insect; changing it to modern ハエ would alter the source.
[c1-l023] Fai, ǒ, ǒta. *Andar de gatinhas, ou raſtei-*
[c1-l023 note] This verbal `Fai` means crawling. The dictionary's contracted inflectional forms `ǒ, ǒta` should not be mistaken for additional nouns.
[c1-l024 >] *ro pello chão como os bichos, &c. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l024 note] Crawling is compared to creatures moving close to the ground. `Item` begins an additional botanical application on the following line.
[c1-l025 >] *Trepar, ou dilatarſe a era, ou outra erua ſeme-*
[c1-l025 note] Here `era` is ivy, not the Portuguese past-tense verb. Creeping or spreading plants extend the bodily sense of はう.
[c1-l026 >] *lhante.*
[c1-l026 note] This completes `ſeme- / lhante`, “similar,” qualifying other plants like ivy. No independent lexical material begins here.
[c1-l027] Fai aruqi, u, ita. *Ir de gatinhas, ou raſteiro*
[c1-l027 note] `Fai aruqi` is crawling along, combining はい with 歩き. The Portuguese again specifies movement close to the ground.
[c1-l028 >] *pollo chão.*
[c1-l028 note] `pollo chão` completes the preceding definition, “along the ground.” Its spelling is preserved rather than modernized to `pelo`.
[c1-l029] Faiban. *Hũa taboinha ſobre que poem o copo.*
[c1-l029 note] `Faiban` denotes a small board on which a cup is placed, compatible with a cup stand or tray. The following entry has another sense of the same spelling.
[c1-l030] Faiban. Rǒjeqi. *Deſordem, & pertur-*
[c1-l030 note] The scan supports `Faiban`, not `Faibun`. `Rǒjeqi` ロウゼキ and Portuguese disorder describe the disarray of a drinking gathering; the later `Faibun` is distribution.
[c1-l031 >] *bação que fica no Zaxiqi, depois de muito*
[c1-l031 note] The disorder remains in the `Zaxiqi`, 座敷. That Japanese room name is embedded in the Portuguese definition, not an example sentence.
[c1-l032 >] *beber.*
[c1-l032 note] `beber` completes “after much drinking.” It explains the circumstances of the disorder rather than adding a new action to the Japanese entry.
[c1-l033] Faibun. Cubari, vaquru. *Repartição.*
[c1-l033 note] This `Faibun` is 配分: `Cubari, vaquru` supplies distributing and dividing. Portuguese `Repartição` confirms the contrast with `Faiban` above.
[c1-l034] Faibuqi. *Certa prata boa.*
[c1-l034 note] `Faibuqi` is a grade of good silver, compatible with 灰吹 silver. The definition gives quality but does not itself describe the refining process.
[c1-l035] Faicacari, u, atta. *Ir de gatinhas a criança*
[c1-l035 note] `Faicacari` describes a child crawling toward someone. The following line supplies the parent as destination.
[c1-l036 >] *pera o pay, ou mãy, &c. ¶ Item, Subir*
[c1-l036 note] The child approaches father or mother. `Item` then introduces the parallel action of a climbing plant, not another child example.
[c1-l037 >] *a era, ou outra erua ſemelhante polla aruore,*
[c1-l037 note] Ivy or a similar plant climbs a tree. This is a botanical extension of the preceding crawling verb.
[c1-l038 >] *ſebe, &c.*
[c1-l038 note] `ſebe` adds a hedge to the possible supports for the climbing plant. The short line closes that definition.
[c1-l039] Faicai. *Certo genero de verſos, ou cantigas im-*
[c1-l039 note] `Faicai` is 俳諧. The Portuguese categorizes it as a kind of verse or song; its evaluative description continues below.
[c1-l040 >] *perfeitas que hum faz, ou muitos juntos, vſan-*
[c1-l040 note] The verses may be made by one person or collaboratively. `imperfeitas` is the compiler's characterization, not a claim that the transcription is incomplete.
[c1-l041 >] *do de palauras ordinarias, & pouco polidas.*
[c1-l041 note] Ordinary, unpolished words characterize the verse style in this definition. This supports the haikai identification without imposing a modern genre definition.
[c1-l042] Faichǔ. Sacazzuqino vchi. *Dentro no copo,*
[c1-l042 note] `Sacazzuqino vchi` is サカヅキノ ウチ, “inside the cup,” explaining `Faichǔ` 杯中. The Portuguese agrees directly.
[c1-l043 >] *ou taça.*
[c1-l043 note] `ou taça` adds another Portuguese word for a cup or bowl. It completes the preceding spatial definition.
[c1-l044] Faicocu. Yaburetaru cuni. *Reino deſtruido.*
[c1-l044 note] `Yaburetaru cuni`, “a ruined country,” identifies `Faicocu` as a devastated realm. Keep the historical attributive `-taru` in the interpretation.
[c1-l045] Faidate. *Armas que cobrem as pernas de cima*
[c1-l045 note] `Faidate` refers to armor covering the upper legs. The Portuguese definition locates it anatomically rather than merely calling it clothing.
[c1-l046 >] *até os joelhos.*
[c1-l046 note] The protection extends to the knees, completing the preceding armor definition. This fits the thigh-protection sense of はいだて.
[c1-l047] Faide, zzuru, eta. *Sair de gatinhas.*
[c1-l047 note] `Faide, zzuru, eta` describes crawling out. The ending belongs to the historical 出づ series; `zzuru` is not an independent lexical headword.

[c2-l001] Faiden. Vogamijo. *Certo oratorio que eſta*
[c2-l001 note] `Faiden` is 拝殿, explained as `Vogamijo`, a place of worship. Its position relative to the shrine is specified below.
[c2-l002 >] *diante da caſa do Cami.*
[c2-l002 note] The oratory stands before the house of the `Cami`, the deity. This is a Japanese religious term embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c2-l003] Faidocu. Vogami yomu. *Ler com reuerẽcia.*
[c2-l003 note] `Vogami yomu` means reading reverently, explaining 拝読. The two Japanese verbs and Portuguese `Ler com reuerẽcia` agree.
[c2-l004] Faje. *Hum certo peixe.*
[c2-l004 note] This `Faje` ハゼ denotes a fish. The sparse definition does not justify identifying a narrower species.
[c2-l005] Faje. *Eſteiras feitas de hũa certa erua, ou*
[c2-l005 note] A second `Faje` denotes mats made from a particular plant. Do not merge it with the fish entry despite identical spelling.
[c2-l006 >] *palha groſſa pera dependurar das portas, va-*
[c2-l006 note] The mats use coarse straw and are hung at doorways. The final `va-` continues as verandas below.
[c2-l007 >] *randas, &c.*
[c2-l007 note] `va- / randas` completes the list of places where the mats hang. The line belongs to the material-object definition.
[c2-l008] Faje. *Bate, ou arroz torrado, & arrebentado.*
[c2-l008 note] This `Faje` is parched or popped rice. Portuguese `torrado, & arrebentado` supports the bursting/popping sense of はぜ.
[c2-l009 >] ¶ Momi fajeni natta. *O arroz eſta ja*
[c2-l009 note] `Momi fajeni natta` means the rice has become popped rice. The predicate `ni natta` is the change-of-state construction.
[c2-l010 >] *arrebentado, ou com o âmago ſaido pera fora.*
[c2-l010 note] The Portuguese explains that the interior of the grain has emerged. This describes popping, not germination or ordinary cooking.
[c2-l011] Fai. l, fainoqi. *Hũa aruore de que fazem*
[c2-l011 note] `Fai, l, fainoqi` gives a tree name and “fai tree.” Its wood is identified by the uses listed in the next two lines.
[c2-l012 >] *os Faxis com que comem em Iapão, & que*
[c2-l012 note] The wood makes `Faxis`, eating chopsticks. The Japanese pluralized term belongs inside the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l013 >] *metem entre as duas canas do arco.*
[c2-l013 note] It is also inserted between the two bamboo members of a bow. This second use is important botanical context but does not prove a species identification.
[c2-l014] Faifi. Vogami firaqu. i, Faiqen. *Ler car-*
[c2-l014 note] `Vogami firaqu` means respectfully opening, with `Faiqen` as a related expression. The Portuguese narrows this to reading letters reverently.
[c2-l015 >] *tas com reuerencia.*
[c2-l015 note] `car- / tas` completes “letters.” Respectful reading supplies the sense of the preceding 拝披-related entry.
[c2-l016] Faifocu. Yabure niguru. i, Gunginga cuz-
[c2-l016 note] `Yabure niguru` describes defeat and flight. `Gunginga` can be parsed as 軍陣が, with historical `gi` in the voiced palatal series; the army gloss below supports that reading without a spelling correction.
[c2-l017 >] zururu. *Deſtruirſe, & fugir o exercito.*
[c2-l017 note] Join `cuz- / zururu` as 崩るる. The Portuguese states that the army is broken and flees, supporting the defeat sense of `Faifocu`.
[c2-l018] Faifu. *Prouiſão que ſe daa, ou paſſa pera ti-*
[c2-l018 note] `Faifu` is an authorization or written order. The purpose begins with collecting something in the following line.
[c2-l019 >] *rar algũa finta, ou fazer pagar algũa couſa.*
[c2-l019 note] `finta` is a levy or assessment here. The document authorizes collecting a levy or making a payment, not merely distributing ordinary correspondence.
[c2-l020 >] *&c. ¶* Faifuuo iruru. *Dar eſta pro-*
[c2-l020 note] `Faifuuo iruru` illustrates issuing or delivering this authorization. The Portuguese `pro-` continues as `provisão` below.
[c2-l021 >] *uiſão, ou papel pera eſte effeito.*
[c2-l021 note] The paper is given for the stated fiscal purpose. This finishes the example and confirms the documentary sense of `Faifu`.
[c2-l022] Faiga. *Ir ao paço do Rei em certo tempo de feſ-*
[c2-l022 note] This `Faiga` describes going to the king's palace at a festive time. It is a ceremonial visit, not simply private rejoicing.
[c2-l023 >] *ta a viſitalo.*
[c2-l023 note] `viſitalo` specifies visiting the ruler. The short line completes the first `Faiga` definition.
[c2-l024] Faiga. Vogami yorocobu. *Reuerenciar a-*
[c2-l024 note] `Vogami yorocobu` combines reverence with rejoicing, compatible with 拝賀. This second entry explains the action rather than the ceremonial occasion.
[c2-l025 >] *legrandoſe.*
[c2-l025 note] `a- / legrandoſe` completes rejoicing. It modifies the reverential action described in the preceding line.
[c2-l026] Faigacu. Sutaretaru gacumon. *Eſtudo, ou*
[c2-l026 note] `Sutaretaru gacumon` is neglected or abandoned learning, explaining `Faigacu` 廃学. The Portuguese continues with forgotten studies.
[c2-l027 >] *letras eſquecidas ja, & deixadas. ¶* Fai-
[c2-l027 note] The studies have been forgotten and left aside. `Fai-` begins the verbal example continued below.
[c2-l028 >] gacu ſuru. *Eſquecerſe do que hum tem*
[c2-l028 note] `Fai- / gacu ſuru` means allowing one's learning to lapse. Portuguese explains forgetting what one has learned.
[c2-l029 >] *aprendido.*
[c2-l029 note] `aprendido` closes that object clause. This line completes the loss-of-learning example, not a new entry.
[c2-l030] Faigan. Cambaxeuo vogamu. i, vǒ meni
[c2-l030 note] `Cambaxeuo vogamu` concerns reverently seeing a person's face. `vǒ meni cacaru` is the respectful meeting expression; segmentation should be read across the next line.
[c2-l031 >] cacaru. *Venerar vendo peſſoa nobre.*
[c2-l031 note] `cacaru` completes お目にかかる. Portuguese specifies seeing a noble person with reverence, supporting `Faigan` 拝顔.
[c2-l032] Faigun. Icuſa yabururu. i, Ginga cuzzu-
[c2-l032 note] `Icuſa yabururu` and `Ginga cuzzu-` describe an army or formation breaking. `Ginga` can be read 陣が here; do not confuse it with 銀河.
[c2-l033 >] ruru. *Deſtruirſe o exercito.*
[c2-l033 note] `cuzzu- / ruru` finishes “break down.” Portuguese `Deſtruirſe o exercito` confirms the military defeat sense.
[c2-l034] Faijen. Ienuo cubaru. *Opor as meſas de*
[c2-l034 note] `Faijen` is 配膳, so distributing meal trays is expected. The transcribed `Ienuo` has an anomalous initial; retain it provisionally rather than pretending its kana hint supplies a coherent Japanese explanation.
[c2-l035 >] *Iapão por ordem no Zaxiqi.*
[c2-l035 note] The Japanese tables or trays are arranged in order in the `Zaxiqi`. This contextualizes the expected 膳 without authorizing replacement of the anomalous letter above.
[c2-l036] Fai iri, u, itta. *Entrar de gatinhas.*
[c2-l036 note] `Fai iri` combines crawling with entering. The gloss `Entrar de gatinhas` confirms that this is a movement verb, not a 拝 compound.
[c2-l037] Faiji. Vogami tçucayuru. *Seruiço q̃ ſe faz,*
[c2-l037 note] `Vogami tçucayuru` is serving respectfully, compatible with 拝事. Portuguese begins the definition as service performed for someone.
[c2-l038 >] *com reuerencia a peſſoa honrada.*
[c2-l038 note] The recipient is an honored person, and the manner is reverent. This completes the social relationship expressed by the Japanese gloss.
[c2-l039] Faijǒ. Vogami, tatematçuru. *Offerecer, ou*
[c2-l039 note] `Vogami, tatematçuru` explains a respectful offering. `Faijǒ` is in the series of honorific 拝 compounds, not the crawling family.
[c2-l040 >] *dar a peſſoa nobre algũa couſa.*
[c2-l040 note] The offering is something given to a noble person. Portuguese supplies the recipient and object absent from the short Japanese gloss.
[c2-l041] Faiju. Vogami vquru. *Receber algũa cou-*
[c2-l041 note] `Vogami vquru` means receiving reverently, explaining 拝受. The movement of the gift is opposite to the preceding offering entry.
[c2-l042 >] *ſa de peſſoa honrada com reuerencia.*
[c2-l042 note] Something is received from an honored person with reverence. Read this as completion of the receiving definition.
[c2-l043] Faimauari, u, atta. *Andar à roda em gati-*
[c2-l043 note] `Faimauari` means crawling around. Its literal bodily sense is extended to climbing plants in the next line.
[c2-l044 >] *nhas, ou ir trepãdo a era polla aruore em roda.*
[c2-l044 note] The ivy winds around a tree. The Portuguese uses the same circular motion to connect the human and botanical applications.
[c2-l045 >] ¶ *Aliquando,* Permet. *Andar apos al-*
[c2-l045 note] `Aliquando, Permet.` marks an occasional metaphorical sense. The action becomes following someone persistently, continued below.
[c2-l046 >] *guem com liſonjas pera auer algũa couſa delle.*
[c2-l046 note] The follower uses flattery to obtain something. This is a social metaphor of crawling around someone, not another description of ivy.
[c2-l047] Faimǒ. i, Xinnenxi ſauagu. *Eſquecerſe per-*
[c2-l047 note] `Faimǒ` introduces forgetful confusion; the Portuguese definition continues onto f166. `Xinnenxi` is linguistically uncertain here: do not invent a segmentation or kanji merely from the generated reading.
