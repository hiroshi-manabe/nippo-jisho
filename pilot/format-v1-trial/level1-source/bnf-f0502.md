[c1-l001] Ten-v. Tenno vôzora. *Ceo.*
[c1-l001 note] Ten-u is explained by ten no oozora, the sky or heaven. The Portuguese Ceo is the direct gloss; the printed v and circumflex are retained.
[c1-l002] Ten-vn. i, Tenno facarai. *Prouidencia di-*
[c1-l002 note] Ten-un is glossed as heaven's ordering or providence. Tenno facarai supplies the Japanese paraphrase; the final di- begins diuina on the next row.
[c1-l003 >] *uina, ou do ceo.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes diuina and adds 'or of heaven', finishing the definition of providence. This short continuation is a separate physical line.
[c1-l004] Ten-von. Tenno von. *Beneficios do ceo, ou*
[c1-l004 note] Ten-on is heavenly favor or beneficence. The positional v in von represents the beginning of on, and the Portuguese alternatives continue below.
[c1-l005 >] *de Deos.*
[c1-l005 note] 'Of God' completes the alternative to benefits from heaven. Deos is the source's historical spelling, not a modernized replacement.
[c1-l006] Tenurui. *Homem frio em ſuas obras, ou tar-*
[c1-l006 note] Tenurui describes a person slow or lacking vigor in his actions. The Portuguese tar- continues as tardio, explaining the figurative sense of coldness.
[c1-l007 >] *dio de mãos. ¶ Item, Homem hum pouco*
[c1-l007 note] Completes tardio de mãos, slow with one's hands, and begins a second description of a somewhat languid person. The scan supports hum, not OCR ham.
[c1-l008 >] *molle, & vagaroso.*
[c1-l008 note] Molle and vagaroso finish the description as soft or slow-moving. The first word has the rounded o of molle, correcting OCR melle without modernizing the doubled l.
[c1-l009] Tenxei. i, Tẽnẽ. *Naturalmente, ou natureza.*
[c1-l009 note] Tensei is explained by tennen, naturally or by nature. The abbreviated Tẽnẽ retains both printed nasal marks; the OCR's Tenxeiai, Tene obscured the explanatory i and the marked vowels.
[c1-l010] Tenxi. i, Teiuǒ. *Rey. Vt,* Tenxi xǒgunno
[c1-l010 note] Tenshi is identified with teioo, a king or emperor. Vt introduces the example naming ruler and shogun; Rey corrects the OCR's dropped e.
[c1-l011 >] cǒqetotemo xǒjiuo manucare yezu. *A-*
[c1-l011 note] The example continues with noble lineage and the inability to escape birth and death: kooke totemo shooji wo manukare ezu. The Portuguese A- begins Ainda on the next row.
[c1-l012 >] *inda que ſeja Rey, capitão, ou nobre não pode*
[c1-l012 note] Completes Ainda and translates the concession: even a king, captain, or noble cannot escape what follows. The three ranks correspond to the Japanese example above.
[c1-l013 >] *eſcapar de nacer, & morrer.*
[c1-l013 note] Birth and death complete the things no rank can evade. The historical spelling nacer is retained as printed.
[c1-l014] Tenxin. *Coração, ou vontade del Rey, ou*
[c1-l014 note] Tenshin here denotes the heart or will of a king, with a divine alternative continuing below. The next Tenxin entry is a separate homophonous sense.
[c1-l015 >] *de Deos.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes 'the heart or will of God'. This brief line belongs to the first Tenxin definition, not the following headword.
[c1-l016] Tenxin. Tenno macoto. *Verdade diuina.*
[c1-l016 note] This Tenxin is paraphrased as ten no makoto, divine truth. The scan prints continuous Verdade rather than OCR Verda de.
[c1-l017] Tenxocu. Tẽno xocubut. *Comer, ou manjar*
[c1-l017 note] Tenshoku is celestial food, paraphrased by ten no shokubutsu. Tẽno has a nasal abbreviation and xocubut ends in the historical checked t, both preserved.
[c1-l018 >] *celeſtial.*
[c1-l018 note] Celestial modifies the food or eating named in the preceding row. It completes the gloss on its own physical line.
[c1-l019] Ten xu. Tenno nuxi. *Senhor do ceo. Pala-*
[c1-l019 note] Tenshu, literally heaven's lord, is glossed as Lord of heaven. The clear internal space in Ten xu is retained, unlike the unspaced architectural Tenxu below. The final Pala- starts a comment identifying the expression's ecclesiastical setting.
[c1-l020 >] *ura dos liuros da igreja.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes Palaura: a word used in the books of the church. This is the dictionary's usage explanation for the preceding religious term.
[c1-l021] Tenxu. *Torre de muitos ſobrados que ſe faz de*
[c1-l021 note] The second Tenshu is a many-storied tower. The gloss continues below with its material and use in Japanese fortresses, distinguishing it from Lord of heaven.
[c1-l022 >] *madeira em Iapão nas fortalezas.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes the tower description: constructed of wood in Japan's fortresses. The gloss identifies the architectural tenshu without importing a later technical definition.
[c1-l023] Tenya. *Tenda, ou botica ondeſe vendem di-*
[c1-l023 note] Tenya is a shop or stall selling various goods. The close ondeſe and the divided di- are retained from the print.
[c1-l024 >] *uerſas couſas.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes diuerſas couſas, various things, specifying what is sold in the shop. It is a genuine short continuation.
[c1-l025] Tenyacu. *Encargo, ou obrigação que poem o*
[c1-l025 note] This Tenyaku is an imposed charge or obligation. The headword begins with the same capital T as neighboring entries, not OCR lowercase r.
[c1-l026 >] *ſenhor a todos de fazerẽ algũa obra. ¶* Tẽ-
[c1-l026 note] The lord imposes the work on everyone. The following Tẽ- starts the Japanese example and is roman, whereas the surrounding explanation is italic; both abbreviation tildes remain unexpanded.

[c1-l027 note] Together with Tẽ- above, the example concerns assigning or imposing tenyaku. Expected yacuuo is abraded at the c position; the visible provisional ya uuo is retained instead of supplying an unestablished consonant. Latin l introduces caquru as an alternative to atçuru.
[c1-l028 >] *eſta obrigação, ou encargo em commum.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes the example's translation: the lord imposes this obligation or charge collectively. This confirms the administrative sense of the preceding headword.
[c1-l029] Tenyacu. *Fiſicos principaes del Rey.*
[c1-l029 note] This separate Tenyaku entry names the king's principal physicians. Fiſicos has its historical medical meaning; the worn initial T is restored from the scan rather than OCR F.
[c1-l030] Tenyacu. *Promeſſa celestial, ou de Deos.*
[c1-l030 note] A third Tenyaku denotes a heavenly or divine promise. The letters form celestial, correcting the OCR's transposed celestail. The provisional st classification is retained because the abraded ligature does not establish an independent long-s correction.
[c1-l031] Tenye. Tenno ixǒ. *Veſtido celeſtial, ou de*
[c1-l031 note] Ten'e is paraphrased as heaven's clothing. The Portuguese gives celestial clothing and continues with an angelic alternative below.
[c1-l032 >] *Anjo.*
[c1-l032 note] Anjo completes the alternative 'clothing of an angel'. It belongs to Tenye and is not a new headword.
[c1-l033] Tenzo. *Certo officio, ou cargo entre os Ienxùs*
[c1-l033 note] Tenzo is an office among the Zen community, called Ienxùs in the Portuguese sentence. The following row identifies responsibility for the kitchen; Ienxùs is an embedded Japanese sect name and a typeface-review term.
[c1-l034 >] *co no official, ou prefeito da cuzinha.*
[c1-l034 note] The role is compared with an officer or supervisor of the kitchen. The damaged co no is retained graphically; the context suggests como, but enlargement does not securely establish the missing or damaged strokes of m.
[c1-l035] Tenzui. *Sinal, ou prenoſtico do ceo.*
[c1-l035 note] Tenzui is a sign or omen from heaven. Prenoſtico is retained as the historical form shown in the scan, without modernizing its vowels.
[c1-l036] Teppan. *Laminas de ferro. S.*
[c1-l036 note] Teppan denotes sheets or plates of iron. The final S is the upright written-language label, separate from the italic Portuguese definition.
[c1-l037] Teppeqi. Curoganeno cabe. *Parede de fer-*
[c1-l037 note] Teppeki is explained as kurogane no kabe, an iron wall. The Portuguese fer- has initial f, not OCR long s, and continues with ro below.
[c1-l038 >] *ro. ¶* Guinzan teppeqi. *Montes de*
[c1-l038 note] Ro completes ferro. The Japanese example ginzan teppeki pairs mountains of silver with walls of iron; its Portuguese translation begins here.
[c1-l039 >] *prata, & paredes de ferro.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes the image as silver mountains and iron walls. The two materials correspond directly to ginzan and teppeki.
[c1-l040] Teppô. *Eſpingarda.*
[c1-l040 note] Teppoo is a firearm. The headword is consistently roman and the Portuguese Eſpingarda italic; the OCR's alternating character-level italics and extra i have no basis in the scan.
[c1-l041] Teppôno dai. *Coronha da eſpingarda.*
[c1-l041 note] Teppoo no dai is the firearm's stock, glossed coronha. The scan shows a continuous word, not OCR Coro nha.
[c1-l042] Teppôzucume. *O por cõ a eſpingardaria em*
[c1-l042 note] Teppoozukume denotes bringing pressure on someone with gunfire or a body of firearms. The abbreviated cõ is preserved, and em introduces the continuation aperto.
[c1-l043 >] *aperto a alguem.*
[c1-l043 note] Em aperto a alguem completes the idea of putting someone in a difficult or constrained position with firearms. It is the continuation of the preceding definition.
[c1-l044] Teqi. Cataqi. *Inimigo. ¶* Teqiuo ſuru.
[c1-l044 note] Teki is equated with kataki, enemy. The following example teki wo suru begins the expression for making oneself an enemy; capital T replaces the OCR's r.
[c1-l045 >] l, naſu. *Fazerſe inimigo.*
[c1-l045 note] Latin l introduces nasu as an alternative to suru. The Portuguese explains both as becoming an enemy; the initial i of inimigo is present in the cramped italic text.
[c1-l046] Teqian. Cataqi cangayuru. *Inuenção, ou*
[c1-l046 note] Tekian is an enemy's devising or stratagem, paraphrased by Cataqi cangayuru. It begins a new entry at the column's ordinary headword margin, so the spurious continuation indentation is removed.
[c1-l047 >] *ardil dos inimigos. Vt,* Teqianni iru. *Cair*
[c1-l047 note] Ardil dos inimigos completes the stratagem definition. Tekian ni iru means falling into an enemy's plot; Cair carries its Portuguese translation into the next column.
