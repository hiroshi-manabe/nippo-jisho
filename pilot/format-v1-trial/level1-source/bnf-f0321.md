[c1-l001] Minzurito. *Vt,* Minzurito xita agiuai. *Gos-*
[c1-l001 note] Minzurito describes a mild or unassertive quality: the first example concerns flavour. The full initial M is present in the scan; Gos- begins Portuguese gosto across the line break.
[c1-l002 >] *to dalgũa couſa como comer leue, & de pouco*
[c1-l002 note] Completes gosto and explains a light food with little flavour. The nasal abbreviation in dalgũa is preserved, not expanded to a modern spelling.
[c1-l003 >] *goſto. Permet.* Minzurito xita fito. *Homẽ*
[c1-l003 note] The weak-flavour definition ends here; the figurative example then applies minzurito to a person. Homẽ begins the Portuguese characterization completed below.
[c1-l004 >] *ſingello, & candido.*
[c1-l004 note] The person is simple and candid: this completes the figurative example, rather than introducing another headword.
[c1-l005] Miǒ. Curaxi. *Couſa eſcura, ou encuberta, &*
[c1-l005 note] Myoo is explained by kurashi, dark, and by Portuguese descriptions of something obscure or hidden. The caron in Miǒ is kept distinct from a circumflex.
[c1-l006 >] *que não ſe pode ver bem. Vt,* Miǒno cago.
[c1-l006 note] The obscure thing cannot be clearly seen; myoo no kago illustrates unseen protection. The Japanese example is upright after the italic Vt marker.
[c1-l007 >] *Fauor occulto dos Camis, & Fotoques.*
[c1-l007 note] The example means the hidden favour or protection of kami and Buddhas. The scan supports the single word occulto, not OCR o culto; Camis and Fotoques occur inside the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l008] Miǒban. i, Aſuno bãguei. *Amenhaã à tarde.*
[c1-l008 note] Myooban is tomorrow evening, explained by asu no bangei. The scan shows a nasal-abbreviation mark in bãguei; the Portuguese final tilde belongs to the second a of Amenhaã.
[c1-l009] Miǒban. i, Dǒſa. *Pedra vme.*
[c1-l009 note] This second myooban entry denotes alum, not tomorrow evening. The separate second pass found a faint downward-pointing caron in Dǒſa, unlike the clear circumflex in Fôuo below; the mineral gloss pedra vme and historical v are retained.
[c1-l010] Miǒbat. *Castigo do ceo occulto.*
[c1-l010 note] Myoobat denotes hidden punishment from heaven. Final t is the dictionary's checked-coda spelling; it is not expanded to a modern open syllable.
[c1-l011] Miǒbiô. Fôuo aqiramuru. *O entender bẽ*
[c1-l011 note] The Japanese explanation hoo wo akiramuru concerns understanding the law or doctrine clearly. Bẽ abbreviates bem, whose object follows on the next row.
[c1-l012 >] *a ley.*
[c1-l012 note] The law or doctrine is the object of entender above, completing the explanation of Miǒbiô. This short continuation is a separate printed line.
[c1-l013] Miǒcan. *Beneplacito dos Camis, ou Fotoques.*
[c1-l013 note] Myookan is the approval or pleasure of kami or Buddhas. Camis and Fotoques are embedded names in the Portuguese explanation, not additional headwords.
[c1-l014 >] *Vt,* Xinmei, Butdano miǒcan. *O com-*
[c1-l014 note] Shinmei and Butda supply the divine and Buddhist referents in the example of myookan. The Portuguese com- continues into comprazeremſe below; the historical Butda spelling is retained.
[c1-l015 >] *prazeremſe, ou folgarem os Camis, & Foto-*
[c1-l015 note] Completes the verb for taking pleasure or being pleased. Foto- is the physically divided beginning of Fotoques; it is not an independent Japanese example.
[c1-l016 >] *ques com algũa couſa.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes Fotoques and specifies being pleased with something. This concludes the example of divine approval begun two rows above.
[c1-l017] Miǒchô. Aquru axita. *Amenhaã polamenhaã.*
[c1-l017 note] Myoochoo is the next morning, explained by akuru ashita. The Portuguese repeats the morning sense; its joined polamenhaã and the two different Japanese vowel marks are preserved.
[c1-l018] Miǒdai. *O que està em lugar de outro, ou lu-*
[c1-l018 note] Myoodai denotes a representative or substitute, someone in another's place. The divided lu- begins lugar in the following line's lugar tenente.
[c1-l019 >] *gar tenente. ¶* Miǒdaini tatçu. *Estar,*
[c1-l019 note] Lugar tenente completes the representative gloss. Myoodai ni tatsu is to stand or act in another person's place; its translation continues below.
[c1-l020 >] *ou ſer posto em lugar de outro. ¶* Miǒdai
[c1-l020 note] Completes the example as being placed in another's stead. A second example begins with myoodai, whose object particle and verb follow on the next line.
[c1-l021 >] uo tatçuru. *Dar, ou por alguem ẽ ſeu lugar.*
[c1-l021 note] Myoodai wo tatsuru means appointing someone in one's place. The possessive is ſeu, with long s rather than OCR f; ẽ is retained as the printed abbreviation for em.
[c1-l022] Miǒdǒ. *O interior, ou vontade incognita dos*
[c1-l022 note] Myoodoo is explained as an unseen interior disposition or unknown will. The possessors of this will are named on the following physical line.
[c1-l023 >] *Camis, & Fotoques.*
[c1-l023 note] Kami and Buddhas complete the preceding definition of an inscrutable divine will. This is Portuguese running explanation, not a list of Japanese synonyms.
[c1-l024] Miǒga. *Boa dita. Vt,* Miǒgamonai fito.
[c1-l024 note] Myooga is good fortune or blessing; the example myooga mo nai hito negates it, describing an unfortunate person. Its Portuguese explanation follows below.
[c1-l025 >] *Homẽ deſditoſo, ou mal afortunado. ¶* Miǒ-
[c1-l025 note] The gloss describes an unlucky or ill-fortuned person. Miǒ- begins another example of the same fortune sense and continues as gano on the next row.
[c1-l026 >] gano tçuqita fito. *Idem. As vezes ſe*
[c1-l026 note] Myooga no tsukita hito is a person whose good fortune has run out. Idem equates the examples; the Portuguese then starts describing an additional grateful use of the expression.
[c1-l027 >] *diz, agradecendo muito os beneficios, ou fauores*
[c1-l027 note] Continues the usage explanation: the expression can convey strong gratitude for benefits or favours. This shifts the force of the negative expression away from simple misfortune.
[c1-l028 >] *que fazem a alguem mais do que elle merece,*
[c1-l028 note] The favours are greater than the recipient deserves. This relative clause explains the self-deprecating gratitude expressed by the Japanese example below.
[c1-l029 >] *ou eſpera. Vt,* Miǒgamonai coto.
[c1-l029 note] The benefits also exceed expectation. Myooga mo nai koto illustrates the grateful, undeserving sense just described, not merely a second assertion of bad luck.
[c1-l030] Miǒga. *Hũa erua que tem a folha como gin-*
[c1-l030 note] This separate myooga entry names an herb with leaves like ginger. The botanical comparison distinguishes it from the preceding good-fortune homograph; gin- continues below.
[c1-l031 >] *giure.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the printed gin-giure, ginger, as the comparison for the herb's leaves. The physical division and historical u/v spelling are retained.
[c1-l032] Miǒgan. Aqiracana manaco. *Olhos claros.*
[c1-l032 note] Myoogan is explained by akirakana manako, clear eyes. The scan supports g in Miǒgan and cl in claros, correcting the OCR forms Miǒaan and daros.
[c1-l033 >] *¶* Miǒganno fito. *Homẽ ſabio, & douto.*
[c1-l033 note] Myoogan no hito extends clear sight figuratively to a wise and learned person. Only one paragraph mark is printed; the OCR doubled it.
[c1-l034] Miǒgǒ. *Nome que commummente ſe aplica*
[c1-l034 note] Myoogoo is a name or appellation, here particularly one applied to sacred beings. The Portuguese relative clause is completed on the following row.
[c1-l035 >] *a Camis, & Fotoques. ¶* Midano miǒgǒ
[c1-l035 note] The names are those of kami and Buddhas. Mida no myoogoo begins the example concerning the name of Amida and continues below with its verb.
[c1-l036 >] uo tonayuru. *Inuocar o nome de Amida.*
[c1-l036 note] Mida no myoogoo wo tonayuru means invoking or reciting Amida's name. The printed historical tonayuru is preserved; Amida is embedded in the Portuguese translation.
[c1-l037] Miǒgo. *Vt,* Miǒgouo toru. *Conjeiturar*
[c1-l037 note] Myoogo wo toru introduces an astrological act of conjecture. The following lines, not the sound of the headword alone, specify that it concerns a person's eventual death.
[c1-l038 >] *ſobre o fim, ou morte de cada hum vendo a cõ-*
[c1-l038 note] The conjecture concerns each person's end or death through examination of the natal constellation. The abbreviated cõ- begins that Portuguese word across the physical break.
[c1-l039 >] *ſtelação em que naceo, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes cõ-ſtelação and specifies the constellation at birth. Naceo is the historical printed spelling and is not modernized.
[c1-l040] Miǒgonen. l, miǒmiǒnen. *Daqui a dous*
[c1-l040 note] Myoogonen and myoomyoonen are alternatives for the year after next. The lowercase l is the alternative marker, and dous takes annos on the following row.
[c1-l041 >] *annos.*
[c1-l041 note] Completes daqui a dous annos, two years from now. The short row belongs to the preceding pair of temporal expressions.
[c1-l042] Miǒgonichi. Aquru fino nochi. *Depois*
[c1-l042 note] Myoogonichi is explained by akuru hi no nochi, after the following day. Depois begins the matching Portuguese expression for the day after tomorrow.
[c1-l043 >] *damenhaã.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes depois damenhaã, after tomorrow. The joined spelling and tilde on the final a are retained as printed.
[c1-l044] Miǒguichi. *Peixe tainha.*
[c1-l044 note] Miǒguichi is glossed as tainha, a mullet. The brief definition supports the fish sense but does not warrant imposing a modern Japanese spelling or species identification.
[c1-l045] Miǒji. *Alcunha, ou ſobre nome.*
[c1-l045 note] Myooji is a byname or surname. The Portuguese physically separates ſobre nome; the transcription does not combine it into the modern single word.
[c1-l046] Miǒjo. *Fauor, ajuda, ou ſaluação que nesta*
[c1-l046 note] Myoojo denotes favour, aid, or deliverance in this life. Nesta links directly to vida below and is retained with its provisional st representation.
[c1-l047 >] *vida dão os Camis, & Fotoques, como cuidão*
[c1-l047 note] The gloss attributes this worldly aid to kami and Buddhas according to the belief described at the top of column 2. The scan supports vida, Camis, Fotoques and cuidão, correcting the OCR's malformed words without endorsing the source's religious judgment.

[c2-l001 >] *os gentios. Vt,* Miǒjouo auogu. *Pedir,*
[c2-l001 note] Continues column 1: the preceding religious claim is attributed to the people whom the compiler calls gentios. Myoojo wo aogu means seeking or invoking that aid; it is not a new headword here.
[c2-l002 >] *ou inuocar eſte fauor, &c.*
[c2-l002 note] Completes the translation of myoojo wo aogu as invoking this favour. The continuation closes the entry that crossed from column 1.
[c2-l003] Miǒjǒ. Aqiracana foxi. *Estrela dalua.*
[c2-l003 note] Myoojoo is explained by akirakana hoshi, a bright star. The scan's joined dalua is best understood as d'alva, the dawn-star expression with historical u/v, not OCR da lua, 'of the moon'; the faint i after q remains discernible.
[c2-l004] Miǒmiǒnen. *Daqui a dous annos, ou depois*
[c2-l004 note] Myoomyoonen means two years from now or the year after next. The alternative Portuguese paraphrase continues on the following row.
[c2-l005 >] *do anno que vem.*
[c2-l005 note] Completes depois do anno que vem, after the coming year. This confirms the year-after-next interpretation rather than simply next year.
[c2-l006] Miǒmiǒnichi. *Daqui a dous dias, ou depois*
[c2-l006 note] Myoomyoonichi is the corresponding expression for two days from now. The scan supplies the final s of dous, lost in the OCR.
[c2-l007 >] *damenhãa.*
[c2-l007 note] Completes depois damenhãa, the day after tomorrow. Enlargement supports the retained tilde over the first a of the final pair here; it is not regularized to the final-a placement seen in other occurrences.
[c2-l008] Miǒmocu. *Terminos de cada ſiencia, ou pa-*
[c2-l008 note] Myoomoku denotes the terminology of a discipline or art. The initial sequence is Miǒ, not the OCR's Mſǒ; pa- begins palavras on the next row.
[c2-l009 >] *lauras determinadas que ſe vſão nas artes, &*
[c2-l009 note] Completes palavras and describes particular established words used in arts and disciplines. The historical vowel/consonant letters in lauras and vſão are preserved.
[c2-l010 >] *ſciencias.*
[c2-l010 note] Sciences completes the description of specialized terminology. The initial ſc contrasts with the preceding occurrence's ſiencia; the spelling variation is retained.
[c2-l011] Miǒmon. *i, Hipocreſia. ¶* Miǒmonni
[c2-l011 note] Myoomon is glossed here as hypocrisy or ostentatious piety. The printed i, is an explanatory marker; Hipocreſia replaces the OCR's intrusive r, and the Japanese example begins before the line ends.
[c2-l012 >] jifiuo ſuru. *fazer eſmola por hipocreſia.*
[c2-l012 note] Myoomon ni jihi wo suru is giving charity for show. The translation explicitly calls the act hypocritical; its initial lowercase fazer is retained.
[c2-l013] Miǒmonjin. *Hipocrita.*
[c2-l013 note] Myoomonjin denotes a hypocrite, a person displaying the quality described in the previous entry. The -jin ending identifies the person rather than the act.
[c2-l014] Miǒmonna. *Idem. ¶* Miǒmonna coto-
[c2-l014 note] Myoomonna is the corresponding descriptive form, referred back by Idem. Its example begins 'hypocritical things' and continues with the object particle and verb below.
[c2-l015 >] uo ſuru. *Fazer couſas de hipocreſia.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes myoomonna koto wo suru, doing hypocritical things. The scan reads hipocreſia with i rather than the OCR's hipocreſta.
[c2-l016] Miǒnen. Aquru toxi. *Anno que vem.*
[c2-l016 note] Myoonen is the coming year, explained by akuru toshi. It contrasts with the year-after-next term earlier in this column.
[c2-l017] Miǒnichi. Aqurufi. *Amenhãa.*
[c2-l017 note] Myoonichi is tomorrow, explained by akuru hi. The printed Aqurufi remains joined, and the occurrence's retained Amenhãa is not regularized to match other double-a spellings.
[c2-l018] Miǒnit. *Idem.*
[c2-l018 note] Myoonit has the same tomorrow sense. Its final t is a checked-coda variant of the preceding -nichi form, not a missing word ending to supply.
[c2-l019] Miǒno xǒran. *O ver dos Camis, & Fotoques*
[c2-l019 note] Myoo no shooran describes the seeing or observation of kami and Buddhas. A faint caron is present in Miǒno, omitted by the OCR; the next line specifies that this seeing is hidden from humans.
[c2-l020 >] *occulto aos homens.*
[c2-l020 note] Hidden from humans completes the description of divine observation. Occulto modifies the preceding act of seeing rather than introducing a separate hidden object.
[c2-l021] Miǒqen. *Idem. ¶* Miǒqenni tçuqete
[c2-l021 note] Myooken is equated with the preceding unseen divine observation. The example begins 'in view of myooken' and supplies its consequence below.
[c2-l022 >] ſono voſore ari. *Por quanto os Camis, &*
[c2-l022 note] Sono osore ari states that there is cause for fear or reverence. The Portuguese explains why, and the scan has capitalized Camis rather than the OCR's lowercase form.
[c2-l023 >] *Fotoques vem tudo, he couſa pera temer.*
[c2-l023 note] Kami and Buddhas see everything, hence the cause for fear in the example. Vem has the sense of seeing here; its printed form is not modernized to veem.
[c2-l024] Miǒqiǒ. Aqiracana cagami. *Eſpelho cla-*
[c2-l024 note] Myookyoo is a clear mirror, explained by akirakana kagami. The final cla- begins the Portuguese adjective claro across the line break.
[c2-l025 >] *ro. ¶ Permet.* Miǒqiǒuo teraſu. *Dar*
[c2-l025 note] After completing claro, the entry gives a figurative use: myookyoo wo terasu, shining the clear mirror. Its Portuguese equivalent begins with dar and is completed below.
[c2-l026 >] *bom exemplo.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes dar bom exemplo, setting a good example. This makes explicit the figurative ethical sense of the clear-mirror expression.
[c2-l027] Miǒqua. *Grande fogo. Vt,* Miǒquaua qi-
[c2-l027 note] Myookwa denotes a great fire. The scan supports fogo rather than ſogo, and the example reads Miǒquaua with the topic particle ua, not OCR Miǒquana; qi- starts the verb continued below.
[c2-l028 >] yegataxi. *O grande fogo apagaſe mal.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes qiyegataxi, difficult to extinguish. Together the example says that a great fire is hard to put out, exactly the sense of apagaſe mal.
[c2-l029] Miǒri. *Hipocreſia, ou deſejo de louuor, &*
[c2-l029 note] Myoori is treated critically as hypocrisy or desire for praise and gain. The list continues on the next row rather than ending with louuor.
[c2-l030 >] *intereſe, ou cubiça. ¶* Miǒrino futatçu-
[c2-l030 note] Interest or greed completes the definition. Myoori no futatsu begins the example of two bonds; the faint caron in the repeated Miǒrino is retained from the scan rather than omitted as in OCR.
[c2-l031 >] ni fodaſaruru. l, tçunagaruru. *Estar*
[c2-l031 note] Ni hodasaruru, alternatively tsunagaruru, describes being bound by the two things. The two Japanese verbs correspond to the Portuguese estar preso that continues below.
[c2-l032 >] *preſo da hipocreſia, & proprio intereſe que*
[c2-l032 note] Completes estar preso and identifies the bonds as hypocrisy and personal interest. The relative clause continues into the following short line.
[c2-l033 >] *ſão dous laços.*
[c2-l033 note] The two motives are explicitly called two snares. This completes the explanation of futatsu and the binding verbs in the Japanese example.
[c2-l034] Miǒriô. Aqiracani ſatoru. *Penetrar clara-*
[c2-l034 note] Myooryoo is explained by akirakani satoru, understanding or discerning clearly. The Portuguese adverb claramente is physically divided after clara-.
[c2-l035 >] *mente as couſas do* Buppò.
[c2-l035 note] Completes the definition as clearly penetrating matters of Buddhist doctrine. Buppò is an embedded Japanese term, visibly upright against the italic Portuguese, and retains its grave accent.
[c2-l036] Miǒrio i, Cami fotoqeno naixô. *O interi-*
[c2-l036 note] This separate Miǒrio concerns the inner disposition of kami and Buddhas, explained by kami hotoke no naishoo. No clear stop is printed between the headword and i,; interi- continues below.
[c2-l037 >] *or, & vontade dos Camis, & Fotoques.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes interior and pairs it with the will of kami and Buddhas. The two sacred names remain part of the Portuguese gloss, distinct from the Japanese explanation in the preceding row.
[c2-l038] Miǒtan. *Cabeça. Vt,* Miǒtan vchi vara-
[c2-l038 note] This myootan means head. Its example concerns striking or breaking a head and runs through the divided vara-rena; the unrelated tomorrow-morning homograph follows below.
[c2-l039 >] rena. *Guardainão vos quebre a cabeça.* B.
[c2-l039 note] Completes the warning about having one's head broken. The joined Guardainão is retained, and the final upright B. is the low-register label, not part of the italic sentence.
[c2-l040] Miǒtan. Miǒnichino aſa. *Amanhãa pella*
[c2-l040 note] The second myootan means tomorrow morning, explained by myoonichi no asa. It is distinct from the preceding head sense; the Portuguese phrase continues below.
[c2-l041 >] *manhãa.*
[c2-l041 note] Completes pella manhãa, in the morning. The double-a form and its locally printed tilde placement are retained rather than silently modernized.
[c2-l042] Miǒxeqi. Aquru yǔbe. i, Aſuno banguei.
[c2-l042 note] Myooseki is the following evening, explained by akuru yuube and asu no bangei. This occurrence spells out the n in banguei, unlike the abbreviated bãguei earlier on the page.
[c2-l043 >] *Tarde dà manhãa.*
[c2-l043 note] The Portuguese gives tomorrow evening. Its printed dà and manhãa are retained; the temporal sense follows the Japanese explanations rather than a literal modern reading of morning.
[c2-l044] Miǒxu. i, Fiacuxǒno votona. *Cabeça dos*
[c2-l044 note] Myooshu is explained as the elder or head among cultivators, hyakushoo no otona. This is a new flush-left entry, not an indented continuation as in the provisional layout.
[c2-l045 >] *lauradores.*
[c2-l045 note] Cultivators completes cabeça dos, the head of the farming community. The historical lauradores spelling is retained.
[c2-l046] Miǒya. Aquru yo. *Noite que vem.*
[c2-l046 note] Myooya is the coming night, explained by akuru yo. The headword begins at the regular entry margin; its provisional indentation has been removed.
[c2-l047] Mippô. *Abelha que faz mel.* S.
[c2-l047 note] Mippoo is a honey-producing bee. The damaged initial M and the two p letters are supported by the scan; upright S. labels written usage. This body entry is distinct from the repeated catchword below it.
