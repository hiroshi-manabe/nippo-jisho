[c1-l001 >] *os meninos fazem correr, tirando lhe às*
[c1-l001 note] This continues f166's straw ring: children make it roll and shoot at it. The moving target explains the otherwise ambiguous object name `Fama`.
[c1-l002 >] *ſetadas. ¶* Famauo iru. *Tirarẽ os me-*
[c1-l002 note] `Famauo iru` means shooting at the ring. Portuguese repeats the children's action; `me-` continues as `meninos` below.
[c1-l003 >] *ninos a este circulo de palha com frechas.*
[c1-l003 note] The target is explicitly the straw ring, and the projectiles are arrows. This rules out interpreting the preceding play as a ball game.
[c1-l004 >] ¶ Famayumi. *Arco com que tirão a eſte*
[c1-l004 note] `Famayumi` is the bow used for shooting at that ring. The compound's second part is 弓, not a separate verb.
[c1-l005 >] *circulo.*
[c1-l005 note] `circulo` closes the bow definition by referring back to the ring. It is a physical continuation, not new vocabulary.
[c1-l006] Famabata. i, Miguiua. *Borda da praya.*
[c1-l006 note] `Famabata` and `Miguiua` identify the shore's edge. The mechanical reading ミギワ reflects the printed spelling rather than requiring modern みぎわ notation in Level 1.
[c1-l007] Famabe. *Idem.*
[c1-l007 note] `Famabe` has the same meaning as the immediately preceding shore-edge entry. `Idem` does not refer back to the children's target.
[c1-l008] Famabiſaxi. *Terra que eſta aolongo do mar*
[c1-l008 note] `Famabiſaxi` is coastal land with a particular eroded formation. The following line explains what the waves have done to it.
[c1-l009 >] *gaſtada, & cauada das ondas.*
[c1-l009 note] Waves have worn and hollowed out the land. This physical description is more specific than simply a beach or coastline.
[c1-l010] Famabô. *Hũa certa aruorezinha, ou erua*
[c1-l010 note] `Famabô` denotes a small tree or large herb growing at the beach. The botanical identification needs the following medicinal and regional information.
[c1-l011 >] *grande da praya que he boa pera lauatorio dos*
[c1-l011 note] The plant is useful for washing the feet. This is the dictionary's reported use, not a modern recommendation or proof of species identity.
[c1-l012 >] *pès chamada Nigunde na India.*
[c1-l012 note] The Indian name `Nigunde` is supplied as a comparison. It is not Japanese, and an exact modern botanical identification needs an external source.
[c1-l013] Famabutçu. *Erua aſſi chamada. No* cami
[c1-l013 note] `Famabutçu` is a plant with a regional alternative name. `cami` refers to the upper-region usage, not a deity in this phrase.
[c1-l014 >] *ſediz,* Cauara yomogui.
[c1-l014 note] The alternative is `Cauara yomogui`, 河原蓬. This identifies the lexical relationship without changing the regional headword's spelling.
[c1-l015] Famachi. *Hum certo peixe do mar.*
[c1-l015 note] `Famachi` is a sea fish, compatible with はまち. The brief gloss alone does not establish a precise modern size or age category.
[c1-l016] Famachidori. *Maſſarico, paſſaro que anda*
[c1-l016 note] `Famachidori` is a shore bird, rendered by Portuguese `Maſſarico`. Treat that as the compiler's bird comparison, not a modern taxonomic equivalence.
[c1-l017 >] *pollas prayas.*
[c1-l017 note] The bird frequents beaches. This completes the habitat description and supports the 浜 compound.
[c1-l018] Famaguiua. *Borda da praya.*
[c1-l018 note] `Famaguiua` means the edge of the beach. `gui` is hard ギ here, contrasting with bare `gi` in the historical ヂ series.
[c1-l019] Famaguri. *Ameijoas.*
[c1-l019 note] `Famaguri` is ハマグリ; Portuguese `Ameijoas` gives clams. The bilingual match is straightforward but does not settle every zoological distinction.
[c1-l020] Famanadexico. *Hũa erua.*
[c1-l020 note] `Famanadexico` names a plant. The gloss only says “an herb,” so the compound 浜撫子 is a reading aid, not a confirmed species identification.
[c1-l021] Famari, ru, atta. *Acaruarſe, ou meterſe*
[c1-l021 note] `Famari` describes sinking or becoming deeply lodged. The Portuguese examples below distinguish this from the modern abstract sense alone.
[c1-l022 >] *muito como na lama, caua, poço &c. ¶* Do-
[c1-l022 note] Mud, a pit, and a well are places one may become embedded. `Do-` begins the Japanese mud example.
[c1-l023 >] roni famaru. *Atolar na lama. ¶ Ali-*
[c1-l023 note] `Do- / toni famaru` means becoming stuck in mud. `Ali-` begins an editorial marker for the following metaphorical extension.
[c1-l024 >] *quando per met.* Xeqenno cotoni famaru.
[c1-l024 note] `Xeqenno cotoni famaru` is becoming absorbed in worldly affairs. `permet.` marks metaphorical use, not a Japanese word.
[c1-l025 >] *Eſtar muito metido nas couſas do mundo.*
[c1-l025 note] The Portuguese says being deeply involved in worldly things. This confirms the figurative, rather than muddy or physical, reading of the example.
[c1-l026 >] ¶ Acuni famaru. *Eſtar entregue, & me-*
[c1-l026 note] `Acuni famaru` means being immersed in evil. The Portuguese uses the moral category of sins in the continuation.
[c1-l027 >] *tido nos peccados.*
[c1-l027 note] `metido nos peccados` completes involvement in sins. This is the moral extension of becoming trapped or embedded.
[c1-l028] Famauogui. *Hũa erua dagoa doce como ca-*
[c1-l028 note] `Famauogui` denotes a freshwater plant resembling reeds. The spelling gives ハマヲギ, but the short gloss does not resolve an exact species.
[c1-l029 >] *niços.*
[c1-l029 note] `ca- / niços` completes “reeds.” The line is the end of the plant comparison, not another entry.
[c1-l030] Famaxe, uru, eta. *Dar de comer a bois, & beſ-*
[c1-l030 note] `Famaxe` means feeding cattle or other beasts. The causative relationship to animal eating is clearer when read with the example below.
[c1-l031 >] *tas. ¶* Vmani cutçuuauo famaſuru.
[c1-l031 note] `Vmani cutçuuauo famaſuru` literally puts the bit into the horse's mouth. This extends the feeding/holding-in-the-mouth sense rather than describing actual food.
[c1-l032 >] *Por o freo ao cauallo.*
[c1-l032 note] Portuguese explains putting the bridle on the horse. It confirms the tack-related interpretation of `cutçuuauo` above.
[c1-l033] Famayumi. *Hum arcozinho pequeno com que*
[c1-l033 note] `Famayumi` is a small bow used by children. It repeats as a headword the compound illustrated near the top of this page.
[c1-l034 >] *atirão os meninos. Vide,* Fama.
[c1-l034 note] The cross-reference `Fama` points to the ring-target game. It should not be interpreted as the beach homograph.
[c1-l035] Fame, uru, eta. *Meter, ou acaruar como na*
[c1-l035 note] `Fame` is the transitive counterpart of fitting or sinking something in. The gloss begins with putting or embedding an object.
[c1-l036 >] *lama, caua, poço, &c. ¶ Item, Com*
[c1-l036 note] Mud, a pit, and a well repeat the physical settings of `Famari`. `Item` introduces a constructional use with another verb.
[c1-l037 >] *outro verbo, encaxar algum pao em outro. Vt,*
[c1-l037 note] The compound describes fitting one piece of wood into another. Portuguese explicitly signals that another verb participates.
[c1-l038 >] Yerifamuru, qirifamuru, &c. ¶ Yauo
[c1-l038 note] `Yerifamuru` and `qirifamuru` are fitting-related compounds; their exact first elements deserve lexical caution. `Yauo` starts an arrow example continued below.
[c1-l039 >] famuru. *Por aſeta no arco.*
[c1-l039 note] `Yauo famuru` is setting an arrow on the bow. The Portuguese identifies the object and action without requiring an eating interpretation of `famu`.
[c1-l040] Famecuſa. *Entulho. A propria palaura he*
[c1-l040 note] `Famecuſa` means fill or rubble. The compiler explicitly says another form is the proper word, supplied on the next line.
[c1-l041 >] Vmecuſa.
[c1-l041 note] `Vmecuſa` ウメクサ is that preferred form. The dictionary's correction remains a cross-reference, not permission to erase `Famecuſa`.
[c1-l042] Famet. Yabure foroburu. *Deſtruição.*
[c1-l042 note] `Yabure foroburu` explains 破滅, destruction. The headword's final `-t` represents a historical checked ending, not a lost following vowel to restore.
[c1-l043] Fami. *Comer de animaes como de bois, ou ca-*
[c1-l043 note] This nominal `Fami` is animal eating. Cattle are one example, and the list continues below.
[c1-l044 >] *uallos, & paſſaros.*
[c1-l044 note] Horses and birds complete the animal list. The line belongs to the feeding sense, not the following bit-in-the-mouth noun.
[c1-l045] Fami. i, Cutçuuano cucumi. *Parte do freo,*
[c1-l045 note] `Cutçuuano cucumi` identifies the mouth-held part of a bit. `Fami` here names tack rather than the act of eating.
[c1-l046 >] *ou ferro que entra na boca do cauallo.*
[c1-l046 note] The iron enters the horse's mouth. This completes the anatomical location of the bit component.
[c1-l047] Fami, u, ǒda. *Comer aues, animais, pei-*
[c1-l047 note] The verbal `Fami, u, ǒda` describes animals eating. Read the Portuguese animals as the eaters in context, confirmed by the bird example across the column break.

[c2-l001 >] *xes, &c. ¶* Toriga yeuo famu. *Os paſſa-*
[c2-l001 note] `Toriga yeuo famu` is “the birds eat food.” The subject marker `ga` removes the ambiguity that Portuguese infinitival wording might create.
[c2-l002 >] *ros comem.*
[c2-l002 note] `Os paſſa- / ros comem` completes “the birds eat.” It confirms that birds are the agents, not the food.
[c2-l003] Famicayeri, u, etta. *Tornarem os porcos*
[c2-l003 note] `Famicayeri` describes wild boars returning to feed. The reason for the return is specified in the next lines.
[c2-l004 >] *monteſes a buſcar o paſto que lhes tinhão posto*
[c2-l004 note] They seek food that has been put out for them. The final phrase identifies that food as bait.
[c2-l005 >] *por negaça.*
[c2-l005 note] `negaça` marks the lure or bait. This makes the example a hunting context rather than ordinary husbandry.
[c2-l006] Famimono. *Comida de beſtas, ou de paſſaros.*
[c2-l006 note] `Famimono` is food for beasts or birds. The noun is built around animal feeding, consistent with the preceding family.
[c2-l007] Famo. *Nome de hum peixe.*
[c2-l007 note] `Famo` is a fish name, compatible with ハモ. No further species details are printed in this very short definition.
[c2-l008] Famǒdo. *O que vai mariſcar à praya.*
[c2-l008 note] `Famǒdo` names someone gathering shellfish on the shore. The shore-worker interpretation depends on the full gloss, not the long-vowel hint alone.
[c2-l009 >] ¶ *Item, O que trabalha nas marinhas.*
[c2-l009 note] The term also applies to a worker in saltworks (`marinhas`). This broadens the occupation beyond shellfish gathering.
[c2-l010] Famuqi, u, uita. i, Mucai, cǒ. *Encontrar-*
[c2-l010 note] `Famuqi` is glossed by facing or confronting. The printed `Mucai, cǒ` has an unusual abbreviated paradigm; do not mechanically normalize it from modern morphology.
[c2-l011 >] *ſe na guerra.*
[c2-l011 note] The encounter is in war. This supplies the adversarial context for the preceding facing verb.
[c2-l012] Famure, ruru, eta. *Baterem os paſſaros, ou*
[c2-l012 note] `Famure` describes birds beating or striking. The next line identifies wings and water as the relevant objects.
[c2-l013 >] *darem com as aſas na agoa.*
[c2-l013 note] Birds strike the water with their wings. This is a wing-beating action, not an unspecified fight among birds.
[c2-l014] Famuxa. *Soldados ordinarios.*
[c2-l014 note] `Famuxa` is an ordinary soldier, compatible with 葉武者. The definition concerns rank rather than an individual warrior's name.
[c2-l015] Fan. Iy. *Arroz cozido.*
[c2-l015 note] `Fan` is 飯, cooked rice. `Iy` is the accompanying Japanese gloss for rice, not Portuguese or an inflection of `Fan`.
[c2-l016] Fan. *Nones, contrario de Chô, que he pa-*
[c2-l016 note] This `Fan` is the odd-number category opposed to `Chô`, even. It differs from the rice homograph immediately above.
[c2-l017 >] *res. ¶ Item, A metade, mas não ſe vſa ſe-*
[c2-l017 note] The line completes “even numbers,” then introduces the half sense. The compiler qualifies how that latter meaning is used.
[c2-l018 >] *não com algũa compoſição, ou de pois de outra*
[c2-l018 note] “Half” occurs in compounds or after another word. This is a grammatical usage restriction, not a ban on the preceding odd-number sense.
[c2-l019 >] *palaura. Vt,* Fanbun. *A metade.*
[c2-l019 note] `Fanbun` is 半分, “a half,” illustrating the compound use. The Portuguese equivalent is direct.
[c2-l020 >] ¶ Fitotçu fan. *Hum, & meo.*
[c2-l020 note] `Fitotçu fan` means one and a half. It illustrates `fan` following a quantity rather than forming a single lexical headword.
[c2-l021] Fan. *Sinal de cartas, &c. ¶* Fanuo ſu-
[c2-l021 note] This `Fan` is a signature or mark on a letter. `Fanuo ſu-` begins the act of placing that mark.
[c2-l022 >] yuru. *Por ſeu ſinal, ou aſſinarſe.*
[c2-l022 note] `ſu- / yuru` completes the placing verb. Portuguese equates it with signing one's name or putting one's mark.
[c2-l023] Fan. *Emprenſa, ou impreſſão. ¶* Fanni fi-
[c2-l023 note] This `Fan` is a printing block or impression. The following Japanese expressions concern producing or printing it, not signing a letter.
[c2-l024 >] raqu, ſuru, qizamu, vocoſu, ſuritçuqu-
[c2-l024 note] Join `fi- / raqu` and `ſuritçuqu- / ru` across the boundaries. The listed verbs concern opening/issuing, carving, raising, and printing in the block-printing context.
[c2-l025 >] ru. *Imprimir.*
[c2-l025 note] `ru` completes the final Japanese verb, followed by “print.” It is a continuation of the list, not a separate verb entry.
[c2-l026] Fana. *Nariz. ¶* Fanauo camu. *A-*
[c2-l026 note] `Fana` is 鼻 here. `Fanauo camu` means blowing the nose; its Portuguese translation is divided across the next line.
[c2-l027 >] *ſoarſe. ¶* Fanauo ſuſuru. *Soruer o ra-*
[c2-l027 note] `A- / ſoarſe` finishes blowing the nose. `Fanauo ſuſuru` then means sniffing mucus inward, a contrasting action.
[c2-l028 >] *nho dos narizes. ¶* Fanauo fiqu. *Eſpir-*
[c2-l028 note] The mucus belongs to the nose. `Fanauo fiqu` begins a sneezing example, whose gloss continues below.
[c2-l029 >] *rar. ¶* Fanauo xicamuru. *Fazer ru-*
[c2-l029 note] `Eſpir- / rar` completes sneezing. `Fanauo xicamuru` is wrinkling the nose, not the preceding action repeated.
[c2-l030 >] *gas no nariz. ¶* Fanauo tacǒ ſuru. *En-*
[c2-l030 note] `Fanauo tacǒ ſuru` literally raises the nose and figuratively means becoming proud. The adverbial form belongs to 高く, not a separate noun.
[c2-l031 >] *ſoberbecerſe. ¶* Fanauo tçuqu. *Cair em*
[c2-l031 note] The pride gloss ends here. `Fanauo tçugu` is then explained as falling out of a lord's favor; do not force the idiom into its literal nasal meaning.
[c2-l032 >] *deſgraça do ſenhor. ¶* Fanaga taru. *Cor-*
[c2-l032 note] The lord's displeasure completes that idiom. `Fanaga taru` begins the next example, a running nose.
[c2-l033 >] *rer o ranho dos narizes: mas fana perſi não ſe*
[c2-l033 note] The Portuguese explicitly warns that `fana` by itself does not mean mucus, despite the running-nose phrase. This lexical caveat should survive the commentary.
[c2-l034 >] *toma por ranho. ¶* Fanauo ſoroyuru. *Eſ-*
[c2-l034 note] The warning concludes, followed by `Fanauo ſoroyuru`. Literally aligning noses is explained as horses standing together.
[c2-l035 >] *tarem muitos caualos juntos. ¶* Fanaqire-
[c2-l035 note] Many horses together finish that image. `Fanaqire-` begins a separate figurative expression continued below.
[c2-l036 >] vji. *Vaca forra. i, Peſſoa que anda liure,*
[c2-l036 note] The questionable `vji` remains as printed; expected 牛 would suggest `vxi`. The Portuguese explains a free cow, then a free-ranging person; `Peſſoa...` is Portuguese and is rendered italic accordingly.
[c2-l037 >] *& à vontade como vaca q̃ ſe lhe cortou a põ-*
[c2-l037 note] The person goes freely like a cow whose tethering part has been cut. This explains the metaphor rather than asserting a modern proverb identification.
[c2-l038 >] *ta do beiço por onde eſtaua amarrada.*
[c2-l038 note] The cut is at the lip where the animal was tied. That physical detail connects freedom to the nose-cut expression above.
[c2-l039] Fana. *Roſa, ou flor. ¶* Fanaga chiru.
[c2-l039 note] Now `Fana` means 花, a flower or rose. `Fanaga chiru` is flowers falling or scattering, distinct from the preceding nose vocabulary.
[c2-l040 >] *Eſpalharemſe, ou cairem as roſas. ¶* Fa-
[c2-l040 note] The flowers fall or scatter. `Fa-` starts the transitive counterpart on the following line.
[c2-l041 >] nauo chiraſu. *Eſpalhar roſas. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l041 note] `Fanauo chiraſu` is scattering flowers. `Item` introduces a figurative action whose scope must be read below.
[c2-l042 >] *Pelejar valeroſa, & galhardamente, ou fazer*
[c2-l042 note] The figurative sense includes fighting valiantly and gallantly. It is not simply a literal flower-scattering ceremony.
[c2-l043 >] *outra obra bem, & com destreza. ¶* Fa-
[c2-l043 note] Another skillful or well-executed action can also be meant. `Fa-` then begins the next flower-arranging example.
[c2-l044 >] nauo tatçuru. *Por ramalhete de roſas em*
[c2-l044 note] `Fanauo tatçuru` means setting up a bouquet. The vessel holding it is named in the following line.
[c2-l045 >] *hum vaſo, a que chamão Quaſin. ¶* Fa-
[c2-l045 note] The vessel is called `Quaſin`, a Japanese term within Portuguese prose. This concludes the arrangement example and starts the next one.
[c2-l046 >] nauo voru. *Colher roſas com o pè. ¶* Fa-
[c2-l046 note] `Fanauo voru` is picking flowers with the stalk. The final `Fa-` belongs to the subsequent intransitive example.
[c2-l047 >] naga xibomu. *Engelharſe a roſa, ou flor.*
[c2-l047 note] `Fanaga xibomu` is a flower withering or shriveling. Portuguese `Engelharſe` supports the loss-of-freshness sense, not another picking action.
