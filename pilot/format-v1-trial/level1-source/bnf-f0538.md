[c1-l001] Vmareqi, uru, ita. *Nacendo vir a este*
[c1-l001 note] Umareki describes coming into this world through birth. The Portuguese Nacendo vir begins the definition, whose object continues on the next row; the printed principal forms remain unexpanded.
[c1-l002 >] *mundo.*
[c1-l002 note] Mundo completes vir a este mundo, coming into this world. This short continuation belongs to Umareki and is not a separate entry.
[c1-l003] Vmaretçuqi, qu, ita. *Nacer, ou ter algũa*
[c1-l003 note] Umaretsuki is being born with, or naturally possessing, a quality. The Portuguese introduces the innate-property sense developed in the example below.
[c1-l004 >] *couſa natural. ¶* Vmaretçuita catagui-
[c1-l004 note] The gloss ends with a natural quality, then Umaretsuita catagui- begins the statement that a disposition is inborn. The printed hyphen divides the Japanese example across physical rows.
[c1-l005 >] gia. *He cuſtume natural, ou com que naceo.*
[c1-l005 note] Gia completes catagui gia: it is an inborn disposition. The Portuguese paraphrase specifies a custom or character with which one was born; cuſtume is preserved as printed.
[c1-l006 >] *¶* Donni. l, riconni vmaretçuqu. *Na-*
[c1-l006 note] Donni and riconni give the alternatives of being born dull or intelligent. The marker l introduces the second alternative; Na- begins the Portuguese translation on the next row.
[c1-l007 >] *cer naturalmente paruo, ou diſcreto.*
[c1-l007 note] Cer completes Nacer: to be born naturally foolish or discerning. Diſcreto supplies the positive counterpart of paruo and explains riconni above.
[c1-l008] Vmaretçuqi. *Natureza, ou natural. ¶* V-
[c1-l008 note] The noun Umaretsuki is glossed as nature or natural disposition. The final V- starts a repetition of the headword in the following example.
[c1-l009 >] maretçuqino yoi. l, varui fito. *Homem*
[c1-l009 note] The example contrasts a person with a good or bad innate disposition. Maretçuqino joins the preceding V-, and Homem begins the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l010 >] *de bom, ou ruim natural.*
[c1-l010 note] De bom, ou ruim natural completes the description of a person of good or bad disposition. It continues the preceding Japanese example without adding a new sense.
[c1-l011] Vmarevochi, uru, ita. *Nacer: palaura com*
[c1-l011 note] Umarevochi denotes being born, with particular attention to the moment of birth. The following Portuguese rows delimit that moment rather than supplying a second headword.
[c1-l012 >] *que declarão o tempo, em que hum nace logo*
[c1-l012 note] The definition says this word expresses the time when someone is born, immediately on emerging. The phrase logo em continues into the next physical line.
[c1-l013 >] *em ſaindo do ventre da mãy.*
[c1-l013 note] Emerging from the mother's womb completes the definition of the birth moment. The scan shows a tilde in mãy; it is retained rather than regularized to modern mãe or the OCR's unmarked may.
[c1-l014] Vmarevochini. *De pouco nacido, ou logo em*
[c1-l014 note] Umarevochini means just after, or immediately upon, being born. De pouco nacido and logo em nacendo give the two closely related temporal paraphrases.
[c1-l015 >] *nacendo.*
[c1-l015 note] Nacendo completes logo em nacendo, immediately upon birth. The short row remains part of the Umarevochini entry.
[c1-l016] Vmarevotori, u, otta. *Nacer inferior a ou-*
[c1-l016 note] Umarevotori means being born inferior or falling below one's ancestry. The Portuguese comparison starts with inferior a ou- and continues on the following row.
[c1-l017 >] *tro, ou degenerar dos pays, ou antepaßados.*
[c1-l017 note] Tro completes outro, followed by degenerating from one's parents or ancestors. The clause explains inherited inferiority, preserving the printed antepaßados spelling.
[c1-l018] Vmavmaxij. *Couſa goſtoſa, & muito ſabro-*
[c1-l018 note] Umaumashii first describes something tasty and very savoury. The final sabro- continues as sabrosa on the following row; the figurative application is introduced afterwards.
[c1-l019 >] *ſa. ¶ Item, permet. Quando hum faz*
[c1-l019 note] Sa completes sabrosa, then the entry turns to a figurative use when someone does something well. The compressed permet. marker is left as printed.
[c1-l020 >] *bem algũa couſa que contenta, ſe diz* Vma v-
[c1-l020 note] The figurative use concerns doing something pleasingly well. Vma v- begins the quoted expression umaumashii shiyoo, which crosses the line boundary.
[c1-l021 >] maxij xiyǒ. *Modo de fazer as couſas bem*
[c1-l021 note] Maxij completes the divided adjective, and xiyǒ denotes the manner of doing something. The Portuguese gloss begins a description of a well-executed way of acting.
[c1-l022 >] *com artificio, ou a goſto dos outros. ¶* V-
[c1-l022 note] Skill or artifice, and pleasing other people, complete the figurative explanation. The scan has a terminal V- starting the next example, not the OCR's isolated V followed by an italic full stop.
[c1-l023 >] ma vmaxǔ monouo yǔ fitogia. *He ho-*
[c1-l023 note] The example describes someone who says things agreeably or skilfully. Ma completes the preceding V-, and the Portuguese He ho- begins the corresponding description of the speaker.
[c1-l024 >] *mem que diz as couſas com graça, & arti-*
[c1-l024 note] Mem completes homem: a man who speaks with grace and artifice. The final arti- is physically divided and continues on the following row.
[c1-l025 >] *ficio, &c.*
[c1-l025 note] Ficio completes artificio and closes the description of the skilful speaker. The following derived forms belong to the same adjective but occupy their own row.
[c1-l026] Vmavmaxiſa. Vmavmaxǔ.
[c1-l026 note] Umaumashisa and umaumashuu give the noun and adverbial forms of the preceding adjective. The scan supports long ſ in Vmavmaxiſa, correcting OCR f; this is a morphological reading aid, not grounds for modernizing the spelling.
[c1-l027] Vmavoi. *Almocreue.*
[c1-l027 note] Umavoi is a carrier or driver of pack animals, matched by Portuguese Almocreue. The printed vo sequence is preserved in the historical spelling.
[c1-l028] Vmaya. *Eſtrebaria.*
[c1-l028 note] Umaya is a stable, directly glossed by Eſtrebaria. This short, complete entry is independent of the following station-name compound.
[c1-l029] Vmayagi. *Lugares, ou eſtancias, onde ha ca-*
[c1-l029 note] Umayagi names places or stations providing post horses. The visible initial in Lugares is L, not OCR D; ca- begins the word for horses on the next row.
[c1-l030 >] *ualos dapoſta pera correos q̃ paſſão depreſa.*
[c1-l030 note] The horses are for couriers travelling quickly. The phrase retains the printed dapoſta and abbreviation q̃ rather than expanding or respacing them.
[c1-l031] Vmayaſaburai. *Lugar nas caſas dos nobres*
[c1-l031 note] Umayasaburai denotes a named area in noble households. The Portuguese locates that area relative to the horse stable in the following two rows.
[c1-l032 >] *aßi chamado, que eſta logo junto, ou de fronte*
[c1-l032 note] The named area stands immediately beside or opposite the stable. The spatial expression de fronte continues into the next row; aßi is the printed historical form.
[c1-l033 >] *da eſtrebaria dos caualos.*
[c1-l033 note] The stable for the horses completes the location of the umayasaburai. It is the reference point of junto or de fronte above.
[c1-l034] Vmayoroi. *Armas, ou cubertas do cauallo*
[c1-l034 note] Umayoroi is horse armour or protective covering for a horse. The Portuguese distinguishes this equipment from an ordinary stable or saddle.
[c1-l035 >] *pera pelejar.*
[c1-l035 note] Pera pelejar gives the purpose of the horse covering: fighting. It completes the Umayoroi entry.
[c1-l036] Vmazacuri. *Couas, ou poças que nos caminhos*
[c1-l036 note] Umazacuri denotes hollows or puddle-like depressions left in roads. The definition continues with their formation by animals in mud.
[c1-l037 >] *de lama deixão as beſtas quando paßão.*
[c1-l037 note] These depressions are left by passing beasts on muddy roads. This completes the hoof-impression sense rather than describing deliberate excavation.
[c1-l038] Vmazoroye. *O aiuntar, ou fazer gente de ca-*
[c1-l038 note] Umazoroye is gathering or mustering mounted troops. The phrase gente de ca- continues with cauallo on the next row.
[c1-l039 >] *uallo: pouco vſado.*
[c1-l039 note] Vallo completes cauallo, and pouco vſado marks the expression as little used. The usage qualification belongs to the whole preceding entry.
[c1-l040] Vmazume. *Molher eſteril, ou que não pare.*
[c1-l040 note] Umazume is a woman who does not bear children, glossed as sterile. The printed historical wording and spelling are preserved without changing the definition.
[c1-l041] Vmazuqi. *Peßoa inclinada a cauallos.*
[c1-l041 note] Umazuqi means a person fond of horses. The scan supports Vmazuqi and inclinada, correcting the OCR intrusions in Vmnazuqi and inclixada; the opening is abraded but the remaining letter sequence and horse gloss agree. [c1-l042] Vmazzure. *Iunta de caualos que andão no cã-*
[c1-l042 note] Umazzure first denotes a group of horses roaming in the countryside. The nasal abbreviation in cã- is preserved across its physical division.
[c1-l043 >] *po. ¶ Item permet. Companhia de gente*
[c1-l043 note] Po completes the countryside reference, then a figurative use introduces a company of people. Their idle character is specified on the following row.
[c1-l044 >] *vadia, & ocioſa.*
[c1-l044 note] Vadia and ocioſa describe the company as wandering and idle. This completes the figurative extension of the horse-group expression.
[c1-l045] Vme. *Certa fruita como ameixias, ou a-*
[c1-l045 note] Ume names a fruit compared with plums, and then its tree. The scan reads ameixias, with a final s rather than OCR r; the tree name starts a- at the right edge.
[c1-l046 >] *meixieira. ¶* Vmenoqi. *Eſta ameixieira.*
[c1-l046 note] Meixieira completes ameixieira, followed by Umenoqi for this tree. Both the fruit and tree senses remain within the printed explanation.
[c1-l047] Vme, ru, eta. *Entulhar. Vt,* Foriuo v-
[c1-l047 note] Ume is the verb for filling in or filling up, glossed Entulhar. The headword has no j: the OCR Vjme arose from the damaged initial area. Foriuo v- begins the example of filling a ditch, continued at the top of column 2.

[c2-l004 note] Temperar a quẽte specifies moderating the hot water with the cold addition. The tilde in quẽte remains an unexpanded abbreviation.
[c2-l005] Vmeague, uru, eta. *Entulhar ate cima.*
[c2-l005 note] Umeague means filling something up to the top. The following Heike quotation illustrates filling an entire valley.
[c2-l006 >] *¶* Tani fitotçuuo Feiqeno ninju xichiman
[c2-l006 note] The Japanese quotation begins with one valley and the Heike force of seventy thousand. Xichiman carries the numerical meaning; the excess above that total is supplied by amari below.
[c2-l007 >] amariuo motte vmeague. Feiq. *Lib.*
[c2-l007 note] Amariuo motte vmeague completes the description of filling with more than the stated number. Feiq. is a citation label, and italic Lib. introduces the book number on the next row.
[c2-l008 >] *3. Enchendo, & entulhãdo ate cima hum*
[c2-l008 note] Book 3 is cited, followed by a Portuguese rendering of filling right to the top. The translation's object, one valley, continues into the next line.
[c2-l009 >] *valle com mais de ſetenta milhomens mortos da*
[c2-l009 note] The valley is filled with more than seventy thousand dead men. The tight printed milhomens is retained rather than separated according to modern word spacing.
[c2-l010 >] *parte do Feique.*
[c2-l010 note] Parte do Feique identifies the dead as belonging to the Heike side. This closes the quoted illustration of filling the valley. Feique remains italic as printed, while it is recorded as an embedded Japanese name for optional typeface review.
[c2-l011] Vmeauaxe, uru, eta. *Miſturar, & tempe-*
[c2-l011 note] Umeauaxe means mixing or tempering one thing with another. The scan has e in Vmeauaxe, not the OCR's Vmcauaxe; its printed principal forms remain unchanged.
[c2-l012 >] *rar hũa couſa com outra como vinho com a-*
[c2-l012 note] The definition describes tempering one substance with another, exemplified by wine and water. The final a- starts agoa across the physical line break.
[c2-l013 >] *goa, &c.*
[c2-l013 note] Goa completes agoa and closes the mixing example. The small continuation belongs wholly to Umeauaxe.
[c2-l014] Vmeboxi. *Certas Ameixias ſalgadas, & ſe-*
[c2-l014 note] Umeboxi denotes salted plums, with the drying process described below. The printed Portuguese capital in Ameixias is preserved.
[c2-l015 >] *cas ao ſol.*
[c2-l015 note] Cas completes secas: the plums are dried in the sun. This process explains the compound umeboshi without changing the historical headword spelling.
[c2-l016] Vmecuſa. *Entulho, ou couſa cõ q̃ entulhão as*
[c2-l016 note] Umecusa names fill or material used to fill excavations. The Portuguese abbreviations cõ and q̃ remain unexpanded; the object as cauas continues below.
[c2-l017 >] *cauas. ¶ Item, permet. Soldados, ou gente*
[c2-l017 note] Cauas completes the literal filling material definition, then a figurative sense introduces soldiers or people. The military application is explained in the next two rows.
[c2-l018 >] *que ſe manda diante como entulho, porque logo*
[c2-l018 note] These people are sent ahead as expendable filling, because they are expected to fall immediately. The imagery connects the military use directly to the literal entulho.
[c2-l019 >] *ande ſer mortos, ou ficar no campo.*
[c2-l019 note] The soldiers are to be killed or left on the field. The printed ande is retained, without supplying letters or modernizing it to a contemporary Portuguese construction.
[c2-l020] Vmenoqi. *Vide Vme.*
[c2-l020 note] Umenoqi cross-refers to Ume, the fruit-tree entry near the end of column 1. The referenced Japanese word Vme is printed within the italic cross-reference apparatus.
[c2-l021] Vmeqi, qu, eita. *Gemer.*
[c2-l021 note] Umeqi denotes groaning, directly glossed by Gemer. The printed qu and eita are abbreviated principal forms and remain unexpanded; the derived kana hint is not diplomatic text.
[c2-l022] Vmezome. *O tingir com pao que he como de*
[c2-l022 note] Umezome is dyeing with wood likened to plum wood. The preparation of that material is specified in the following row.
[c2-l023 >] *ameixieira cozido, ou feruido nagoa.*
[c2-l023 note] The wood is cooked or boiled in water to make the dye. The printed nagoa contraction and the paired cozido or feruido are retained.
[c2-l024] Vmezu. *Salmoura de certa fruita como amei-*

[c2-l028] Vmezzuqe. *Ameixias ſalgadas, ou de cõſerua.*
