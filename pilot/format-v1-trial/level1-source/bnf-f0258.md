[c1-l001] Icanimo. *Muito.*
[c1-l001 note] Ikanimo is the emphatic adverb glossed simply as 'very' or 'much'; the entry is not an interrogative despite the following related forms.
[c1-l002] Icanito xite. *Adu. De q̃ maneira, ou como.*
[c1-l002 note] Ikanito shite asks in what manner or how. The Portuguese q̃ is an abbreviation in the print and is retained rather than expanded to que.
[c1-l003] Icanixitemo. *De qualquer maneira que ſeja.*
[c1-l003 note] Ikanishitemo is glossed as 'in whatever manner it may be'; the next example tests the construction with a negative predicate.
[c1-l004 >] *¶* Icanixitemo naranu. *Por nenhũa via*
[c1-l004 note] Ikanishitemo naranu means that something cannot be done by any means. The Portuguese negative formulation continues into pode ser on the following row.
[c1-l005 >] *pode ſer.*
[c1-l005 note] Pode ser completes 'by no means can it be'; this is the short Portuguese continuation of the preceding negative example, not an independent entry.
[c1-l006] Icaqe, uru. *Tirar com frecha, ou eſpingar-*
[c1-l006 note] Ikake is firing at something with an arrow or firearm. The scan has c in Icaqe, not OCR e, and a line-end hyphen dividing eſpingar-da; the historical frecha is retained.
[c1-l007 >] *da, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] Da completes eſpingarda, the firearm paired with an arrow in the preceding row. The ampersand and c continue the printed 'etc.' formula.
[c1-l008] Icaqe, uru. *Refazer, ou ſoldar algum va-*
[c1-l008 note] This second ikake entry concerns repairing a metal vessel by casting or soldering, not shooting. The final va- belongs to vaſo on the next row.
[c1-l009 >] *ſo de metal quebrado fundindo aquella parte*
[c1-l009 note] The broken metal vessel is repaired by melting or casting the missing part; the relative clause continues into que falta below. No modern technical term is substituted.
[c1-l010 >] *que falta.*
[c1-l010 note] Que falta specifies the part that is missing and completes the repair definition; its brevity does not change its status as a physical body row.
[c1-l011] Icaracaxi, ſu, aita. *Enteſar algũa parte do*
[c1-l011 note] Ikarakashi, ikarakasu, ikarakaita describes stiffening or setting a body part forcefully. The initial is an upright capital I; aita is retained as the historical principal part rather than modernized.
[c1-l012 >] *corpo com brio, ou ira. Vt,* Catauo icara-
[c1-l012 note] The gloss specifies pride or anger, and kata wo ikarakasu begins the example of squaring or stiffening the shoulders. The divided icara- continues in the next row.
[c1-l013 >] caſu. *Enteſar os hombros. i, Moſtrarſe ſo-*
[c1-l013 note] Caſu completes ikarakasu. The Portuguese explains both stiffening the shoulders and displaying pride; the final ſo- continues as ſoberbo.
[c1-l014 >] *berbo. ¶* Figiuo icaracaſu. *Idẽ. ¶* Me-
[c1-l014 note] Berbo completes ſoberbo. Hiji wo ikarakasu gives the elbow counterpart and Idẽ refers back to the same meaning; Me- begins the following eye example.
[c1-l015 >] uo icaracaſu. *Regalar os olhos.*
[c1-l015 note] Me- plus uo gives the eye as object in me wo ikarakasu. Regalar os olhos here means opening or staring with the eyes, not giving a gift.
[c1-l016] Icari. *Ancora. ¶* Icariuo vtçu, l, vo-
[c1-l016 note] Ikari is an anchor. Ikari wo utsu and the divided alternative vorosu introduce dropping or lowering the anchor; the l is the alternative marker, not part of the Japanese.
[c1-l017 >] roſu. *Ancorar. ¶* Icariuo vocoſu, l,
[c1-l017 note] Roſu completes vorosu, lowering the anchor. The new example ikari wo vokosu, alternatively toru, concerns weighing it rather than dropping it.
[c1-l018 >] toru. *Leuar ancora.*
[c1-l018 note] Toru completes the alternative verb in the anchor example; leuar ancora means weighing or lifting the anchor, retaining the historical u/v spelling.
[c1-l019] Icari, u, atta. *Agastarſe.*
[c1-l019 note] Ikari, ikaru, ikatta is becoming angry. Agastarſe supplies that meaning; the r is not added to the abbreviated u principal part in the canonical text.
[c1-l020] Icari. *Ira. ¶* Icariuo voſayuru, l, na-
[c1-l020 note] This ikari is anger, distinct from the preceding anchor noun. The example introduces restraining or soothing anger, with na- continued as nadamuru below.
[c1-l021 >] damuru, xizzumuru. *Reprimir, ou apla*
[c1-l021 note] Nadamuru and shizumuru complete the alternatives for calming anger. The Portuguese apla continues into aplacar without a securely printed terminal hyphen, so none is supplied.
[c1-l022 >] *car a ira. ¶* Icariuo vocoſu. *Agaſtarſe.*
[c1-l022 note] Car completes aplacar a ira. Ikari wo vokosu then illustrates arousing anger or becoming angry, as the repeated Agaſtarſe confirms.
[c1-l023] Icaricuji. *Ancoragem.*
[c1-l023 note] Ikarikuji is the anchorage term in this cluster; the initial capital I beside the rule was omitted by OCR. The scan's Ancoragem is kept without selecting a narrower modern nautical sense.
[c1-l024] Icarigoye. *Voz de ira, ou agastamento.*
[c1-l024 note] Ikarigoe is an angry voice. The worn headword is read Icarigoye from its surviving initial and medial strokes together with the explicit anger gloss, not OCR Acaigoye; the medial r is faint.
[c1-l025] Icarimanaco. *Olhos irados.*
[c1-l025 note] Ikarimanako denotes angry eyes, directly matched by olhos irados. It is a separate compound headword, not a continuation of the voice example.
[c1-l026] Icarizzura. *Rosto, ou ſembrante agastado.*
[c1-l026 note] Ikarizura is an angry face or expression. The scan supports a capital I and the doubled zz; the Portuguese retains its printed historical forms rather than being modernized.
[c1-l027] Icaruga. *Paßarinho aßi chamado.*
[c1-l027 note] Ikaruga is described only as a small bird of that name. The capital I and the damaged letters of Paßarinho are recovered from the scan and bird context; no species identification is inserted.
[c1-l028] Icaſama. *Adu. Poruentura.*
[c1-l028 note] Ikasama is labelled an adverb and glossed poruentura, 'perhaps' or 'perchance'. This historical sense should not be replaced by the modern cheating sense.
[c1-l029] Icaſamanimo. *Adu. Idem.*
[c1-l029 note] Ikasamanimo receives the same adverbial meaning through Idem. The longer form is a genuine separate entry, with the repeated adverb label.
[c1-l030] Icata. *Forma de pilouros, veronicas, &c.*
[c1-l030 note] Ikata is a mould for cast objects, with projectiles and devotional images among the examples in the Portuguese list. The printed pilouros and veronicas remain unchanged.
[c1-l031] Icatei. *De qualquer maneira.*
[c1-l031 note] Ikatei is glossed 'in whatever manner'. It leads into the attributive form ikateino on the next row rather than forming one headword with it.
[c1-l032] Icateino. *Qualquer. Vt,* Icateino coto
[c1-l032 note] Ikateino means 'whatever' or 'any'; ikateino koto begins the example that is completed by naritomo below.
[c1-l033 >] naritomo. *Quelquer couſa que ſeja.*
[c1-l033 note] Naritomo completes ikateino koto naritomo, 'whatever the thing may be'. The scan supports the unusual printed Quelquer, which is preserved rather than normalized to Qualquer.
[c1-l034] Icayexi, u, eita. *Tornar a tirar, ou reſponder*
[c1-l034 note] Ikaeshi is shooting back or returning fire. The scan supports eita, not OCR cita, as the abbreviated past form; the definition continues with the projectile or weapon below.
[c1-l035 >] *com tiro de frecha, eſpingarda, &c.*
[c1-l035 note] The response is made with an arrow or firearm shot. Frecha and eſpingarda retain the source's historical spellings and complete the verb definition.
[c1-l036] Icayǒna. *Qualquer, ou de qualquer maneira.*
[c1-l036 note] Ikayoona is the adjectival 'whatever' or 'of whatever kind'. The visible caron distinguishes this printed long-o notation from a circumflex.
[c1-l037] Icayǒni. *Adu. Em que maneira, ou em*
[c1-l037 note] Ikayooni is an adverb meaning 'in what manner' or 'in whatever manner'. The right edge shows em without a terminal hyphen; the preposition continues syntactically, not as a divided word.
[c1-l038 >] *qualquer maneira.*
[c1-l038 note] Qualquer maneira completes the second paraphrase of ikayooni. The scan's e in maneira corrects OCR mancira, without changing the word's historical spelling.
[c1-l039] Icayǒnimo. *Adu. Em qualquer maneira*
[c1-l039 note] Ikayoonimo is glossed 'in whatever manner'; the concessive or indefinite force is completed by que seja on the next physical row.
[c1-l040 >] *que ſeja.*
[c1-l040 note] Que ſeja completes 'whatever manner it may be'. It belongs to the preceding adverbial entry and is not a separately translated Japanese fragment.
[c1-l041] Icayǒni naritomo. *Adu. Idem.*
[c1-l041 note] Ikayooni naritomo is assigned the same adverbial sense by Idem. The phrase remains spaced as printed rather than joined to match neighbouring forms.
[c1-l042] Icayǒno. *Qualquer. Vt,* Icayǒno fi-
[c1-l042 note] Ikayoono is attributive 'whatever'. The example begins ikayoono fi- and continues with ma, making fima the Japanese word for time or opportunity here.
[c1-l043 >] ma irutomo, &c. Mon. *Qualquer oc-*
[c1-l043 note] The example concludes fima irutomo, followed by the upright citation label Mon. The Portuguese says 'whatever occupation'; the printed Qualquer is one word, and oc- continues below.
[c1-l044 >] *cupação que tenhais.*
[c1-l044 note] Cupação completes occupação, and que tenhais addresses the reader's occupation or business. The translation remains literal to the dictionary even where it is not a word-for-word rendering of the Japanese.
[c1-l045] Icazzuchi. *Trouão, ou coriſco.*
[c1-l045 note] Ikazuchi is thunder or a thunderbolt, as trouão and coriſco indicate. The doubled zz is part of the Jesuit spelling and is not simplified.
[c1-l046] *Algũs vocabulos ſe deixarão aqui que parecem de*
[c1-l046 note] An editorial explanation begins: some words resembling counting expressions are left here. The scan has ſe rather than OCR ſo; this is body text, not a running header.
[c1-l047 >] *contas, mas não o ſão, por que ſe não vſão cõ-*
[c1-l047 note] The explanation says these are not ordinary counting forms and are not commonly used beyond the listed expressions. The thin medial long s gives vſão, not OCR vão; cõ- continues at the top of column 2.
