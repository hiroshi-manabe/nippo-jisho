[c1-l001 >] *tirſe cedo, ou de preßa. ¶* Tôcara deta.
[c1-l001 note] Tirſe completes Par- on the preceding page, describing departure early or in haste. Tôcara deta supplies a past-tense example of having set out promptly.
[c1-l002 >] *Ià hà pedaço que ſahio com preßa.*
[c1-l002 note] The Portuguese explains that he left hurriedly some time ago. Initial Ià means already; the following verb is the historical ſahio, not the OCR’s ſabio.
[c1-l003] Tô. *Particula, que ſe ajunta no cabo de outras*
[c1-l003 note] Tô is the suffix meaning and so forth or the like, appended to other expressions. Its worn initial is T, matching the local alphabetical sequence, rather than F.
[c1-l004 >] *palauras, como quẽ diz, &c. Eo de mais, ou*
[c1-l004 note] The definition equates the suffix with et cetera and the rest. The printed Eo is retained without silently regularizing its spacing.
[c1-l005 >] *outras couſas. Vt,* Goyôtô vôxe tçuqe-
[c1-l005 note] Goyôtô vôxe tçuqe- begins a deferential request to be assigned any business or other matters. The Japanese imperative is completed by rarei on the following row.
[c1-l006 >] rarei. *Mandaime, que farei tudo o que vos*
[c1-l006 note] Rarei completes the request to give an order; the Portuguese says to command the speaker, who will do whatever is needed.
[c1-l007 >] *comprir, & o de mais.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes the promised service: whatever is required, and the rest. Comprir preserves the historical vowel of the Portuguese verb.
[c1-l008] Tô. *Rota, ou canas de Bengala. ¶* Tô-
[c1-l008 note] This separate Tô denotes rattan or Bengal cane. The example repeats Tô at the physical line end; it is unrelated to the preceding suffix.
[c1-l009 >] no tçuye. *Bordão de cana de Bengala.*
[c1-l009 note] Tô no tçuye is a cane staff or walking stick. The gloss explicitly specifies Bengal cane.
[c1-l010] Tô. i, Atama. *Cabeça. mas não ſe vſa ſẽ al-*
[c1-l010 note] Tô is explained by atama, head, and is said to occur in compounds. The faint initial T and the headword’s flush-left position are restored from the scan.
[c1-l011 >] *gũa compoſição. Vt,* Gitô, *&c. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l011 note] Gitô illustrates the compound use of the word for head; the next paragraph marker introduces an additional sense, continued below.
[c1-l012 >] *Codea da buſtella, ou ſarna.*
[c1-l012 note] The additional meaning is the crust of a scab or skin lesion. Buſtella is retained as the printed historical form.
[c1-l013] Tǒ. *Hũa piramide alta, ou torre de madeira.*
[c1-l013 note] Tǒ denotes a tall wooden tower or pagoda, described as a high pyramid. The caron is distinct from the circumflex in the preceding Tô entries.
[c1-l014] Tǒ. *Aue aßi chamada de cor vermelha.*
[c1-l014 note] This homograph is described simply as a red bird of that name; the wording does not justify imposing a modern species identification.
[c1-l015] Tǒ. *Grelo dalface, moſtarda, &c. Vt,* Tǒ-
[c1-l015 note] Tǒ denotes the flower stalk or shoot of lettuce, mustard, and similar plants. The damaged middle of moſtarda contains the long s missing from OCR.
[c1-l016 >] ga tatçu. *Crecer o grelo, ou gomo das eruas.*
[c1-l016 note] Tǒ ga tatçu means the stalk or shoot rising; the Portuguese describes the growth of the plant’s shoot or bud.
[c1-l017 >] *¶* Qirino tǒ. *Gomo, ou flor deſta aruo-*
[c1-l017 note] Qiri no tǒ illustrates the bud or flower of the kiri tree. The headword’s repeated r in Qiri below confirms the r-shaped letter misread as t in Qitino.
[c1-l018 >] *re Qiri.*
[c1-l018 note] Re completes aruore; italic Qiri is the tree name embedded in the Portuguese explanation, not a fresh headword.
[c1-l019] To. *Conjunção copulatiua: &, ou com.*
[c1-l019 note] To is the connective translated as and or with. The Portuguese grammatical label is conjunction; the scan prints a colon after copulatiua.
[c1-l020 >] *¶ Item, Adu. Vt,* Maittato mǒſu.
[c1-l020 note] The entry next gives the quotative use of to, classified here as Adu. Maittato mǒſu means reporting that someone has come.
[c1-l021 >] *Dizem que veo.*
[c1-l021 note] Dizem que veo translates the preceding quotation: they say that he came. Veo is retained as the historical form.
[c1-l022] Toate. *O aualiarſe, ou venderſe o arroz, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Toate denotes valuation or sale of rice at ten measures for a stated price. The gloss begins the explanation continued through the next rows.
[c1-l023 >] *a dez medidas por algum certo preço. Vt,*
[c1-l023 note] Specifies ten measures for some fixed price and introduces the example with italic Vt.
[c1-l024 >] Comega toate ſuru. *Valer o arroz a dez*
[c1-l024 note] Comega toate ſuru states that the rice is valued at the rate described. The Portuguese phrase ends at dez and continues with medidas below.
[c1-l025 >] *medidas por hũ maz, ou por algũ certo preço.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes the price example as ten measures for one maz or another fixed price. The historical currency name and abbreviation tildes are preserved.
[c1-l026] Tǒba. i, Tǒ. *Certa torre alta de madeira.*
[c1-l026 note] Tǒba is equated with Tǒ and defined as a tall wooden tower, continuing the pagoda sense encountered earlier.
[c1-l027] Tǒbachi. *Meliùs,* Tǒbat. Ataru bachi. *Caſ-*
[c1-l027 note] Tǒbachi is referred to the preferred form Tǒbat and paraphrased by ataru bachi: punishment that befalls someone. The first caron is worn, but its surviving branch and the clear Tǒbachi cross-reference below favour this reading; the subsequently consulted NINJAL headword index (202510) agrees. Meliùs is the printed preference label.
[c1-l028 >] *tigo, ou juſtiça que vem ſobre alguem.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes caſtigo and adds justice coming upon a person, clarifying the punitive sense of bachi.
[c1-l029] Tobai, bǒ. *Arremeter o falcão, ou fazer*
[c1-l029 note] Tobai, with principal form bǒ, concerns a falcon launching itself or trying to break free. The following row completes the action.
[c1-l030 >] *porſe ſoltar, & voar.*
[c1-l030 note] Porſe ſoltar and voar describe freeing itself and flying; the phrase remains attached to the falcon entry.
[c1-l031] Tobai cacari, u. *Idem.*
[c1-l031 note] Tobai cacari is given the same sense as the preceding falcon expression. The abbreviated principal form u is retained as printed.
[c1-l032] Tǒban. Ataru ban. *Vigia que toca, ou per-*
[c1-l032 note] Tǒban is a turn of guard duty, paraphrased by ataru ban, the watch that falls to someone. The gloss continues with pertence below.
[c1-l033 >] *tence a alguem. Vt,* Tǒbande gozaru.
[c1-l033 note] Completes the sense of a watch belonging to a person; Tǒbande gozaru means that it is one’s turn on duty. The small mark above o is faint but has a downward-pointing shape, supporting the chosen caron; it is less clear than the headword’s mark above.
[c1-l034] Tǒbari. *Agulha da China.*
[c1-l034 note] Tǒbari is defined as a Chinese needle. The downward mark over o is a caron, unlike the OCR circumflex.
[c1-l035] Tobari. *O porſe atraueſado na porta pera não*
[c1-l035 note] Tobari here means standing across a doorway so as to prevent passage. It is distinct from the preceding long-vowel needle entry.
[c1-l036 >] *deixar paßar a alguem. Vt,* Tobariuo ſuru.
[c1-l036 note] Completes the obstruction of someone’s passage and gives Tobariuo ſuru as the expression for doing so.
[c1-l037] Tǒbat. *Vide,* Tǒbachi.
[c1-l037 note] Tǒbat refers back to Tǒbachi, the punishment entry. The downward marks, particularly the clear one in the cross-reference, support carons rather than the OCR’s first circumflex.
[c1-l038] Tobatçuqi, qu, ita. l, Tofatçuqu. *Ser,*
[c1-l038 note] Tobatçuqi and the alternative Tofatçuqu concern restless or bustling activity. The Japanese f in the alternative is not the long s of a Portuguese word.
[c1-l039 >] *ou andarinquieto, ou com eſtrondo, & traqui-*
[c1-l039 note] The gloss describes being restless, noisy, and engaged in commotion. Traqui- continues as traquinada on the next physical row.
[c1-l040 >] *nada. ¶* Tobatçuqu. *l*, Tobatçuita mo-
[c1-l040 note] Completes traquinada and begins adjectival examples with Tobatçuqu or Tobatçuita modifying mono across the line break.
[c1-l041 >] no. *Homem deſaſoſegado, ou atabalhoado*
[c1-l041 note] No completes mono: a restless or flustered person. The Portuguese qualification continues below.
[c1-l042 >] *em fazer as couſas.*
[c1-l042 note] Em fazer as couſas limits the preceding description to the way the person performs tasks, completing the entry.
[c1-l043] Tobera. *Aruore aßi chamada.*
[c1-l043 note] Tobera is identified simply as a tree of that name. The brief gloss does not itself provide a botanical classification.
[c1-l044] Tobet. *Quantidade, ou porção que ſe toma por*
[c1-l044 note] Tobet denotes a quantity or portion taken for each ten measures, with the measure context supplied in the following row.
[c1-l045 >] *cada dez medidas que ſe medẽ, ou dão darroz.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes the assessment on each ten measures of rice measured or distributed. The damaged d of medidas corrects the OCR’s spurious internal space.
[c1-l046] Tobi. *Minhoto.*
[c1-l046 note] Tobi is the bird glossed as minhoto, a kite. This noun is separate from the verb beginning on the next line.
[c1-l047] Tobi, bu, ôda. *Voar. ¶ Item, Saltar.*
[c1-l047 note] Tobi, bu, ôda gives the principal forms of the verb for flying, with a second sense of jumping. The scan supplies the missing i and the accented ôda; the historical principal part is not modernized.

[c2-l001] Tobiagari, ru, atta. *Voar alto, ou pera cima.*
[c2-l001 note] Tobiagari denotes flying high or upward. The row closes with a period after cima, not the OCR’s line-end hyphen.
[c2-l002] Tobiariqi, qu, uita. *Andar voando, ou*
[c2-l002 note] Tobiariqi describes going about by flying or hopping. The printed principal part uita is retained as historical morphology.
[c2-l003 >] *ſaltando como coruos. ¶ Item, per met.*
[c2-l003 note] The comparison is to crows hopping or flying about; the next clause explicitly begins a figurative application.
[c2-l004 >] *O andar como ſaltando, ou inquieto como mi-*
[c2-l004 note] The figurative sense is restless or hopping movement, with the comparison to children completed below.
[c2-l005 >] *ninos traueſos, &c.*
[c2-l005 note] Ninos completes mininos, mischievous children, illustrating the restless behaviour described in the preceding rows.
[c2-l006] Tobicacari, ru, atta. *Voando dar ſobre al-*
[c2-l006 note] Tobicacari means flying or leaping at something. The Portuguese begins an explanation of striking down upon an object.
[c2-l007 >] *gũa couſa como minhoto, &c. mas mais*
[c2-l007 note] A kite is the initial comparison; mais begins the qualification that this expression is used more properly in another context.
[c2-l008 >] *propriamente ſe diz de homem, ou animal q̃*
[c2-l008 note] The preferred context is a person or animal attacking another. The final q̃ is a printed abbreviation and is not expanded.
[c2-l009 >] *dà ſobre outro, ou arremete com furia.*
[c2-l009 note] Completes the description as falling upon another or charging furiously, explaining the forceful sense of tobicacari.
[c2-l010] Tobicaqeri, ru, ta. *Voar. Vt,* Sorauo
[c2-l010 note] Tobicaqeri means flying through the air; Sorauo begins an example with the sky or air as its object. The abbreviated principal form ta is preserved as printed.
[c2-l011 >] tobicaqeru tori. *Paßaros que voão pello ar.*
[c2-l011 note] Tobicaqeru tori completes the phrase for birds flying through the sky; the Portuguese translates it directly as birds flying through the air.
[c2-l012] Tobicayeri, ru, etta. *Voando tornar. Item,*
[c2-l012 note] Tobicayeri first means returning in flight. A further sense of rebounding or springing back follows.
[c2-l013 >] *Tornar, & ſaltar pera tras como ſeta que dà*
[c2-l013 note] Explains returning or jumping backward like an arrow that strikes something. The object of dà continues below.
[c2-l014 >] *nalgũa pedra.*
[c2-l014 note] Nalgũa pedra completes the arrow example: striking a stone and rebounding. It is a separate physical continuation row.
[c2-l015] Tobichiri, ru, itta. *Voar eſpalhandoſe por di-*
[c2-l015 note] Tobichiri means scattering in flight. The Portuguese word diuerſas is physically divided after di-.
[c2-l016 >] *uerſas partes. ¶ Item, Saltar, ou bulir*
[c2-l016 note] Completes the scattering into various directions, then adds the sense of jumping or moving about a great deal.
[c2-l017 >] *muito de cà pera là.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes the restless-motion sense as moving a great deal from here to there. The grave accents in cà and là are retained.
[c2-l018] Tobicoye, uru, eta. *Paſſar de voo, como*
[c2-l018 note] Tobicoye is passing over something in flight, with the obstacle examples supplied on the next row.
[c2-l019 >] *por cima dalgũs montes, aruores, &c.*
[c2-l019 note] Mountains and trees illustrate what is crossed or flown over. The abbreviated plural dalgũs remains unexpanded.
[c2-l020] Tobicudari, ru, atta. *Decer voando, ou*
[c2-l020 note] Tobicudari denotes descending by flying or, as completed below, jumping down. Decer preserves the historical Portuguese spelling.
[c2-l021 >] *ſaltando.*
[c2-l021 note] Saltando supplies the alternative manner of descent, jumping, and completes the preceding definition.
[c2-l022] Tobifaxiri, u, itta. *Correr a toda preßa, ou*
[c2-l022 note] Tobifaxiri is running at full speed. The gloss continues with the combination of jumping and running below.
[c2-l023 >] *ſaltar, & correr.*
[c2-l023 note] Saltar and correr complete the vigorous-motion sense of the preceding verb; the short continuation is not a new entry.
[c2-l024] Tobifi. *Faiſca de fogo que ſalta.*
[c2-l024 note] Tobifi is a flying spark of fire. Here fi means fire, and the Portuguese describes a spark that jumps.
[c2-l025] Tobiiuo. l, tobivuo. *Peixe voador.*
[c2-l025 note] Tobiiuo and tobivuo are alternative names for the flying fish. The printed internal i/u/v spellings are retained independently.
[c2-l026] Tobimeguri, ru, utta. *Andar às voltas*
[c2-l026 note] Tobimeguri describes moving in circles, specifically in flight as completed by the following word.
[c2-l027 >] *voando.*
[c2-l027 note] Voando completes the circling-flight definition, remaining a separate physical row.
[c2-l028] Tobimuxi. *Hum bichinho aßi chamado que ſal-*
[c2-l028 note] Tobimuxi is a small insect described through its jumping movement. The definition does not require identification beyond the named insect.
[c2-l029 >] *ta como pulga.*
[c2-l029 note] Ta completes ſalta, and the comparison is to a flea. This is a description of motion, not an assertion that the insect is itself a flea.
[c2-l030] Tǒbin. *Hũs bules, ou fraſcos de metal que ſer-*
[c2-l030 note] Tǒbin names metal pots or flasks; their purpose is specified on the next line. The marked o is preserved as a caron.
[c2-l031 >] *uem de ter agoa quente.*
[c2-l031 note] Completes the use of the vessels for holding hot water. Agoa, visible in the crop, corrects OCR agon without modernizing to agua.
[c2-l032] Tobinoqi, qu, oita. *Voando, ou ſaltando a-*
[c2-l032 note] Tobinoqi describes moving away by flying or jumping. The principal part oita supports the -noqi stem; the abraded round vowel is o, not OCR e.
[c2-l033 >] *fastarſe.*
[c2-l033 note] Fastarſe completes afastarſe across the line boundary, explaining movement away from a place or object.
[c2-l034] Tǒbiǒ. Saxiataru yamai. *Doença preſente,*
[c2-l034 note] Tǒbiǒ is paraphrased as saxiataru yamai, the illness currently affecting someone. Both marked o vowels retain their printed carons.
[c2-l035 >] *ou dagora.*
[c2-l035 note] Ou dagora restates the sense as the present or current illness. It is the completion of the preceding gloss.
[c2-l036] Tobiǒxi. *Sestros que tangem certas molheres*
[c2-l036 note] Tobiǒxi denotes rattling ritual instruments, glossed as sestros, which certain women play. The description of these performers follows.
[c2-l037 >] *chamadas,* Mico*, diante do Cami. ¶* To-
[c2-l037 note] The women are called Mico and play before the Cami. Both Japanese terms occur inside the Portuguese explanation; To- begins the separate example at the right edge.
[c2-l038 >] biǒxiuo auaſuru. *Tanger, outocar estes*
[c2-l038 note] Biǒxiuo auaſuru completes the example for playing these instruments; the Portuguese begins its equivalent with tanger and tocar.
[c2-l039 >] *ſestros.*
[c2-l039 note] Sestros names the instruments again and completes the translation. Its visible indentation is restored; it is a short continuation, not a new headword.
[c2-l040] Tobira. *Porta de couceira.*
[c2-l040 note] Tobira is a hinged or pivoted door, described by Portuguese porta de couceira.
[c2-l041] Tobiſagari, ru, atta. *Decer voando.*
[c2-l041 note] Tobiſagari means descending in flight, parallel to the earlier tobicudari entry.
[c2-l042] Tobiſari, ru, atta. *Aleuantarſe o paſſaro,*
[c2-l042 note] Tobiſari describes a bird rising or taking flight. The Portuguese explanation continues on the next line.
[c2-l043 >] *ou acolherſe voando.*
[c2-l043 note] Ou acolherſe voando adds the sense of retreating or making away in flight.
[c2-l044] Tobitachi, tçu, atta. *Aleuantarſe voando.*
[c2-l044 note] Tobitachi is rising into flight. The entry then develops a figurative use referring to the heart.
[c2-l045 >] *¶ Per met.* Cocoro tobitatçu. *Alegrar-*
[c2-l045 note] Cocoro tobitatçu describes the heart leaping with joy; the Portuguese begins alegrar-se, rejoicing. The damaged r in Alegrar- corrects OCR Alegiar-.
[c2-l046 >] *ſe muito, ou ſaltar de prazer.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes alegrar-se as being very joyful or jumping for pleasure. The second expression makes the figurative connection to flight and jumping explicit.
[c2-l047] Tobivatari, ru,atta. *Voando paßar a algũ lugar.*
[c2-l047 note] Tobivatari means passing to another place in flight. The printed v in vatari represents wa; OCR y would obscure the verb wataru.
