[c1-l007] Vôame. *Grande chuua.*
[c1-l007 note] Ōame denotes heavy rain, literally great rain. The headword has a visible circumflex, and chuua preserves the printed historical u/v spelling.
[c1-l008] Voba. *Tia. Em algũas partes do Ximo,* Vbaqi.
[c1-l008 note] Oba means aunt; Ubaqi is given as a regional form in parts of Ximo. The printed Vbaqi is retained without inserting an expected o, and Ximo occurs within the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l009] Vobaco. *Hũa erua que ſerue pera mezinha.*
[c1-l009 note] Obako is described as an herb used medicinally. The gloss supplies the practical use without requiring a more precise botanical identification, and no long-vowel mark is added to the headword.
[c1-l010] Vôbacu. *Ceuada.*
[c1-l010 note] Ōbaku is equated with barley. The short Portuguese gloss ceuada retains its historical consonantal u.
[c1-l011] Vobamuco. *Tio marido da tia.*
[c1-l011 note] Obamuko means an uncle by marriage, specifically the aunt's husband. The compound relation is stated explicitly in the Portuguese.
[c1-l012] Vǒban. *Hũa festa que ſe faz ao Dairi, ou a*
[c1-l012 note] Ōban names a feast or ceremonial offering for the Dairi or a lord named on the next row. Enlargement establishes the downward caron in Vǒban, unlike the circumflex in Vôame; Dairi is embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l013 >] *algum Yacata pello Xǒguachi leuando lhe toda*
[c1-l013 note] The offering is made to a yakata at the New Year, Xǒguachi, with various gifts. The o in Xǒguachi carries a caron rather than the OCR tilde; Yacata and Xǒguachi are Japanese terms within the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l014 >] *a maneira de caça, peſcado, &c.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes the feast's gifts: all kinds of game and fish. The visible a gives peſcado, correcting OCR peſcodo without modernizing the historical long s.
[c1-l015] Voban. *Hũas meſas altas com pees, em que*
[c1-l015 note] Oban here denotes tall tables or trays with feet. The scan reads altas, not OCR alias; this unaccented entry is distinct from the ceremonial Vǒban above. The supplied NINJAL index records Vôban with an editorially supplied vowel mark; no secure mark is established in this impression, so the unmarked baseline remains.
[c1-l016 >] *comem algums ſenhores.*
[c1-l016 note] Certain lords eat at these tables. The printed algums is preserved with its historical ending rather than changed to modern alguns.
[c1-l017] Vobana. *Hũa flor de certa erua alta dos cam-*
[c1-l017 note] Obana is the flower or plume of a tall field grass. The gloss is divided at cam-/pos and does not itself supply a botanical species name.
[c1-l018 >] *pos que ſerue como panha.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes campos and states that the grass serves like straw. The print appears to read panha, retained as a source anomaly rather than silently corrected to expected palha.
[c1-l019] Vobaqi. *Tia. Vide.* Voba.
[c1-l019 note] Obaki is another word for aunt, referred to oba. The two Japanese forms surround the Portuguese definition and cross-reference marker.
[c1-l020] Vobaqi muco. *Tio marido da propria tia: mas*
[c1-l020 note] Obaki muko denotes the husband of one's own aunt. The final mas introduces a qualification about the preferred expression on the next line.
[c1-l021 >] *o proprio he,* Vobamuco.
[c1-l021 note] The dictionary identifies obamuko as the proper term. Vobamuco therefore occurs inside the Portuguese metalinguistic explanation rather than beginning a new entry.
[c1-l022] Vobaxima. i, Rancan. *Balaustes, ou enco-*
[c1-l022 note] Obashima is equated with rankan, a balustrade or railing. The Portuguese second equivalent continues as enco-/stos, describing a protective or supporting rail.
[c1-l023 >] *ſtos de varanda, ponte, &c.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes encostos and gives balconies and bridges as locations for these railings. The inherited st/ſt expansion is retained under the project's deferred ligature policy.
[c1-l024] Vobi. *Cingidouro. ¶* Vobiuo toqu. *De-*
[c1-l024 note] Obi is a sash or girdle. Obi wo toku means undoing it, with the Portuguese desatar divided after De-.

[c1-l025 note] Completes desatar, undoing the ribbon or sash; the next example, obi wo suru, means putting it on. The printed afita spacing is retained.

[c1-l028 >] uo vtçu. *Fazer cingidouro como quem faz*

[c1-l029 >] *cordão.*

[c1-l030] Vobiuo voru. *Tecer cingidouro.*
[c1-l030 note] Obi wo oru is weaving a sash, distinct from the preceding braiding and cord-making examples. Initial v in voru represents the vowel of oru.
[c1-l031] Vobinauoxi. i, Fibo, l, fimo vo toxi. *O dei-*
[c1-l031 note] Obinaoshi is equated with hibo or himo otoshi, the change from children's fastening cords to an obi. The damaged o and the printed internal space in vo toxi are retained cautiously; the clause continues as deixar on the next row.

[c1-l032 note] Completes deixar and explains ceasing to fasten the sash behind, as children customarily do. The gloss describes a change in dress rather than literal repair of a belt.
[c1-l033 >] *os mininos ate os noue annos.*

[c1-l034] Vobicaxi, ſu, ita. *Eſpantar a alguem com*

[c1-l035 note] The frightening is done by cries or shouts. Tem de continues into cerco, describing besiegers rather than an unrelated obligation construction.
[c1-l036 >] *cerco algũa fortaleza pera inquietarem os que*

[c1-l038] Vobiqi, u, ijta. *Fazer vir, ou ſair os ini-*
