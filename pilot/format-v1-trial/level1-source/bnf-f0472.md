[c1-l001] Tanomizucunai. *Couſa de poucas eſperanças.*
[c1-l001 note] Tanomizucunai describes something offering little hope. The entry's example on the following lines applies the same lack of confidence to a person.

[c1-l002 note] The example Tanomizucunai fito describes a person in whom little confidence can be placed. The Portuguese relative clause continues on the next line.
[c1-l003 >] *ſe pode pouco confiar.*
[c1-l003 note] This completes the description of an unreliable person. It closes the example under Tanomizucunai rather than introducing another headword.
[c1-l004] Tanomo. i, Ta. *Varzeas.* P.
[c1-l004 note] Tanomo is equated with Ta, fields or flooded agricultural ground. The terminal roman P. marks poetic usage and is separate from the italic Portuguese definition.
[c1-l005] Tanomoxij. *Couſa em que ſe tem eſperança, ou*
[c1-l005 note] Tanomoxij concerns something in which one has hope. The alternative phrasing, having confidence, continues on the next physical line.
[c1-l006 >] *o ter confiança.*
[c1-l006 note] The second formulation describes having confidence and completes the main definition. The following indented forms are derivatives within the same entry.
[c1-l007 >] Tanomoxiſa.
[c1-l007 note] Tanomoxiſa is the related nominal form, printed alone without a separate Portuguese definition. Its indentation and roman type connect it to Tanomoxij.
[c1-l008 >] Tanomoxǔ. *Adu. ¶* Tanomoxǔ
[c1-l008 note] Tanomoxǔ is marked as an adverb. The pilcrow introduces an example beginning with the same form; its verb and explanation follow on the next line.
[c1-l009 >] vomô. *Ter eſperanças, ou confiar.*
[c1-l009 note] Vomô completes the example Tanomoxǔ vomô, to have hopes or trust. This line closes the adverbial example under Tanomoxij.
[c1-l010] Tanomu. *Vt,* Tanomuno tçuitachi. *Pri-*
[c1-l010 note] Tanomu is illustrated by Tanomuno tçuitachi. The Portuguese translation begins the first day, divided Pri- / meiro across physical lines.
[c1-l011 >] *meiro dia da oitaua lũa.*
[c1-l011 note] The expression denotes the first day of the eighth lunar month. The nasal mark in lũa and the historical calendrical wording are retained.
[c1-l012] Tanôna. *Vide.* Tanô.
[c1-l012 note] Tanôna is a cross-reference to Tanô, the earlier entry about many accomplishments. Its circumflex and the printed punctuation after Vide are preserved.
[c1-l013] Tanoxij. *Vt,* Tanoxij fito. *Rico, & abaſ-*
[c1-l013 note] Here Tanoxij is illustrated by a wealthy or well-provided person. This historical material-abundance sense is not flattened into the modern association with enjoyment.
[c1-l014 >] *tado. ¶* Tanoxij coto. *Abundancia,*
[c1-l014 note] The opening fragment completes abaſtado. A new example, Tanoxij coto, introduces abundance, with an additional Portuguese equivalent on the following line.
[c1-l015 >] *& fartura. Vt,* Qenai fucqi xite tanoxij-
[c1-l015 note] The example concerns a prosperous household. Qenai fucqi xite is the incorporated reading; the u in fucqi and portions of xite are abraded, so the nonblocking uncertainty is recorded.
[c1-l016 >] coto caguiri nacatta. Feiq. *lib. 2. Acaſa eſ-*
[c1-l016 note] Coto completes the divided Japanese example, followed by the roman citation label Feiq. The Portuguese reference and translation are italic; Acaſa retains its joined source spacing.
[c1-l017 >] *tãdo rica, & abastada, a abũdancia era immẽſa.*
[c1-l017 note] The translation says that, with the household rich and well supplied, its abundance was immense. The printed nasal abbreviations and spelling immẽſa are retained.
[c1-l018] Tanoximi. *Contentamento, & prazeres.*
[c1-l018 note] Tanoximi is defined as contentment and pleasures. This noun entry is separate from the following verbal headword, even though their printed stems coincide.
[c1-l019 >] *¶* Tanoximiga tçuquru. *Acabarenſe*
[c1-l019 note] The first example describes pleasures coming to an end. The Portuguese infinitive construction continues below and belongs to the noun entry Tanoximi.
[c1-l020 >] *os gostos. ¶* Tanoximini ſacayuru.

[c1-l021 note] This translates the preceding example as being in great delights and prosperity. It closes the examples under the noun Tanoximi.
[c1-l022] Tanoximi, u, unda. *Gozar ſe, ou darſe a*

[c1-l024] Tanoximi ſacaye, uru, eta. *Florecer cõ proſ-*

[c1-l026] Tanpan. *A*rai-i. *S*oſǒna mexi. *Comer*

[c1-l027 >] *mal feito, & groſeiro. Vt,* Tanpannimo

[c1-l029 >] *comer, & groſeiro matarei a fome.*
[c1-l029 note] The translation concludes that the speaker will satisfy hunger even with bad, coarse food. It closes the first Tanpan entry.

[c1-l031 note] The fragment completes azinhaure, the historical verdigris comparison. This is part of the source's medicinal description, not a present-day treatment recommendation.
[c1-l032] Tanpat. Mijicaqi camigue. *Cabellos curtos*

[c1-l033 >] *da cabeça.*
[c1-l033 note] Da cabeça specifies hair of the head. This continuation closes the short-hair definition under Tanpat and does not begin a separate entry.
[c1-l034] Tãpei. *Catana, ou outra arma ſe melhãte. ¶* Tan-
[c1-l034 note] Tãpei denotes a sword or comparable weapon. The abraded p is supported by the repeated form in the example; the visible spacing in ſe melhãte is retained.

[c1-l037 note] This completes the description of combat ending in both opponents' deaths or one killing the other. The nasal forms are retained as source orthography.
[c1-l038] Tanpo. *Hũ vaſo em que metem agoa quente*

[c1-l039 >] *peraſe quentar pondo o apardeſi na cama.*
[c1-l039 note] The vessel is placed beside oneself in bed for warmth. The joined source forms peraſe and apardeſi are preserved instead of respaced into modern Portuguese.
[c1-l040] Tanpopo. *Hua erua aſsi chamada.*
[c1-l040 note] Tanpopo is described simply as an herb bearing that name. The mixed long and short s in aſsi is retained; no botanical identification is added to the canonical text.
[c1-l041] Tanpu. *Vt,* Tanpuni canǒ. i, fucaqu cocoro-
[c1-l041 note] Tanpu is illustrated by Tanpuni canǒ, followed by a Japanese paraphrase about deep feeling. The printed canǒ. i, sequence is separated correctly, not fused into a word.

[c1-l044] Tanquano cuchibiru. *Peiços vermelhos, &*
[c1-l044 note] Tanquano cuchibiru describes red and attractive lips. The source's Peiços is retained, with the comparison to a named flower continuing below.

[c1-l045 note] The lips are compared to a flower called Botan. Botan is a whole Japanese term embedded in Portuguese explanation and is recorded in typeface_terms, unlike an ordinary headword.
[c1-l046] Tanqei. *Certa feição de candieiro de Iapão.*
[c1-l046 note] Tanqei denotes a particular form of Japanese lamp. The source gives no further construction details on this complete single-line entry.
[c1-l047] Tanqi. Mijicai qi. *Colera, ou agaſtamento.*
[c1-l047 note] Tanqi, glossed by Mijicai qi, means anger or irritation. Its examples continue at the top of the next column and must stay connected to this headword.
