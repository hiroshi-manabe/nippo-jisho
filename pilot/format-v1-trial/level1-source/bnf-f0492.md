[c1-l001] Tçuridôro. *Lanterna que ſe dependura.*
[c1-l001 note] Tsuridooro is a hanging lantern. The circumflex in dôro is visible and retained; the Portuguese specifies suspension rather than a standing lamp.
[c1-l002] Tçuri ito. *Linha de peſcar.*
[c1-l002 note] Tsuri ito is fishing line, directly matched by linha de pescar. The headword has the cedilla of tç, which the OCR omitted.
[c1-l003] Tçurimono. *Couſa dependurada: particular-*
[c1-l003 note] Tsurimono means a suspended object; the gloss narrows this general meaning to a particular utensil below. The colon and divided particular- are visible.
[c1-l004 >] *mente ſe toma por hũa panela de ferro, que ſer*
[c1-l004 note] Completes particularmente and identifies an iron pot. The printed line ends with ser, continuing as ue below, without a supplied terminal hyphen; ferro has the two r types.
[c1-l005 >] *ue no Chanoyu.*
[c1-l005 note] Ser-ue is the historical spelling of serve, completing the description of a pot used in chanoyu, the preparation and service of tea. Chanoyu is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l006] Tçǔriqi. *Forças penetratiuas, ou qualidades*
[c1-l006 note] Tsuuriqi denotes supernatural powers, introduced as penetrating powers or glorious qualities. The sequence tçǔriqi is supported by the types and the bundled NINJAL entry; the OCR's Teǔiriqi was malformed.
[c1-l007 >] *glorioſas que ſão ſeis. Primeira,* Iinqiǒtçǔ.
[c1-l007 note] The explanation enumerates six powers. Iinqiǒtçǔ is the first, the power of supernatural movement explained below; the faint narrow i between q and ǒ is retained from the enlarged scan, not silently omitted as in the OCR.
[c1-l008 >] *Velocidade no andar. Segũda,* Tenguentçǔ.
[c1-l008 note] The first power is glossed as speed of movement. Tenguentçǔ introduces heavenly sight as the second; the printed n and t before çǔ restore the corrupted OCR ending.
[c1-l009 >] *Vista penetratiua de tudo. Terceira,* Tenni-
[c1-l009 note] The second power sees through everything. Tenni- begins the third power, heavenly hearing; the two n types are retained rather than OCR Tenmi, and the physical division is preserved.
[c1-l010 >] tçǔ. *Ouuir todas as couſas do mundo. Quar*
[c1-l010 note] Tçǔ completes Tenni-tçǔ. Its gloss is hearing everything in the world; Quar continues as ta on the next row, with no added hyphen at the right edge.
[c1-l011 >] *ta,* Taxin çǔ. *Saber os corações de todos.*
[c1-l011 note] Quar-ta introduces the fourth power, knowing everyone's heart or thoughts. The scan appears to print Taxin çǔ without a t before çǔ; this anomalous but usable reading is retained rather than regularized to the expected tsuu spelling.
[c1-l012 >] *Quinta,* Rojintçǔ. *Não ter macula nenhũa,*
[c1-l012 note] Rojintçǔ is the fifth power, freedom from stain or imperfection. The o after R is abraded but has no securely visible long-vowel mark, so none is supplied from the expected Buddhist term.
[c1-l013 >] *nem imperfeição. Sexta,* Xucumiǒtçǔ. *Sa-*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the fifth definition and names the sixth power, knowledge of previous lives or the past. Xucumiǒtçǔ replaces the OCR's extra a and confused t; the final length mark is faint but best read as a caron like the neighboring çǔ forms.
[c1-l014 >] *ber todo o paßado.*
[c1-l014 note] Sa-ber completes the preceding line's verb: knowing all the past. The ß-shaped sort is preserved rather than expanded into a different sequence.
[c1-l015] Tçuriſague, uru, eta. *Dependurar algũa cou*
[c1-l015 note] Tsurisage, tsurisaguru, tsurisageta means hanging something down. The headword's u and long ſ are visible; cou continues as ſa below without an invented line-end hyphen.
[c1-l016 >] *ſa pera baixo.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes cousa pera baixo, an object suspended downward. This short continuation belongs to tsurisage and is not a separate entry.
[c1-l017] Tçuritôdai. *Candieiro que ſe dependura.*
[c1-l017 note] Tsuritoodai is a hanging lamp or lampstand. Candieiro is the printed Portuguese spelling; the circumflex distinguishes the actual tô type from a normalized long-vowel notation.
[c1-l018] Tçuriǔdo. *Peſcador de cana.*
[c1-l018 note] Tsuriudo is a fisherman using a rod. The gloss reads pescador, with a d rather than the OCR's ci, and the marked u in the Japanese form is retained.
[c1-l019] Tçurizauo. *Cana de peſcar.*
[c1-l019 note] Tsurizao is a fishing rod. The historical zauo spelling is preserved, with the Portuguese confirming the object rather than a person.
[c1-l020] Tçǔro. Touoru michi. *Caminho que ſe paſ-*
[c1-l020 note] Tsuuro is explained by tooru michi, a road one passes along. Paſ- starts the divided Portuguese verb, while the Japanese synonym remains roman and is not a typeface-toggle target.
[c1-l021 >] *ſa, ou anda. Vt,* Tçǔroga tomatta, l,
[c1-l021 note] The gloss completes passa ou anda. Tsuuro ga tomatta begins an example about a blocked route; the alternative marker l introduces qireta on the following row.
[c1-l022 >] qireta. *Estão os caminhos tomados, ou im-*
[c1-l022 note] Qireta gives the alternative 'cut off'; the translation says the roads are occupied or obstructed. The divided im- belongs to impedidos below, and the source's provisional st allograph is retained.
[c1-l023 >] *pedidos.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes impedidos, 'obstructed'. This short physical row closes the blocked-road example before the unrelated crane entry.
[c1-l024] Tçuru. *Grou.*
[c1-l024 note] Tsuru here means the crane bird, glossed grou. It must remain separate from the homonymous bowstring entry immediately below.
[c1-l025] Tçuru. *Corda do arco.* Tçuruuo ſaſu. *Fa-*
[c1-l025 note] Tsuru now means a bowstring. Tsuru wo sasu is glossed as making bowstrings; the damaged initial is the same T form as neighboring entries, not OCR F.
[c1-l026 >] *zer cordas de arco. ¶* Tçuruvotouo furu.
[c1-l026 note] Fa-zer completes 'make bowstrings'. Tsuruoto wo furu introduces sounding or testing the string; the printed votouo sequence is retained rather than modernized to an expected segmentation.
[c1-l027 >] *Prouar com a mão a corda fazendoa ſoar.*
[c1-l027 note] The example means testing the string with the hand by making it sound. The tilde belongs to a in mão, and the historical joined fazendoa is retained.
[c1-l028 >] *¶* Tçuruuo caquru. *Por a corda no arco*
[c1-l028 note] Tsuru wo kakuru means putting the string on the bow. The scan supplies this physical row only once; the overlapping OCR c1-l029 was a duplicate detection, not additional text.
[c1-l030 >] *¶* Tçuruuo fazzuſu. *Tirar a corda do arco.*
[c1-l030 note] Tsuru wo hazusu is removing the bowstring, directly matched by tirar. The overlapping OCR c1-l031 repeats the beginning of this same row and is removed without loss of text.
[c1-l032] Tçurube. *Balde.*
[c1-l032 note] Tsurube is a bucket, specifically relevant to the well-bucket expressions that follow. The initial T and the medial u are faint but compatible with the repeated tsurube forms below.
[c1-l033] Tçurubedachi. l, tçurubedachini. *Feição*
[c1-l033 note] Tsurubedachi and tsurubedachini describe the vertical manner associated with a hanging well bucket. Feição begins the Portuguese explanation of that manner; the first letter is F, not OCR T.
[c1-l034 >] *de quem poem, ou dependura balde em poço*
[c1-l034 note] Explains lowering or hanging a bucket in a well. This supplies the physical image for the steep or sheer feature described on the next rows.
[c1-l035 >] *pera declarar couſa imgreme como rocha talha-*
[c1-l035 note] The expression denotes something steep, like a cut rock face. Enlargement supports the printed imgreme spelling; it is retained rather than regularized to modern íngreme. Talha- continues as da below.
[c1-l036 >] *da ou grande precipicio.*
[c1-l036 note] Completes talhada and adds a great precipice. The definition concerns a sheer drop, not a different kind of bucket.
[c1-l037] Tçurubenaua. *Corda do balde de tirar agoa.*
[c1-l037 note] Tsurubenawa is the rope of a bucket used to draw water. The scan and surrounding tsurube entries support naua rather than OCR uaua; agoa is left in its historical spelling.
[c1-l038] Tçurubucuro. *Saco da corda do arco.*
[c1-l038 note] Tsurubukuro is a bag for the bowstring. The scan gives saco da corda, correcting the OCR's Saro and de, without confusing this string compound with the preceding well-bucket terms.
[c1-l039] Tçurucabura. *Aſelha da corda do arco por on-*
[c1-l039 note] Tsurukabura denotes the loop or eye of the bowstring. On- begins onde, whose continuation explains how the loop is fitted to the bow.
[c1-l040 >] *de ſe mete, & poem no arco.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes onde: the loop is the part through which the string is put onto the bow. The short continuation closes the preceding technical definition.
[c1-l041] Tçurucaqe. *Official de cordas de arco, que tẽ*
[c1-l041 note] Tsurukake denotes a maker or worker of bowstrings. The following relative clause describes the trade's distinctive clothing; the abbreviated tẽ is retained.
[c1-l042 >] *particular trajo em Iapão. ſ. touca na cabeça,*
[c1-l042 note] The worker has a particular costume in Japan, specified as a head covering. The abraded wording is particular trajo; the Portuguese abbreviation ſ. introduces the particulars.
[c1-l043 >] *& Catabira vermelha curta.*
[c1-l043 note] Adds a short red katabira garment to the costume. Catabira is a Japanese clothing term embedded in the Portuguese sentence; the central tabira types survive sufficiently to correct the OCR's Cavira.
[c1-l044] Tçurucaqe. *Vide,* Tçurunabe.
[c1-l044 note] This separate tsurukake entry refers the reader to tsurunabe, the iron-pot entry in the next column. Its left alignment is restored; it is not a continuation of the preceding costume description.
[c1-l045] Tçurucubi. *Hũa laya de bules de vinho d*
[c1-l045 note] Tsurukubi names a type of crane-necked wine vessel. The headword and laya are heavily abraded but supported by the surviving strokes and the following compound; only d is securely visible at the right edge, so an expected final e is not supplied.
[c1-l046 >] *bronzo como galhetas grandes, ou guindes.*
[c1-l046 note] The vessels are bronze, compared with large cruets or guindes. Guindes is retained as the visible historical vessel-name rather than replaced with a more familiar modern term.
[c1-l047] Tçurucubino fiotan. *Cabaças que tem peſco-*
[c1-l047 note] Tsurukubi no fiotan denotes crane-necked gourds. The damaged cubi sequence is supported by the preceding headword and bundled NINJAL identification; no secure circumflex is visible over fiotan here, despite the index's marked form.
[c1-l048 >] *ço como de grou. ¶ Item, Qualquer outro*
[c1-l048 note] Completes pescoço, a neck like a crane's, and broadens the term to other vessels. The right-edge letters of Qualquer outro are pale; the surviving forms and the following vaſo support the chosen reading.
[c1-l049 >] *vaſo desta figura, ou feição.*
[c1-l049 note] Completes the extension to any vessel of this shape or form. The abraded initial vaſo is recovered from the visible v, a, and long-ſ remnants in the same physical row, not inserted as a new line.

[c2-l044 >] *Homem deſauenturado, ou infeliz.*

[c2-l045 >] Tçutanaſa.

[c2-l046 note] Tsutanou is the adverbial form associated with tsutanai. The final character is o with a visible caron, not the OCR's ſi; its short indented row remains separate.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 note] Anticipates continuarſe at the beginning of f493. The complete visible catchword is retained as italic page furniture under its original stable ID.
