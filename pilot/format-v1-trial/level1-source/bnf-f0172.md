[c1-l001] Faqiidaxi, ſu, aita. *Vide* Faqidaxi, ſu.
[c1-l001 note] The headword points back to `Faqidaxi`, hakidashi, “sweeping out.” The extra printed `i` is retained rather than silently regularized; `Vide` means “see.”
[c1-l002] Faqimono. *Calçado como çapatos, alparcas*
[c1-l002 note] `Faqimono` is ハキモノ, footwear. The gloss starts with shoes and sandals, not the sweeping sense of the neighboring `faqi` compounds.
[c1-l003 >] *de Iapão, &c.*
[c1-l003 note] “Of Japan, etc.” completes the footwear examples on the preceding line; this fragment is Portuguese, not a new entry.
[c1-l004] Faqinogoi, ô, ôta. *Varrendo alimpar.*
[c1-l004 note] ハキノゴイ combines sweeping and wiping clean. Portuguese `Varrendo alimpar` means cleaning by sweeping; the listed endings belong to the conjugation of nogou.
[c1-l005] Faqiǒ. Vare cagami. *Eſpelho quebrado. S.*
[c1-l005 note] `Vare cagami` is ワレ カガミ, a broken mirror, supporting the Sino-Japanese compound 破鏡. The long-vowel mark in the headword is preserved as transcribed rather than changed solely from the expected lexical reading.
[c1-l006] Faqiſoye, uru, eta. *Cingir mais hũa eſpada*
[c1-l006 note] ハキソエ means wearing an additional sword: `cingir` here is girding on a weapon. This is another meaning of haku, distinct from sweeping or putting on footwear.
[c1-l007 >] *comprida, ou montante.*
[c1-l007 note] The additional weapon is described as a long sword or a montante; this completes the definition of `Faqiſoye`.
[c1-l008] Faqiſute, uru, eta. *Varrendo lançar fora.*
[c1-l008 note] ハキステ is sweeping away/discarding. The Portuguese explicitly combines sweeping with throwing out, confirming the compound's second element sute.
[c1-l009] Faqitame, uru, eta. *Varrendo ajuntar. Vt,*
[c1-l009 note] ハキタメ means gathering by sweeping. The listed -muru form appears in the following example, introduced by `Vt`.
[c1-l010 >] Chiriuo faqitamuru. *Varrẽdo ajũtar o ciſco.*
[c1-l010 note] チリヲ ハキタムル is gathering dust or rubbish by sweeping. Portuguese `cisco` identifies the small refuse being collected; the nasal abbreviations are retained.
[c1-l011] Faqitate, uru, eta. *Varrendo aleuantar co-*
[c1-l011 note] ハキタテ is raising something through sweeping. The gloss continues with `co-` plus `mo`, “such as,” not a Japanese fragment.

[c1-l012 note] Together with the preceding fragment this reads “such as dust, etc.” Historical `poo` is the dust stirred up by sweeping.
[c1-l013] Faqizoye. *O cingir mais hũ montante menor,*

[c1-l014 >] *ou mais curto.*
[c1-l014 note] “Or shorter” further specifies the additional sword; it is a continuation of the preceding noun definition.
[c1-l015] Fara. *Campo raſo.*
[c1-l015 note] ハラ here is 原, an open plain or field. The next headword has the same sound but a different sense.
[c1-l016] Fara. *Barriga.* Farauo tatçuru. *Agas-*
[c1-l016 note] ハラ here is 腹, the belly. ハラヲ タツル introduces the idiom for becoming angry; the Portuguese word continues as `Agas-taſe` on the next line.

[c1-l018 note] With preceding `Fa-`, this is ハラヲ キリ, with alternative verbs kakikiru and suru. The Portuguese begins the explanation of cutting one's belly; isolated `l` is Latin “or,” not Japanese.

[c1-l019 note] The first gloss ends “the belly, or kill oneself.” A new honorific example begins ハラヲ メサ-, continued by the next row; do not read the fragment as a complete verb.
[c1-l020 >] iuru. *Cortar a barriga peſſoa nobre, ou ma-*
[c1-l020 note] The sense is a noble person's cutting their belly, followed by “or killing oneself.” Japanese `meſa-iuru` is suspicious: the expected honorific is mesaruru. The first letter of this damaged continuation needs particular care; the transcription is not normalized from grammar alone.

[c1-l022 note] ハラガ イル and ハラヲ スユル express anger subsiding or being settled. `Aplacarſe a ira` confirms the emotional, rather than anatomical, sense.

[c1-l029] Faradachi. *Agaſtamento.*

[c1-l035 note] This completes `der-rubando`, “felling enemies,” and closes the military example of farafarato.
[c1-l036] Faragaqe. *Cinta, ou corda com que cingem,*
[c1-l036 note] ハラガケ is a belly strap or rope. The following line identifies the animal and explains its restraining purpose, distinguishing it from the armor entries.

[c1-l040 note] “Siblings, children of different mothers” translates the preceding Japanese phrase. It identifies maternal difference without requiring a modernized spelling of mãys.
[c1-l041] Faragome. *Criança que fica no ventre da*
[c1-l041 note] ハラゴメ names a child still in the womb. The definition is qualified by the father's death on the following line, not simply pregnancy in general.

[c1-l044 note] Joined to `Morre-`, this describes the father dying while the child is still in the womb. The attached pronoun `lhe` refers to the child; `ven-` continues ventre below.

[c1-l045 note] The maternal womb completes the first sense. `Item` introduces a related sense involving offspring of an animal, continued below.

[c1-l046 note] This second sense concerns offspring remaining in a dead mother's womb. It differs from the first sense, where the father has died.
[c1-l047] Faragomori. *Corçozinho que eſta na barriga*
[c1-l047 note] ハラゴモリ is a small unborn deer in its mother's belly. The scan reads `Faragomori`, also supported by the NINJAL headword index; the OCR's extra i produced an implausible -myori sequence. The gloss continues in column 2.
