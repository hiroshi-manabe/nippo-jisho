[c1-l001] Sabaqite. *Negociador, ou o que adminiſtra.*
[c1-l001 note] Sabakite is the person who manages or negotiates, continuing the preceding page's sabaki entries. The scan supplies the missing t in the headword and the complete Portuguese Negociador and adminiſtra.
[c1-l002] Sabi. *Ferrugem. ¶* Sabiuo nogô. *A-*
[c1-l002 note] Sabi means rust. The first example describes wiping rust away, with the historical printed nogô retained; the Portuguese Alimpar is divided after A-.
[c1-l003 >] *limpar a ferrugem. ¶* Sabiuo migaqu.
[c1-l003 note] Completes the rust-cleaning gloss and adds sabi wo migaku, polishing away the rust. The two examples share the same object, not separate senses of Sabi.
[c1-l004 >] l, votoſu. *Idem. ¶* Sabiga tçuqu. *To-*
[c1-l004 note] Otosu is offered as an alternative to migaku. Sabi ga tsuku then introduces becoming rusty, with the Portuguese Tomar beginning at the line end.
[c1-l005 >] *mar ferrugem, ou fazer ſe ferrugento.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes Tomar ferrugem and restates it as becoming rusty. The printed separation fazer ſe is retained in this Portuguese reflexive construction.
[c1-l006] Sabi, uru, ita. *Tomar ferrugem o ferro, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] Sabi, sabiru, sabita gives the rusting verb's principal forms. The gloss explicitly applies it to iron, distinguishing the verb from the preceding noun.
[c1-l007 >] *Vt,* Catanaga ſabita. *A catana esta ferru-*
[c1-l007 note] Katana ga sabita means the sword has rusted. The Portuguese adjective ferrugenta continues below; catana is the historical Portuguese sword loan.
[c1-l008 >] *genta. ¶ Itẽ, Estar algũa couſa ſolitaria ou*
[c1-l008 note] Completes ferrugenta, then introduces a separate sense of being solitary. The source extends the verb beyond literal rusting.
[c1-l009 >] *deſacompanhada. ¶ Itẽ, per met. Estar*
[c1-l009 note] Being unaccompanied completes the solitude sense. The following per met. introduces a figurative use; the scan shows deſacompanhada without the OCR's internal break.
[c1-l010 >] *algũa couſa ja velha, ou çaſada, &c. ¶* Sa-
[c1-l010 note] The figurative sense concerns something old or worn. The unusual printed çaſada is retained without supplying an expected nasal; Sa- begins the following Japanese example.
[c1-l011 >] bita tocoro. *Lugar, ou caſa que està ja velha,*
[c1-l011 note] Sa- plus bita gives sabita tokoro, an aged or deteriorated place. The gloss specifies a place or house that has already become old.
[c1-l012 >] *& danificada.*
[c1-l012 note] The house is also described as damaged. This short conjunction phrase closes the figurative example rather than beginning the rusty-sword entry.
[c1-l013] Sabigatana. *Eſpada ferrugenta.*
[c1-l013 note] Sabigatana is a rusty sword, matching espada ferrugenta directly. The g reflects the printed compound and is not replaced by the separate noun katana.
[c1-l014] Sabixij. *Couſa ſolitaria, ou estar ſolitario.*
[c1-l014 note] Sabishii denotes a solitary condition or being alone. The source's paired noun-like and verbal Portuguese descriptions are preserved.
[c1-l015 >] Sabixiſa. Sabixǔ.
[c1-l015 note] Sabishisa gives the corresponding noun and sabishuu an adverbial form. Both are upright Japanese forms; neither requires an invented Portuguese definition.
[c1-l016] Sabiya. *Seta ferrugenta, ou ruim: palaura com*
[c1-l016 note] Sabiya literally describes a rusty or poor arrow. The ensuing explanation concerns a soldier's self-deprecating use of the expression.
[c1-l017 >] *q̃ algum ſoldado diz mal, ou deſfaz na ſua ſe-*
[c1-l017 note] A soldier uses the phrase to disparage his own arrow modestly. The scan supports q̃ algum and deſfaz; the second consonant in deſfaz is f, not another long s.
[c1-l018 >] *ta falando humilmente.*
[c1-l018 note] Ta completes ſeta and the explanation closes with speaking humbly. It is self-deprecation by the soldier, not an accusation that another person's arrow is defective.
[c1-l019] Sabiye. *Agoa dalgum pego, ou remanſo do*
[c1-l019 note] Sabiye denotes water in a pool or quiet reach of a river. The scan supports remanſo; the final o of do is faint but its residual curve and the continuation rio support that reading.
[c1-l020 >] *rio, que està turua por nam correr muito.*
[c1-l020 note] The water is turbid because it does not flow much. The historical negative nam is retained rather than replaced by modern não.
[c1-l021] Saburai. *Fidalgo, l, homem honrado.*
[c1-l021 note] Saburai denotes a nobleman or honorable man. The printed saburai is retained rather than replaced by the more familiar modern samurai.
[c1-l022] Saburai, ǒ, ǒta. *Ser, auer, &c.* S.
[c1-l022 note] The homonymous verb saburau is glossed as being or existing. Its principal forms retain their carons, and the upright S marks written usage.
[c1-l023] Saca. *Ladeira.*
[c1-l023 note] Saka means a slope or incline. This complete short entry starts a new series and is independent of the preceding saburai forms.
[c1-l024] Sacabayaxi. *Ramo feito à maneira de vaſou-*
[c1-l024 note] Sakabayashi is a branch arrangement made like a broom. Vaſou- is physically divided before the remainder of the Portuguese word below.
[c1-l025 >] *ra, que ſe poem às portas das tauernas. No*
[c1-l025 note] Completes vaſoura and locates the branch sign at tavern doors. No begins a regional usage statement on the next line.
[c1-l026 >] *Ximo ſe diz Fote.*
[c1-l026 note] The source says this is called Fote in Ximo. Both Ximo and Fote are Japanese terms within the Portuguese explanation; the capital X is visible.
[c1-l027] Sacabiǒxi. *Compaßo ou modo de tanger deſcom*
[c1-l027 note] Sakabyooshi describes playing out of time or in a contrary rhythm. The small caron over o is visible; deſcom continues below without an added hyphen.
[c1-l028 >] *paßado, ou deſordenado ſem conſonancia.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes deſcompaßado and explains the performance as disordered and without consonance. The printed double-s ligature spelling is retained.
[c1-l029] Sacabucuro. *Saco por onde ſe coa o arroz que*
[c1-l029 note] Sakabukuro is the bag through which the rice mash is strained. The relative clause describing that rice continues on the next physical row.
[c1-l030 >] *eſtà ja feito em vinho.*
[c1-l030 note] The rice has already been made into the drink called vinho in the Portuguese gloss. The historical terminology is preserved instead of replaced by a modern brewing description.
[c1-l031] Sacabuguiǒ. *Copeiro, ou o que tem cuidado*
[c1-l031 note] Sakabugyoo is the officer or attendant responsible for drink, compared to a cupbearer. The final long-vowel mark is a caron in this impression.
[c1-l032 >] *do vinho.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes the responsibility specified above: care of the wine or rice drink. The two-word continuation remains a separate physical row.
[c1-l033] Sacabune. *Tina em que ſe eſpreme o arroz de*
[c1-l033 note] Sakabune is the vat in which rice mash is pressed. The Japanese vessel name is explained through a Portuguese brewing comparison.
[c1-l034 >] *que ſe faz vinho.*
[c1-l034 note] The rice is used to make the drink called vinho. This completes the definition of the pressing vat and introduces no separate operation.
[c1-l035] Sacadachi. *O voltear com os pès pera cima.*
[c1-l035 note] Sakadachi describes turning upside down with the feet upward. The grave in pès is preserved rather than normalized to a modern accent.
[c1-l036 >] *Vt,* Sacadachiuo ſuru.
[c1-l036 note] Sakadachi wo suru illustrates performing this inverted movement or stance. The object particle remains attached in the printed Sacadachiuo.
[c1-l037] Sacague. *Mostras, ou reliquias deter bebido vi-*
[c1-l037 note] Sakage denotes signs or traces of having drunk alcohol. The visible terminal mark after vi is a hyphen, continuing vinho on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] *nho. ¶* Iccǒ ſacaguenimo vorinai. Mon.
[c1-l038 note] Completes vinho, then quotes an emphatic denial of showing any sign of drinking. Mon. is an upright source label; the example's historical negative vorinai is retained.
[c1-l039 >] *Por nenhum caſo tenho bebido vinho, nẽ ras*
[c1-l039 note] The translation denies having drunk at all and begins denying even a trace. The printed nẽ is abbreviated, and no hyphen is added after ras.
[c1-l040 >] *to, ou ſinal delleha em mĩ.*
[c1-l040 note] To completes rasto; there is no trace or sign of the drink in the speaker. The tightly set delleha retains the scan's word spacing; the restored e and final mĩ correct the draft's missing vowel and mã.
[c1-l041] Sacagu. *Cabelos da cabeça, que nacem aoreues.*
[c1-l041 note] This entry concerns hair growing against its normal direction. The visibly printed final u in Sacagu and joined aoreues are retained rather than regularized from lexical expectation.
[c1-l042] Sacago. *Criança, que nace às aueſas. i, Com*
[c1-l042 note] Sakago is a child born in reverse presentation. The explanatory i introduces the specification that follows, rather than another Japanese example.
[c1-l043 >] *os pès diante.*
[c1-l043 note] The child is described as coming feet first. This completes the historical dictionary's definition without adding a modern medical classification.
[c1-l044] Sacagura. *Adega de vinho.*
[c1-l044 note] Sakagura is a wine or sake storehouse. The letter after gu is r, correcting OCR Sacaguta; the Portuguese adega confirms the building sense.
[c1-l045] Sacai. *Termo, ou arraya, confins, limite.*
[c1-l045 note] Sakai means a boundary, border, or limit. The damaged initial is S, not B, and the following entry is a separate verb.
[c1-l046] Sacai, ǒ, ǒta. *Contrariar, ou ſer contrario.*
[c1-l046 note] Sakai, sakau, sakauta describes opposing or contradicting. The printed abbreviated long-vowel forms retain carons and are not replaced by modern sakarau forms.
[c1-l047 >] *Vt,* Itçumo fitono cotobani ſacǒ fitogia.
[c1-l047 note] Itsumo hito no kotoba ni sakau hito ja describes someone who always contradicts others. The scan restores the missing t in fitono; the Portuguese translation begins in column 2.

[c2-l002 >] *tros dizem. ¶* Cocoroni ſacǒ. *Dar no*
[c2-l002 note] Completes outros dizem, then illustrates kokoro ni sakau. The new phrase concerns going against someone's feelings, with its Portuguese gloss continuing below.
[c2-l003 >] *coração a alguem. ¶* Qinguen. l, chǔ-
[c2-l003 note] The gloss completes the affront-to-the-heart expression. The next example offers kingen or chuugen, good or loyal counsel, and divides chǔ- at the physical edge.
[c2-l004 >] guen mimini ſacǒ. *As boas couſas, ou*
[c2-l004 note] Chuugen mimi ni sakau describes good counsel as disagreeable to the ear. The source begins its Portuguese paraphrase with good things or advice.
[c2-l005 >] *bons conſelhos communmente não agradão aos*
[c2-l005 note] The gloss states that good advice commonly does not please its hearers. Agradão is a single printed word, not the OCR's a gradão.
[c2-l006 >] *ouuidos.*
[c2-l006 note] The ears complete the proverb's Portuguese wording. This single-word continuation belongs to the counsel example rather than the boundary noun below.
[c2-l007] Sacaime. *Termo, confins, ou remate dalgum*
[c2-l007 note] Sakaime is a border, boundary, or endpoint. The kind of land bounded is specified on the following physical row.
[c2-l008 >] *chão, reino, ou terra, &c.*
[c2-l008 note] The definition applies to a plot, realm, or land. It completes Sacaime rather than introducing a dispute about such boundaries.
[c2-l009] Sacairon. *Contenda ſobre a demarcação das*
[c2-l009 note] Sakairon is a dispute over demarcation. The Portuguese object of that demarcation continues onto the next row.
[c2-l010 >] *terras, do chão das caſas, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] The disputed boundaries concern land or house plots. This closes the contention entry before the related term for reaching a settlement.
[c2-l011] Sacaivadan. *Pazes, ou concertos, que fa-*
[c2-l011 note] Sakai wadan denotes peace or an agreement about a boundary. The Portuguese fazem is divided after fa- at the right edge.
[c2-l012 >] *zem os que confinão, ouſão vizinhos ſobre os*
[c2-l012 note] The agreement is made by adjoining landholders or neighbors. The tightly joined ouſão is preserved as printed rather than silently respaced.
[c2-l013 >] *limites do chão, terras, &c.*
[c2-l013 note] The settlement concerns the limits of plots or lands. This finishes the boundary-agreement definition.
[c2-l014] Sacama. *Contra marè, ou rolheiros da marẽ.*
[c2-l014 note] Sakama is a contrary tidal current or eddy. The second tide word retains the draft's marẽ: its worn, compact mark is less distinct than the first marè, and is not normalized to match it.
[c2-l015] Sacamacu. *Vt,* Sacamacu nami. l, miz-
[c2-l015 note] Sakamaku is exemplified by waves or water running contrary to the current. Miz- continues as zu below; the printed double-z sequence is retained across the break.
[c2-l016 >] zu. *Ondas, ou agoas, que correm encon-*
[c2-l016 note] Completes mizzu and begins the Portuguese explanation of opposing waves or waters. Encon- is divided before the next row's tradas.
[c2-l017 >] *tradas, ou pera tras como rolheiros.*
[c2-l017 note] Completes encontradas and compares the reverse-flowing water to eddies. The row finishes the sakamaku example rather than defining a separate current.
[c2-l018] Sacamatçugue. *Cabelos das peſtanas virados*
[c2-l018 note] Sakamatsuge denotes eyelashes turned in the wrong direction. The description continues with how those hairs contact the eye.
[c2-l019 >] *de maneira & encontrados, q̃entrão nos olhos.*
[c2-l019 note] The lashes are turned so that they enter or press into the eyes. Maneira corrects OCR mancira; the abbreviated q̃ is not expanded in the transcription.
[c2-l020] Sacame. *O aueſo do pao, ou madeira que he*
[c2-l020 note] Sakame concerns the reverse grain of wood. The relative clause explains the difficulty caused by working against its direction.
[c2-l021 >] *difficultoſa de laurar por ir contra o direito das*
[c2-l021 note] The wood is difficult to work because the cutting direction opposes its grain. The scan supports the doubled f in difficultoſa.
[c2-l022 >] *veas. ¶* Sacameni naru. l, iru. *Per*
[c2-l022 note] Veas completes the grain description. Sakame ni naru or iru introduces a figurative extension, with Per met. divided across the physical rows.
[c2-l023 >] *met. Leuar a couſa não por razão, mas por co-*
[c2-l023 note] The figurative sense is pursuing a matter not through reason but through anger. Co- begins colera on the following row.
[c2-l024 >] *lera, ou teima, &c. como quãdo hum alterca,*
[c2-l024 note] Completes colera and adds obstinacy, illustrated by someone arguing. The printed abbreviated quãdo is retained.
[c2-l025 >] *& não ſe deixa leuar da razão, mas de ſua pai-*
[c2-l025 note] The argumentative person follows personal passion rather than reason. Pai- is the first part of paixão on the next physical line.
[c2-l026 >] *xão, opinião, &c.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes paixão and adds personal opinion. This closes the figurative against-the-grain sense before the defensive-obstacle entries.
[c2-l027] Sacamogari. *Huã maneira de eſtrepes de cana*
[c2-l027 note] Sakamogari is a kind of pointed defensive obstacle made from cane. The tilde is visibly over the final a in Huã and is not moved to u.
[c2-l028 >] *ou pao, ou ferro que ſe poem virados pera baixo*
[c2-l028 note] Wood and iron are alternative materials, with the points directed downward. These details belong to the historical object description.
[c2-l029 >] *ſobre o muro ao redor das fortalezas, ou de ou-*
[c2-l029 note] The obstacles are placed above walls around fortifications or other enclosures. Ou- is divided before the next row's tras.
[c2-l030 >] *tras cercas.*
[c2-l030 note] Completes outras cercas, extending the definition to other enclosed sites. The brief continuation does not belong to the following headword Sacamogui.
[c2-l031] Sacamogui. Sacaſamani xigueruqi. *Paos,*
[c2-l031 note] Sakamogi is explained by sakasama ni shigeru ki, trees or branches arranged upside down. The Japanese explanation remains upright and is not a Portuguese embedded-term toggle.
[c2-l032 >] *ou aruores com os ramos, ou eſgalhos viradas*
[c2-l032 note] The Portuguese identifies wood or trees with their branches turned downward. Its printed gender agreement is preserved rather than revised grammatically.
[c2-l033 >] *pera baixo, q̃ poem amarradas a hũas eſtacas ao*
[c2-l033 note] The branches point downward and are tied to stakes. The nasal marks in q̃ and hũas are retained; the sentence continues with the position of the defensive barrier.
[c2-l034 >] *derredor das fortalezas, pera que os inimigos*
[c2-l034 note] These stakes surround fortifications. The purpose clause introduces the enemy whose ascent the downward-pointing branches obstruct.
[c2-l035 >] *não poßão liuremente ſubir. ¶* Sacamogui
[c2-l035 note] The obstacle prevents enemies from climbing freely. A paragraph mark introduces an example beginning Sacamogui; the printed double-s form in poßão is retained.
[c2-l036 >] uo fiqu. *Por eſtes paos, ou aruores com os*
[c2-l036 note] Sacamogui uo fiqu means to set out this defensive timber. The Portuguese gloss begins with Por in its older infinitive use and continues the description of the branches.
[c2-l037 >] *ramos pera baixo deſta maneira.*
[c2-l037 note] The installation is explicitly described as having its branches downward. This completes the military sense rather than describing ordinary tree planting.
[c2-l038] Sacamori. *Folguedo de muitos õde ha muito beber.*
[c2-l038 note] Sacamori denotes a drinking gathering or party. The gloss stresses both numerous participants and abundant drinking; the nasal abbreviation õde is preserved.
[c2-l039 >] *¶* Sacamoriuo ſuru. *Fazer beberetes, ou*
[c2-l039 note] The example turns the noun into an activity with ſuru. Beberetes refers here to drinking entertainments, not merely small individual drinks.
[c2-l040 >] *folguedos onde hà muito beber.*
[c2-l040 note] The alternative explanation again emphasizes a drinking party. The accent over hà is a grave mark, not the nasal mark transcribed in the draft.
[c2-l041] Sacamucai. *Refreſco de vinho, &c. que ſe*
[c2-l041 note] Sacamucai is a welcoming refreshment sent to meet an arriving traveler. The scan reads the abbreviation &c., not &ca; the purpose of the refreshment continues below.
[c2-l042 >] *manda ao encontro de quem vem por caminho*
[c2-l042 note] The refreshment is sent out to meet someone approaching by land. The relative clause continues directly into the following line's alternative mode of arrival.
[c2-l043 >] *ou embarcação.*
[c2-l043 note] Arrival by boat is included alongside arrival by road. This short continuation finishes the definition of the welcoming refreshment.
[c2-l044] Sacamuqe. *Eſpigas das vnhas.*
[c2-l044 note] Sacamuqe denotes hangnails or torn skin beside the nails. In this anatomical context eſpigas is figurative; the scan reads vnhas, correcting the draft's vnbas.
[c2-l045] Sacan. *Vt,* Sacan naru. *Proſperar, ou*
[c2-l045 note] Sacan is illustrated by Sacan naru, to flourish or become prosperous. The Portuguese presents prosperity and vigor as related senses.
[c2-l046 >] *eſtar florente, & em ſeu vigor.* S.
[c2-l046 note] The gloss completes the sense of flourishing and being in full vigor. The terminal S. is kept outside the italic Portuguese as a separate printed label.
[c2-l047] Sacana. *Couſa de comer como carne, & peixe*
[c2-l047 note] Sacana is introduced as something to eat, exemplified by meat and fish. The definition continues at the top of the next page; no closing punctuation is supplied here.
