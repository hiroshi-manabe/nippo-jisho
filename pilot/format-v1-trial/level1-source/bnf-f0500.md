[c1-l001] Tenchǔ xu. *Criados honrados como moços da*
[c1-l001 note] Tenchuu-shuu denotes honoured household attendants, compared here with chamber servants. The damaged opening and marked final vowel support Tenchǔ xu, subsequently corroborated by the NINJAL headword index, rather than the stale hint Fenchi.
[c1-l002 >] *camara, do Cubo, ou Yacata.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the household comparison with the Cubo and Yacata, Japanese titles embedded in Portuguese. The final title reads Yacata in the scan, not OCR Yarata. The embedded titles retain the locally printed italic type, confirmed against the upright Japanese headwords.
[c1-l003] Tencot. *Habilidade de fazer com as mãos, ou*
[c1-l003 note] Tenkot describes dexterity in gestures or bodily imitation. The checked final t is retained; the Portuguese begins the explanation with skill of the hands.
[c1-l004 >] *meneos do corpo varios trejeitos como arreme-*
[c1-l004 note] The explanation extends from the hands to bodily movements and varied tricks. Corpo has a very faint r and varios a weak final s; these contextually coherent readings remain qualified where the impression is abraded.
[c1-l005 >] *dar, &c. ¶* Tencotna fito. *Homem*
[c1-l005 note] Dar completes arremedar, to imitate. Tencotna fito is a person with this ability; the repeated t and c correct the malformed OCR Teneotna.
[c1-l006 >] *deſta habilidade.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes homem desta habilidade, a person possessing the dexterity just described. The damaged internal letters of habilidade do not support the OCR's habilicade.
[c1-l007] Tenden. Vtata meguru. *Reuolução, ou ro-*
[c1-l007 note] Tenden, paraphrased as utata meguru, denotes revolving or passing from one position to another. The Portuguese ro- continues into rodeo below.
[c1-l008 >] *deo de hũa parte a outra. Item aliquando, Mu-*
[c1-l008 note] Completes rodeo and introduces an occasional additional sense of change. Item aliquando is the dictionary's explanatory apparatus, and Mu- continues into mudança.
[c1-l009 >] *dança, ou variedade de tempos, ou de couſa*
[c1-l009 note] Mudança and variedade describe changes of times or of something transmitted onward. This is a continuation of tenden rather than an independent entry.
[c1-l010 >] *que corre por varias mãos, ou lugares. ¶* Co-
[c1-l010 note] The thing passes through different hands or places. Co- starts the Japanese example kono oshie on the following row.
[c1-l011 >] no voxiye tenden xite qita. *Eſta doutrina*
[c1-l011 note] Kono oshie tenden shite kita says that this teaching has been passed down. The scan supports xite, the te-form of suru, rather than OCR xire.
[c1-l012 >] *veo por dedução por diuerſas peßoas, ou tẽpos.*
[c1-l012 note] The Portuguese explains transmission through different people or periods. Dedução here describes a chain of derivation or transmission; its worn second vowel is not grounds for the OCR dadução.
[c1-l013] Tendô. *Algum veſtido de ſeda, ou peça que*
[c1-l013 note] Tendoo is a silk garment or piece given as a present. The next row identifies the recipients; the small circumflex is weak but is retained independently of the clearer carons in the following tendoo entries.
[c1-l014 >] *ſe dà a molheres publicas. Item, Perturbação.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes the gift definition as something given to public women, then adds disturbance as another sense. Perturbação corrects the OCR's damaged internal consonants without modernizing the surrounding spelling.
[c1-l015] Tendǒ. l, Tentǒ. *Vt,* Tendǒ memǒ.
[c1-l015 note] Tendoo, alternatively tentoo, introduces the phrase tendoo memoo, moral or intellectual inversion and confusion. All three visible o marks are carons here; this differs from the preceding entry's circumflex.
[c1-l016 >] *O ir aueſo da razão, virtude, ou juſtiça, ou*
[c1-l016 note] The phrase is glossed as going against reason, virtue, or justice. The scan supports virtude; the historical aueſo is preserved without supplying a second s.
[c1-l017 >] *andar errado, & fora do bom caminho.* S.
[c1-l017 note] Continues the gloss as wandering away from the good path. Caminho corrects OCR camanho, and the final upright S marks written usage.
[c1-l018] Tendǒ. *Em cima do ceo, ou ceo.*
[c1-l018 note] This separate tendoo entry denotes heaven or what is above the sky. Its caron and separate entry status must not be assimilated to the neighboring celestial-child entry.
[c1-l019] Tendô. *Minino celeſtial que cuſtuma eſtar jũto*
[c1-l019 note] Tendoo here is a celestial child, commonly shown beside a Buddha. The scan supports Tendô and celeſtial; Minino and cuſtuma retain the historical Portuguese forms.
[c1-l020 >] *do Fotoque.*
[c1-l020 note] Do Fotoque completes the description of the celestial child as being beside a Buddha. Fotoque is a Japanese religious term embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l021] Tendocu. *Ler algum liuro principalmente hum*
[c1-l021 note] Tendoku describes reading a book in a particular abbreviated fashion. The next rows specify a Buddhist scripture and explain which parts are actually read; principalmente corrects the OCR ending.
[c1-l022 >] *q̃ chamão Daifanya deſdo principio te o cabo,*
[c1-l022 note] The named scripture is Daifanya, the Daihannya text. The Portuguese specifies proceeding from beginning to end; the Japanese book name is embedded in this explanation and is retained as printed.
[c1-l023 >] *não lendo todas as regras, ou paginas, mas em*
[c1-l023 note] The reading does not include every line or page. This negative qualification is essential to the account of tendoku and is completed by the selective-reading phrase below.
[c1-l024 >] *alguns lugares.*
[c1-l024 note] Alguns lugares completes the explanation: only selected places are read. The final n-s sequence supports alguns rather than OCR algums.
[c1-l025] Tenebana. i, Tenebai. *Vagaroſo de mãos.*
[c1-l025 note] Tenebana is equated with tenebai and glossed as slow with the hands. The two Japanese forms are a headword and synonym, not terms embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c1-l026] Tenga. *Dignidade de Quambacu.*
[c1-l026 note] Tenga denotes the dignity or rank of Quambacu. The title Quambacu occurs inside the Portuguese explanation; the headword begins with a damaged T, not F.
[c1-l027] Tenga. i, Amano gaua. *Via lactea.*
[c1-l027 note] This distinct tenga is the Milky Way, paraphrased as ama no gawa. Via lactea supplies the astronomical identification directly.
[c1-l028] Tengai. Ten futa, l, vouoi. *Sobre ceo, ou pa-*
[c1-l028 note] Tengai is a canopy, paraphrased by ten futa and ooi, a cover. The Portuguese sobre ceo and divided paleo describe a covering held overhead, not the separate literary sense of heaven below.
[c1-l029 >] *leo. ¶* Tengaiuo ſaſu. l, ſaxicazaſu. *Le-*
[c1-l029 note] Tengai wo sasu or sashikazasu means holding a canopy aloft. The initial long s in ſaſu is supported by comparison with ſaxicazaſu; it is not the f suggested by OCR.
[c1-l030 >] *uar o paleo aleuantado.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes levar o paleo aleuantado, carrying the canopy raised. The historical paleo is retained rather than modernized to pálio.
[c1-l031] Tengai. *Ceo.* S.
[c1-l031 note] A separate tengai means heaven in written usage. The final S is an upright usage label, outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l032] Tengue. i, Tenno xita. *De baixo do ceo.*
[c1-l032 note] Tenge is explained by ten no shita, beneath heaven. It begins the paired above-and-below expression continued in the following rows.
[c1-l033 >] *¶* Tenjǒ tengue. *Em cima, & de bai-*
[c1-l033 note] Tenjoo tenge denotes above and below heaven. The scan supports tengue rather than OCR zengue; the Portuguese bai- continues into baixo.
[c1-l034 >] *xo do ceo.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*de Deos.*
[c1-l034 note] Xo completes baixo do ceo. The far-right parenthesis and de Deos are physically printed here but complete the following tenguen gloss, Olhos, ou o ver de Deos; their displaced placement is explicitly retained.
[c1-l035] Tenguen. Teno manaco. *Olhos, ou o ver*
[c1-l035 note] Tengen is paraphrased as ten no manako and glossed as God's eyes or sight. Its final de Deos is displaced to the far right of the preceding physical row; printed Teno is retained without supplying an additional n.
[c1-l036] Tenguen. *Mandado, ou recado do ceo.*
[c1-l036 note] This separate tengen denotes a command or message from heaven. The complete short gloss establishes a distinct entry from the preceding heavenly-eye sense.
[c1-l037] Tengu. Tenno inu. *Diabo.*
[c1-l037 note] Tengu is paraphrased literally as heaven's dog and glossed as a devil. This preserves the dictionary's own religious interpretation without substituting a modern folklore description.
[c1-l038] Tenguai. Tenno foca. *Fora do ceo.*
[c1-l038 note] Tengai, printed Tenguai with the historical glide, is explained as ten no hoka, outside heaven. It is distinct from the earlier canopy headword despite the modern reading overlap.
[c1-l039] Tenguan. Tenno camuri. *Coroa, ou grinal-*
[c1-l039 note] Tenguan is heaven's crown or headdress. Grinal- begins the Portuguese word for a wreath, completed below.
[c1-l040 >] *da como de anjos, ou gente celeſtial.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes grinalda and likens the crown to those of angels or celestial people. The imagery explains the preceding heavenly-crown compound.
[c1-l041] Ten-i. Tenno ixei. *Poder, ou mageſtade*
[c1-l041 note] Ten-i is heavenly authority or majesty, paraphrased as ten no isei. The scan supports a damaged capital T and the internal hyphen, not lowercase ren-i.
[c1-l042 >] *de Deos, ou de Rey.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the authority gloss by applying it to God or a king. The short row belongs to ten-i rather than to the next physician entry.
[c1-l043] Ten-i. Tenno cuſuxi. *Medico celeſtial.*
[c1-l043 note] A distinct Ten-i is a heavenly physician, paraphrased as ten no kusushi. The printed internal hyphen belongs to the headword; OCR incorrectly detached its final i as an explanatory marker.
[c1-l044] Tenifa. *Modo de falar conforme, ou contra*
[c1-l044 note] Teniha concerns the manner of speaking in relation to grammatical rules. The Portuguese deliberately allows both conformity with and violation of those rules.
[c1-l045 >] *as leis da grammatica.*
[c1-l045 note] As leis da grammatica completes the reference to grammatical rules. This short continuation remains a separate physical line.
[c1-l046] Tenji, zuru, ita. *Mudar, ou virar.*
[c1-l046 note] Tenji, tenzuru, tenjita means changing or turning. The principal-part abbreviation ita is retained as printed and interpreted with the stem, without rewriting it as a full form.
[c1-l047 >] *¶* Qiuo tenzuru, l, tenji cayuru. *Tro-*
[c1-l047 note] Ki wo tenzuru or tenji kayuru introduces changing one's mental state. Tro- starts trocar, whose remaining syllable and explanation continue at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *car o coração como de triſte em alegre, &c.*
[c2-l001 note] Car completes Tro-car across the column boundary. The example describes changing the heart or state of mind from sadness to happiness; the opening is car, not OCR tar.
[c2-l002 >] *¶* Q ino tenjita fito. *Homem delgado de*
[c2-l002 note] Ki no tenjita hito denotes a sharp or discerning person. The printed separation in Q ino is retained as spacing evidence, while its Japanese interpretation is a single ki followed by no. The scan also supplies the omitted paragraph mark.
[c2-l003 >] *engenho, & ſagaz. ¶* Monono fonuo ten-
[c2-l003 note] Engenho and sagaz complete the description of mental acuity. Monono fonuo ten- begins the next example, putting reading marks on a book; the scan supports initial M and fonuo rather than OCR Nonono fonno.
[c2-l004 >] zuru. *Por ſinais nas letras dos liuros pera ſa-*
[c2-l004 note] Zuru completes tenzuru. The Portuguese explains placing sinais, reading signs, on the letters in books; the n in sinais corrects OCR simais.
[c2-l005 >] *ber ler.*
[c2-l005 note] Ber completes saber, explaining that the marks enable the book to be read. This brief continuation belongs to the preceding verbal example.
[c2-l006] Tenjin. *Hum certo Cami.*
[c2-l006 note] Tenjin is identified simply as a particular kami. Cami is a Japanese religious term within the Portuguese gloss; this brief definition alone does not warrant adding a more specific identification.
[c2-l007] Tenjin. *Comer como merenda que ſe da depo-*
[c2-l007 note] A different tenjin denotes a snack or light repast served after the principal meal. The division depo- continues into depois below.
[c2-l008 >] *is do conuite do jantar. ¶* Tenjinuo cǔ.
[c2-l008 note] Completes depois do conuite do jantar. Tenjin wo kuu illustrates eating this repast; the marked u is preserved as printed.
[c2-l009 >] *Comer eſta merenda. ¶* Tenjinuo coxi-
[c2-l009 note] The first Portuguese phrase translates eating the snack. The next Japanese example begins tenjin wo koshirayuru, preparing it, with the verb divided after coxi-.
[c2-l010 >] rayuru. *Aparelhar eſta merenda.*
[c2-l010 note] Rayuru completes the historical verb koshirayuru, and aparelhar means preparing the repast. The form is not modernized to koshiraeru.
[c2-l011] Tenjǒ. Tenno vye. *Em cima dos ceos.*
[c2-l011 note] Tenjoo is explained as ten no ue, above the heavens. This begins a heavenly-location sense distinct from the palace and ceiling entries below.
[c2-l012 >] *¶* Tenjǒ ſuru. *Subir ao ceo. ¶* Ani-
[c2-l012 note] Tenjoo suru means ascending to heaven. Ani- starts the word Anima in the following example; it is part of that quoted sentence, not an independent Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l013 >] ma tenjǒ vtagai naxi. *Eſtar a alma no ceo,*
[c2-l013 note] Anima tenjoo utagai nashi expresses certainty that the soul is in heaven. The borrowed Anima remains within the Japanese example, and the Portuguese translation begins on this row.
[c2-l014 >] *ou ter ſubido ao ceo não ha que duuidar.*
[c2-l014 note] The translation states that there is no doubt the soul is in heaven or has ascended there. This completes the certainty expressed by utagai nashi.
[c2-l015] Tenjǒ. *Paços reais.*
[c2-l015 note] This separate tenjoo denotes the royal palace. The short Portuguese gloss distinguishes it from both heavenly ascent and the ceiling of a house.
[c2-l016] Tenjǒ. *Forro da caſa. ¶* Tenjǒuo faru.
[c2-l016 note] Tenjoo here means a ceiling or house lining. Tenjoo wo haru illustrates putting up that lining; the phrase continues in Portuguese below.
[c2-l017 >] *Fazer forro à caſa.*
[c2-l017 note] Fazer forro à casa completes the ceiling example. The grave accent on à is retained as the visible Portuguese mark.
[c2-l018] Tenjǒbito. *Nobres da caſa do Dairi que po*
[c2-l018 note] Tenjoobito are nobles of the Dairi household with privileged access. Dairi is the Japanese court title embedded in the Portuguese gloss; the printed po is divided from dẽ without an added hyphen.
[c2-l019 >] *dẽ entrar dentro dos paços, ou camara do Rey.*
[c2-l019 note] Dẽ completes podẽ: these nobles may enter the palace or the king's chamber. The weak final word is read Rey; its y is abraded, but residual lower ink and the complete royal-court explanation support that reading.
[c2-l020] Tenjǒguiri. *Certa verruma pequenina.*
[c2-l020 note] Tenjoogiri names a small boring tool. The enlarged scan supports verruma, a gimlet or auger, rather than OCR verrama.
[c2-l021] Tenjǒ tengue. *Em cima, & de baixo do ceo.*
[c2-l021 note] Tenjoo tenge denotes above and below heaven. This repeats the expression already illustrated under tengue in column 1.
[c2-l022] Tenju. *Carauelhas da viola.*
[c2-l022 note] Tenju denotes the tuning pegs of a viola-like stringed instrument. The headword's T and especially n are badly abraded; the remaining types, alphabetic position, gloss, and subsequently consulted NINJAL index support Tenju rather than OCR Ie ju.
[c2-l023] Tenma. Tçutaye vma. *Caualo, ou caualga-*
[c2-l023 note] Tenma is paraphrased as tsutaye uma, a relay horse or mount. Caualga- starts the alternate Portuguese designation completed below.
[c2-l024 >] *dura que ſe dà degraça de lugar em lugar por*
[c2-l024 note] Completes caualgadura: a mount provided free from place to place. The faint final word is por, introducing the authority under whose orders the service is supplied.
[c2-l025 >] *mandado dalgum ſenhor, &c. ¶ Per met.*
[c2-l025 note] The mount is supplied on a lord's command. Per met. introduces a figurative extension illustrated on the next row and remains unexpanded in the transcription.
[c2-l026 >] Tenmani torarete xinrǒ ſuru. *Trabalhar*
[c2-l026 note] Tenma ni torarete shinroo suru describes being pressed into unpaid service and labouring. The scan supports xinrǒ, with a small r, rather than the malformed xiniǒ of the initial hint; Trabalhar is the Portuguese verb.
[c2-l027 >] *de balde ſem receber paga. ¶ Item, Caualo*
[c2-l027 note] The labour is done for nothing, without receiving payment. A further literal sense of tenma begins with Item, Caualo and continues below.
[c2-l028 >] *q̃ hũ toma daluguer pagando algũa couſa.*
[c2-l028 note] The additional sense is a horse hired in exchange for payment. The abbreviation hũ refers to a person taking the horse; the initial letter is h, not OCR b.
[c2-l029] Tenma. *Demonio. ¶* Tenma fajun. *Idẽ.*
[c2-l029 note] This tenma means a demon, with Tenma fajun offered as an equivalent expression. The second Tenma corrects OCR Tenina; Idẽ remains the printed abbreviated reference back to the same meaning.
[c2-l030] Tenma. i, Faxifune. *Embarcação pequena,*
[c2-l030 note] Another tenma is a small boat, equated with hashibune. The scan supports Faxifune, with n rather than the second u in OCR Faxifuue.
[c2-l031 >] *como eſquife, &c.*
[c2-l031 note] The small craft is compared to a skiff. This continuation completes the boat sense and does not qualify the demon entry above.
[c2-l032] Tenma guedǒ. *Ley maa dos diabos.*
[c2-l032 note] Tenma gedoo is characterized by the compilers as the evil law of devils. The initial vowel is e in Tenma, not OCR Tonma; the historical religious judgment is preserved as the source's wording.
[c2-l033] Tenmei. *Mandado, ou ordem do ceo, ou de*
[c2-l033 note] Tenmei denotes a command or ordinance from heaven or God. The right edge contains de, whose faint e was mistaken for a hyphen by OCR.
[c2-l034 >] *Deos. ¶* Tenmeiuo ſomuqu. *Quebrar*
[c2-l034 note] Deos completes the preceding gloss. Tenmei wo somuku means disobeying the heavenly command; the Portuguese Quebrar begins its translation.
[c2-l035 >] *o mandamento de Deos, ou ir contra a von-*
[c2-l035 note] The translation explains breaking God's command or acting against his will. The faint divided von- is wholly Portuguese italic text, not a roman Japanese n.
[c2-l036 >] *tade de Deos.*
[c2-l036 note] Tade completes vontade de Deos, God's will. This short continuation closes the disobedience example.
[c2-l037] Tenmǒ. i, Tenno ami. *Rede, ou laço do*
[c2-l037 note] Tenmoo is paraphrased as ten no ami, heaven's net. The Portuguese adds the image of a snare, elaborated through punishment below.
[c2-l038 >] *ceo em que hum vem a cair por ſeus peccados*
[c2-l038 note] One falls into this heavenly net because of one's sins. The scan supports ſeus, with a tall initial long s, rather than OCR seas; the possessive belongs to the sinner described.
[c2-l039 >] *ſendo caſtigado, ou justiçado demaneira q̃ não*
[c2-l039 note] Punishment or judicial execution makes escape impossible. The scan gives a single não, not the duplicated OCR nãõo; historical demaneira and the provisional st typography are retained.
[c2-l040 >] *pode eſcapar. ¶* Tenmôni cacaru. *Cair*
[c2-l040 note] Tenmoo ni kakaru illustrates falling into this net. The example's visible circumflex in Tenmôni is retained independently of the caron on the entry headword.
[c2-l041 >] *nesta rede.*
[c2-l041 note] Nesta rede completes cair, falling into this net. It closes the punitive metaphor of the tenmoo entry.
[c2-l042] Tenmocu. *Porſolana de Iapão por onde ſe be-*
[c2-l042 note] Tenmoku is a Japanese ceramic vessel from which one drinks. Porſolana is chosen for the abraded pottery word; the o after ſ and n near the end are weak and can resemble c and r, so this reading remains qualified rather than modernized to porcelana.
[c2-l043 >] *be o Cha, ou mezinha, &c.*
[c2-l043 note] Be completes bebe: the vessel is used for tea or medicine. Cha and mezinha are retained as the printed words in the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l044] Tenmon. *Porta, ou entrada do ceo que os gẽ-*
[c2-l044 note] Tenmon here denotes a gate or entrance to heaven. The damaged headword is distinct from the following homonymous astronomical entry; gẽ- begins the Portuguese word gentios.
[c2-l045 >] *tios dizem auer noue em numero.*
[c2-l045 note] The compilers report that non-Christian believers count nine such entrances. Gentios is source terminology, and the stated number is preserved without importing a cosmological interpretation.
[c2-l046] Tenmon. *Sciencia de astronomia com aqual ſe*
[c2-l046 note] A separate tenmon is the science of astronomy. The scan supports the continuous word astronomia rather than OCR astro nomia; the description continues below.
[c2-l047 >] *computa o curſo das estrellas, & o nacimento,*
[c2-l047 note] Astronomy is described as calculating the course of the stars and a birth or rising, nacimento, whose precise referent continues beyond the supplied page. The isolated lower-right vid. is separately classified as a catchword; the next-page continuation has not been inspected.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *vid.*
[u2b-l001 note] Isolated lower-right catchword. The final ink blob is retained as the aligned OCR point in vid.; it could be a heavily filled or abraded a, giving vida. Comparison with large irregular punctuation elsewhere in the supplied scans does not establish contrary letter evidence, and the next-page continuation has not been inspected.
