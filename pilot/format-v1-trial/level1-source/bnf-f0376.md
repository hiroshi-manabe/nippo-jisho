## column-1-part-1 [column] Column 1, part 1

[c1-l001] Nuſumitori, u, otta. *Furtar.*
[c1-l001 note] Nusumitori means stealing or taking by theft. This opening confirms the preceding page's Nuſu- catchword without turning that catchword into running text.
[c1-l002] Nuta. *Certa maneira de ſalſa, ou eſcaueche,*
[c1-l002 note] Nuta is described as a sauce or pickling preparation. The dictionary's Portuguese comparison is retained rather than replaced by a modern recipe definition.
[c1-l003 >] *com que concertão Namaſu, &c. ¶* Nu-
[c1-l003 note] Explains preparing namasu with the sauce and begins the compound nutanamasu. Namaſu occurs within Portuguese prose, while the final Nu- starts a Japanese expression.
[c1-l004 >] tanamaſu. *Namaſu feito cõ este eſcaueche.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes nutanamasu, defined as namasu made with this preparation. The scan has Namaſu feito, not the OCR's fused Namaſuafeito; cõ retains its nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l005] Nutaaye. *Certa iguaria feita com este eſcaue-*
[c1-l005 note] Nutaaye names food prepared with this dressing. The divided eſcaue-che is a Portuguese word, not a change of headword.
[c1-l006 >] *che. ¶* Nutaayeni ſuru. *Concertar o co-*
[c1-l006 note] Nutaaye ni suru means preparing food in this manner. The final co- begins comer in the following continuation.
[c1-l007 >] *mer desta maneira.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes o comer desta maneira, preparing the food this way. This closes the culinary entry.
[c1-l008] Nutana. l, nutanamono. *Peßoa çuja, mal*
[c1-l008 note] Nutana or nutanamono describes a dirty or slovenly person. The alternative marker l and the printed cedilla in çuja are retained.
[c1-l009 >] *compoſta, deſcuidada, ou priguiçoſa.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes the description as ill-kept, careless, or lazy. The historical priguiçoſa is not normalized to a modern spelling.
[c1-l010] Nutanamaſu. *Vide,* Nuta.
[c1-l010 note] This independent headword refers the reader back to Nuta, where the compound has already been explained. Nuta is the upright cross-reference target after italic Vide.
[c1-l011] Nuxi. *Dono, ſenhor, ou ſenhora de qualquer*
[c1-l011 note] Nushi denotes the owner or master, male or female, of something. The scan supports ſenhora rather than OCR ſenbora.
[c1-l012 >] *couſa.*
[c1-l012 note] Completes de qualquer couſa, extending ownership to any object. This ends the first of the separate nushi senses.
[c1-l013] Nuxi. *Vos, ou tu, falando com gente baixa.*
[c1-l013 note] Records nushi as second-person address, glossed vos or tu when speaking to people considered lower in status. This is the source's register description.
[c1-l014] Nuxi. *Official de charoar com Vruxi.*
[c1-l014 note] Here nushi is a lacquer craftsman, distinct from the preceding owner and pronoun senses. Vruxi names the material within the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l015] Nuxiya. *Caſa onde ſe faz esta obra de Vruxi.*
[c1-l015 note] Nushiya is the workshop where this lacquer work is done. Vruxi is again a Japanese material name embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c1-l016] Nuye. *Paßaro aßi chamado.*
[c1-l016 note] Nue is identified only as a bird with this name. The brief gloss does not itself describe the later familiar composite creature.

[c1-l017 initial=6] PAppato. *Adu. Modo de*
[c1-l017 note] The decorated P belongs to Appato, giving PAppato, an adverb of sudden rising or spreading. OCR mistook the initial's ornament for a paragraph mark and lost the headword; its full six-line block is included in the owning crop.
[c1-l018 >] *ſe aleuantar po, ondas, laba-*
[c1-l018 note] Describes dust, waves, or flame rising. The scan reads ondas, not OCR onãas; laba- continues into labareda below.
[c1-l019 >] *reda, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes labareda, a tongue or blaze of flame. The decorative initial alongside this continuation is not additional text.
[c1-l020 >] Pararito. l, fararito. *Adu.*
[c1-l020 note] Gives pararito and fararito as alternative adverbs. The initial letter is P in the first form and f in the second; their contrast is visible and should not be flattened to two identical forms.
[c1-l021 >] *Modo de ſoar algũa couſa que*
[c1-l021 note] Introduces the sound made by something falling. The scan's ſoar corrects OCR ſoor; the subject continues on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *cae como grãos, &c. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l022 note] Gives falling grains as the sound example and then introduces another sense. The verb is cae, not OCR cac, and this continuation is physically indented beside the initial.
[c1-l023 >] *Totalmente, ou ſem ficar nada. Vt,* Para-
[c1-l023 note] Adds 'completely, without anything remaining'. Para- begins the Japanese example; its P is upright, not part of italic Vt.
[c1-l024 >] rito tatta. *Aleuantarãoſe todos ſem ficar*
[c1-l024 note] Completes pararito tatta, all getting up or departing with none remaining. The Portuguese explanation continues below.
[c1-l025 >] *nenhum.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes the statement that no one remained. This belongs to pararito tatta, not the following destruction example.
[c1-l026] Pararito vchicuzzuſu. *Deſtruir totalmente ſẽ*
[c1-l026 note] Pararito uchikuzusu means destroying completely. The P belongs to the upright Japanese example, and ſẽ abbreviates the Portuguese sem.
[c1-l027 >] *ficar nada.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes the total-destruction gloss: leaving nothing. It closes the extended pararito entry.
[c1-l028] Patto. *Adu. Modo deſe leuantar, & eſpa-*
[c1-l028 note] Patto describes something suddenly rising and spreading. The scan has two t types, agreeing with patto in the example below; OCR Pato lost one.
[c1-l029 >] *lhar algũa couſa como fumo. Vt,* Chiqemu-
[c1-l029 note] Completes espalhar and gives smoke as an example. Chiqemu- starts chikemuri, the blood mist or vapor described in the quotation. Native enlargement in the second pass reveals the faint third stroke of m, correcting OCR n.
[c1-l030 >] riga patto tatta. Yax. *O fumo, ou va*
[c1-l030 note] Completes chikemuri ga patto tatta, the blood vapor suddenly rising. Yax. is an upright citation label; va continues into vapor without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l031 >] *por do ſangue ſe aleuantou, & eſpalhou.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes vapor and translates the blood mist rising and spreading. This confirms the interpretation of the divided Japanese compound.
[c1-l032] Paxxito. *Adu. Modo de acertar com a ſe-*
[c1-l032 note] Paxxito describes striking or hitting decisively. The gloss begins with an arrow and continues to a lance below; the initial P is upright.
[c1-l033 >] *ta, ou com lança, &c. Vt,* Paxxito atat-
[c1-l033 note] Completes seta and gives the example paxshito atatta, landing a hit. The Japanese P is not part of the italic example-introducer Vt.
[c1-l034 >] ta. *Deu, ou acertou bem no aluo, &c.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes atatta and explains hitting the target squarely. The physical split is retained rather than joining the Japanese verb across rows.

[c2-l010 initial=6] QE. *Vt,* Qe ſuru. *Transfi-*
[c2-l010 note] The decorated Q and following E form the headword QE; they are not an isolated numeral 1. Ke suru is glossed as transforming, with Transfi- continuing below. The complete crop shows the initial and ornament beside six text rows; the seventh continuation remains indented below the ornament.
[c2-l011 >] *gurarſe, ou aparecer noutra for*
[c2-l011 note] Completes transfigurar-se and describes appearing in another form. The scan has for, not OCR ſor, and no terminal hyphen is added to this physical division.
[c2-l012 >] *ma, ou figura, como o Demo-*
[c2-l012 note] Completes forma, then compares the transformation to the devil appearing in human guise. The divided word is Demo-nio, not the OCR's Deino-nio.
[c2-l013 >] *nio no corpo humano, &c.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes Demonio and the example of appearing in a human body. This is the dictionary's religious illustration of transformation.
[c2-l014 >] *¶* Fotoqe fitoto qe xita-
[c2-l014 note] Begins an example of a Buddha manifesting as a human. Fotoqe is the printed Japanese spelling here; xita- begins the honorific expression completed below.
[c2-l015 >] mǒ. *O Fotoque apareceo em*
[c2-l015 note] Completes xitamǒ and translates the Buddha's appearance in human form. The decorative Q's lower strokes were misrecognized as extra characters; those artifacts are removed, while the actual mǒ and Portuguese text are retained.
[c2-l016 >] *figura humana.*
[c2-l016 note] Completes the translation as a human appearance. This last continuation remains indented below the decorative initial, which ends beside the preceding mǒ row.
[c2-l017] Qe. *Cauſa, ou achaq̃ dalgũa couſa. Vt,* Sono qe
[c2-l017 note] Ke is defined as the cause or occasion of something. The final mark on achaq̃ abbreviates the ending of achaque; the example begins sono ke ni below rather than adding a modernized definition.
[c2-l018 >] ni varucatta. *Porißo foy mal, ou mao.* B.
[c2-l018 note] Completes sono ke ni warukatta, explained as being bad or going badly for that reason. The scan reads foy, not OCR for; B. is an upright low-register label.
[c2-l019] Qe. *Riſca. Vt,* Qeuo fiqu. *Riſcar.*
[c2-l019 note] Ke here means a ruled line; ke wo hiku means drawing such a line. The paired Riſca and Riſcar distinguish the noun from the action.
[c2-l020] Qe. *Couſa ordinaria, ou de cote: não ſe vſa*
[c2-l020 note] Ke denotes the ordinary or everyday, contrasted with festive or exceptional use. The Portuguese states that this sense is not used alone, continuing below.
[c2-l021 >] *ſem compoſição. Vt,* Qeno qirumono. *Ve-*
[c2-l021 note] Completes the warning that ke occurs in compounds and gives ke no kirumono, everyday clothing. The scan reads compoſição and qirumono, correcting both malformed OCR words.
[c2-l022 >] *ſtido de cote, & ordinario. ¶* Qefare. *Tẽ*
[c2-l022 note] Completes vestido and introduces kehare, the ordinary/festive contrast. The right-edge Tẽ begins tempo with a nasal abbreviation, not OCR rẽ.
[c2-l023 >] *po ordinario ſem festa, & concurſo de gente,*
[c2-l023 note] Describes ordinary time without a festival or gathering of people. This is the first half of the contrast explained by kehare.
[c2-l024 >] *& tempo feſtiual, &c. ¶* Qeno fucu.
[c2-l024 note] Adds festive time and introduces ke no fuku, ordinary clothing. The example remains upright Japanese after the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l025 >] *Vestido ordinario: hoc vide infràſuo loco.*
[c2-l025 note] Defines ordinary clothing and directs the reader to its own entry below. The mark in infrà is a grave, not the OCR tilde; the tight printed join with ſuo is retained.
[c2-l026] Qe. *Cabelos. ¶* Qega tatçu. *Arripia-*
[c2-l026 note] Ke here means hair; ke ga tatsu means the hair standing on end. Arripia- continues as remſe below, with a small visible line-end division mark rather than a sentence stop.
[c2-l027 >] *remſe os cabelos. ¶* Qeuo ſuru. *Muda*
[c2-l027 note] Completes the hair-standing-on-end explanation, then introduces shedding feathers or hair. The printed Qeuo ſuru is retained even though the construction may seem unusual to a modern reader.
[c2-l028 >] *rem as aues a pena, ou animaes os cabelos.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes Mudarem and describes birds changing feathers or animals shedding hair. No extra verb form is supplied from modern expectation.
[c2-l029] Qe. *Eſcama de peixe. ¶* Qeuo toru. *Eſ-*
[c2-l029 note] Ke also denotes fish scales in the usage described here. Ke wo toru is glossed as scaling a fish, continued below.
[c2-l030 >] *camar o peixe. Palaura que ſe vſa em algũas*
[c2-l030 note] Completes eſcamar and restricts this lexical use geographically. The restriction continues into Cami on the next row.
[c2-l031 >] *partes do Cami.*
[c2-l031 note] Limits the fish-scale word to some parts of Cami. The regional Japanese name occurs within the Portuguese explanation and is marked for optional whole-word font review.
[c2-l032] Qe, ru, eta. *Dar com o pè para diante. Vt,*
[c2-l032 note] Ke, keru, keta is the historical kicking verb, here striking forward with the foot. The following quotation illustrates the action.
[c2-l033 >] Axiuo motte fuxitaru macurauo fattatozo
[c2-l033 note] Begins the example of kicking a pillow lying down with one's foot. The Japanese macura and emphatic fattatozo are preserved as printed.
[c2-l034 >] qetariqeru. Taif. *Lib. 2. Deu, ou*
[c2-l034 note] Completes the kicking action and supplies the source label and book 2. Taif. is retained as the chosen reading of the worn upright label; its final f is less secure than the rest and is not silently expanded to a title.
[c2-l035 >] *botou com o pè a almofada que estaua no chão*
[c2-l035 note] Translates striking or propelling the pillow that was on the floor with the foot. The historical Portuguese estaua and chão remain unchanged.
[c2-l036 >] *derepente. ¶* Mariuo qeru. *Iugar à pel*
[c2-l036 note] Finishes the sudden kicking example, then introduces mari wo keru, playing ball with the foot. The scan reads pel, not OCR pol, and no terminal hyphen is supplied before la below.
[c2-l037 >] *la com o pè como he custume em Iapão.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes pella and specifies the Japanese custom of kicking the ball. Iapão is Portuguese, not an embedded Japanese word for the typeface-term list.
[c2-l038] Qeague, uru, eta. *Dando com o pè aleuantar,*
[c2-l038 note] Keage means raising something by a kick. The scan ends with aleuantar and a comma, not OCR aleuantao.; its objects continue on the next page.
