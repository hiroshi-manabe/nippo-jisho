[c1-l001] *do. vt,* Vmauo fiqiyoſuru. *Chegar o caualo.*
[c1-l001 note] The opening do completes puxando on f195. Uma wo hikiyosuru means drawing the horse closer, matching chegar o caualo.
[c1-l002] Fiqizan. *Diminuir nas contas. Vide,* Giſan.
[c1-l002 note] Hikizan is subtraction. Diminuir nas contas confirms the arithmetic sense and the damaged initial F; Gisan is a cross-reference, not a continuation.
[c1-l003] Fiqizuri, ru, utta. *Leuar araſto, ou arrastar.*
[c1-l003 note] Hikizuri means dragging along. The printed conjugational ending utta is retained, also recorded by NINJAL, rather than regularized from modern hikizutta.
[c1-l004] Fiqua. *Vt,* Fiquaracuyô. *Roſas, & fo-*
[c1-l004 note] Hikwa raku-yō evokes falling blossoms and leaves. The scan shows a circumflex in racuyô; roſas is the dictionary's floral gloss, not necessarily a modern botanical restriction.
[c1-l005 >] *lhas que caem. S.*
[c1-l005 note] Lhas completes folhas: the leaves fall together with the flowers. S marks written usage of the preceding expression.
[c1-l006] Fiquan. *Criado.*
[c1-l006 note] Hikwan here denotes a servant or retainer, directly glossed criado. It is separate from the following two emotional terms.
[c1-l007] Fi quan. Canaximi, yorocobi. *Triſteza,*
[c1-l007 note] The visibly separated Fi quan pairs sorrow and joy: canaximi and yorocobi. Treating it as the preceding retainer word would obscure the bilingual contrast.
[c1-l008 >] *& alegria. S.*
[c1-l008 note] Alegria completes the paired definition, sorrow and joy. The final S is a usage label rather than Japanese text.
[c1-l009] Fira. *Vt*, Teno fira. *Palma da mão.*
[c1-l009 note] Te no hira is the palm of the hand. The example establishes the broad flat-surface sense shared with the following sword example.
[c1-l010 >] *¶* Catanano fira. *Champa da eſpada.*
[c1-l010 note] Katana no hira denotes the flat of a sword blade. Eſpada restores the missing letter in the OCR's e pada; the gloss is not about a separate object.
[c1-l011 >] *¶* Firauchini ſuru, *l*, catanano firade v-
[c1-l011 note] Hirauchi ni suru and katana no hira de utsu are alternatives for striking with the blade's flat. The final v belongs to divided vtçu.
[c1-l012 >] tçu. *Dar deſpaldeiradas.*
[c1-l012 note] Tçu completes utsu. Dar deſpaldeiradas describes blows with a sword's flat and confirms the preceding Japanese construction.
[c1-l013] Firabari. *O estender, ou alargar a caſa como*
[c1-l013 note] Hirabari refers here to extending a house or its enclosure, not the lancet sense below. The following line supplies the means of extension.
[c1-l014 >] *fazendo algũa ſebe, ou eſtendendo cortinas.*
[c1-l014 note] The explanation specifies a hedge or stretched curtains. Preserve the printed nasal abbreviation in algũa rather than expanding it.
[c1-l015 >] *&c. Vt,* Firabariuo vtçu. *Acrecentar,*
[c1-l015 note] Hirabari wo utsu is the construction illustrated. The damaged abbreviation is Vt, introducing an example, not a pilcrow followed by t.
[c1-l016 >] *ou estender a caſa deſta maneira. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l016 note] The gloss completes enlarging the house in this manner. Item then introduces the homonymous implement below.
[c1-l017] Firabari. *Lanceta de ſangrar caualos, ou*
[c1-l017 note] Hirabari now means a lancet for bloodletting, used on horses or people. This is a distinct lexical sense under the repeated form.
[c1-l018 >] *homẽs. Vt,* Firabariuo tatçuru. *Dar lã-*
[c1-l018 note] Homẽs, people, completes the object list. Hirabari wo tatsuru means applying the lancet; lã begins the divided Portuguese lancet-stroke word.
[c1-l019 >] *çetada pera tirar ſangue.*
[c1-l019 note] Çetada completes lãçetada: a puncture made to draw blood. The nasal abbreviation and physical line break are retained.
[c1-l020] Firabira. *Adu. Vt,* Firabirato xitaru mo
[c1-l020 note] Hirabira to shitaru mono describes a spread-out, broad thing. Both scan and the repeated example resolve the damaged initial headword as Firabira.
[c1-l021 >] no. *Couſa larga, & eſtendida.*
[c1-l021 note] No completes mono. The Portuguese describes something broad and extended; the compressed Couſalarga is left as printed.
[c1-l022] Firacado. *Certa feição de portal, ou fron-*
[c1-l022 note] Hirakado is a form of gateway or frontage. Its damaged middle letters are supported by the gate gloss and the NINJAL entry.
[c1-l023 >] *teſpicio.*
[c1-l023 note] Teſpicio completes fronteſpicio, the building frontage. This is a continuation, not another Japanese headword.
[c1-l024] Firachi. i, Feigi. *Terra praina.*
[c1-l024 note] Hirachi is equated with heiji, level ground. Terra praina preserves the historical Portuguese form rather than modern plana.
[c1-l025] Firacubi. *Aparte larga do peſcoço do caualo.*
[c1-l025 note] Hirakubi denotes the broad part of a horse's neck. The animal qualification distinguishes it from an unrestricted anatomical term.
[c1-l026] Firacuchi. *Bibora. No* Cami *ſe diz,* Cu-
[c1-l026 note] Hirakuchi is a viper. The regional label Cami introduces the alternative kuchihami, divided across the next line.
[c1-l027 >] chifami.
[c1-l027 note] Chifami completes Cu-chifami, kuchihami. It is the regional snake name, not an independent entry beginning chi.
[c1-l028] Firacugue. *Certo grao de dignidade dos Cũgues*
[c1-l028 note] Hirakuge denotes a rank among ordinary court nobles. The Portuguese-adapted Cũgues refers to kuge and remains distinct from the Japanese headword.
[c1-l029 >] *ordinarios.*
[c1-l029 note] Ordinarios qualifies the nobles in the preceding definition, completing that rank description.
[c1-l030] Firada, l, firata. *Embarcação larga, & bai*
[c1-l030 note] Hirada or hirata is a broad, low boat. Latin l introduces the alternative pronunciation rather than a Japanese consonant.
[c1-l031 >] *xa como ſe vſa nos rios.*
[c1-l031 note] Xa completes baixa; the example is the kind of low vessel used on rivers. No additional word is supplied at the unmarked break.
[c1-l032] Firagama. *Caldeirão, ou panella de ferro lar-*
[c1-l032 note] Hiragama is a broad iron kettle or pot. The scan and the vessel gloss support gama rather than the OCR's gania.
[c1-l033 >] *ga, & baixa com hũa cinta ao redor.*
[c1-l033 note] Ga completes larga, then baixa and the encircling band further describe the pot. The OCR's Sà was a misread continuation, not an accented word.
[c1-l034] Firagana. *Certa laya de letras de Iapão.*
[c1-l034 note] Hiragana names a kind of Japanese writing. The familiar lexical identity is corroborated by the scan, which restores the damaged F and n.
[c1-l035] Firagumo. *Certas aranhas que tem o corpo, ou*
[c1-l035 note] Hiragumo denotes flat-bodied spiders. The gloss starts with their body and continues with the back in the next line.
[c1-l036 >] *coſtas largas q̃ estão metidas nalgum bolſo, ou*
[c1-l036 note] Costas largas describes broad backs; the spiders occupy a pouch-like web. Costas and estão restore meaningful letters lost by OCR.
[c1-l037 >] *tea que tem feito em algum canto, &c.*
[c1-l037 note] The web is made in a corner or similar place. This completes the spider description rather than defining tea as a Japanese word.
[c1-l038] Firaguqeno vobi. *Cingidouro largo como*
[c1-l038 note] Hiraguke no obi is a broad sash sewn like a tube or sheath. The damaged initial F is recoverable from the scan and headword reference.
[c1-l039 >] *bainha.*
[c1-l039 note] Bainha supplies the sheath-like construction of the sash. It completes como rather than beginning another item.
[c1-l040] Firai. *Couſa larga, & delgada.*
[c1-l040 note] Hirai means broad and thin. The two indented forms below are its nominal and adverbial derivatives, not unrelated headwords.
[c1-l041 >] Firaſa.
[c1-l041 note] Firasa is the nominal derivative, breadth or flatness, of hirai. Its indentation links it to the preceding adjective.
[c1-l042 >] Firǒ.
[c1-l042 note] Firǒ is the contracted adverbial form associated with hirai. Preserve the printed caron without converting the Level 1 text to a modern spelling.
[c1-l043] Firajǒ. *Fortaleza que eſta em terra praina.*
[c1-l043 note] Hirajō is a fortress on level ground, a flatland castle. The Portuguese definition makes the damaged j legible as part of jō rather than iō.
[c1-l044] Firamecaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer reluzir, ou re-*
[c1-l044 note] Hiramekasu means making something flash or shimmer by movement. It is transitive, unlike hirameku at the next column's opening.
[c1-l045 >] *ſplandecer bulindo como arma, Catana, &c.*
[c1-l045 note] The examples are moving weapons or a katana. Catana is embedded in Portuguese explanatory syntax and is eligible for a typeface toggle.
[c1-l046 >] *¶* Vǒguiuo firamecaſu. *Menear, & vi*
[c1-l046 note] Ōgi wo hiramekasu means waving a fan. The damaged spelling Vǒguuo is retained as seen, rather than silently normalizing it to the clearer Vǒguiuo elsewhere on the page.
[c1-l047 >] *rar o abano.*
[c1-l047 note] Rar completes virar: moving and turning the fan. The line belongs to the example above, not a footer.

[c2-l001] Firameqi, u, eita. *Tremolarem as bandei*
[c2-l001 note] Hirameku is fluttering, exemplified by flags. Bandei continues into bandeiras on the next line.
[c2-l002 >] *ras, bulir papel, &c. com o vento. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l002 note] Paper moves in the wind; bulir supplies the coherent Portuguese verb where OCR read bualir. Item introduces the shining-weapon sense.
[c2-l003 >] *Reluzir a eſpada, ou armas que ſe mouem, ou*
[c2-l003 note] A sword or other moving weapon gleams. This continues hirameku, not the preceding transitive hiramekasu entry.
[c2-l004 >] *meneão.*
[c2-l004 note] Meneão completes the movement clause: weapons that are moved or waved. It is Portuguese verbal text, not a separate label.
[c2-l005] Firami, u, ǒda. *Encolherſe, ou murcharſe*
[c2-l005 note] Hiramu means shrinking or wilting. The following fear context gives the figurative sense of recoiling or losing courage.
[c2-l006 >] *cõ medo, &c. ¶ Item, No Ximo chorar:*
[c2-l006 note] Fear motivates the shrinking; in Ximo the word can also refer to crying. The regional limitation matters to the second sense.
[c2-l007 >] *meliùs,* Naqi firamu.
[c2-l007 note] Naqi firamu, naki-hiramu, is offered as the preferable expression for the crying sense. Meliùs is editorial Latin and remains italic.
[c2-l008] Firani. *Adu. Humildemente, ou em todo*
[c2-l008 note] Hirani is an adverb meaning humbly or emphatically in any case. Humildemente, not Hamildemente, is supported by the printed vowel and the examples.
[c2-l009 >] *caſo. Vt,* Firani tanomu. *Pedir encare*
[c2-l009 note] Hirani tanomu is an earnest request. Encare continues into encarecidamente and translates the force of the adverb.
[c2-l010 >] *cidamente. ¶* Firani fuſu. *Proſtrarſe*
[c2-l010 note] Hirani fusu means prostrating oneself. Proſtrarſe restores the damaged r in the Portuguese verb, consistent with de bruços below.
[c2-l011 >] *de bruços.*
[c2-l011 note] De bruços specifies lying face down. It completes the prostration gloss, not a further Japanese expression.
[c2-l012] Firaqe, uru, eta. *Abrirſe como porta, &c.*
[c2-l012 note] Hirakeru is to open, as a door opens. The inflectional listing is followed by a cosmological example.
[c2-l013 >] *¶* Ame, tçuchi firaqe fajimatte yori.
[c2-l013 note] Ame tsuchi hirake hajimatte yori means since heaven and earth began to open or separate. The doubled t in fajimatte is supported by the scan and Japanese morphology.
[c2-l014 >] *Depois de ſe ſeparar, & aparecer. i, Deſe*
[c2-l014 note] The gloss says after separation and appearance; i introduces an explanatory restatement. Deſe is retained across the physical break before formar.
[c2-l015 >] *formar o ceo, & a terra. ¶ Item, Abri*
[c2-l015 note] The restatement is the formation of heaven and earth. Item then shifts to flowers opening, with Abri continued below.
[c2-l016 >] *remſe as roſas, ou flores. Vt,* Fanaga fi-
[c2-l016 note] Flowers open; hana ga begins the Japanese example. The fi at the edge belongs to firaquru, not an independent word.
[c2-l017 >] raquru. *Mas mais propriamente ſe diz* Fa
[c2-l017 note] Firaquru completes hirakuru, but the dictionary explicitly prefers the alternative construction on the next line. That usage distinction should not be normalized away.
[c2-l018 >] naga firaqu.
[c2-l018 note] Naga completes Fa-naga, hana ga hiraku. The preferred form is hiraku rather than the preceding hirakuru.
[c2-l019] Firaqi, u, aita. *Abrir. ¶* Monuo fira-
[c2-l019 note] Hiraku means to open; mon wo hira-qu opens a gate. The repeated verb and visible letters correct both Abir and fita in the draft.
[c2-l020 >] qu. *Abrir a porta da rua. ¶* Manaco-
[c2-l020 note] Qu completes hiraku. Manaco begins the next example, opening the eyes; its particle is carried onto the following line.
[c2-l021 >] uo firaqu. *Abriros olhos. ¶* Fuxinuo
[c2-l021 note] Manako wo hiraku means opening one's eyes. Fushin wo then introduces the figurative resolution of doubt.
[c2-l022 >] firaqu. *Soltar a duuida. ¶* Fanaga fi-
[c2-l022 note] Fushin wo hiraku is dispelling a doubt, matching soltar a duuida. The next hana ga example returns to a flower opening.
[c2-l023 >] raqu. *Abrira roſa. ¶* Fachiuo firaqu.
[c2-l023 note] Raqu completes hiraku in the flower example. Hachi wo hiraku then introduces begging with an alms bowl.
[c2-l024 >] *Pedir eſmola, propriamente ſe diz de Bonzos,*
[c2-l024 note] The alms-begging use is specifically said of bonzes. Propriamente is an explanatory adverb, not part of the Japanese example.
[c2-l025 >] *ou religioſos. ¶* Qiǒuo fanni, l, fangui
[c2-l025 note] Religiosos restates bonzes. Kyō wo han ni or hangi ni hiraku describes printing texts; the damaged Qiǒ is read in that Buddhist-book context, not as an isolated rǒ.
[c2-l026 >] ni firaqu. *Imprimir liuros. ¶* Qiyet
[c2-l026 note] Ni firaqu completes the printing expression. Qiyet begins the checked-final-t expression for great joy, continued with no mayu below.
[c2-l027 >] no mayuuo firaqu. *Alegrarſe muito.*
[c2-l027 note] Kietsu no mayu wo hiraku evokes unknitting the brow in joy. Alegrarſe muito confirms the emotional meaning without expanding printed Qiyet.
[c2-l028 >] *¶* Riunuo firaqu. *Alcançar victoria.*
[c2-l028 note] Riun wo hiraku means obtaining victory or favorable fortune. The second enlarged scan pass resolves Riunuo, rather than the first-pass Riuuo; the Portuguese alcançar victoria supports the interpretation.
[c2-l029 >] *¶* Vǒguiuo firaqu. *Abrir o abano.*
[c2-l029 note] Ōgi wo hiraku is opening a fan. The caron is visible here, while the word's mechanics are independent of modern kana normalization.
[c2-l030 >] Curauo firaqu. *Abrirem os gentios as por*
[c2-l030 note] Kura wo hiraku introduces opening a storehouse. The Portuguese identifies this as a non-Christian New Year custom.
[c2-l031 >] *tas do gudão pollo anno nouo nalgum dia bom,*
[c2-l031 note] The storehouse doors are opened on a good day at New Year. Bom, not hom, supplies the adjective required by dia and visible in the scan.
[c2-l032 >] *& alegre. ¶* Dǒguuo firaqu. *Tirar*
[c2-l032 note] The good, cheerful day completes that custom. Dōgu wo hiraku then concerns displaying an implement for the first time.
[c2-l033 >] *a primeira vez em publico algũa peça, &v-*
[c2-l033 note] A piece is first brought out publicly. Primeira and publico repair OCR letter errors; the final v begins vſar below.
[c2-l034 >] *ſar della. ¶* Chauanbiraqiuo ſuru. *V-*
[c2-l034 note] Vſar della completes using that piece. Chawanbiraki wo suru describes first use of a tea bowl; ſuru corrects the semantically impossible iuru.
[c2-l035 >] *ſar a primeira vez dalgũa Chauan, & dar*
[c2-l035 note] The gloss says using a bowl for the first time and showing it. The visible hauan is retained despite the expected chawan-derived form.
[c2-l036 >] *mostra della. ¶ Item,* Firaqi, u. *En-*
[c2-l036 note] Della completes showing the bowl. A further hiraki sense begins, describing withdrawal in combat.
[c2-l037 >] *colherſe no jugar das armas, ou retirarſe.*
[c2-l037 note] In fencing, hiraku can mean contracting or drawing back. This contrasts with the ordinary opening sense above.
[c2-l038 >] *¶* Xitçuqegatano tçume firaqi. *Nas*
[c2-l038 note] Shitsuke-gata no tsume hiraki concerns advancing and retreating in courteous conduct. Xitçuqe, not Mitçuqe, is supported by the scan and etiquette gloss.
[c2-l039 >] *corteſias, agaſalhados, &c. o ſair com fer*
[c2-l039 note] Courtesies and hospitality illustrate energetic approach. Fer continues into fervor below; no hyphen is supplied merely for lexical completion.
[c2-l040 >] *uor, & retirarſe a ſeu tempo. ¶.* Mai, dã
[c2-l040 note] Withdrawal comes at its proper time. Mai and the divided dãgui introduce dance and discourse as the next applications.
[c2-l041 >] gui, nadono tçume firaqi. *O ſair com fer*
[c2-l041 note] Mai, dangui nado no tsume-hiraki describes measured advance and relaxation in dancing, preaching and other performances. The second pass resolves nadono, connecting the examples with “and the like”.
[c2-l042 >] *uor no canto, bailo, prègaçaõ, &c, & reti-*
[c2-l042 note] Singing, dancing and preaching exemplify the energetic phase. Reti continues into retirarſe and completes the contrasting withdrawal.
[c2-l043 >] *rarſe, ou aquietarſe aſeu tempo.*
[c2-l043 note] The performer withdraws or becomes quiet at the appropriate time. This closes the long series under hiraku.
[c2-l044] Firaqiauaxe, uru, eta. *Deſencontrarſe cõ as*
[c2-l044 note] Hirakiawaseru describes meeting or crossing in sword blows. The printed headword restores the damaged F without changing its inflectional endings.
[c2-l045 >] C*atanas nos golpes, ou talhos da eſgrima.*
[c2-l045 note] The encounter is between katanas in fencing strokes. This specifies the martial sense rather than a generic meeting.
[c2-l046] Firaqido. *Porta queſe abre ſobre couceira.*
[c2-l046 note] Hirakido is a hinged door. Couceira names the hinge-side support and is distinct from the OCR's couccira.
[c2-l047] Firaqiuacare, uru, eta. *Retirarſe, & diui-*
[c2-l047 note] Hirakiwakareru means drawing apart or separating. The missing c is recoverable in the scan and supported by wakareru; diui continues physically below.
