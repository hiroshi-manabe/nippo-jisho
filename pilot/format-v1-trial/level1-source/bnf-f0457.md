[c1-l001 >] miuo ſuri, ru. *Moertinta. ¶* Sumiuo
[c1-l001 note] Miuo completes Su- from the preceding page, giving sumi wo suri, suru, grinding ink. The next example begins sumi wo again; Moertinta retains the printed spacing.
[c1-l002 >] nuru. *Tingir algũa couſa de preto por cima*
[c1-l002 note] Sumi wo nuru means coating something with ink, here coloring its surface black. The instrument is supplied by the following continuation.
[c1-l003 >] *com o pincel.*
[c1-l003 note] The black coating is applied with a brush. This short line is visibly indented like the preceding continuation, not aligned as a new entry.
[c1-l004] Sumi. *Caruão. ¶* Sumiuo yaqu. *Fazer*
[c1-l004 note] This sumi is charcoal, distinct from ink. Sumi wo yaku means making charcoal by burning wood; the Portuguese sentence continues below.
[c1-l005 >] *caruão. ¶* Sumiuo vocoſu. *Fazer fogo*
[c1-l005 note] After completing the charcoal-making gloss, sumi wo okosu introduces kindling a charcoal fire. The historical spelling vocoſu is retained.
[c1-l006 >] *de caruão. ¶* Sumiuo tçugu. *Ir ceuã-*
[c1-l006 note] Sumi wo tsugu is feeding or replenishing charcoal. The Portuguese ceuã- begins the verb for feeding the fire.
[c1-l007 >] *do o fogo cõ caruão. ¶* Sumiga nadaru-
[c1-l007 note] The fire is fed with charcoal. Sumi ga nadaruru then begins the contrasting example of the charcoal fire dying down.
[c1-l008 >] ru. *Irſe apagando, & fazendo em cinza*
[c1-l008 note] Ru completes nadaruru. The Portuguese describes the fire gradually going out and turning to ash, rather than simply being extinguished at once.
[c1-l009 >] *o fogo de caruão.*
[c1-l009 note] The delayed subject is the charcoal fire. This closes the sequence of examples under charcoal, before the separate corner sense.
[c1-l010] Sumi. *Canto da caſa, ou de outra couſa diuidi-*
[c1-l010 note] Sumi now means a corner of a house or another object. The divided Portuguese word continues the geometric description below.
[c1-l011 >] *da em quatro partes, ou mais.*
[c1-l011 note] The object is described as divided into four or more parts. This completes the historical corner definition without substituting a modern geometric formulation.
[c1-l012] Sumiacano faco. *Caxinha das poſturas, ou a-*
[c1-l012 note] Sumiaka no hako is a small box for women's cosmetics or adornments. The scan supports no faco rather than the draft's malformed ſio faco.
[c1-l013 >] *feites das molheres.*
[c1-l013 note] Feites completes afeites, women's adornments or cosmetics. This is still the contents of the small box, not another Japanese entry.
[c1-l014] Sumiaraxi, ſu, aita. *Deixar ir desfazendoſe*
[c1-l014 note] Sumiarashi means letting one's dwelling deteriorate through neglect. The visible m restores the headword; aita is retained as its historical principal-part ending.
[c1-l015 >] *a caſa em que hum mora por não a concertar, nẽ*
[c1-l015 note] The house falls into disrepair because its resident does not mend it. Concertar retains the source's spelling for putting things in order.
[c1-l016 >] *repairar. Vt,* Iyeuo ſumi araſu.
[c1-l016 note] Ie wo sumi-arasu illustrates neglecting the house one occupies. The printed separation in ſumi araſu is retained rather than joined from morphological expectation.
[c1-l017] Sumibi. *Fogo de caruão.*
[c1-l017 note] Sumibi is a charcoal fire, a transparent compound matching the short Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l018] Sumica. *Morada, ou habitação.*
[c1-l018 note] Sumika means a dwelling or habitation. The scan supports the initial h in habitação, correcting OCR babitação.
[c1-l019] Sumicane, uru, eta. *Nãoſe aueriguar a ra-*
[c1-l019 note] This sumikane describes a reason or dispute failing to be settled. The negative Portuguese gloss distinguishes it from a successfully concluded matter.
[c1-l020 >] *zão, ou demanda, &c.*
[c1-l020 note] Zão completes razão; a lawsuit or dispute is the alternative object. The line closes the unresolved-matter sense.
[c1-l021] Sumicane, uru, eta. *Eſtar, ou morar cõ dif-*
[c1-l021 note] The homonymous sumikane means having difficulty remaining or living somewhere. Its relation to residence distinguishes it from the preceding settlement sense.
[c1-l022 >] *ficuldade em algum lugar.*
[c1-l022 note] This completes dificuldade em algum lugar, difficulty living in a particular place. It remains a continuation of the preceding verb entry.
[c1-l023] Sumi cane. *Tinta, & eſquadra de carpintei.*
[c1-l023 note] Sumi kane names the carpenter's ink and square. The printed terminal mark after carpintei is retained rather than silently converted into a conventional hyphen.
[c1-l024 >] *ro. ¶* Sumi caneuo atçuru. *Por tinta,*
[c1-l024 note] Ro completes carpinteiro. Sumi kane wo atsuru illustrates applying the ink and square; the scan restores caneuo rather than OCR caieuo.
[c1-l025 >] *& a eſquadra pera tomar medida.*
[c1-l025 note] The carpenter uses the square and ink for taking measurements. This closes the example under sumi kane.
[c1-l026] Sumichiguiri, u, itta. *Meliùs,* Sumiqiri, u.
[c1-l026 note] Sumichigiri is referred to the preferred sumikiri, becoming completely clear. The scan restores the headword and itta; Meliùs is wholly italic Latin apparatus.
[c1-l027 >] *Aclararſe muito, ou de todo agoa, &c. Vt,*
[c1-l027 note] The definition is water becoming very or entirely clear. The printed sequence de todo agoa is retained without supplying an extra article.
[c1-l028 >] Mizzuga ſumichiguitta. B.
[c1-l028 note] Mizu ga sumichigitta illustrates water that has cleared completely. The upright final B marks low-register usage according to the supplied key.
[c1-l029] Sumidocoro. *Lugar q̃ ſe habita, ou morada.*
[c1-l029 note] Sumidokoro is a place where one lives, or a dwelling. Lugar corrects OCR Ligar, while the abbreviated q̃ remains unexpanded.
[c1-l030] Sumifate, tçuru, eta. *Morar em algum lu-*
[c1-l030 note] Sumihate, sumihatsuru refers to remaining resident somewhere to the end of one's life. The Portuguese locative phrase continues below.
[c1-l031 >] *gar atè a morte.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes lugar atè a morte: living in that place until death. The endpoint is essential to the compound's meaning.
[c1-l032] Sumigaxira. *Caruão não bem queimado que fu-*
[c1-l032 note] Sumigashira is incompletely burned charcoal that smokes when put on a fire. The damaged m is restored rather than retaining Sunigaxira.
[c1-l033 >] *mega quando ſe poem no fogo.*
[c1-l033 note] Mega completes fumega, 'smokes', and the condition is being placed on the fire. This explains the charcoal's incomplete charring.
[c1-l034] Sumigui. *Traues q̃ ſaem fora nos cãtos da caſa.*
[c1-l034 note] Sumigi denotes beams projecting at the corners of a house. Both q̃ and cãtos retain the printed nasal or abbreviation marks.
[c1-l035] Suminare, uru, eta. *Eſtar acuſtumado a mo-*
[c1-l035 note] Suminare means becoming accustomed to living somewhere. The source's acuſtumado is retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l036 >] *rar em algum lugar.*
[c1-l036 note] Rar completes morar and the habituation concerns residence in a particular place, not familiarity with a person.
[c1-l037] Sumiqirazu. i, Voxiqi. *Meſas quadradas de*
[c1-l037 note] Sumikirazu refers to fully square trays or tables, equated here with oshiki. The definition emphasizes corners that have not been cut off.
[c1-l038 >] *todo, ou que não ſão rombas nos cantos.*
[c1-l038 note] The corners are not blunted or rounded. Rombas, with b, corrects OCR romhas and explains the negative kirazu in the headword.
[c1-l039] Sumire. *Hũa erua aßi chamada cuja raiz he*
[c1-l039 note] This sumire is a herb with an edible root. The scan reads erua rather than erus; the root comparison follows below.
[c1-l040 >] *como alho, & ſe come.*
[c1-l040 note] The root resembles garlic and is eaten. This historical identification should not be conflated automatically with every modern plant called sumire.
[c1-l041] Sumire. *Nome de hũa flor.*
[c1-l041 note] A second sumire is simply named as a flower. The separate entry is preserved rather than merged with the edible-root definition.
[c1-l042] Sumiſaxi. *Pena do carpinteiro.*
[c1-l042 note] Sumisashi is the carpenter's ink-marking pen. The Portuguese pena denotes the writing or marking implement here.
[c1-l043] Sumitçubo. *Tinteiro dos carpinteiros.*
[c1-l043 note] Sumitsubo is the carpenter's ink container or inking tool, explained literally as the carpenters' inkwell.
[c1-l044] Sumiuabi, uru, ita. *Habitar com enfada-*
[c1-l044 note] Sumiwabi describes living somewhere with weariness or distress. The Portuguese divides enfadamento across this and the next line.
[c1-l045 >] *mento, ou tristeza em algum lugar.*
[c1-l045 note] Weariness and sadness characterize residence in that place, completing the emotional sense of the preceding compound verb.
[c1-l046] Sumiuatari, u, atta. *Aclararſe muito, ou*
[c1-l046 note] Sumiwatari means becoming very clear or clear throughout. The objects clarified are explicitly supplied in the following continuation.
[c1-l047 >] *por todas as partes a agoa, ar, &c.*
[c1-l047 note] Both water and air can become clear throughout. The scan restores todas as partes and the omitted ar, which the draft had obscured.

[c2-l001] Sumiyacana. *Couſa apreßada, ou ligeira. Itẽ,*
[c2-l001 note] Sumiyakana describes something swift or prompt; the dictionary also supplies a clear or lucid sense in the continuation.
[c2-l002 >] *couſa clara. ¶* Sumiyacana fito. *Ho-*
[c2-l002 note] After the clarity sense, sumiyakana hito illustrates a prompt, efficient person. This continuation is indented in the scan.
[c2-l003 >] *mem diligente, & expedito.*
[c2-l003 note] Homem is completed across the break, and the person is diligent and efficient. The row shares the continuation indentation.
[c2-l004 >] Sumiyacani.
[c2-l004 note] Sumiyakani is the corresponding adverb, 'swiftly' or 'promptly'. The print provides no separate Portuguese gloss, so none is added to the canonical text.
[c2-l005 >] Sumiyacaſa.
[c2-l005 note] Sumiyakasa is the nominal derivative, swiftness or promptness. Its separate short physical row is retained without inventing an omitted definition.
[c2-l006] Sumiyaqi. *Caruoeiro.*
[c2-l006 note] Sumiyaki is a charcoal burner or charcoal maker, matching the occupational Portuguese carvoeiro in its printed spelling.
[c2-l007] Sumiye. *Pintura de agoada de tinta preta.*
[c2-l007 note] Sumi-e is painting with a wash of black ink. The Portuguese description explicitly concerns ink wash rather than coloring a surface solid black.
[c2-l008] Sumizome. *Veſtido preto, que veſtem por*
[c2-l008 note] Sumizome is explained through a black outer garment worn by Buddhist clergy. The relative clause continues below.
[c2-l009 >] *cima os* Bõzos. *¶* Sumizomeno coromo.
[c2-l009 note] Bonzos are the wearers of the outer garment. Sumizome no koromo gives the Japanese expression; Bõzos is an upright Portuguese borrowing here, not an embedded Japanese form to flag.
[c2-l010 >] *Idem.*
[c2-l010 note] Idem assigns the same black-robe meaning to the preceding Japanese expression. It is a complete short continuation, not omitted text.
[c2-l011] Sumizzu. *Vt,* Sumizzuuo tçuqu. l, A-
[c2-l011 note] Sumizu is introduced through sumizu wo tsuku and the alternative awasuru. These concern squaring or aligning corners, explained below.
[c2-l012 >] uaſuru. *Pondo a eſquadra, ou regra em al-*
[c2-l012 note] Awasuru is completed across the break. The carpenter places a square or straightedge against an object with corners to align it.
[c2-l013 >] *gũa obra de cantos, ou eſquinas endireitala,*
[c2-l013 note] The object has corners or angles; the purpose is to straighten it. Gũa completes algũa from the preceding physical row.
[c2-l014 >] *ou proporcionala de maneira que não ſaya hum*
[c2-l014 note] The work is proportioned or aligned so that no one corner projects too far. The negative condition continues below.
[c2-l015 >] *canto muito fora. ¶ Per met.* Sumiz-
[c2-l015 note] After completing the literal corner-alignment sense, the entry introduces a figurative use of sumizu, divided after Sumiz-.
[c2-l016 >] zuuo tçuqu. *Ser muito miudo em notar o q̃*
[c2-l016 note] Figurative sumizu wo tsuku means scrutinizing what others do with excessive minuteness. The scan supports o q̃, not the OCR's osq̃.
[c2-l017 >] *os outros fazem, tachando qualquer faltinha,*
[c2-l017 note] The scrutiny becomes fault-finding, criticizing every small defect in other people's actions. Faltinha carries the diminutive force explicitly.
[c2-l018 >] *&c. ¶* Sumizzuuo tçuqu fito. *Ho-*
[c2-l018 note] Sumizu wo tsuku hito applies the expression to a person. The Portuguese homem is divided at the physical break.
[c2-l019 >] *mem demaſiadamente exacto, & miudo em*
[c2-l019 note] The person is excessively exacting and minute, not merely diligent. The long ſ in demaſiadamente corrects the draft's j.
[c2-l020 >] *notar, & tachar as obras alheas, &c.*
[c2-l020 note] The excessive exactness concerns noticing and criticizing the work of others. This closes the figurative woodworking metaphor.
[c2-l021] Sumǒ. *Luta. ¶* Sumǒuo toru. *Lutar.*
[c2-l021 note] Sumoo is wrestling, with sumoo wo toru meaning to wrestle. The scan has Sumǒuo without the draft's intrusive a.
[c2-l022] Sumodori. *O tornar de balde ſem achar o que*
[c2-l022 note] Sumodori is returning empty-handed or fruitlessly, not finding what one sought. The relative clause continues below.
[c2-l023 >] *buſcaua. Vt,* Sumodori ſuru. *Idem.*
[c2-l023 note] The unsuccessful search is completed by buscava. Sumodori suru expresses performing that fruitless return, with Idem reusing the gloss.
[c2-l024] Sumomo. *Certa laya de ameixias, ou amei-*
[c2-l024 note] Sumomo is a kind of plum or plum tree. The source gives both fruit and tree, dividing the second Portuguese noun across the line break.
[c2-l025 >] *xieiras.*
[c2-l025 note] Completes ameixieiras, the alternative referring to the trees rather than just their plum-like fruit; the two-line division is retained.
[c2-l026] Sun. *Medida de meo dedo indice que he o eſpã-*
[c2-l026 note] Sun is explained by a bodily measure, half the index finger. The worn letter in Medida is d, not the draft's l; the nasal abbreviation and terminal division remain printed.
[c2-l027 >] *ço dantre os dous nòs do meyo. Vt,* Iſſun,
[c2-l027 note] The measure is the space between the two middle finger joints. The cedilla completes eſpãço; the example Iſſun has two long-s forms, not the draft's dotted i before ſ.
[c2-l028 >] niſun. *¶ Itẽ, Medida. Vt,* Sunuo to-
[c2-l028 note] Niſun follows the one-sun example with two sun. A second sense is measurement generally, introducing the expression Sunuo toru across the line break.
[c2-l029 >] ru. *Tomar medida.*
[c2-l029 note] Completes Sunuo toru and gives its Portuguese equivalent, to take a measurement; this short continuation is not another entry.
[c2-l030] Suna. *Area.*
[c2-l030 note] Suna is sand; Area is the historical Portuguese spelling here and is not expanded to modern areia.
[c2-l031] Sunadori. *Peſcaria, ou peſcador. ¶* Su-
[c2-l031 note] Sunadori can denote fishing or a fisherman. The Japanese expression beginning Su- continues on the next physical row.
[c2-l032 >] nadoriuo ſuru. *Peſcar.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes Sunadoriuo ſuru, to fish. The divided Japanese word and italic Portuguese infinitive remain distinct.
[c2-l033] Sunagane. *Area preta de que fazem ferro*
[c2-l033 note] Sunagane is the black sand from which iron is made. The scan supports Area, not Atca; no terminal point is supplied where the line shows none.
[c2-l034] Sunago. *Area. ¶ Item, Salpicado de ou-*
[c2-l034 note] Sunago first means sand, then a sprinkling of gold or silver. The second sense begins here and the word ouro continues on the next row.
[c2-l035 >] *ro, ou de prata moida à maneira de area ſe-*
[c2-l035 note] The precious metal is ground and scattered like sand. Moida has no visible nasal mark; the separate following à bears the accent. The final ſe- continues into ſemeada.
[c2-l036 >] *meada. ¶* Sunagono biǒbu. *Biǒbus*
[c2-l036 note] Completes ſemeada and introduces folding screens decorated with this metallic sprinkling. The Portuguese borrowing Biǒbus, like biǒbu, shows a small angular mark over o rather than the draft's tilde.
[c2-l037 >] *ſalpicados de ouro, ou prata. ¶* Suna-
[c2-l037 note] The screens are sprinkled with gold or silver. Suna- starts the next compound but remains on this physical row.
[c2-l038 >] godami. *O Salpicar, ou ſemear de ouro,*
[c2-l038 note] Completes Sunagodami, the act of sprinkling or sowing gold; the Portuguese definition continues with silver on the following row.
[c2-l039 >] *ou prata. ¶* Sunago damiuo ſuru. *Idẽ.*
[c2-l039 note] Completes the gold-or-silver definition and gives a verbal expression of the same meaning. The printed space in Sunago damiuo is retained rather than harmonized with the headword.
[c2-l040] Sunauachi. *Adu. Logo, realmente; &c.*
[c2-l040 note] Sunauachi is labelled an adverb and glossed as immediately or really. The punctuation after realmente is a semicolon in the scan, not the draft's comma.
[c2-l041] Sunauona. *Couſa recta, ou inteira como ley,*
[c2-l041 note] Sunauona describes something upright or sound, with law as the first example. The historical spelling is preserved rather than modernized to a current Japanese form.
[c2-l042 >] *gouerno, &c. ¶* Sunauona fito. *Ho-*
[c2-l042 note] Government completes the institutional examples; Sunauona fito applies the quality to a person, whose Portuguese description starts with divided Homem.
[c2-l043 >] *mem recto, & inteiro.*
[c2-l043 note] Completes the description of an upright, honest person, not a separate sense or headword.
[c2-l044] Sunauoni. *Adu.*
[c2-l044 note] The adverb corresponding to Sunauona is Sunauoni. The scan reads auo in the middle, not the draft's ato; only the grammatical label is printed as its explanation.
[c2-l045] Sunbiô. Sunno naye. *Arroz nacido de no-*
[c2-l045 note] Sunbiô, with the Japanese paraphrase Sunno naye, refers to newly sprouted rice. The b is worn; its lower bowl and the lexical sequence support retaining the supplied headword, including its circumflex.
[c2-l046 >] *uo de altura de meo dedo.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes novo and specifies seedlings half a finger high, tying their height to the unit sun used in the surrounding entries.
[c2-l047] Sunbun. *Medida de meo dedo, & decima*
[c2-l047 note] Sunbun is explained through half-finger and tenth-part measures; the definition continues on the next page. The separate lower-margin parte is a catchword, not another body row.
