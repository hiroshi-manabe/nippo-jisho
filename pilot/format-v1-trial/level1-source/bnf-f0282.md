## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001 >] taximuru coto nacare. *Não criem em ti*
[c1-l001 note] Continues a Japanese prohibition from the preceding page: koto nacare means that something must not be allowed. The Portuguese begins “Do not let ... take root in you”; the missing beginning of the Japanese word is not supplied on this page.
[c1-l002 >] *raizes os maos deſejos.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the prohibition: evil desires must not take root. Raizes and maos retain their printed historical spelling, and the row remains separate from the following headword.
[c1-l003] Ianen. Yocoximano vomoi. *Ruins penſa-*
[c1-l003 note] Ianen is janen, an evil or improper thought, explained by yocoxima no vomoi. Ruins penſa- starts the matching Portuguese plural “bad thoughts” across the physical line break.
[c1-l004 >] *mentos. Vt,* Ianenuo farǒ. *Botar fora*
[c1-l004 note] Ianenuo farǒ is to drive away evil thoughts. The Portuguese completes penſamentos and begins “cast out”; the caron in farǒ is retained rather than regularizing the verb spelling.
[c1-l005 >] *os maos penſamentos.*
[c1-l005 note] Os maos penſamentos completes the object of Botar fora: casting out evil thoughts. This short continuation belongs to Ianen, not to the following Iaqen entry.
[c1-l006] Iaqen. Yocoximani miru. *O ver julgãdo mal*
[c1-l006 note] Iaqen is jaken, here seeing or judging perversely. The initial capital I is visible beside the rule and is restored to the truncated OCR aqen; julgãdo keeps its abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l007 >] *ou deitando à maa parte.* Bup. *¶ Item,*
[c1-l007 note] The first sense is construing something in a bad light; Bup. labels that sense as Buddhist usage. Item then introduces a further meaning. Bup. is upright, including its stop, rather than partly italic.
[c1-l008 >] *Crueldade. ¶* Iaqen fǒitnaru fito. *Ho-*
[c1-l008 note] The added sense is cruelty. Iaqen fǒitnaru fito describes a cruel, unrestrained person; the historical checked ending in fǒit- should not be expanded to a modern -itsu spelling. Ho- begins Homem below.
[c1-l009 >] *mem muito cruel, ou deshumano.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes Homem: a very cruel or inhuman person. The printed deshumano is retained here; it need not be made typographically identical to the divided deſ-humano in the next entry.
[c1-l010] Iaqenna. *Couſa cruel, ou homem cruel, & deſ-*
[c1-l010 note] Iaqenna is the adjectival form “cruel”. The scan gives capital I and Portuguese homem, not OCR laqenna and bomem; the final deſ- continues as humano below.
[c1-l011 >] *humano.*
[c1-l011 note] Humano completes deſ-humano, “inhuman”, in the preceding adjectival definition. It is not a separate headword or an affirmative definition of humanity.
[c1-l012] Iaqeô. Yocoximano tauamure. *Brincos deſ-*
[c1-l012 note] Iaqeô is explained as improper amusement, Yocoximano tauamure. The Portuguese begins a description of improper or indecent games; the printed circumflex is preserved without forcing a modern pronunciation into the canonical text.
[c1-l013 >] *honestos.*
[c1-l013 note] Honestos completes deſ-honestos, qualifying the games as improper or indecent. The physical division is retained; the letters are not a separate positive gloss “honest”.
[c1-l014] Iaqi. Yocoximano qi. *Bafo ruim, ou peſti-*
[c1-l014 note] Iaqi is jaki, an evil or harmful influence, paraphrased as yocoxima no qi. The Portuguese begins “bad or pestilential breath”, continuing across the line.
[c1-l015 >] *lente. S. ¶* Iaqi aru. *Auer algũa do*
[c1-l015 note] Lente completes peſti-lente and S. marks written usage. Iaqi aru illustrates the presence of a harmful influence or contagious illness; do continues into doença without a visible line-end hyphen.
[c1-l016 >] *ença contagioſa, como peste, ou vento peſtife-*
[c1-l016 note] Ença completes doença. Plague and a pestilential wind are given as examples of this harmful influence; peſtife- continues as ro on the next row.
[c1-l017 >] *ro. ¶* Iaqiga tachiſaru. *Acabarſe eſte*
[c1-l017 note] Iaqiga tachiſaru means the evil or pestilential influence departs. Acabarſe eſte begins the Portuguese rendering of that cessation; the Japanese ſ is retained in tachiſaru.
[c1-l018 >] *vento ruim, ou doença contagioſa, ou de ruim*
[c1-l018 note] Continues the things that cease: the harmful wind, contagious disease, or a disease associated with bad breath or air. The gloss preserves the source’s explanatory framework rather than a modern medical diagnosis.
[c1-l019 >] *bafo. S.*
[c1-l019 note] Bafo completes the reference to harmful breath or air. The following upright S. is the written-register label, not part of the italic Portuguese word.
[c1-l020] Iaracu. Yocoximano *t*anoximi. *Torpes, ou*
[c1-l020 note] Iaracu is jaraku, improper pleasure, explained by yocoxima no tanoximi. Torpes begins the Portuguese assessment of these pleasures as base or disordered.
[c1-l021 >] *deſordenados gostos. ¶* Iaracuuo tanoxi-
[c1-l021 note] Deſordenados gostos completes “disordered pleasures”. Iaracuuo tanoxi- begins a prohibition against enjoying them; the explicit terminal hyphen belongs to the Japanese verb.
[c1-l022 >] muna. *Não vos deis a torpes, & ruins de*
[c1-l022 note] Muna completes tanoximuna, “do not take pleasure in it”. The Portuguese likewise prohibits giving oneself to base delights. Its final de has no visible hyphen and is not supplied with one.
[c1-l023 >] *leitaçoẽs.*
[c1-l023 note] Leitaçoẽs completes the divided deleitaçoẽs, “delights”. The tilde is kept on the printed vowel rather than normalized to the modern spelling.
[c1-l024] Iare. *Meliùs,* Zare. *Zombaria, ou graça.*
[c1-l024 note] Iare means jesting or playfulness; the apparatus recommends Zare as the preferable form. Meliùs is an italic editorial word, while Zare is the Japanese cross-reference.
[c1-l025] Iare, uru, eta. *Zombar. Meliùs,* Za-
[c1-l025 note] The verb iare-/iaruru means to jest. Meliùs recommends zare-/zaruru; Za- is a Japanese form in upright type and continues below, not a partly italic Portuguese fragment.
[c1-l026 >] re, uru.
[c1-l026 note] Re, uru completes the recommended alternative Zare, uru from the preceding row. The two principal-form elements remain on their own printed line.
[c1-l027] Iarecoto. *Meliùs,* Zarecoto. *Zombaria.*
[c1-l027 note] Iarecoto means a jesting remark. The recommended form is visibly Zarecoto, correcting the OCR vowel in Zarocoto; the Portuguese again gives jesting.
[c1-l028 >] *Vt,* Iarecotouo yǔ. *Dizer zombarias, ou*
[c1-l028 note] Iarecoto wo yuu means saying joking things. A real word space separates Portuguese Dizer and zombarias; Vt, belongs to the italic introductory apparatus.
[c1-l029 >] *gracejar.*
[c1-l029 note] Gracejar, “to joke”, is the alternative Portuguese rendering of the example above. It completes the preceding ou and is not a separate dictionary entry.
[c1-l030] Iaregui. *Meliùs,* Zaregui. *O morder o ca*
[c1-l030 note] Iaregui, with preferred form Zaregui, is playful biting. The Portuguese begins the example of a horse biting; ca continues into caualo without a printed hyphen.
[c1-l031 >] *ualo, ou cão brincando.*
[c1-l031 note] The continuation completes caualo, and cão adds a dog: the biting is done in play. The source’s u/v spelling is retained rather than modernized to cavalo.
[c1-l032] Iaregurui. *Meliùs,* Zaregurui. *Brinco de*
[c1-l032 note] Iaregurui, preferably Zaregurui, denotes boisterous joking or playful fooling. Brinco de starts the Portuguese noun phrase and is completed below.
[c1-l033 >] *zombaria.*
[c1-l033 note] Zombaria completes brinco de zombaria, a jesting form of play. Its short physical row must not be merged with the following Iaremono entry.
[c1-l034] Iaremono. *Meliùs,* Zaremono. *Chocarrei-*
[c1-l034 note] Iaremono, preferably Zaremono, is a joker or buffoon. Chocarrei- begins the Portuguese name for that person and continues on the next row.
[c1-l035 >] *ro, ou homem que com graças, ou zombaria*
[c1-l035 note] Ro completes Chocarreiro. The gloss then describes a man who, through wit or jesting, produces the action stated on the next line.
[c1-l036 >] *faz rir.*
[c1-l036 note] Faz rir, “makes people laugh”, completes the description of the joker. The scan shows the boundary between the two words; OCR fazrir incorrectly joined them.
[c1-l037] Iaro. Yocoximano michi. *Ruim caminho,*
[c1-l037 note] Iaro is an evil path, explained by yocoxima no michi. The Portuguese presents the same metaphor of a bad road rather than a literal description of poor road conditions.
[c1-l038 >] *ou caminho da maldade. Vt,* Iaroni iru.
[c1-l038 note] The gloss specifies the path of wickedness. Iaroni iru is entering that path; Vt, introduces the upright Japanese example within the italic apparatus.
[c1-l039 >] *Entrar no caminho da maldade, ou ſenſuali-*
[c1-l039 note] Entrar, not OCR Enirar, gives the action of entering the path of wickedness or sensuality. The end of ſenſuali- continues on the following physical row.
[c1-l040 >] *dade.*
[c1-l040 note] Dade completes ſenſualidade, the second object of the metaphorical “entering”. This is the conclusion of Iaro, not material belonging to Iaſui.
[c1-l041] Iaſui. Yocoximani facaru. *Iuizo temerario,*
[c1-l041 note] Iaſui is jasui, an unjust or suspicious inference, explained by Yocoximani facaru. Iuizo temerario describes rash judgment; the long ſ within the headword is retained.
[c1-l042 >] *ou mà ſoſpeita.*
[c1-l042 note] Ou mà ſoſpeita adds “bad suspicion” to the preceding definition. The grave in mà and the two long-s forms are kept as printed.
[c1-l043] Iatai. Iano tai. *Figura de ſerpente.*
[c1-l043 note] Iatai is a serpent’s form, paraphrased Iano tai, “the form of a serpent”. Contextual enlargement shows that the apparent mark above the o belongs to the descending ſ in the preceding row; Ianǒ is therefore corrected to unaccented Iano.
[c1-l044] Iaxi, ſuru, ita. *Retribuir. Vide,* Ia.
[c1-l044 note] The verb is glossed “to recompense”, with a reference to Ia. The Portuguese reads Retribuir rather than OCR Retrihuir; the target Ia is upright and begins with capital I, not lowercase l.
[c1-l045] Iaxin. Yocoximana xinca. *Mao criado.*
[c1-l045 note] Iaxin here denotes an evil retainer, yocoximana xinca. This is the “bad minister” sense, distinct from the following column’s Iaxin meaning a malicious heart.
[c1-l046 >] *Vt,* Qenxin vchini arutoqinba, jaxin fo
[c1-l046 note] The example contrasts Qenxin, wise retainers, with jaxin, bad ones: when the former are inside, the latter are outside. The final fo continues into foca on the next row without an added hyphen.
[c1-l047 >] canari; jaxin vchini aru toqinba, qenxin
[c1-l047 note] Ca completes foca in focanari, “are outside”. The second clause reverses the situation and continues at the top of column 2. The visible semicolon after canari replaces the OCR comma; the row is indented like the surrounding continuation, not a flush-left entry.

## column-2-part-1 [column] Column 2, part 1

[c2-l001 >] tauoru. Xix. *Eſtando bõs, & prudentes*
[c2-l001 note] Tauoru completes the previous column’s contrast: the good retainers fall when bad ones are inside. Xix. is the upright citation label; the Portuguese begins its explanation with good and prudent servants.
[c2-l002 >] *criados na caſa, os ruins vão fora, & estando os*
[c2-l002 note] When prudent servants are in the house, the bad ones leave. The second half begins the reversed condition, with bad retainers inside; the complete clause continues below.
[c2-l003 >] *ruins dentro, os bõs caem. i, não aturão.*
[c2-l003 note] The good men then “fall”, clarified as not remaining or enduring. This completes the citation begun in column 1 and should not be attached to the new Iaxin entry.
[c2-l004] Iaxin. Iano cocoro. i, *Coração refalſado, &*
[c2-l004 note] This Iaxin is a malicious or deceitful heart, explained by Iano cocoro. Here ia has the sense of wickedness; the entry should not be conflated either with the preceding bad-retainer homophone or with the serpent-form entry Iatai.
[c2-l005 >] *maligno. ¶ Item, per met. Malicia,*
[c2-l005 note] Maligno completes the heart’s characterization. Item, per met. introduces a figurative use involving malice; the abbreviated apparatus is preserved without expansion.
[c2-l006 >] *& refolho de molher cioſa, ou que tem ruins*
[c2-l006 note] The figurative definition concerns the hidden malice of a jealous woman or one involved in improper love. This is the dictionary’s gendered example, not an added generalization.
[c2-l007 >] *amores.*
[c2-l007 note] Amores completes ruins amores, the improper attachments mentioned in the preceding line. The short row closes the figurative sense of Iaxin.
[c2-l008] Iaxǒ. Yocoximanaru coto, maſaxij coto. i,
[c2-l008 note] Iaxǒ contrasts the perverse with the right: yocoximanaru coto and maſaxij coto. The explanatory i, sends the reader into the synonym Zenaku on the next line.
[c2-l009 >] Ienacu. *Virtude, & maldade, ou deſordẽ,*
[c2-l009 note] Ienacu is zenaku, good and evil. The Portuguese contrasts virtue and wickedness, then disorder and justice; the abraded word reads deſordẽ, not the OCR’s fragmented deſoo dẽ. The tilde remains unexpanded.
[c2-l010 >] *& justiça. ¶* Iaxǒuo vacatçu. *Fazer*
[c2-l010 note] Justiça completes the contrast with disorder. Iaxǒuo vacatçu means distinguishing right from wrong; its object is the opposed pair, not just the first member.
[c2-l011 >] *differença da virtude, & maldade pera via*
[c2-l011 note] The translation describes making a distinction between virtue and wickedness. Pera via begins the explanation of using that distinction in the activities listed below.
[c2-l012 >] *de julgar, ou apremiar, &c.*
[c2-l012 note] The distinction is applied through judging or apremiar. The source’s verb is retained rather than modernized or replaced with a different verb inferred from the moral context.

## column-2-part-2 [column] Column 2, part 2

[c2-l013 initial=2] IEcchi. *Não poder ſaber, ou alcançar com o*
[c2-l013 note] IEcchi denotes something beyond one’s knowledge or understanding. The two-line enlarged I is real printed text, omitted by the OCR; the following E is also capital. The Portuguese construction continues with entendimento below.
[c2-l014 >] *entendimento. S.*
[c2-l014 note] Entendimento completes “unable to know or grasp with the understanding”. The row sits beside the lower part of the enlarged I and is an indented continuation; S. is the upright written-register label.
[c2-l015] Ieccon. Xitano ne. *Raiz da lingua. S.*
[c2-l015 note] Ieccon is zekkon, the tongue’s root, explained by xita no ne. The matching Portuguese anatomical gloss closes with the upright written-register label S.
[c2-l016] Ieccu. Cuuo qiru. *O cortar pollo meo hũs cer-*
[c2-l016 note] Ieccu is zekku, explained literally as cutting a verse. The Portuguese describes dividing certain Chinese verses in the middle; cer- continues into certos.
[c2-l017 >] *tos verſos da China. Vt,* Ieccuno xi. *Ver*
[c2-l017 note] The definition concerns Chinese verse. Ieccuno xi names a poem of this form; the final Ver starts the Portuguese versos without a supplied line-end hyphen.
[c2-l018 >] *ſos diuididos em duas partes.*
[c2-l018 note] Sos completes versos, described as divided into two parts. This is the dictionary’s explanation of the poetic form, preserved without replacing it with a modern technical definition.
[c2-l019] Iecqiǒ. i, Iinrin tayeta tocoro. *Lugar deſ-*
[c2-l019 note] Iecqiǒ is an isolated place. Enlargement gives Iinrin, jinrin or human society, in “a place where human presence has ceased”, not OCR ſinrin, “forest”. The deserted-place gloss corroborates this reading; the historical caron remains unchanged.
[c2-l020 >] *habitado, ou deſerto. S.*
[c2-l020 note] Habitado completes deſ-habitado, “uninhabited”, followed by “deserted”. The initial italic h is not the OCR’s b, and S. is the upright usage label.
[c2-l021] Iecu. Yoicu. *Boa ſentença, ou dito. S.*
[c2-l021 note] Iecu is a good phrase or saying, explained as yoi cu. The scan joins Yoicu and retains that spacing; the final S. marks written usage rather than forming part of the italic gloss.
[c2-l022] Iefi. i, Yoxi axi. *Bem, & mal. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l022 note] Iefi is zehi in the sense “right and wrong”, explained by yoxi axi. The abbreviated Itẽ introduces an additional use on the next row; its tilde is not expanded.
[c2-l023 >] Iefi. i, Iefitomoni. *Em todo caſo. Vt,* Ie-
[c2-l023 note] Iefi is now explained through Iefitomoni, “in any case/by all means”. The visible initial is I, not OCR l. The example begins Ie- in upright type and continues as fi cudaſarei below.
[c2-l024 >] fi cudaſarei. *Em todo caſo mo dai, ou cõcedei.*
[c2-l024 note] Iefi cudaſarei is an emphatic request to give or grant something. The Portuguese says “give or grant it to me in any case”; the nasal abbreviation in cõcedei is retained.
[c2-l025] Iefini. *Adu. Ou por ſi, ou por não, ou em*
[c2-l025 note] Iefini is zehi ni, an adverb meaning regardless of yes or no, or at all events. The capital I is visible and replaces the OCR’s long ſ; Adu. belongs to the italic grammatical apparatus.
[c2-l026 >] *todo caſo, ou por todas as vias.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes the alternatives “in any case” and “by every means”. Both explain the force of the preceding adverb rather than introducing independent Japanese forms.
[c2-l027] Iefitomo, l, jefitomoni. *Idem.*
[c2-l027 note] Iefitomo and jefitomoni have the same emphatic meaning. The printed l is the Latin alternative marker; the second Japanese form genuinely begins with lowercase j.
[c2-l028] Iejecuri. *Apreßado, ou tabalhoado no falar.*
[c2-l028 note] Iejecuri describes hurried, confused speech. The gloss calls the speaker hasty or muddled in speaking; the historical form tabalhoado is not modernized.
[c2-l029] Iejecuri, u, utta. *Falar a tabalhoadamente,*
[c2-l029 note] The verbal entry describes speaking in a confused rush. The missing initial I is restored from the scan; its principal forms agree with the supplied NINJAL index (139d12), consulted as a lexical check, not as authority for this copy’s spacing.
[c2-l030 >] *& de maneira que ſe naõ entenda bem.*
[c2-l030 note] The speech is delivered so that it cannot be understood well. This completes the manner described by tabalhoadamente; the tilde in naõ is kept over the printed o.
[c2-l031] Ien. Yoxi. *Virtude. ¶* Ienni fiqi iruru.
[c2-l031 note] Ien is zen, goodness or virtue, explained by yoxi. Ienni fiqi iruru is drawing someone into virtue. The Portuguese noun is Virtude, not OCR Viriude.
[c2-l032 >] *Trazer alguem à virtude. ¶* Ienni tachi-
[c2-l032 note] Trazer alguem à virtude translates the preceding example. A second example starts Ienni tachi- and continues with iru and the alternative tachiagaru below.
[c2-l033 >] iru, l, tachiagaru. *Conuerterſe deixan-*
[c2-l033 note] Ienni tachiiru or tachiagaru describes turning toward virtue. The Portuguese begins conversion by leaving something behind; deixan- continues as deixando on the following row.
[c2-l034 >] *do os vicios, & tornando à virtude. ¶* Ien
[c2-l034 note] The conversion consists in abandoning vices and returning to virtue. Ien at the right begins another Japanese example, completed on the next physical row.
[c2-l035 >] ni qiuamaru. *Conſumarſe na virtude.*
[c2-l035 note] With the preceding Ien, ni qiuamaru means becoming perfected in virtue. The beginning of qiuamaru is abraded: the surviving descender and later uamaru support this contextual reading, while OCR niauiamaru is not coherent Japanese. The damaged q/i region remains a nonblocking reading caveat.
[c2-l036 >] *¶* Ienuo tçumu. *Fazer muitas obras bo-*
[c2-l036 note] Ien wo tçumu is accumulating good deeds. The scan supports tçumu, without the OCR’s extra final i; the Portuguese starts “perform many good works”, with boas divided at the line end.
[c2-l037 >] *as, & de virtude. ¶* Ienni ſuſumu. *A-*
[c2-l037 note] As completes boas, and the gloss adds “works of virtue”. Ienni ſuſumu means advancing in virtue; the Portuguese A- begins Animarſe on the next physical row.
[c2-l038 >] *nimarſe, & excitarſe à virtude, ou ir por diã-*
[c2-l038 note] The example is explained as encouraging or stirring oneself toward virtue, or progressing in it. The abbreviated diã- starts diante and retains both its tilde and line-end hyphen.
[c2-l039 >] *te na virtude.*
[c2-l039 note] Te completes diante: going forward in virtue. This closes the long moral-sense entry before the next, homophonous Ien.
[c2-l040] Ien. Maye, l, ſaqi. *Antes*: *mas não ſe vſa ſe*
[c2-l040 note] This Ien means “before”, with Japanese equivalents maye and ſaqi. The Portuguese warns that it is not used except in combination; the qualification continues below.
[c2-l041 >] *não com algũa compoſição. Vt,* Gojen, l,
[c2-l041 note] The combining-form restriction is completed by “with some composition”. Gojen and the alternative continued below illustrate such compounds; l is the Latin marker “or”.
[c2-l042 >] ſonjenni. *Diante de peſſoa nobre, ou ſan-*
[c2-l042 note] Sonjenni completes the alternative expression for being before a noble or sacred person. The wording is social or reverential rather than simply a geographical position.
[c2-l043 >] *cta. ¶* Iengo. *Diante, & de tras, ou*
[c2-l043 note] Cta completes sancta. Iengo then illustrates the paired expression “before and behind”, extended to temporal order in the following row.
[c2-l044 >] *antes, & depois.*
[c2-l044 note] Antes, & depois supplies “before and after”, the temporal interpretation of Iengo. It remains the final physical continuation of this positional/temporal entry.
[c2-l045] Ien, *Meſa com comer. ¶* Ienuo ſuyuru.
[c2-l045 note] This Ien is zen, a meal table or tray with food. Ienuo ſuyuru means setting it in place; the translation starts on the next page. The scan restores initial I and shows a comma after the headword, not the OCR stop.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *Por*
