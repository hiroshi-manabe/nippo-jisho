[c1-l001] Muxu. Cazu naxi. *Couſa ſem conto, ou ſem*
[c1-l001 note] Muxu is explained by kazu nashi, without number, and glossed as countless. The scan has Cazu naxi, not OCR Cazuinaxi; no extra dotted i is present.
[c1-l002 >] *numero.* S.
[c1-l002 note] Numero completes the description as numberless. The final S. is an upright written-language label, outside the italic explanation.
[c1-l003] Muxu. Nuxinaxi. *Sem dono.* Bup.
[c1-l003 note] This separate muxu means having no owner, as nushi nashi explains, and carries the upright Buddhist label Bup.; its period belongs with that label.
[c1-l004] Muxucô. i Cazu caguirimo naicô. *Contos*
[c1-l004 note] Muxucô denotes an immeasurably long span of aeons. Cazu caguirimo naicô supplies the Japanese explanation; both circumflexes and the bare i are retained.
[c1-l005 >] *de contos de annos, ou ſeculos.*
[c1-l005 note] The Portuguese completes “countless counts of years or centuries.” It explains the scale of the aeons rather than adding another headword.
[c1-l006] Muyacu. i, Yeqimo naicoto. *Couſa deſne-*
[c1-l006 note] Muyacu is explained as eki mo nai koto, something of no benefit. Deſne- begins the Portuguese adjective for unnecessary.
[c1-l007 >] *ceßaria, ou ſem proueito.*
[c1-l007 note] Ceßaria completes deſneceßaria, followed by “without benefit.” The historical ß-like type is retained rather than expanded to modern ss.
[c1-l008] Muyacuna. *Idem.*
[c1-l008 note] Muyacuna is the adjectival form with the same meaning as the preceding entry. Idem refers to uselessness or lack of benefit.
[c1-l009] Muyen. Yen naxi. *Deſemparo, ou orfãoda-*
[c1-l009 note] Muyen, explained by en nashi, denotes lack of support or orphanhood. The short two-stroke terminal mark is read as a worn hyphen in orfãoda-/de, not the OCR period; the historical spelling is not modernized.
[c1-l010 >] *de. ¶ Item,* Yuye naqu xite. *i, De gra-*
[c1-l010 note] De completes orfãodade. The additional sense, yue naku shite, is glossed as freely or without payment; the explanatory i, is italic and gra- continues below.
[c1-l011 >] *ça, ou ſem preço. ¶* Muyenni fodocoſu.
[c1-l011 note] Ça completes graça, “free of charge,” contrasted with payment. Muyenni fodocoſu is the Japanese example of giving in this way.
[c1-l012 >] *Dar, ou repartir de graça.*
[c1-l012 note] The example is translated as giving or distributing freely. This completes the second sense of muen.
[c1-l013] Muyenjo. *Tera, ou ermida deſemparada*
[c1-l013 note] Muyenjo is a temple or hermitage lacking support. Tera is a Japanese word embedded in the Portuguese explanation and is printed in its italic type.
[c1-l014 >] *ſem renda, nem fregueſes, &c.*
[c1-l014 note] The temple has neither revenue nor parishioners or supporters. The lightly printed e in nem is retained within the clear Portuguese construction.
[c1-l015] Muyenna. *Deſemparado, & ſem abrigo*
[c1-l015 note] Muyenna is the adjectival form meaning unsupported or without shelter. The scan shows nn, not the OCR sequence mn.
[c1-l016 >] *de parentes, &c.*
[c1-l016 note] The missing protection is specifically that of relatives and others. This completes the description of the unsupported person.
[c1-l017] Muyo. Amaru coto naxi. *O não ſobejar.*
[c1-l017 note] Muyo is explained by amaru koto nashi: having nothing left over or in excess. The absence of a vowel-length mark distinguishes it from the following Muyô entry.
[c1-l018 >] *Vt,* Muyo, muqet. *Não ſobejar nem fal-*
[c1-l018 note] Muyo, muqet expresses neither surplus nor deficiency. The repeated word begins with M, not OCR N, and the final bare t in muqet remains unexpanded.
[c1-l019 >] *tar nada.* S.
[c1-l019 note] Tar completes faltar: nothing is either superfluous or missing. The closing written-language label S. is upright.
[c1-l020] Muyô. Mochijru coto naxi. *Couſa deſneceſ-*
[c1-l020 note] Muyô is explained by mochiiru koto nashi, not using something, and glossed as unnecessary. The circumflex is visibly different from the unmarked Muyo above.
[c1-l021 >] *ſaria, & ſem proueito.*
[c1-l021 note] The gloss finishes unnecessary and adds lack of benefit. The two long-s characters of the divided adjective remain on their respective physical lines.
[c1-l022] Muyocu. Yocu naxi. *O não ter cubiça.*
[c1-l022 note] Muyocu is explained by yoku nashi, absence of desire, and specifically glossed as not being covetous. The historical Portuguese cubiça is retained.
[c1-l023] Muyocuna. *Peßoa ſem cubiça.*
[c1-l023 note] Muyocuna describes a person without covetousness. Its adjective-forming na belongs to the headword, not to the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l024] Muyôna. *Couſa deſneceßaria, & ſem proueito.*
[c1-l024 note] Muyôna is the adjective meaning unnecessary or of no benefit. The scan has Muyôna rather than the spurious extra i of OCR Muiyôna; the circumflex remains.
[c1-l025] Muzai. Tçumi naxi. *Sem peccado.* S.
[c1-l025 note] Muzai is explained by tsumi nashi, without sin or fault. The upright S. marks written usage, separate from the italic Portuguese.
[c1-l026] Muzan. *Compaixão, ou piedade.*
[c1-l026 note] Muzan is glossed as compassion or pity. This older sense is read from the printed explanation rather than replaced by a modern dominant meaning.
[c1-l027] Muzanna. *Peßoa, ou couſa pera ſe ter cõpai-*
[c1-l027 note] Muzanna applies to a person or thing deserving compassion. The abbreviated cõpai- carries its tilde over o and continues below.
[c1-l028 >] *xão, & piedade della.*
[c1-l028 note] Xão completes the divided word for compassion, followed by pity for that person or thing. Both the tilde and the continuation’s lower-case x belong to the print.
[c1-l029] Muzanni. *Adu.*
[c1-l029 note] Muzanni is identified as the adverb corresponding to the compassion-related adjective. Adu. is italic grammatical apparatus.
[c1-l030] Muzǒ. *Palaura com que ſe mostra ter piedade,*
[c1-l030 note] Muzǒ is a word used to express pity. The caron belongs to the Japanese vowel, and the Portuguese description continues on the next row.
[c1-l031 >] *& compaixão dalguem.* X.
[c1-l031 note] The phrase completes the expression of compassion for someone and ends with upright X., the regional label. Enlargement preserves a faint final o in compaixão, omitted by OCR.
[c1-l032] Muzǒna. *Peßoa, ou couſa que moue a com-*
[c1-l032 note] Muzǒna describes a person or thing that moves one to compassion. The gloss breaks after com-, and the caron is retained over the headword’s o.
[c1-l033 >] *paixão.*
[c1-l033 note] Paixão completes compaixão across the line break. The x is badly abraded, but its faint surviving marks and the complete gloss support this reading rather than OCR pui ào.
[c1-l034] Muzǒni. *Adu.*
[c1-l034 note] Muzǒni is the corresponding adverb. Its caron remains distinct from the circumflex in Muyô earlier in the column.
[c1-l035] Muzuto. *Adu. Vide,* Munzuto.
[c1-l035 note] Muzuto is an adverb referred to Munzuto for its definition. The reference target is upright Japanese text following italic Vide.
[c1-l036] Muzumuzuto. *Idem.*
[c1-l036 note] Muzumuzuto is assigned the same adverbial meaning through Idem. This is the final dictionary line of the M section; the following marks are ornaments, not words.

## column-2-part-1 [column] Column 2, part 1

[c2-l001 initial=6] NA. *Nabo. ¶* Nauo tçu-
[c2-l001 note] The decorated N belongs to the following printed A., forming NA, the turnip entry. It spans six physical rows and is restored as an enlarged initial with a taller crop. Nabo, not OCR Nobo, supplies the Portuguese meaning.
[c2-l002 >] mu. *Colher nabos.*
[c2-l002 note] Mu completes na wo tsumu, glossed as gathering turnips. The visible portion of the large N at the left belongs only to the preceding line’s headword.
[c2-l003 >] Na. *Nome, ou fama. ¶* Na-
[c2-l003 note] This Na denotes a name or fame, distinct from the turnip entry. The beginning Na- of its example is printed beside the same six-line initial.
[c2-l004 >] uo matdaini nocoſu. *Dei-*
[c2-l004 note] Na wo matdai ni nokosu means leaving one’s name to later generations. Enlargement supports matdaini, with i before ni, rather than OCR matdani; Dei- starts its Portuguese translation.
[c2-l005 >] *xar ſeu nome, ou fama aos vin*
[c2-l005 note] Xar completes deixar, leaving one’s name or fame to posterity. Vin continues into douros without a printed terminal hyphen.
[c2-l006 >] *douros. ¶* Nauo tçuqu-
[c2-l006 note] Douros completes vindouros, future generations. Na wo tsukuru begins the next example, assigning a name, while the ornamental initial ends beside this row.
[c2-l007 >] ru. *Por nome. ¶* Nauo toru. *Tomar*
[c2-l007 note] Ru completes tsukuru, translated as giving a name. Na wo toru then introduces acquiring a name or reputation.
[c2-l008 >] *nome, ou alcançar fama. ¶* Nauo xiruſu.
[c2-l008 note] The translation completes acquiring fame. Na wo shirusu begins the example of recording or writing one’s name.
[c2-l009 >] *Eſcreuer ſeu nome. ¶* Nauo vxinǒ. *Per-*
[c2-l009 note] After translating writing one’s name, na wo ushinǒ introduces losing honour or reputation. The initial v and caron are retained as printed.
[c2-l010 >] *der a honra, ou fama. ¶* Nauo todomuru.
[c2-l010 note] The lost object is honour or fame. Na wo todomuru begins the contrasting expression for leaving or preserving one’s name.
[c2-l011 >] *Deixar ſeu nome, ou fama. ¶* Nauo cuta-
[c2-l011 note] Na wo todomuru is translated as leaving one’s name or fame. Na wo kuta- begins the contrasting example concerning damage to reputation.
[c2-l012 >] ſu. *Perder do bom nome, ou menoſcabar a*
[c2-l012 note] Su completes kutasu, glossed as losing a good name or diminishing fame. The printed Portuguese Perder do bom nome is retained without grammatical modernization.
[c2-l013 >] *fama. ¶* Nauo aguru. *Alcançar no-*
[c2-l013 note] After closing the damaged-reputation example, na wo aguru introduces gaining a name or honour. The translation continues its divided no- below.
[c2-l014 >] *me, ou honra.*
[c2-l014 note] Me completes nome, followed by honour. This closes the series of examples under the name-and-fame sense of Na.
[c2-l015] Naba. l, qinoco. *Cugumelos.* X. *No*
[c2-l015 note] Naba, alternatively qinoco, means mushrooms. The upright X. marks regional usage; italic No begins a contrast with usage in Cami on the next row.
[c2-l016 >] *Cami, & mais vniuerſalmente ſe diz,* Cu-
[c2-l016 note] Cami is the regional designation embedded in the Portuguese sentence. The text says that there, and more generally, the term is Cu-/ſabira rather than naba.
[c2-l017 >] ſabira.
[c2-l017 note] ſabira completes the Japanese reference word Cuſabira. The worn initial long ſ remains identifiable; this short row ends the regional comparison.
[c2-l018] Nabe. *Panela, ou tacho.*
[c2-l018 note] Nabe is glossed as a pot or pan. The source’s panela and tacho identify the vessel rather than its material.
[c2-l019] Nabete. *P.* i, Voxinabete. *Geeralmẽte.*
[c2-l019 note] Nabete is marked for poetic use and explained by oshinabete, generally. P. is italic here; the printed initial V and the tilde over e in Geeralmẽte are retained.
[c2-l020] Nabeteno. *P. Couſa comũa, & geral.*
[c2-l020 note] Nabeteno is the corresponding attributive expression for something common or general, again marked P. The nasal abbreviation is comũa, with the tilde over u.
[c2-l021] Nabezumi. *Fumo, ou felugem que està pegada*
[c2-l021 note] Nabezumi denotes soot adhering to cooking pots, not OCR Nobezumi. The worn headword vowel is a. Enlargement shows a small grave in està; felugem and the provisional st sequence are retained.
[c2-l022 >] *nas panelas.*
[c2-l022 note] Nas panelas identifies pots as the surface to which the soot sticks. This completes nabezumi rather than introducing the following verb.
[c2-l023] Nabicaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer inclinar, ou do-*
[c2-l023 note] Nabicaxi is the causative verb for making something incline or bend. The principal part aita is a historical form and remains literal.
[c2-l024 >] *brar. ¶* Fitono xitagai tçuqu cotoua
[c2-l024 note] Brar completes dobrar. The Japanese quotation concerns people adhering in obedience; the scan supports xitagai, with t, rather than OCR xiragai.
[c2-l025 >] macotoni fuqu cajeno cuſa qiuo nabica-
[c2-l025 note] The quotation compares that obedience with blowing wind bending grass and trees. Its physical division nabica-/ſuga is retained, as are the printed cuſa and qi forms.
[c2-l026 >] ſuga gotocu. Feiq. *Lib. 1. O ſojei-*
[c2-l026 note] Suga gotocu completes the comparison. Feiq. is an upright citation label; italic Lib. 1 identifies the book, and ſojei- begins the Portuguese translation.
[c2-l027 >] *tarem ſe lhe os homẽs, era como vento que in-*
[c2-l027 note] The people’s submitting to him is likened to wind bending vegetation. The scan joins como, not com o; the tilde over e in homẽs remains unexpanded.
[c2-l028 >] *clina, & menea facilmẽte as eruas, & plãtas.*
[c2-l028 note] The wind inclines and easily moves the herbs and plants. The final word is plãtas, with a nasal-abbreviation tilde, rather than the garbled OCR plilas.
[c2-l029] Nabiqe, uru, eta. *Dobrar, ou render, ou*
[c2-l029 note] Nabiqe means to bend, bring into submission, or overcome. The principal forms uru and eta remain part of the printed headword record.
[c2-l030 >] *vencer: commummente ſe vſa com algum outro*
[c2-l030 note] The entry adds overcoming someone and says the verb is commonly used with another verb. The historical doubled consonants in commummente are retained.
[c2-l031 >] *verbo. Vt,* Fiqi nabiquru. *Mouer, &*
[c2-l031 note] After stating that the verb combines with another verb, fiqi nabiquru illustrates moving or drawing someone toward an inclination. Vt, is italic apparatus.
[c2-l032 >] *inclinar a alguem. ¶* Fito no cocorouo
[c2-l032 note] The first example concerns inclining someone. Fito no cocorouo starts the next example, whose object is a person’s heart or disposition.
[c2-l033 >] iy nabiquru. *Mouer, & dobrar o co-*
[c2-l033 note] Iy nabiquru describes influencing through speech. The Portuguese translates moving or bending the heart, with co- continued below.
[c2-l034 >] *ração dalguem com palauras.*
[c2-l034 note] Ração completes coração; the person’s heart is moved by words. This closes the speech-related example of nabiqe.
[c2-l035] Nabiqi, u, ijta. *Inclinarſe, ou dobrarſe.*
[c2-l035 note] Nabiqi is the intransitive counterpart, inclining or bending. The gloss reads Inclinarſe, with n, not OCR Inclirarſe; ijta is the printed principal form.
[c2-l036 >] *¶* Cajeni cuſa, qiga nabiqu. *As eruas,*
[c2-l036 note] Kaze ni kusa, ki ga nabiku describes grass and trees bending in the wind. The comma after cuſa belongs to the printed Japanese example.
[c2-l037 >] *& aruores ſe inclinão com o vento. ¶* Fi-
[c2-l037 note] The Portuguese completes the bending of trees in the wind. Fi- begins the next example about a human heart.
[c2-l038 >] tono cocoro nabiqu. *Renderſe, ou dobrar-*
[c2-l038 note] Fito no kokoro nabiku is glossed as a person’s heart yielding or bending. The printed tono begins mid-word from Fi- on the preceding row.
[c2-l039 >] *ſe o coração dalguem.*
[c2-l039 note] ſe completes dobrarſe, with a person’s heart as the subject. This short continuation closes the figurative application.
[c2-l040] Nabiqi xitagai, ǒ, ǒta. *Rẽdendoſe obedecer.*
[c2-l040 note] Nabiqi xitagai combines yielding with obedience. The gloss reads Rẽdendoſe obedecer, with a tilde over e and final -cer, rather than OCR Rãdendoſe obedecor.
