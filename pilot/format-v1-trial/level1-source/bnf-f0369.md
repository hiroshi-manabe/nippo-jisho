[c1-l001 >] *pois da guerra. ¶* Maitte nochi. *Depois*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the previous page's gloss ending in 'after the war'. Maitte nochi means after coming or having come; the second r of guerra is weak but visible, correcting guerta.
[c1-l002 >] *que veo, ou como vier.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the translation of Maitte nochi: after he came, or when he comes. The historical forms veo and vier are retained rather than harmonized in tense.
[c1-l003] Nocoguiri. *Serra.*
[c1-l003 note] Nokogiri is a saw, directly matched by Portuguese Serra. The short definition is complete on this physical row.
[c1-l004] Nocoguirizaca. *Crista de gallo como ſerra.*
[c1-l004 note] Nokogirizaka names a cock's serrated comb, compared with a saw. Two r types are visible in ſerra, not the draft's ſera; Crista retains the provisional st classification.
[c1-l005] Nocori, u, otta. *Ficar, ou ſobejar, &c.*
[c1-l005 note] Nokori, nokoru, nokotta denotes remaining or being left over. The Portuguese gives both staying and surplus; the printed abbreviated principal forms are not expanded.
[c1-l006 >] *¶* Naga nocoru. *Ficar o nome, ou fama.*
[c1-l006 note] Naga is na ga, 'the name' with its subject particle, not the adjective naga-. The example means that one's name or reputation remains, exactly as the Portuguese explains; the printed joining is preserved.
[c1-l007] Nocori. *O que fica, ou restante. ¶* Noco-
[c1-l007 note] The noun nokori denotes what remains or the remainder. The visible terminal hyphen divides Noco-rino in the following example, rather than ending a separate lemma.
[c1-l008 >] rino xu. *Os que ficão.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes nokori no shu, the people who remain. The gloss is plural, 'those who stay', so xu refers to a group rather than to the abstract remainder alone.
[c1-l009] Nocori todomari, u, atta. *Ficar, ou deter-*
[c1-l009 note] Nokori-todomari means remaining behind or staying. The gloss divides deter-se across the row boundary; the final dash is visibly printed.
[c1-l010 >] *ſe. Vt,* Imaua corefodono mini natte, no-
[c1-l010 note] Completes deter-se, then begins 'now, having come to such a condition'. Mini is mi ni, referring to the speaker's circumstances; no- continues as nocori on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] cori todomari anuonni ite, nanini xôzo.
[c1-l011 note] Completes the example: remaining alive and at peace, what shall I do? Anuonni corresponds to an'on ni, and xôzo is the interrogative construction in the quotation, not an independent headword. Its circumflex is visible.
[c1-l012 >] Feiq. *Lib. 1. Eu agora feito, & para-*
[c1-l012 note] The upright Feiq. citation is followed by italic 'Book 1'. The translation begins 'I, now made and brought ...', with para-do continued below; the source label is eligible for whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l013 >] *do deſta maneira ficando viuo em paz que ei de*
[c1-l013 note] Completes parado and describes being reduced to this condition while remaining alive in peace. Que ei de introduces the final question, preserving printed ei rather than modern hei.
[c1-l014 >] *fazer?*
[c1-l014 note] Finishes the question 'what am I to do?' The mark after fazer is a question mark, not the OCR's separate ẽ; there is no additional word on this short row.
[c1-l015] Nocotte. *Palaura que ſe vſa nas contas. i,*
[c1-l015 note] Nokotte is identified as a word used in reckoning or accounts. The following i, introduces its equivalent Resta on the next row. Separate second-pass enlargement confirms the slanted italic i, so the draft's continuous italic apparatus is retained rather than the first-pass upright interpretation.
[c1-l016 >] *Resta.*
[c1-l016 note] 'Remains' or 'balance remaining' completes the accounting usage of nokotte. This brief continuation is a full physical row, not furniture.
[c1-l017] Nocoxi, ſu, oita. *Deixar.*
[c1-l017 note] Nokoshi, nokosu, nokoita is the transitive 'leave'. The oita principal form is preserved as historical s-row i-onbin rather than replaced by modern nokoshita.
[c1-l018] Nocoxivoqi, u, oita. *Deixar. Vt,* Coqiǒ-
[c1-l018 note] Nokoshi-oki means leaving something or someone behind. The full stop after Deixar is visible; Coqiǒ- begins kokyo, the home country in the following example, with a caron over o.
[c1-l019 >] ni nocoxi vocaxerareta Qitanocatano
[c1-l019 note] Continues 'the wife whom he had left in his home country'. Qitanocata is the honorific term for a wife here; the honorific verb and the printed spacing of nocoxi vocaxerareta are preserved.
[c1-l020 >] cotouo aqetemo, curetemo vomouareta,
[c1-l020 note] Says that he thought of her at dawn and at dusk, rendered below as day and night. The final mark has a descending tail and is transcribed as a comma, not the draft's period.
[c1-l021 >] Feiq. *Lib. 4. De dia, & de noite ſe lẽ-*
[c1-l021 note] Feiq., Book 4, identifies the quotation. The Portuguese begins 'by day and by night he remembered'; the abbreviation tilde and the terminal hyphen in lẽ- are both retained.
[c1-l022 >] *braua de ſua molher que deixara na patria.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes lembrava and specifies his wife left in his homeland. The scan reads patria, not parta; molher is retained as the historical spelling printed here.
[c1-l023] Nocqeni. *Adu. Modo de olhar, ou ficar*
[c1-l023 note] Nokke ni is an adverbial expression for looking or being turned upward. The cq cluster belongs to the Japanese spelling and is not normalized to a modern romanization.
[c1-l024 >] *virado pera cima. Vt,* Nocqeni ſoru. *Do-*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the upward-facing description and gives nokke ni soru, bending backward with the face upward. The printed hyphen divides Portuguese dobrar-se, not the Japanese verb.
[c1-l025 >] *brarſe virado com o roſto pera cima. Itẽ ficar*
[c1-l025 note] Completes dobrar-se and explains bending with the face upward. Cima is visible despite a stray mark above its c, correcting dima; Itẽ introduces the additional sense of being astonished.
[c1-l026 >] *eſpãtado.* Nocqeni tauoruru. *Cair de costas.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes 'be astonished', then gives the example of falling backward onto one's back. The Japanese tauoruru is left in its historical form, and the tilde abbreviation in eſpãtado is not expanded.
[c1-l027] Nocubi. *Eſpaço hum pouco aberto que fica en-*
[c1-l027 note] Nokubi denotes a somewhat open gap or saddle in the terrain. The definition continues with the two hills or mountains on the next row; en- has a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l028 >] *tre monte, & monte.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes entre and locates the opening between one mountain and another. This is the continuation of the terrain definition, not a new entry.
[c1-l029] Nodera. *Varelas, ou ermidas que estão no*
[c1-l029 note] Nodera refers to temples or hermitages out in the countryside. Varelas is the printed Portuguese designation for religious buildings; no specific sect is supplied by this gloss.
[c1-l030 >] *campo.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the location 'in the countryside'. The short word belongs to Nodera and is not a running catchword.
[c1-l031] Nodo. *Garganta. ¶* Nodouo ximuru.
[c1-l031 note] Nodo is the throat. Nodo wo shimuru gives the example of tightening or constricting the throat, whose Portuguese translation occupies the following row.
[c1-l032 >] *Apertar agarganta, ou afogar.*
[c1-l032 note] Translates the example as squeezing the throat or choking. The compact agarganta spacing is retained from the locally aligned draft; afogar has the suffocation sense in this context, not necessarily drowning.
[c1-l033] Nodobuye. *Guela, ou nò da garganta.*
[c1-l033 note] Nodobue is glossed as the gullet or the throat's knot. The definition points to the throat structure without warranting a more precise modern anatomical identification; the printed grave in nò is retained.
[c1-l034] Nodocana. *Couſa tranquilla, & ſerena.*
[c1-l034 note] Nodokana describes something tranquil and serene. The following two indented forms belong to this adjective's small paradigm rather than to omitted Portuguese definitions.
[c1-l035 >] Nodocani.
[c1-l035 note] Nodokani is the adverbial form, 'tranquilly'. Its indentation and lack of a separate gloss link it to Nodocana above.
[c1-l036 >] Nodoqeſa.
[c1-l036 note] Nodokesa is the corresponding quality, tranquility. There is no secure accent over o: the draft's Nodǒqeſa arose from nearby ink, and the unaccented form also recurs in the cross-reference below.
[c1-l037] Nodocauaqi, u, aita. *Ter ſede.*
[c1-l037 note] Nodo-kawaki describes having a dry throat, hence being thirsty. The abbreviated verb forms and the compound's unbroken printed spacing are retained.
[c1-l038] Nôdocu. *Vt,* Cuſurino nôdocu. *Calida-*
[c1-l038 note] Nôdocu is illustrated by kusuri no nodoku, the favorable and harmful qualities of a medicine. Both occurrences visibly have a circumflex; the Portuguese Calida-des is divided by a printed terminal mark.
[c1-l039 >] *des boas, & conuenientes que tem algũa mezi-*
[c1-l039 note] Completes 'good and suitable qualities which a medicine possesses'. The tilde is over u in algũa, and mezi- continues into mezinha on the next physical row.
[c1-l040 >] *nha com certas couſas, ou comeres, &. &*
[c1-l040 note] Completes mezinha and refers to its compatibility with certain things or foods. The unusual printed sequence '&. &' is retained rather than silently expanded to '&c. &'.
[c1-l041 >] *outras nociuas, & repugnantes.*
[c1-l041 note] Contrasts other qualities that are harmful or incompatible. This finishes the medicine entry's opposition between beneficial and adverse properties.
[c1-l042] Nodocubi. *Peſcoço.*
[c1-l042 note] Nodokubi is glossed as the neck. It is a separate compound entry, not an extension of the medicinal Nôdocu above.
[c1-l043] Nodoqeſa. *Vide,* Nodocani.
[c1-l043 note] Refers the noun nodokesa back to nodokani in the preceding paradigm. Vide is italic apparatus, while the Japanese cross-reference is upright and embedded within that apparatus.
[c1-l044] Nodoſaqi. *Boca da garganta, ou guela por on-*
[c1-l044 note] Nodosaki denotes the opening of the throat or gullet. The long s in the headword is distinct from f, and the gloss divides onde across the physical line ending.
[c1-l045 >] *de ſe engole o comer.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes onde and identifies the passage through which food is swallowed. The infinitive comer is used substantivally for food in the explanation.
[c1-l046] Nodoua. *Gòla, ou armas da garganta.*
[c1-l046 note] Nodowa is a protective collar or throat armor. A small grave accent is visible over the o of Gòla; the gloss is about armor, not an ordinary garment collar alone.
[c1-l047] Nôfit. Yoqi fude. *Bom eſcriuão.*
[c1-l047 note] Nôfit is the checked-coda spelling corresponding to no-hitsu, skill with the brush; Yoqi fude explains it as a good brush/hand. The Portuguese identifies a good scribe. All three portions are legible in the native crop, replacing the draft's severely garbled entry without adding a missing row.

[c2-l001] Nôfu. *Laurador que cultiua os campos.*
[c2-l001 note] Nofu is a farmer who cultivates the fields. The circumflex in the headword and historical Laurador/cultiua spellings are retained.
[c2-l002] Nogare, uru, eta. *Eſcapar. Vt,* Imaua
[c2-l002 note] Nogare means escaping. The example begins ima wa, 'now'; Vt is italic apparatus introducing the Japanese sentence rather than part of the headword.
[c2-l003 >] faya nogaruru tocoromo nai. Tog. *Ia*
[c2-l003 note] Completes 'now there is no longer any place to escape'. Tog. is an upright citation label; the following italic Ia begins the Portuguese translation and is not part of that label.
[c2-l004 >] *agora não ha lugar, nem modo de eſcapar.*
[c2-l004 note] Translates the absence of both a place and a means of escape. The historical unaccented ha and long s in eſcapar are preserved.
[c2-l005] Nogaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer eſcapar, ou liurar.*
[c2-l005 note] Nogashi is the transitive counterpart: causing someone to escape or setting someone free. The printed aita ending is historical i-onbin, not a missing shi sequence.
[c2-l006] Nogayeri. *O tornar do campo depois de enter-*
[c2-l006 note] Nogaeri is the return from the field after a funeral rite. The terminal enter- continues into enterrarem below; it is a visible physical division.
[c2-l007 >] *rarem, ou queimarem algum corpo morto.*
[c2-l007 note] Completes the alternatives of burying or burning a dead body. This explains the specific funeral context of the return, not simply any walk back from the fields.
[c2-l008] Nogayeriſǒ. *Nome de hũa erua.*
[c2-l008 note] Nogaeriso is identified only as an herb's name. The scan supports the long s and caron over the final o; the gloss does not justify supplying a modern species.
[c2-l009] Nôguei. l, Gueinô. *Artes, ou habilidades.*
[c2-l009 note] Nogei and geino are alternative orderings for arts or skills. The lowercase l is the dictionary's 'or' marker, not the numeral one; both long-o marks are circumflexes here.
[c2-l010] Nogui. *Pragana do trigo, arroz, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] Nogi is the awn or bristle of wheat, rice, and similar grain. Pragana supplies this botanical part without implying the whole husk.
[c2-l011] Noguicu. *Boninas do campo.*
[c2-l011 note] Nogiku is glossed as flowers of the field, here the wild chrysanthemum-type name. The Portuguese remains its broad printed wording rather than a supplied taxonomic label.
[c2-l012] Nôguiô. *Cultiuaçaõ dos campos.*
[c2-l012 note] Nogyo denotes cultivation of the fields. The two circumflexes belong to the Japanese form; the Portuguese nasal mark is retained on the final o in the draft's Cultiuaçaõ after comparison with the scan.
[c2-l013] Nôgi. *Guardarſe bem, ou terſe ſemſe danar co-*
[c2-l013 note] Nogi concerns keeping well without spoiling. The printed compact ſemſe and the divided co-mo are retained; the following examples specify perishable things.
[c2-l014 >] *mo mezinhas, peixe, fruita, &c. ¶* Nô-
[c2-l014 note] Completes como and lists medicines, fish, and fruit. The next Japanese example starts with the visibly divided Nô- of Nôgino.
[c2-l015 >] gino arumono. i, Tabauaruru mono.
[c2-l015 note] Nogi no aru mono means something with keeping quality, paraphrased as tabawaruru mono. Two small traces over the u of arumono were enlarged on the second pass; they do not securely establish a printed tilde, so the aligned unaccented reading is retained with this caveat. The following gloss establishes the sense of keeping well.
[c2-l016 >] *Couſa que ſe guarda, & conſerua bem. ¶* Nô-
[c2-l016 note] Translates the positive example as a thing that keeps and is well preserved. The small full stop after bem is visible, and a second Nô- begins the contrasting negative example.
[c2-l017 >] gino nai mono. *Couſa que naõ dura nem ſe*
[c2-l017 note] Completes nogi no nai mono, a thing lacking keeping quality. Nai, visibly beginning with n, replaces OCR mai; the Portuguese confirms the negative sense, 'does not last'.
[c2-l018 >] *conſerua.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes 'nor is preserved'. This short continuation closes the negative example and the Nôgi entry.
[c2-l019] Nogoi, ô, ôta. *Alimpar, ou eſpanar.*
[c2-l019 note] Nogoi denotes wiping clean or dusting. The printed forms use o and circumflexed long o; they are not normalized to modern nugui/nuguu.
[c2-l020] Nôgu. i, Cǒſacuno dǒgu. *Inſtrumentos do*
[c2-l020 note] Nogu is paraphrased as kosaku no dogu, implements for cultivation. The two carons in the paraphrase are visibly distinct from the headword's circumflex.
[c2-l021 >] *campo, ou lauoura.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes 'implements of the field or farming'. Lauoura retains the historical u/v spelling, and this row is part of the tool definition.
[c2-l022] Nogutçu. *Huns ferros, ou chapas que eſtaõ*
[c2-l022 note] Nogutsu names iron pieces or metal plates. The following row locates them on a saddle, so the gloss should not be interpreted from the modern sound of kutsu alone.
[c2-l023 >] *na ſella do caualo.*
[c2-l023 note] Places the metal fittings on the horse's saddle. This completes the Nogutçu definition and preserves the double l in ſella.
[c2-l024] Nôixǒ. *Veſtidos q̃ ſeruem nos autos.*
[c2-l024 note] No-isho refers to costumes used in theatrical performances. The Portuguese reads autos with t, not OCR aulos; the q-tilde abbreviation is retained without expansion.
[c2-l025] Nôja. i, Nôno arumono. *Peßoa que ſabe al-*
[c2-l025 note] Noja is a person with ability, paraphrased as no no aru mono. The Portuguese begins 'a person who knows some art'; the beta-shaped double-s sort in Peßoa is retained as in the baseline.
[c2-l026 >] *gũa arte.*
[c2-l026 note] Completes alguma arte, 'some art or skill'. The tilde lies over u in this divided word, and the row closes the preceding definition.
[c2-l027] Nôjencazzura. *Cazzura, ou erua de latada q̃*
[c2-l027 note] Nozenkazura is described as a climbing or trellised plant. Cazzura is a Japanese plant-category word embedded in the Portuguese gloss and is therefore marked for optional whole-word typeface review; its actual italic appearance is retained.
[c2-l028 >] *dà flores.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes the description as a climber that bears flowers. The grave accent in dà is printed and is not modernized to an acute.
[c2-l029] Nôjo. i, Yoi monocaqi. *Bom eſcriuão.*
[c2-l029 note] Nojo is a good writer or scribe, paraphrased by yoi monokaki. This is distinct from the following Nǒju, whose long-o mark has the opposite orientation.
[c2-l030] Nǒju. i, Tenno gonaixôni canai tatematçu-
[c2-l030 note] Introduces acceptance by heaven, paraphrased as respectfully according with heaven's inward intention. The headword has a caron, whereas gonaixôni retains the visible circumflex; tatematçuru is physically divided after tçu.
[c2-l031 >] ruuo yǔ. *O agradar algũa couſa aos Camis,*
[c2-l031 note] Completes the Japanese paraphrase with 'is called ...'. The Portuguese explains something pleasing the kami; Camis is embedded in that Portuguese sentence, not a standalone Japanese example.
[c2-l032 >] *& Fotoques, ou aceitala, como deprecaçoẽs,*
[c2-l032 note] Continues with the buddhas and their accepting something, such as prayers or supplications. Fotoques is another embedded Japanese religious term, while the printed Portuguese nasal spelling deprecaçoẽs is preserved.
[c2-l033 >] *&c. Vt,* Gonǒju aru, l, naſaruru.
[c2-l033 note] Gives the honorific expressions gon oju aru and gon oju nasaruru for such acceptance. The a after long s in naſaruru is abraded; the surviving spacing and strokes support the baseline reading, retained with this nonblocking caveat.
[c2-l034] Nôjǔ. *Materia, ou ſangue de postema.*
[c2-l034 note] Noju here is discharge or blood from an abscess, distinct from the preceding acceptance entry. Materia has the older bodily-discharge sense in the gloss; no medical advice is implied.
[c2-l035] Nojucu. Noni yadoru. *O dormir, ou agaſa-*
[c2-l035 note] Nojuku is paraphrased as no ni yadoru, lodging in the open field. The Portuguese offers sleeping or taking shelter; agaſa- continues into agaſalharſe below.
[c2-l036 >] *lharſe no campo. Vt,* Nojucu ſuru.
[c2-l036 note] Completes 'take shelter in the field' and gives nojuku suru as the corresponding verb phrase. Vt remains italic, while the Japanese example is upright.
[c2-l037] Nômai. Come. *Arroz.*
[c2-l037 note] Nomai is equated with kome, rice, by both the Japanese synonym and the Portuguese gloss. The synonym is not a Japanese word embedded inside a Portuguese explanation and is not separately tagged.
[c2-l038] Nomame. *Hũa casta de grãos pequenos.*
[c2-l038 note] Nomame is a kind of small pulse or bean. Grãos is read within the plant description rather than as a modern measurement unit; no species is added to the sparse definition.
[c2-l039] Nomi. *Adu. Sòmente.*
[c2-l039 note] The first Nomi on these rows is the limiting adverb 'only'. Its grammatical label and gloss distinguish it from the following homophonous nouns.
[c2-l040] Nomi. *Eſcopro. ¶* Nomiyeriuo ſuru. *A-*
[c2-l040 note] This Nomi is a chisel. Nomiyeri wo suru denotes carving or cutting with it; the Portuguese translation begins with the visibly divided A-brir.
[c2-l041 >] *brir com eſcopro.*
[c2-l041 note] Completes abrir com escopro, cutting or opening with a chisel. The physical row belongs to the preceding example, not a separate definition.
[c2-l042] Nomi. *Torno. ¶* Nomiuo ſaſu. *Pòr o torno.*
[c2-l042 note] Here nomi is an inserted peg or pin, as the sasu example and 'put in the torno' gloss indicate. Torno should not automatically be assigned its modern machine-tool sense; the printed grave in Pòr is retained.
[c2-l043] Nomi. *Pulga.*
[c2-l043 note] This homophone is the flea, directly glossed by Pulga. It forms a complete one-line entry between the peg and drinking senses.
[c2-l044] Nomi, u, ôda. *Beber. ¶ Item, Comer*
[c2-l044 note] Nomi is now the verb 'drink', with the historical long-o past form ôda. An additional sense begins here, eating by swallowing rather than chewing.
[c2-l045 >] *das cobras, & outros animaes que não tem dẽ-*
[c2-l045 note] The dictionary attributes this eating usage to snakes and other animals without teeth for chewing. This reports the source's wording, not an independent zoological claim; dẽ- has both a tilde and a terminal division mark.
[c2-l046 >] *tes pera mastigar.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes dentes pera mastigar, 'teeth for chewing'. The qualification is part of the swallowing sense of nomu; the provisional st classification in mastigar is retained.
[c2-l047] Nomichi. *Caminho do campo, ou montes.*
[c2-l047 note] Nomichi denotes a path through fields or hills. The t in montes is visible, correcting OCR mones; the separate Nomi- below is a catchword anticipating the next page, not a continuation of this definition.
