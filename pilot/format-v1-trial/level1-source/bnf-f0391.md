[c1-l001] Qiji. Teraye cayeru. *O Tornarſe à Tera,*
[c1-l001 note] Kiji means returning to a temple, as Teraye cayeru explains. The scan restores the missing ji in the headword; the capital T in Tornarſe and the Japanese term Tera inside the Portuguese gloss are retained.
[c1-l002 >] *ou Igreja. Vt,* Padreſamano goqijiuo
[c1-l002 note] The example refers respectfully to the priest's return. Padreſamano is one roman Japanese expression here; the OCR incorrectly assigned its initial P to the italic apparatus.
[c1-l003 >] machimaraſuru. *Eſpero a tornada do Pa-*
[c1-l003 note] The Japanese honorific verb completes the statement of awaiting the return. The scan supports machimaraſuru, not machinaraſuru, and tornada rather than tornaãa; the Portuguese priest-word continues below.
[c1-l004 >] *dre a caſa.*
[c1-l004 note] Dre completes Padre, and a caſa specifies the priest's return home. This is the end of the example, not a new definition.
[c1-l005] Qiji. Tattoqi tera. *Tera, ou Igreja ſancta.*
[c1-l005 note] This separate kiji is an honorific temple-name, paraphrased as tattoqi tera. The Portuguese calls it a holy temple or church; the scan restores both the headword and the badly misrecognized gloss.
[c1-l006 >] *Palaura pera honrar a Tera.*
[c1-l006 note] The compiler explicitly says the word honours the temple. Tera is a Japanese noun within this Portuguese explanatory sentence; Palaura, not Falaura, is visible.
[c1-l007] Qiji. *Gallinha do mato, ou perdiz de Iapão.*
[c1-l007 note] The next kiji is the bird, glossed as woodland fowl or Japanese partridge. The scan reads Gallinha with two l types here, unlike the single-l form on the preceding page.
[c1-l008] Qijin. Voni, cami. *Diabo.*
[c1-l008 note] Kijin is paraphrased through oni and kami and glossed as a devil. The dictionary's religious classification is retained without replacing either Japanese synonym or extending the gloss.
[c1-l009] Qijǒ. Xiroye cayeru. *O tornarſe à fortaleza.*
[c1-l009 note] Kijō means returning to a fortress. The scan joins Xiroye, so the OCR's internal space is removed; the caron remains on the headword.
[c1-l010] Qimacaxe. *O fazer ſua vontade liuremente.*
[c1-l010 note] Kimakase is freely doing one's own will. The Portuguese supports the sense of acting according to inclination rather than merely entrusting a task to someone else.
[c1-l011 >] *¶* Qimacaxena fito. *Homem voluntario,*
[c1-l011 note] The example characterizes a person who follows his own will. Voluntario is explained by the following liure and need not be read in the modern volunteer sense.
[c1-l012 >] *& liure.*
[c1-l012 note] The adjective free completes the preceding characterization. The ampersand is printed and retained on this short continuation row.
[c1-l013] Qime, uru, eta. *Reprender aſperamente a*
[c1-l013 note] Kime, kimuru, kimeta is a verb of sharply reprimanding someone. The scan reads Qime rather than OCR Qune and preserves Portuguese Reprender, without inserting the spelling of the next row's related word.
[c1-l014 >] *alguem. Vt,* Fitouo qimuru. *¶* Qi-
[c1-l014 note] The example means reprimanding a person. After the paragraph mark, Qi- begins the compound verb Qimetçuquru on the following physical line.
[c1-l015 >] metçuquru. *Ter reprehendido, ou caſtiga-*
[c1-l015 note] The compound is glossed as having reprimanded or verbally punished someone. Caſtiga- continues below; the printed reprehendido is retained independently of Reprender above.
[c1-l016 >] *do de palaura.*
[c1-l016 note] This completes the translation as punishment by words. It is a verbal-rebuke gloss, not an instruction or a separate physical-punishment entry.
[c1-l017] Qimei. i, Guioy. *Mandado do ſenhor a q̃*
[c1-l017 note] Kimei is a lord's authoritative command, equated with Guioy. The scan reads G rather than OCR C, and separates ſenhor a; q̃ is an abbreviation and is not expanded.
[c1-l018 >] *ſe não pode deixar de obedecer por ſua auto-*
[c1-l018 note] The gloss says the command cannot be left unobeyed because of its authority. Auto- belongs to autoridade, not to a separate word.
[c1-l019 >] *ridade.*
[c1-l019 note] This completes autoridade and closes the command definition. The short continuation remains a body row.
[c1-l020] Qimen. Tattoqi vomote. *Roſto venera-*
[c1-l020 note] Kimen is a respected face or presence, paraphrased by tattoqi vomote. The adjective venerauel is divided physically after venera-.
[c1-l021 >] *uel, ou preſença falando cõ reſpeito. ¶* Qi-
[c1-l021 note] The gloss specifies respectful reference to someone's presence. The example begins Qi-, completing Qimenuo on the next row; this is an indented continuation, not a new entry.
[c1-l022 >] menuo motte mǒxita gotoqu. *Aßi como*
[c1-l022 note] The Japanese refers back to what was said in the addressee's presence. The physical continuation belongs to Qi- above and shares its indentation; Aßi como begins the Portuguese comparison.
[c1-l023 >] *diße preſencialmente o voßa mercè.*
[c1-l023 note] The intended sense is having spoken personally to your worship. The scan appears to print o voßa, although a would be expected syntactically; the OCR-supported o is retained as a possible printed anomaly rather than silently emended. The initial verb reads diße.
[c1-l024] Qimeô. *Couſa marauilhoſa.*
[c1-l024 note] Kimyō denotes something marvellous. The scan restores the circumflex on the headword and the Portuguese sequence marauilhoſa, not the OCR's maraunlhoſa.
[c1-l025] Qimeô fuxigui. *Idem.*
[c1-l025 note] Kimyō fushigi repeats the same marvellous or wondrous sense. Idem is italic reference apparatus, while the entire Japanese expression is roman.
[c1-l026] Qimeôna. *Idem.*
[c1-l026 note] The adjectival form kimyōna is assigned the same meaning. Its -na is part of the printed headword, not supplied editorial morphology.
[c1-l027] Qimet. Vocoſu, meſſuru. *O aleuantar, &*
[c1-l027 note] Kimet is explained through raising and extinguishing or destroying. The Japanese verb reads meſſuru, with two long-s types, correcting OCR meſuaru; the final t of Qimet is retained.
[c1-l028 >] *deſtruir.* Bup.
[c1-l028 note] The Portuguese destruction verb completes the paired explanation. Bup. is an upright Buddhist-domain label, including its full stop, not a partly italic word.
[c1-l029] Qimi. *Goſto, ou ſabor.* S.
[c1-l029 note] This kimi means taste or flavour. The final upright S. marks written usage and distinguishes the entry's apparatus from the following lord-sense.
[c1-l030] Qimi. *Senhor. ¶* Qimiuo vocaſu. *Ma-*
[c1-l030 note] The second kimi is a lord. The example means attacking or violating one's lord, and the Portuguese begins Matar across the line break.
[c1-l031 >] *tar ſeu ſenhor, ou aleuantarſe contra elle, &c.*
[c1-l031 note] The dictionary explains the example as killing one's lord or rising against him. This is a lexical illustration of the preceding Japanese expression.
[c1-l032] Qimiǒ. Bup. i, Namu. *Palaura cõ que*
[c1-l032 note] Kimyō is marked as Buddhist and equated with namu. The following Portuguese explanation describes its function in an invocation; Bup. remains wholly roman.
[c1-l033 >] *ſe hõra o Fotoque, ou Cami inuocandoo, &c.*
[c1-l033 note] The invocation honours a Buddha or kami. Fotoque and Cami are Japanese terms embedded in the Portuguese explanation; hõra and inuocandoo retain their printed abbreviation and spelling.
[c1-l034] Qimo. *Figado, baço, &c. ¶* Qimo, ta-
[c1-l034 note] Kimo is glossed through liver, spleen, and related inward parts. After the paragraph mark, ta- begins the soul-word tamaxij in an example of being stunned.
[c1-l035 >] maxijuo qeſu. *Ficar atonito. ¶* Qi-
[c1-l035 note] This completes the first expression for becoming stunned. The next Qi- begins another kimo expression; the repeated physical divisions must not be collapsed into one row.
[c1-l036 >] mouo qeſu. *Idem. ¶* Qimouo fiya-
[c1-l036 note] Kimo wo kesu has the same stunned sense. The next expression begins kimo wo hiyasu, divided after fiya-; the Japanese remains roman between italic glosses.
[c1-l037 >] ſu. l, tçubuſu. *Idẽ. ¶* Qimonimei-
[c1-l037 note] Hiyasu is completed and tçubuſu is offered as an alternative. Idẽ is abbreviated Idem; the following Qimonimei- begins a different expression about impressing something on the heart.
[c1-l038 >] zuru. *Penetrar o coração, & entranhas.*
[c1-l038 note] Meizuru completes the Japanese phrase and is explained as penetrating the heart and innermost parts. This is the affective force of the expression, not an anatomical procedure.
[c1-l039 >] *¶* Qimouo iru. *Por diligencia, & cui-*
[c1-l039 note] Kimo wo iru is glossed as applying diligence and care. Cui- begins cuidado on the following row, and the paragraph mark distinguishes this example from the preceding one.
[c1-l040 >] *dado em algũa couſa. ¶* Qimoga tçubu-
[c1-l040 note] The care is applied to some matter; a new example then begins kimo ga tçubururu. The actual tçubu- division is kept, as is the tilde on algũa.
[c1-l041 >] ruru. l, qimoguiyega xita. *Eſpantarſe,*
[c1-l041 note] The example is completed and an alternative expression follows, both describing astonishment. The scan reads qimoguiyega, without the extra n inserted by OCR.
[c1-l042 >] *ou ficar atonito.*
[c1-l042 note] The alternative gloss is becoming stunned. The scan has ou ficar, with a word space and one f, not OCR oufficar.
[c1-l043] Qimoiri. *Homem, ou peßoa diligente, & de*
[c1-l043 note] Kimoiri denotes a diligent, attentive person in this gloss. The missing letters of diligente and the ampersand before de are restored from the scan.
[c1-l044 >] *grande cuidado.*
[c1-l044 note] Great care completes the description of the diligent person. It remains part of Qimoiri rather than the following garment entry.
[c1-l045] Qimono. *Veſtido.*
[c1-l045 note] Kimono is directly glossed as clothing or a garment. This one-line entry is distinct from the following expression containing kimo and the particle no.
[c1-l046] Qimono tabane. *Coração, ou meo das ẽtranhas.*
[c1-l046 note] Kimo no tabane is explained as the heart or centre of the inward parts. The scan reads Qimono tabane, not Qinono rabane, and ẽtranhas contains an abbreviation tilde whose missing n is not expanded.
[c1-l047] Qimot. i, Vtçuuamono. *Vaſo, ou caixa ẽ*
[c1-l047 note] Qimot is equated with a vessel or container. The scan restores m in the headword and t in Vtçuuamono; final t and the double u are retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l048 >] *que ſe mete algũa couſa.*
[c1-l048 note] The vessel or box is something into which an object is placed. This last body row closes the left column and remains separate from the bottom rule.
