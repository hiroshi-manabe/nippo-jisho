## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Fanarebanare. *Partes, & pedaços. Vt,*
[c1-l001 note] `Fanarebanare` means separate parts or pieces. The following example applies separation to the body, not merely to people living apart.
[c1-l002 >] Ientai fanarebanareni naru. *Eſtar o corpo*
[c1-l002 note] The body becomes separated into pieces. `Ientai` reads ゼンタイ (全体): the initial I is consonantal J, as in the attested `Ienuo` (膳を). Preserve the printed I; it is not an anomalous spelling requiring correction.
[c1-l003 >] *feito em pedaços, ou deſpedaçado.*
[c1-l003 note] Portuguese explicitly says the body is in pieces or dismembered. This confirms the physical, rather than social, sense of separation in the example.
[c1-l004] Fanarejima. *Ilha apartada.*
[c1-l004 note] `Fanarejima` is a detached or isolated island. The compound's geographic meaning is directly confirmed by `Ilha apartada`.
[c1-l005] Fanareqiri, u, itta. *Apartarſe, ou deſape-*
[c1-l005 note] `Fanareqiri` means completely separating or detaching. The completion force of `-qiri` is expressed by `de todo` below.
[c1-l006 >] *garſe de todo. ¶ Item, Determinarſe, &*
[c1-l006 note] Complete detachment is followed by a second sense, becoming determined. Both involve reaching a decisive state rather than partial separation.
[c1-l007 >] *reſoluerſe. ¶* Fanare qitta fito. *Homẽ*
[c1-l007 note] `Fanare qitta fito` is a resolute person. The Portuguese finishes determination before introducing this example.
[c1-l008 >] *determinado. ¶* Fanare qitta xucqe. *Re-*
[c1-l008 note] After the determined-person gloss, `Fanare qitta xucqe` gives a religious practitioner detached from worldly things. The noun's religious meaning governs the next line.
[c1-l009 >] *ligioſo deſapegado de tudo.*
[c1-l009 note] The practitioner is detached from everything. This completes the renunciatory sense rather than describing someone physically separated from a group.
[c1-l010] Fanaſaqi, u, aita. *Sairem as roſas, ou flores,*
[c1-l010 note] `Fanaſaqi` describes flowers blooming. The paradigm's abbreviated ending is retained; Portuguese supplies the intransitive blossoming sense.
[c1-l011 >] *ou florecerem as aruores, & eruas.*
[c1-l011 note] Trees and herbs flowering extend the definition beyond a single rose. The line completes the same botanical verb.
[c1-l012] Fanaſomeguinu. *Peças de ſeda de Iapão tin-*
[c1-l012 note] `Fanaſomeguinu` denotes pieces of Japanese silk with a specified dye color. `guinu` retains hard ギ in the cloth compound.
[c1-l013 >] *tas de vermelho.*
[c1-l013 note] The cloth is dyed red. This color specification belongs to the textile definition, not the neighboring nose vocabulary.
[c1-l014] Fanaſugi. *Fio, ou lombo do nariz.*
[c1-l014 note] `Fanaſugi` is the ridge of the nose, 鼻筋. Bare `gi` represents the historical ヂ series in this compound.
[c1-l015] Fanaſui. *Hum paſſarinho que come flores.*
[c1-l015 note] `Fanaſui` names a small bird that eats flowers. The gloss alone does not establish a modern species or whether nectar-feeding is intended more narrowly.
[c1-l016] Fanatachibana. *Certa roſa que cheira bem.*
[c1-l016 note] `Fanatachibana` is described as a fragrant flower. Do not substitute a modern plant identification solely from the compound's familiar syllables.
[c1-l017] Fanatate. *Vaſo em que poem flores a maneira*
[c1-l017 note] `Fanatate` is a vessel in which flowers are arranged. The bouquet comparison below identifies its use.
[c1-l018 >] *de ramalhete.*
[c1-l018 note] “Like a bouquet” completes the flower-vessel description. This is not another example of the verb standing or rising.
[c1-l019] Fanatçumi. *O colher flores. Vt,* Fanatçu-
[c1-l019 note] `Fanatçumi` is flower-picking. The example repeats the noun, split before the following line's `miuo`.
[c1-l020 >] miuo ſuru.
[c1-l020 note] `Fanatçumiuo ſuru` means performing flower-picking. The split is physical and must not create a separate `miuo` word in interpretation.
[c1-l021] Fanatçuqini. *Adu. De roſto a roſto, ou*
[c1-l021 note] `Fanatçuqini` is face-to-face or directly opposite. The grammatical label marks an adverb, not an object or flower name.
[c1-l022 >] *de peito. ¶* Fanatçuqini yuqiuǒ. *En-*
[c1-l022 note] The encounter is face-to-face. The enlarged scan reads `yuqiuǒ`, a visibly unusual spelling where 行き会う is expected; preserve its medial letter rather than silently regularizing to `yuqiǒ`.
[c1-l023 >] *contrarſe de roſto a roſto.*
[c1-l023 note] `Encontrarſe de roſto a roſto` confirms meeting face-to-face. This supports the intended verb while leaving its irregular printed spelling visible.
[c1-l024] Fanauo. *Cabreſtilho dos çapatos, ou alparcas*
[c1-l024 note] `Fanauo` is the thong of Japanese footwear, 鼻緒. It is a whole noun here, not `fana` followed by object particle `uo`.
[c1-l025 >] *de Iapão que ſe encaixa entre os dedos dos pes.*
[c1-l025 note] The thong fits between the toes. This physical detail confirms the sandal-part sense of the preceding compound.
[c1-l026] Fanaxi, ſu, aita. *Deſpegar, ou apartar.*
[c1-l026 note] This `Fanaxi` means detaching or separating. Its examples below extend the action to releasing projectiles and animals.
[c1-l027 >] ¶ Yauo fanaſu. *Atirar com frecha.*
[c1-l027 note] `Yauo fanaſu` means shooting an arrow. The basic release meaning remains visible in the Japanese object and verb.
[c1-l028 >] ¶ Teppǒuo fanaſu, l, vtçu. *Deſparar*
[c1-l028 note] `Teppǒuo fanaſu, l, vtçu` gives alternative verbs for firing a gun. The object is the firearm, not the person shot.
[c1-l029 >] *à eſpingarda, &c. ¶* Tori, qedamono
[c1-l029 note] The gun gloss finishes. `Tori, qedamono` then names birds and beasts as the objects of letting go.
[c1-l030 >] uo fanaſu. *Largar, ou ſoltar paſſaros, ou a-*
[c1-l030 note] `uo fanaſu` completes releasing those animals. The Portuguese likewise gives freeing birds or beasts.
[c1-l031 >] *nimaes.*
[c1-l031 note] `a- / nimaes` completes the animal list. This short line closes the release sense before the next homograph.
[c1-l032] Fanaxi, ſu, aita. *Praticar. Vt,* Nochini go-
[c1-l032 note] This `Fanaxi` means talking or conversing. Portuguese `Praticar` is conversational here, not modern “practice” imposed on the definition.
[c1-l033 >] zatte fanaſaxerarei. *Venha voſſa mercè*
[c1-l033 note] `Nochini gozatte fanaſaxerarei` politely invites someone to come later and talk. Read the honorific and request morphology together rather than as independent dictionary forms.
[c1-l034 >] *de pois, & pratique.*
[c1-l034 note] “Later, and converse” completes the Portuguese invitation. It confirms the conversation homograph rather than releasing an object.
[c1-l035] Fanaxicaqe, uru, eta. *Soltar o cão pera to-*
[c1-l035 note] `Fanaxicaqe` here means releasing a dog upon prey. Despite the modern familiar 話しかける, the definition belongs to the release family.
[c1-l036 >] *mar a caça, &c. ¶ Item, Deſparar eſpin-*
[c1-l036 note] The dog takes the quarry; another sense begins with firing a gun. Both maintain the directed-release action.
[c1-l037 >] *garda, bombarda, &c.*
[c1-l037 note] A firearm or cannon completes the list. The line continues the shooting sense, not the conversational homograph.
[c1-l038] Fanaxigai, l, potiùs, fanachigai. *Paſſaro,*
[c1-l038 note] The compiler prefers `fanachigai` to `Fanaxigai`. This expresses a lexical preference without authorizing deletion of either printed form.
[c1-l039 >] *ou animal que ſe cria fora da gayola ſolto.*
[c1-l039 note] A bird or animal is raised loose, outside a cage. The definition supplies the husbandry sense of the compound.
[c1-l040 >] ¶ Fanaxigaini ſuru. *Criar aſſi ſolto fora*
[c1-l040 note] `Fanaxigaini ſuru` means keeping an animal in that free-ranging manner. The Portuguese repetition links it to the preceding definition.
[c1-l041 >] *da gayola, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] Being outside the cage completes the example. The line is a continuation rather than another headword for a cage.
[c1-l042] Fanaxiſute, tçuru, eta. *Deſparar eſpingar-*
[c1-l042 note] `Fanaxiſute` means discharging without aiming at a target. The second verb adds disposal or release rather than an intended hit.
[c1-l043 >] *da, ou arco, ſem apontar a nada. ¶* Tebi-
[c1-l043 note] A gun or bow may be discharged without aiming. `Tebi-` starts a Japanese weapon name continued below.
[c1-l044 >] Yano cuſuriuo fanaxi ſutçuru. *Deſparar*
[c1-l044 note] `Tebi- / Yano cuſuriuo` concerns the weapon's powder charge. The printed capital at the break does not make `Yano` an independent name.
[c1-l045 >] *ſem atirar de propoſito pera não ter a poluora*
[c1-l045 note] The discharge is not aimed at anything, so powder will not remain loaded. `de propoſito` concerns deliberate targeting in this context.
[c1-l046 >] *muito tempo dentro da eſpingarda.*
[c1-l046 note] The powder should not stay inside the gun for a long time. This completes the reason for the discharge, not an additional weapon definition.
[c1-l047] Fanaxite. *Homem que pratica bem, & com*
[c1-l047 note] `Fanaxite` first denotes a good, engaging speaker. This derives from the conversation sense rather than shooting.
[c1-l048 >] *graça. ¶ Itẽ, Homem q̃ atira com eſpingarda.*
[c1-l048 note] The second sense is a person who fires a gun. The one spelling thus covers agents from two different `fanaxi` meanings.

[c2-l001] Fanaxôbi. *Certa roſa vermelha.*
[c2-l001 note] `Fanaxôbi` names a red flower. Its resemblance to modern plant names is not enough to force a botanical identification or change its vowel mark.
[c2-l002] Fanayacana. *Couſa fermoſa, galante, & lu-*
[c2-l002 note] `Fanayacana` means beautiful, elegant, or splendid. The ending `-na` fits the attributive adjectival form.
[c2-l003 >] *ſtroſa, &c.*
[c2-l003 note] `lu- / ſtroſa` completes the lustrous or splendid quality. It belongs to the same adjective, not an additional entry.
[c2-l004] Fanayacani. *Adu. Galantemente, ou lu-*
[c2-l004 note] `Fanayacani` is the adverbial counterpart, “splendidly.” The Portuguese similarly uses adverbial expressions.
[c2-l005 >] *zida, & luſtroſamente. ¶* Sono fitono
[c2-l005 note] The adverbial gloss ends, then `Sono fitono` begins an example about that person's clothing. Keep the genitive with the next line.
[c2-l006 >] xǒzocu ichidãto fanayacani miyeta. *Yax.*
[c2-l006 note] `xǒzocu ichidãto fanayacani miyeta` means the attire looked especially splendid. `Yax.` is a citation label, not part of the Japanese sentence.
[c2-l007 >] *O veſtido daquelle homem parecia mui galante,*
[c2-l007 note] Portuguese translates the man's clothing as appearing very elegant. This confirms that clothing, not the man's behavior, is the subject.
[c2-l008 >] *& luzido.*
[c2-l008 note] “Brilliant” completes the description of the attire. The short line closes the literary example.
[c2-l009] Fanazono. *Iardim de flores.*
[c2-l009 note] `Fanazono` is a flower garden. The Portuguese directly confirms the 花園 compound.
[c2-l010] Fanazzuri. i, Fanaguri. *Hũ circulo de pao-*
[c2-l010 note] `Fanazzuri` is equated with `Fanaguri`, the cattle nose-ring. This repetition lets the material description be compared with f168.
[c2-l011 >] *de era, ou vimes que metem pollas ventas dos*
[c2-l011 note] The scan reads `de era`, ivy, matching f168's ring materials, not wax (`cera`). Withies provide the other flexible material.
[c2-l012 >] *narizes aos bois.*
[c2-l012 note] The ring is placed through cattle nostrils. This completes the object definition and distinguishes it from decorative rings.
[c2-l013] Fanbacama. *Hũa maneira de calçoẽs curtos.*
[c2-l013 note] `Fanbacama` denotes a kind of short trousers or hakama. The half-length implication is supported by the Portuguese, though no exact modern garment is specified.
[c2-l014] Fanben. *Certo comer de tǒfus aſſados, & de-*
[c2-l014 note] `Fanben` is a food made from roasted `tǒfus`. Do not assume the ingredients of modern はんぺん; the historical definition explicitly gives tofu.
[c2-l015 >] *pois cozidos em Miſô.*
[c2-l015 note] After roasting, the tofu is cooked in `Miſô`. This completes the preparation described by the dictionary, not a modern recipe reconstruction.
[c2-l016] Fanberi, u. i, Gozaru, sǒrǒ, &c. *Ser,*
[c2-l016 note] `Fanberi` is the historical polite being verb はんべり. The scan supports `Gozaru`, paired with `sǒrǒ`, rather than the previous `Gozuru` reading.
[c2-l017 >] *eſtar, &c. S.*
[c2-l017 note] “To be, to exist” completes the copular gloss. `S.` remains an editorial label rather than a Japanese syllable.
[c2-l018] Fanbiqi. *A metade de hũa peça de ſeda fina,*
[c2-l018 note] `Fanbiqi` is half a piece of fine silk. The textile measure, not a count of living creatures, governs this use of `-biqi`.
[c2-l019 >] *& delgada chamada Qinu.*
[c2-l019 note] The silk is called `Qinu`. This Japanese material word is embedded in the Portuguese description.
[c2-l020] Fanbun. *A metade.*
[c2-l020 note] `Fanbun` is half. Its simple gloss matches the compound illustrated earlier in the dictionary.
[c2-l021] Fancai. *Vt,* Fancai fancô. Fanbun firaqi,
[c2-l021 note] The scan supports `Fancai`, not `Fincai`, in the half-open/half-closed expression. `Fanbun firaqi` explicitly provides “half open.”
[c2-l022 >] fanbunua fuſagatte aru coto. *Eſtar meo*
[c2-l022 note] `fanbunua fuſagatte aru coto` supplies “the other half is closed.” The Japanese balanced structure is a useful check on the compound above.
[c2-l023 >] *aberto, & meo fechado.*
[c2-l023 note] Portuguese repeats half-open and half-closed. This confirms the contrast rather than an obscure independent `Fincai` term.
[c2-l024] Fanchicu. Madara naru taqe. *Certa laya de*
[c2-l024 note] `Madara naru taqe` means mottled bamboo, supporting 斑竹. The attributive `naru` belongs to the descriptive Japanese phrase.
[c2-l025 >] *bambus de varias cores.*
[c2-l025 note] The bamboo has several colors. This completes the botanical appearance description without naming a modern species.
[c2-l026] Fandai. *Meſa alta de comer. Vt,* Fandai-
[c2-l026 note] `Fandai` is a high dining table. The example begins with the same noun and continues its postposition below.
[c2-l027 >] ni mucǒ, l, tçuqu. *Porſe à meſa.*
[c2-l027 note] `Fandaini mucǒ, l, tçuqu` means facing or taking one's place at the table. The Portuguese gloss expresses sitting or placing oneself there.
[c2-l028] Fandan. Cotouari, u. *Arrezoar como ſo-*
[c2-l028 note] `Fandan` concerns reasoning or deciding, compatible with 判断. `Cotouari, u` supplies the verbal explanation.
[c2-l029 >] *bre algũa demãda, &c. ou determinar, &*
[c2-l029 note] The reasoning may concern a lawsuit or dispute. The line then adds deciding or settling the matter.
[c2-l030 >] *reſoluer como juiz.*
[c2-l030 note] The scan supports `reſoluer`, “resolve,” in the judge comparison, rather than `reſoluar`. This supplies the decision-making sense of the headword.
[c2-l031] Fandan. *A metade de hũa varzea, peça, can-*
[c2-l031 note] This `Fandan` instead means half of something measured by `tan`. Fields and pieces of material show that it is a measure-related homograph.
[c2-l032 >] *ga, & de outras couſas que ſe contão por Ittan,*
[c2-l032 note] `Ittan` names one unit in the Japanese counting series. It belongs inside the Portuguese explanation, not a newly introduced headword.
[c2-l033 >] *nitan, &c.*
[c2-l033 note] `nitan` supplies two units, continuing the same count. The definition concerns halving objects measured in that series.
[c2-l034] Fandô. *Hum vaſo de metal em que tem agoa*
[c2-l034 note] `Fandô` is a metal vessel containing water. Its tea-related use is specified below.
[c2-l035 >] *pera o Chanoyu, ou outros vſos. As vezes*
[c2-l035 note] The water serves `Chanoyu` or other purposes. This is a Japanese practice name embedded in Portuguese prose.
[c2-l036 >] *ainda que ſeja de barro ſe diz,* Fandô.
[c2-l036 note] The name may also apply to an earthenware vessel. Material variation is explicitly allowed by the compiler, so “metal” is not an absolute restriction.
[c2-l037] Fane. *Aſas de paſſaro.*
[c2-l037 note] This `Fane` means wings. The next entry has another sense of the same spelling, not a description of feathers becoming muddy.
[c2-l038] Fane. *Lama que ſalta por de tras nos veſtidos*
[c2-l038 note] The second `Fane` is mud splashed onto clothing from behind. The source of the splash follows below.
[c2-l039 >] *dos çapatos, ou alparcas de Iapão. ¶* Fa-
[c2-l039 note] Japanese shoes or sandals throw up the mud. `Fa-` begins the example noun continued on the next line.
[c2-l040 >] nega agaru. *Salpicaremſe os veſtidos cõ eſ-*
[c2-l040 note] `Fanega agaru` means the splash rises. Portuguese describes the clothes being spattered, translating the result rather than matching the Japanese subject literally.
[c2-l041 >] *ta lama dos çapatos por de tras.*
[c2-l041 note] The mud comes from footwear behind the wearer. This completes the splash example and its physical direction.
[c2-l042] Fane, uru, eta. *Dar couces. ¶* Cubiuo
[c2-l042 note] `Fane` as a verb first means kicking. `Cubiuo` begins a separate beheading example, not a body part being kicked in the first gloss.
[c2-l043 >] fanuru. *Cortar a cabeça. ¶ Item,* Fa-
[c2-l043 note] `Cubiuo fanuru` means cutting off the head. Another sense of the same verb begins after `Item`.
[c2-l044 >] ne, uru. *Aleuantar algum pao, ou porta de*
[c2-l044 note] The next sense is raising wood or a door. The following lines explain leaving it lifted or held aside.
[c2-l045 >] *maneira que fique ſuſpenſa, & pinchada pera*
[c2-l045 note] The object remains suspended and pushed to one side. This is the physical result of lifting it, not a separate adjective entry.
[c2-l046 >] *hũa parte. ¶ Item. Dar paparote com o*
[c2-l046 note] The sideward position completes that sense. A further finger-flicking sense then begins.
[c2-l047 >] *dedo à maneira dos gafanhotos quãdo ſe ſacodẽ*
[c2-l047 note] The flick is compared with grasshoppers shaking themselves. The sentence continues onto f170, where the feet and another verb are supplied.
