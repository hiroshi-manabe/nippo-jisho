[c1-l011 >] Taxicaſa.

[c1-l012] Taxin. Bechino cocoro. *Outro coração.*

[c1-l016] Taxinami, u, ǒda. *Conſeruar, poupar.*
[c1-l016 note] Taxinami initially means preserving or being sparing. The caron in ǒda is visibly retained, even though expectations from other contracted forms could suggest a circumflex.
[c1-l017 >] *¶* Miuo taxinamu. *Ter boa conta conſi-*

[c1-l019 note] After completing the clothing example, geinoo wo tashinamu turns to practising one's arts or accomplishments properly. Vſar bẽ begins the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l020 >] *& com louuor, & a ſeu tempo das artes, &*

[c1-l021 >] *habilidades que hum aprendeo, ou tem.*

[c1-l025] Taxinami. *Reſguardo, policia, honeſtida-*

[c1-l027] Taxintçu. *Lingua que falão entre ſi os feiti-*
[c1-l027 note] Taxintçu is described as a special language used among practitioners of divination or magic. The gloss, rather than a modern technical meaning inferred from the headword, governs this interpretation.
[c1-l028 >] *ceiros, ou adiuinhos com os diſcipulos differen-*

[c1-l030 >] *¶* Aicotoba *He mais corrente, & gèral*

[c1-l031 >] *por lingua com que algũs ſe entendem em par-*

[c1-l032 >] *ticular.*

[c1-l033] Taxǒ. Betno ſugime. *Outra linha, ou gèração.*

[c1-l036 >] *ça, ou a mizade de outros nacimentos. ¶* Ta-

[c1-l037 >] xǒ quǒgô. *Nacimento, ou gèração de mui-*
[c1-l037 note] The divided example continues Taxǒ quǒgô, explained as births or generations over many ages. The gloss itself supplies the long-duration interpretation.

[c1-l043 >] *tra ſeita, ou profißão.*

[c1-l044] Taxu. Amatano nuxi. *Muitos donos, ou ſe-*
[c1-l044 note] Taxu means many owners or masters, explained by amata no nushi. The scan and Japanese phrase support Amatano, not OCR Amaiano.

[c1-l047 note] The maxim says the expert does not reject a manner or means of working. The scan supports qirauazu, not qiranazu; its Portuguese explanation continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l009 >] *feito. ¶ Item, comprir, ou aperfeiçoar.*
[c2-l009 note] Feito completes perfeito. The additional transitive sense is fulfilling or perfecting something, illustrated by the following desire example.

[c2-l011 >] *¶* Taxxite monouo ſuru. *Fazer algũa*

[c2-l012 >] *couſa perfeitamente.*

[c2-l013] Tayaſui. *Couſa facil.*
[c2-l013 note] Tayaſui means easy. The brief Portuguese gloss does not imply a separate moral or evaluative sense.
[c2-l014 >] Tayasǔ. *Adu.*

[c2-l015 >] Tayaſuſa.

[c2-l016] Tayaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer ceſſar, ou interrom-*
[c2-l016 note] Tayaxi is causing something to cease or interrupting its continuation. The following examples concern ending a lineage and destroying a root.

[c2-l017 note] Per completes interromper. Shison wo tayasu introduces ending one's descendants or lineage; the line is physically indented like the surrounding continuations, despite the draft's flush-left metadata.
[c2-l018 >] ſu. *Fazer ceßar, ou acabar a gèração, ou*
[c2-l018 note] Su completes tayasu. The Portuguese explains causing a generation or line of descent to cease or end, with another application following below.

[c2-l019 note] The first explanation also mentions a branch of a sect. Ne wo tayasu begins destroying a root; the visible t in ſeita corrects OCR ſeira.
[c2-l020 >] *ſtruir, ou arrancar a raiz da aruore, &c.*
[c2-l020 note] The literal example is destroying or pulling out a tree's root. Struir completes De-ſtruir at the preceding line end.
[c2-l021 >] *¶ Item, per met. Deſtruir a couſa de raiz.*
[c2-l021 note] The figurative sense is destroying something at its root. The scan supports Deſtruir rather than OCR Deſtrair, consistent with the explicit root image.
[c2-l022] Taye, uru, eta. *Acabarſe de tudo, ceſſar, &c.*
[c2-l022 note] Taye is coming completely to an end or ceasing. The faint initial c of ceſſar is restored; its absence in OCR did not represent a separate historical word.
[c2-l023 >] *¶* Nacaga tayuru. *Quebrarſe a amiza-*
[c2-l023 note] Naka ga tayuru illustrates a friendship or relationship breaking off. The Portuguese amiza- continues into amizade on the following row.
[c2-l024 >] *de. ¶* Michiga tayuru. *Tomarſe, ou*
[c2-l024 note] After completing amizade, michi ga tayuru introduces roads becoming cut off. The Portuguese begins with Tomarſe and continues its alternative explanation below.
[c2-l025 >] *impedirenſe os caminhos. ¶* Inochiga ta-
[c2-l025 note] The blocked-roads example continues here; the following example begins inochi ga tayuru, the ending of a life. The line-final ta- belongs to that Japanese verb.
[c2-l026 >] yuru. *Acabarſe a vida. i, Morrer.*
[c2-l026 note] Life comes to an end, explicitly explained as dying. The Japanese verb completes across the preceding line break; the explanatory i is followed by a visible comma, not the draft's period.
[c2-l027 >] *¶* Iqiga tayuru. *Faltar o follego. ¶* Ato-
[c2-l027 note] Iki ga tayuru means that breath fails; the initial of follego is an f, not a long s. The next example begins ato ga tayuru, concerning failure of succession.
[c2-l028 >] ga tayuru. *Faltar herdeiro, ou ſocceßor.*
[c2-l028 note] The lack of an heir or successor explains the preceding ato ga tayuru. This line starts with ga tayuru in the scan; the doubled c in the historical spelling of successor is visible.
[c2-l029] Tayedaye. *Vt,* Tayedayeni naru. *Irſe*
[c2-l029 note] Tayedaye describes intermittent or dwindling continuity. The example is Tayedayeni naru; the draft's o in that repetition is corrected to the visible e.
[c2-l030 >] *diminuindo, ou a poucando, &c.*
[c2-l030 note] The Portuguese explains a gradual decrease or reduction. The printed separation a poucando is retained rather than silently replacing it with a modern word division.
[c2-l031] Tayegatai. *Sofrer com difficuldade. ¶* Ta-
[c2-l031 note] Tayegatai is difficult to endure. A separate example starts at the end of the line and continues with suffering on the next row.

[c2-l032 note] The example means unbearable suffering, and curuximi has m rather than the draft's n. The crowded Portuguese adjective is read inſofriueis, with a weakly formed medial u; this chosen reading is less certain than the surrounding words.
[c2-l033 >] Tayegataſa.
[c2-l033 note] Tayegatasa is the corresponding abstract noun, the quality or degree of being difficult to endure. It is a separate indented physical row, not another headword at the column margin.
[c2-l034 >] Tayegatǒ.

[c2-l036 >] *ra deſi.*

[c2-l037] Tayema. *Eſpaço, ou interrupção. ¶* Fu-

[c2-l039 >] *interromper as obras.*

[c2-l040] Tayena. l, tayenaru. *Couſa marauilhoſa, ine*
[c2-l040 note] Tayena and tayenaru describe something wondrous or ineffable. The last letters are ine, continuing inefauel on the next line; no secure line-final hyphen is supplied.
[c2-l041 >] *fauel, &c.* S. *Vt,* Tayenaru niuoi.
[c2-l041 note] Fauel completes the adjective ineffable. The upright S. marks written usage according to the supplied dictionary key; Vt then introduces the example beginning with a wonderful fragrance.
[c2-l042 >] iro. coye, &c. *Excellente, & marauilho-*
[c2-l042 note] Iro and coye extend the example to color and voice. The Portuguese repeats that these are excellent and wonderful; marauilho- is one word, not the OCR's separated mar auilho-.
[c2-l043 >] *ſo cheiro, cor, voz, &c.*
[c2-l043 note] The continuation lists fragrance, color, and voice in the same order as the Japanese examples. The s at the beginning completes marauilhoſo across the row break.
[c2-l044 >] Tayeni. *Aduerb.*
[c2-l044 note] Tayeni is explicitly the adverbial derivative. Its indentation and separate line are preserved.
[c2-l045] Tayexi, ſu, eita. l, tayaxi, ſu. *Destruir,*
[c2-l045 note] Tayexi and the alternative tayaxi mean to destroy or bring to an end. The alternative starts with t, not the OCR r; eita remains unchanged. The baseline st transcription in Destruir is retained because the joined italic types do not securely establish a long s.
[c2-l046 >] *fazer ceßar, &c.*
[c2-l046 note] The second Portuguese equivalent is to cause cessation. The entry ends here, before the following verb of silent endurance.
[c2-l047] Tayexinobi, u, ôda. *Sofrer calandoſe, &*
[c2-l047 note] Tayexinobi means to endure silently. The damaged consonant is read b, consistent with the visible stem and the u/ôda conjugation, rather than the draft's f; the final mark is an ampersand, continuing with não on f0479.
