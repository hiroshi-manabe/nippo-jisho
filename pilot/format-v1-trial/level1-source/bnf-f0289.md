[c1-l001] Iinpi. *Segredo de Cami. Vt,* Iinpiuo ara-
[c1-l001 note] Jinpi is a secret of a kami; the following example means revealing it. Restore initial I and the scan’s de Cami rather than do cami.
[c1-l002 >] uaſu. *Manifeſtar o Cami os ſegredos, ou ou*
[c1-l002 note] Uaſu completes arawasu. The kami reveals secrets, or another person does so in its place; Manifeſtar corrects the damaged OCR vowel.
[c1-l003 >] *tro em ſeu lugar.*
[c1-l003 note] Tro completes outro without an inferred hyphen above; em ſeu lugar specifies acting in the kami’s place. An irregular blot above em is not a securely legible accent; no additional character is supplied.
[c1-l004] Iinpô. Camino tacara. *Riquezas do Cami.*
[c1-l004 note] Jinpoo is kami no takara, the kami’s treasures. The Japanese word is Camino, not the OCR’s Canino.
[c1-l005] Iinpǔ. *Cuſtume dos homẽs.* S.
[c1-l005 note] Jinpuu is human custom or manners. The damaged headword begins I, not F, and the final S is an upright written-register label. The second vowel of Cuſtume is worn; u is retained as the usable reading, as is the damaged final ǔ of the headword.
[c1-l006] Iinpuxô. Yoqi jintaito, monono cazunara-
[c1-l006 note] Jinpushoo contrasts a person of good standing with someone not counted as important. The second Japanese description continues in nu hito below.
[c1-l007 >] nu fito. *Homem nobre, & homem baixo.*
[c1-l007 note] Nu hito completes mono no kazu naranu hito. The Portuguese explicitly contrasts a noble person and a low-status person.
[c1-l008 >] *Vt,* Iinpuxôni yorazu. *Não tẽdo deuer*
[c1-l008 note] Jinpushoo ni yorazu means without distinction of personal standing; the Portuguese begins a formulation independent of obligations to either rank.
[c1-l009 >] *nem com altos nem com baixos.*
[c1-l009 note] Neither the high nor the low completes the preceding social contrast. It belongs to the same example, not a separate definition.
[c1-l010] Iinqi. Fitono qi. *Vigor natural do homem.*
[c1-l010 note] Jinki, hito no ki, is a person’s natural vitality. The opening Ii sequence is damaged but recoverable, not the OCR’s M.
[c1-l011 >] *¶ Itẽ, Bafo, ou ar q̃ reſulta de eſtar muita*
[c1-l011 note] A second sense concerns the breath or air produced when many people are gathered. The abbreviation Itẽ and q̃ remain unexpanded.
[c1-l012 >] *gente junta. Vt,* Iinqi fanafada atçuxi. *O-*
[c1-l012 note] Completes the crowded-air definition; jinki hanahada atsushi describes its heat. The scan really prints O- at the edge, despite O being a complete article.
[c1-l013 >] *bafo, ou ar de muita gente he muito quente.*
[c1-l013 note] The breath or air of a crowd is very hot. The first noun is bafo with f, agreeing with the earlier Bafo, not OCR baſo.
[c1-l014] Iinqio. *Falta de força, ou vigor dos rins.*
[c1-l014 note] Jinkyo denotes lack of strength or vitality in the kidneys, as the historical medical gloss explicitly states.
[c1-l015] Iinqiǒtçǔ. *Primeiro dote, ou qualidade dos*
[c1-l015 note] Jinkyootsuu is introduced as the first of six exceptional endowments. Both carons are visible and are not replaced by modern romanization.
[c1-l016 >] *ſeis que poem os Iapoẽs que he velocidade no*
[c1-l016 note] The six qualities are attributed to Japanese accounts; this one is identified as speed in movement, completed by andar on the next row.
[c1-l017 >] *andar.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*profunda.*
[c1-l017 note] Andar completes the speed-of-movement definition. The far-right (profunda. physically precedes the next entry but completes its Portuguese gloss; it is preserved here as displaced text, not joined to andar.
[c1-l018] Iinri. Fucaqi cotouari. *Rezão, ou materia*
[c1-l018 note] Jinri, fukaki kotowari, is a deep reason or subject. Its adjective profunda is printed at the far right of the preceding physical row.
[c1-l019] Iinrin. i, Fito. *Homẽs, ou gente. ¶* Iin-
[c1-l019 note] Jinrin is equated with hito and means people. The example begins Iin- and continues into a place separated from human habitation.
[c1-l020 >] rin fanareta tocoro. *Deſerto, ou deſpouo-*
[c1-l020 note] Jinrin hanareta tokoro denotes a deserted or uninhabited place. The Portuguese deſpouo- continues below.
[c1-l021 >] *ado de gente.*
[c1-l021 note] Ado completes deſpouoado de gente, devoid of inhabitants. This short line remains attached to the desert-place example.
[c1-l022] Iinriǒ. *Terras, ou renda dos Camis.*
[c1-l022 note] Jinryoo comprises lands or revenue belonging to the kami. The caron is retained, and Camis is embedded in Portuguese.
[c1-l023] Iinriqi. Camino chicara. *Força, ou forta-*
[c1-l023 note] Jinriki is kami no chikara, divine strength. The scan has r in the headword, not OCR t; forta- begins fortaleza below.
[c1-l024 >] *leza, & poder dos Camis.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes fortaleza and adds the power of the kami. Camis belongs to the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l025] Iinſô. Vaxiri, u. *Correr ligeiramente.*
[c1-l025 note] Jinsoo is glossed as running lightly or swiftly. The accompanying form visibly begins Vaxiri, not expected Faxiri; preserve this printed anomaly rather than silently emending it.
[c1-l026] Iintai. Iinno tai. *Homem honrado, ou nobre.*
[c1-l026 note] Jintai, explained as jin no tai, refers here to an honourable or noble person, rather than the modern anatomical sense of the homophone.
[c1-l027 >] *¶* Qeccôna jintaigia. *He peßoa de ho-*
[c1-l027 note] Kekkoo na jintai ja evaluates someone as a splendid personage. The Portuguese begins a description of an honourable man and continues below.
[c1-l028 >] *mem honrado, bem vestido, & atauiado, &c.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes homem honrado and specifies being well dressed and adorned. The clothing description explains the outward social impression.
[c1-l029] Iintǒ. *Caminho, ou ley da piedade, & amor*
[c1-l029 note] Jintoo is the way or law of compassion and love. Its object, one’s neighbour, follows on the next line.
[c1-l030 >] *do proximo, que he hũa das virtudes moraes*
[c1-l030 note] Love of one’s neighbour is identified as a moral virtue; the peoples to whom the account attributes it follow below.
[c1-l031 >] *dos Chinas, & Iapoẽs.*
[c1-l031 note] The Chinese and Japanese complete the description of this moral tradition. Both words here are Portuguese ethnonyms.
[c1-l032] Iintocu. *Proueito, ou fruito desta virtude moral.*
[c1-l032 note] Jintoku is the benefit or fruit of the preceding moral virtue. The initial I corrects OCR long s; retain the source’s fruito spelling.
[c1-l033] Iintôna. *Vt,* Iintôna fito. *Homem mo-*
[c1-l033 note] Jintoona modifies a person who is modest or orderly. Its circumflex differs from the caron in the preceding Jintǒ entry and is preserved.
[c1-l034 >] *deſto, quieto, ou regular, & bem criado.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes modeſto and adds quiet, regular and well brought up, describing the person’s behaviour and upbringing.
[c1-l035] Iintôraxij. *Vt,* Iintôraxij fito. *Homem*
[c1-l035 note] Jintoorashii hito describes a dignified, modest person. The example repeats the headword, not OCR untôraxij; this is a new flush-left entry.
[c1-l036 >] *graue, & modeſto, cortes, &c.*
[c1-l036 note] Grave, modest and courteous completes the preceding person-description. The entire row belongs to the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l037] Iin von. Fucaqu touoxi. *Couſa muito anti-*
[c1-l037 note] The first Jin von is glossed fukaku tooshi, deeply remote. Its sense is temporal antiquity, not the favours of the following homographs.
[c1-l038 >] *gua, & diſtante de noſſos tempos.* S.
[c1-l038 note] Gua completes antigua, followed by remoteness from our times. The final upright S marks written usage.
[c1-l039] Iin von. *Beneficios de homem piadoſo, & ami-*
[c1-l039 note] The second Jin von denotes benefits conferred by a compassionate person. Ami- begins the description of someone disposed to do good.
[c1-l040 >] *go de bem fazer.*
[c1-l040 note] Go completes amigo de bem fazer, a person inclined to beneficence. This continuation closes the second Jin von entry.
[c1-l041] Iin von. Fucai von. *Grandes beneficios.*
[c1-l041 note] The third Jin von is fukai on, deep or great favours. It is a separate flush-left entry with its own short gloss.
[c1-l042] Iinxa. *Bom homem. & amigo de bẽ fazer.*
[c1-l042 note] Jinsha is a good, benevolent person. The mark after homem is a period in this impression; the abbreviation bẽ is retained.
[c1-l043] Iinxe. *Eſpaço em quanto hum viue, ou eſtà em*
[c1-l043 note] Jinse denotes the duration of a person’s life or stay in this world. The final verb’s apparent grave accent is retained as eſtà.
[c1-l044 >] *este mundo.*
[c1-l044 note] Este mundo completes the preceding lifespan definition. It is an indented Portuguese continuation, not another temporal expression.
[c1-l045] Iinxei. *Idem. ¶* Iinxei xichijǔ, corai
[c1-l045 note] Jinsei is equated with the preceding lifespan term. The example begins the saying that reaching seventy has been rare since old times.
[c1-l046 >] mare nari. *Chegar a vida do homem atè os*
[c1-l046 note] Mare nari completes the Japanese saying. The Portuguese translation begins “for human life to reach,” followed by the age below.
[c1-l047 >] *70. ainda antiguamente foi couſa rara.* S.
[c1-l047 note] The scan prints the numeral 70, not OCR vo. The translation says this was rare even in antiquity; the final S is upright.

[c2-l001] Iinxeqi. Fitono ato. *Piſadas, ou raſto de*

[c2-l005] Iinxin. Fitono cocoro. *Coração humano.*

[c2-l006] Iinxu. *Senhor, ou amo.*

[c2-l007] Iinyacu. *Mezinha pera os rins.*
[c2-l007 note] Jinyaku is a remedy for the kidneys. The initial I replaces OCR long s; Mezinha retains the historical medical vocabulary.
[c2-l008] Iinyei. Fitono cague. *Sombra de homem,*
[c2-l008 note] Jin’ei, hito no kage, is a person’s shadow or figure. The Portuguese extends the sense beyond a literal shadow below.
[c2-l009 >] *aparencia, ou vulto de homem que aparece*
[c2-l009 note] Adds the appearance or outline of a person who becomes visible, with the distance qualification on the next row.
[c2-l010 >] *de longe.*
[c2-l010 note] De longe means at a distance and completes the description of the human figure. It is indented body text.
[c2-l011] Iinyen yùbi. Fucacu touoxi caſuca. *Couſa*
[c2-l011 note] Jin’en yuubi is paraphrased as deep, remote and subtle. Retain the printed Fucacu spelling and grave over u rather than regularizing them.
[c2-l012 >] *alta, & profunda. Vt,* Iinyen yùbino
[c2-l012 note] The Portuguese gives lofty and profound; the Japanese example begins the same expression modifying kotowari below.
[c2-l013 >] cotouari. *Razão, ou ſentença profunda,*
[c2-l013 note] Kotowari completes the example of a profound reason or proposition. The paired Portuguese nouns explain the intended intellectual sense.
[c2-l014 >] *& copioſa.* S.
[c2-l014 note] Copioſa adds abundance or richness to the proposition’s depth. The final S is an upright written-register label.
[c2-l015] Iinzui. Camino mizzu. *Agoa do Cami. Vt,*
[c2-l015 note] Jinzui is kami no mizzu, water of the kami. Cami occurs within the Portuguese definition; the example follows below.
[c2-l016 >] Iinzuiuo nomu. *Beber agoa, ou vinho*
[c2-l016 note] Jinzui wo nomu means drinking this sacred liquid. Portuguese allows water or wine, with the ritual setting continued below.
[c2-l017 >] *diante do Cami como juramento, ou promeßa*
[c2-l017 note] The liquid is drunk before the Cami as an oath or promise; Cami is the Japanese divine being within the Portuguese explanation, not a new entry.
[c2-l018 >] *de guardar algũa couſa.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes the promise to observe or keep some undertaking. The tilde in algũa and the final period remain visible inside the crop.
[c2-l019] Iinzzǔ. *Hum dos ſeis dotes, que eſcreueo* Xa
[c2-l019 note] Jinzuu denotes a supernatural faculty, here one of six gifts described by Xaca. The Japanese name begins with upright Xa at the end of this physical line and continues below; no unprinted hyphen is added.
[c2-l020 >] *ca, como ſutileza. ¶ Itẽ, Sabeduria ma-*
[c2-l020 note] The opening ca completes Xaca from the preceding row. After the example of subtlety, the paragraph introduces a further sense, superhuman knowledge; the printed ma- divides mais across the next row.
[c2-l021 >] *is que humana com que hum ſabe as couſas diſ-*
[c2-l021 note] Continues the definition of knowledge exceeding ordinary human ability, allowing one to know distant things. Preserve both the initial is completing mais and the final diſ-.
[c2-l022 >] *tantes, & abſentes. ¶* Iinzzǔ, l, jinz-
[c2-l022 note] Completes distant and absent things, then begins the alternative expressions Iinzzǔ, l, jinzzuriqiuo vru. The lower-case j after the alternative marker is printed, and its word continues after jinz-.
[c2-l023 >] zuriqiuo vru. *Receber eſte dote.*
[c2-l023 note] Completes jinzzuriqiuo vru, acquiring the supernatural power; the Portuguese states receiving this gift. The zuriqiuo continuation belongs to the preceding example, not a separate headword.
[c2-l024] Iinzzuriqi. *Idem.*
[c2-l024 note] Iinzzuriqi is a separate headword for the same supernatural power. Idem explicitly refers back to the preceding entry and its acquisition formula.
[c2-l025] Iippô. Macotono nori. *Ley verdadeira.*
[c2-l025 note] Jippoo is explained by macotono nori, true law, matching Ley verdadeira. Both the headword and Japanese synonym are upright; the circumflex on ô is retained.
[c2-l026] Iippon. Fino moto. *Oriente. i, Iapão.*
[c2-l026 note] The first two upright letters read Ii, not long-s, giving Iippon. Fino moto, origin of the sun, is explained as the east and then Japan; the historical form is not modernized to Nippon.
[c2-l027] Iippu. Macotoya inaya. *Verdade, ou fal-*
[c2-l027 note] Iippu, truth or falsity, is unpacked by macoto ya ina ya, whether true or not. Restore the upright Ii initial from the scan and retain the terminal fal-.
[c2-l028 >] *ſidade. Vt,* Cotono jippuga xirenu. *A-*
[c2-l028 note] The example cotono jippuga xirenu says that the truth of a matter is not known. The Portuguese translation starts with A-, continued as inda below; the preceding ſidade completes falſidade.
[c2-l029 >] *inda ſe não ſabe de certo a verdade, ou mẽtira.*
[c2-l029 note] Completes Ainda and explains that the truth or lie is not yet known for certain. Keep the abbreviation mẽtira and the printed sequence de certo rather than modernizing orthography.
[c2-l030 >] *¶* Iippuuo qijte maitta. *Ouuindo a cer-*
[c2-l030 note] Iippuuo qijte maitta means coming after hearing the facts or certainty of the matter. The printed qijte has two dotted vowel letters; the Portuguese begins Ouuindo a cer-.
[c2-l031 >] *teza do que paßa vim.*
[c2-l031 note] Completes certeza do que paßa vim: the speaker came upon hearing what was actually happening. This is the translation of the preceding Japanese example, not a new entry.
[c2-l032] Iiqe. *Varela de Bonzos com ermidas, ou cellas*
[c2-l032 note] Iiqe, a Buddhist temple establishment, is described as a temple of Bonzos with hermitages or cells. Varela is part of the Portuguese gloss, and the description continues below.
[c2-l033 >] *ao redor dentro da meſma cerca.*
[c2-l033 note] The surrounding hermitages or cells lie inside the same enclosure. This completes the temple-establishment definition, with the final period and enclosing right rule visible.
[c2-l034] Iiqen. Mizzucara miru. *Ver por ſi. Vt,*
[c2-l034 note] Iiqen is firsthand seeing: mizzucara miru, seeing for oneself. The scan has upright Ii rather than the provisional ti; Mizzucara keeps its printed double z.
[c2-l035 >] Iiqen jitocu ſuru. *Ver, & entender por ſi.*
[c2-l035 note] The example joins firsthand seeing with personal understanding, matching Ver, & entender por ſi. Restore Ii at the start while preserving jitocu and the long ſ in ſuru.
[c2-l036 >] *¶* Iiqen gacumon. *O eſtudar hum por ſi*

[c2-l037 >] *lendo ſem mestre.*

[c2-l038] Iiqiô. Itçucuximi voxiye. *Doctrina de amor*
[c2-l038 note] Iiqiô is affectionate instruction, expressed by itçucuximi voxiye. The initial is upright Ii, not long-s; preserve the headword circumflex and the cedilla in the Japanese synonym.
[c2-l039 >] *como a do pay pera com o filho.*

[c2-l041 note] The opening mer completes Comer demaſiadamente, eating too much. The next paragraph introduces Iiqiuo tomuru, stopping someone’s eating or food supply.

[c2-l043 >] ru. *Idem.*
[c2-l043 note] The opening ru completes ſuru from the preceding line; Idem gives the same food-withholding meaning. This short physical continuation is fully included in its crop.
[c2-l044] Iiqidǒ. *Refeitorio.*
[c2-l044 note] Iiqidǒ is a dining hall or refectory. Restore the upright Ii initial and the caron on ǒ. The central Portuguese types are worn, especially the open-looking o, but Refeitorio remains the usable reading after enlargement.
[c2-l045] Iiqidome. *Vide,* Iiqi.
[c2-l045 note] Iiqidome is referred back to Iiqi, where withholding food has just been illustrated. Vide is italic apparatus, while the referenced Japanese headword Iiqi is upright, including its initial I.
[c2-l046] Iiqigo. *Meliùs,* Xocugo. Xocuno nochi. *De*
[c2-l046 note] Iiqigo means after eating; the dictionary prefers Xocugo and supplies xocuno nochi. The entry is flush left, not a continuation. Preserve the grave in Meliùs and the unhyphenated De at the right edge.
[c2-l047 >] *pois de comer.*
[c2-l047 note] The opening pois completes De pois de comer, the explanation of after eating. There is no lost further body row here: the separate Iiqi- below is the page catchword.
