[c1-l001] Iſſaiqiǒ. *Todos os liuros de Xaca.*
[c1-l001 note] Issaikyō denotes the complete collection of Buddhist scriptures, glossed as all the books of Shaka. The scan supplies the lost initial Iſſa and clearly reads liuros, not the garbled OCR iuaos; the final vowel has a caron.
[c1-l002] Iſſan. Fitotçuno ſacazzuqi. *Hũa vez de*
[c1-l002 note] Issan is one cup or serving of wine, explained by hitotsu no sakazuki. The Portuguese definition runs into vinho on the next physical row; the initial Japanese letters are present in the scan.
[c1-l003 >] *vinho.*
[c1-l003 note] Vinho completes the preceding definition of a single serving of wine. The short indented continuation is a separate physical row, not an independent entry.
[c1-l004] Iſſan. *Modo de parar o caualo deſpois de cor-*
[c1-l004 note] This separate issan entry names a way of bringing a running horse to a halt. The printed cor- begins correr across the line break and is retained.
[c1-l005 >] *rer inclinandoſe, & parando por tres vezes cõ*
[c1-l005 note] Rer completes correr; the gloss describes leaning and stopping in three successive movements. The visible word is inclinandoſe rather than OCR inchinandoſe, and cõ abbreviates the start of continuadas.
[c1-l006 >] *tinuadas. ¶* Iſſanuo noru. *Fazer parar*
[c1-l006 note] Tinuadas completes the preceding abbreviated continuadas. Issan wo noru illustrates the riding maneuver and introduces the translation continued below.
[c1-l007 >] *o caualo deſta maneira.*
[c1-l007 note] The translation ends with stopping the horse in that manner. The historical spelling and the provisional ß representation are retained rather than normalized.
[c1-l008] Iſſan bararito. *Todos totalmente os de algũa*
[c1-l008 note] Issan bararito refers collectively to everyone belonging to a temple community; the following row specifies the temple and its monks. The repeated Iſſan below and the surviving letters correct OCR iſian.
[c1-l009 >] *varella, ou Bonzos que eſtão naquelle circuito.*
[c1-l009 note] The community is specified as the temple or the monks within its precinct. Varella and Bonzos are the Portuguese explanatory vocabulary here, not additional Japanese headwords.
[c1-l010 >] *¶ Item, permet. Todos os de hũa caſa,*
[c1-l010 note] A figurative extension includes everyone in a house. The compact printed permet. is retained as the explanatory abbreviation rather than expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l011 >] *ou familia. Vt,* Iſſan bararito derareta.
[c1-l011 note] Ou familia completes the household extension. Issan bararito derareta illustrates everyone going out together, without anyone remaining.
[c1-l012 >] *Todos ſem ficar nem hũ ſairão, ou forão fora.*
[c1-l012 note] The Portuguese explicitly says that all went out, leaving not even one behind. Historical ſairão and forão are preserved rather than updated to modern past-tense spellings.
[c1-l013] Iſſanni. *Adu. A todo correr. ¶* Vma
[c1-l013 note] Issanni means at full speed. Adu. labels it an adverb, while Vma starts the horse example continued on the next row.
[c1-l014 >] uo iſſanni caquru. *Dar hũa carreira ao*
[c1-l014 note] Uma wo issanni kakuru illustrates giving a horse a full-speed run. The object particle uo belongs with Vma on the preceding line; the Portuguese also continues below.
[c1-l015 >] *caualo.*
[c1-l015 note] Caualo completes the translation of the full-speed riding example. This indented single-word row is physically present and retained separately.
[c1-l016] Iſſat. *Hũa carta.*
[c1-l016 note] Issat, a checked-coda form corresponding to issatsu, denotes one letter or written communication. The concise Portuguese gloss distinguishes it from the rice-measure homograph below.
[c1-l017] Iſſat. *Dous dedos de arroz, ou de outro legu-*
[c1-l017 note] This issat is a tiny quantity of rice or another pulse, defined by a two-finger pinch. The visible legu- continues into legume and is not an omitted Japanese word.
[c1-l018 >] *me tomando com dous dedos juntos. Vt,* I-
[c1-l018 note] Me completes legume; the quantity is taken between two fingers together. The example begins with a separately printed I- at the right edge, continuing as chigǒ.
[c1-l019 >] chigǒ iſſat. *Decima parte de hũa medida cõ*
[c1-l019 note] Ichigō issat combines a tenth of the larger measure with the small pinch just defined. The initial chigǒ joins the preceding I-, and cõ introduces the additional amount on the next row.
[c1-l020 >] *mais dous dedos de arroz, &c. ¶* Ichi-
[c1-l020 note] The Portuguese adds the two-finger quantity of rice. A second example begins with Ichi-, whose printed terminal hyphen is visible.
[c1-l021 >] gǒ iſſatmo ſanniôuo togueyo. *Dai con-*
[c1-l021 note] Ichigō issat mo sannyō wo togeyo calls for a complete account down to a very small quantity. The historical romanization ſanniôuo is retained; the Portuguese con- continues as conta.
[c1-l022 >] *ta atè da minima medida de arroz, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Ta completes conta: give an account even of the smallest measure of rice. The accent and spelling atè are retained from the scan rather than modernized.
[c1-l023] Iſſǒ. *Hũa noua. Vt,* Goiſſǒ machi mǒ-
[c1-l023 note] Issō here means a piece of news or a message. Goissō machi mōshi-soro is the courteous example begun here; both long-o marks in this row are carons.
[c1-l024 >] xiſoro. *Eſpero por hũa noua, ou auiſo de*
[c1-l024 note] Xiſoro completes the Japanese expression of waiting for news. The Portuguese likewise means awaiting a report or notice and continues with the addressee below.
[c1-l025 >] *voßa merce.*
[c1-l025 note] The courtesy formula vossa mercê completes the addressee of the preceding request for news. Its provisional ß transcription and unaccented merce are kept.
[c1-l026] Iſſǒ. Fitotçuno catachi. *Hũa figura.*
[c1-l026 note] A distinct issō entry means one form or figure, explained by hitotsu no katachi. The mark above the headword o points downward as a caron, so the draft circumflex is corrected without merging this entry with its neighbors.
[c1-l027] Iſsǒ. *Adu. Totalmẽte. ¶* Iſsǒni cataz
[c1-l027 note] Issō is now the adverb entirely or altogether. The mixed long and short s forms are preserved, and the example has a visible caron in Iſsǒni; cataz continues into zuquru without a supplied hyphen.
[c1-l028 >] zuquru. *Dar algũa couſa por ocaſião de ou-*
[c1-l028 note] Zuquru completes katazukuru. The Portuguese describes giving something along with a related thing; enlargement supports Dar and the contiguous ocaſião, correcting OCR Der and the spurious break in o caſão.
[c1-l029 >] *tra q̃ lhe reſponde, como quando hum tomou hũa*
[c1-l029 note] Tra completes outra: the other item corresponds to it. The example begins with someone taking one glove, and the abbreviation q̃ remains unexpanded.
[c1-l030 >] *luua dar lhe a outra, tomou a imagem das cõ-*
[c1-l030 note] The first illustration gives the other glove; the second concerns taking the image attached to prayer beads. The physical cõ- is retained as the beginning of contas.
[c1-l031 >] *tas, darlhe tambem as contas, &c.*
[c1-l031 note] Tas completes contas, and the gloss then says to give the beads as well. This closes the explanation of handing over the corresponding whole rather than only one part.
[c1-l032] Iſſocu. Fitoiqi. *Hum folego. ¶* Iſſocu
[c1-l032 note] Issoku means one breath, explicitly equated with hitoiki. A further expression begins with the repeated Iſſocu and continues below.
[c1-l033 >] xetdan. Fitotçu iqi qiritatçu. *Morrer,*
[c1-l033 note] Issoku setsudan is explained by hitotsu iki kiritatsu, the cutting off of the breath. The faint vowel after F is read as i, not OCR a; the Portuguese Morrer ends with a comma and continues below.
[c1-l034 >] *ou acabarſe o folego. ¶* Iſſocu xetdanno
[c1-l034 note] The first expression means dying or the breath coming to an end. The next example repeats issoku setsudan with genitive no; acabarſe corrects the OCR acayarſe against the surviving b.
[c1-l035 >] miguirini. *No ponto em que hum a caba,*
[c1-l035 note] Migiri ni completes the expression at the time of death. The Portuguese says at the point when someone comes to an end; the conspicuous printed separation a caba is retained instead of silently joining acaba.
[c1-l036 >] *ou morre.*
[c1-l036 note] Ou morre finishes the explanation of the moment of death. The brief continuation belongs to the preceding example, not to the next isson entry.
[c1-l037] Iſſon. *Hum Fotoque.*
[c1-l037 note] Isson denotes one Buddha or Buddhist sacred figure, glossed with the Japanese word Fotoque embedded in Portuguese. The embedded term is printed in the same italic explanation here and is flagged for optional whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l038] Iſſon. *Hum lugar, ou pouoação.*
[c1-l038 note] This homograph is one place or settlement, distinguishing the village sense from the Buddhist figure on the preceding row. Pouoação preserves the source spelling.
[c1-l039] Iſsǒni. *Adu. Vt,* Iſsǒni catazzuquru.
[c1-l039 note] Issōni is the adverb used in the same katazukuru example as above. The scan includes the omitted italic Adu. and supports Iſsǒni with mixed s forms and a caron in both occurrences, not OCR Iisǒni.
[c1-l040 >] *Vide,* Iſsǒ.
[c1-l040 note] Vide directs the reader to the preceding issō entry and its fuller explanation. The Japanese cross-reference is roman after the italic Latin instruction.
[c1-l041] Iſſot. Fitotçu fiqiiru. *Hũa companhia de*
[c1-l041 note] Issot is a company of soldiers, explained with the Japanese phrase hitotsu hikiiru. The checked final t is preserved rather than expanded to modern sotsu.
[c1-l042 >] *ſoldados.*
[c1-l042 note] Soldados completes the military-company gloss. The indented one-word row is retained in its own crop and line record.
[c1-l043] Iſſui. *Eſpaço em quanto ſe faz hum comer de*
[c1-l043 note] Issui is glossed as the interval needed to prepare a rice meal. The image clearly has comer rather than OCR eomer; the rice-meal comparison is developed in the following example.
[c1-l044 >] *arroz. ¶* Banji iſſuino yume nari.
[c1-l044 note] Arroz completes the meal definition. Banji issui no yume nari describes all things as a brief dream, whose duration the Portuguese then compares with preparing rice.
[c1-l045 >] *Todas as couſas ſão como hum breue ſonho, q̃*
[c1-l045 note] The translation says that all things are like a brief dream. The final q̃ begins the relative clause on the next row and remains an unexpanded abbreviation.
[c1-l046 >] *dura em quanto ſe faz, ou concerta hum pou-*
[c1-l046 note] The dream lasts while a little rice is made or prepared. Dura, quanto, ou, and concerta are supported by the surviving strokes, correcting several word-changing OCR errors; pou- is visibly hyphenated.
[c1-l047 >] *co de arroz.*
[c1-l047 note] Co completes pouco and closes both the rice-meal comparison and the left column. This is body text, not a catchword.

[c2-l002 initial=2] ITa. *Taboa.*
[c2-l002 note] Ita means a board. The large initial I extends alongside the following row and belongs here, yielding ITa rather than OCR Ta; its crop is enlarged vertically to contain the entire initial. Taboa, not Tahoa, is visible in the gloss.
[c2-l003 >] Itabiſaxi. *Acrecentamento feito de taboas*
[c2-l003 note] Itabisashi is a boarded addition resembling a veranda. The upright stroke at the left is the preceding entry’s enlarged I, not a paragraph mark; the actual headword begins farther right, beside that initial.
[c2-l004 >] *como varanda em caſa de palha, &c.*
[c2-l004 note] The addition is compared with a veranda on a thatched house. This indented continuation completes the itabisashi definition.
[c2-l005] Itabu. *Certa fruita ſilueſtre de era como figos*
[c2-l005 note] Itabu is described as a wild fruit associated with era and compared with figs. The scan reads ſilueſtre, correcting the missing l in OCR; era is retained literally rather than replaced by a modern botanical identification or spelling.
[c2-l006 >] *de Portugal.*
[c2-l006 note] De Portugal completes the comparison with Portuguese figs. The definition does not itself establish an exact modern species, so none is inserted into the text.
[c2-l007] Itaca. *Soberbo.*
[c2-l007 note] Itaka is glossed as proud or arrogant. This short entry is distinct from the adjectival form itakana that follows.
[c2-l008] Itacana. *Couſa ſoberba. ¶* Itacana mono,
[c2-l008 note] Itakana qualifies something as arrogant. The example begins itakana mono and continues with an alternative wording on the next row.
[c2-l009 >] l, itaca mono. *Homem ſoberbo, & a-*
[c2-l009 note] Latin l introduces the alternative itaka mono. The Portuguese calls the person proud and presumptuous, with a- continuing into atrevido.
[c2-l010 >] *treuido.*
[c2-l010 note] Treuido completes atrevido from the preceding row. The historical u spelling and the separate physical line are preserved.
[c2-l011] Itachi. *Dòninha.*
[c2-l011 note] Itachi is a weasel, matching Portuguese doninha. The first vowel is o, not OCR i, and a small grave-shaped mark is visible above it; Dòninha preserves that local mark. The worn accent is less secure than the letters themselves.
[c2-l012] Itadaqi. *Cucuruta da cabeça, &c. ¶* Ya-
[c2-l012 note] Itadaki is the crown or top of the head. The worn Portuguese word is read as Cucuruta, not OCR Cucueuta; its medial r is a qualified reading. Cabeça has a visible cedilla, and Ya- begins the mountain example.
[c2-l013 >] mano itadaqi. *Cume da ſerra, ou mõte.*
[c2-l013 note] Yama no itadaki means the summit of a mountain or range. Mano joins the preceding Ya-, while the Portuguese gives the corresponding summit sense and retains abbreviated mõte.
[c2-l014] Itadaqi, u, aita. *Por algũa cousa ſobre a ca*
[c2-l014 note] Itadaki supplies the verb’s principal forms and is glossed as putting something on the head. The final ca continues into cabeça without a visible hyphen; the short s in cousa is retained.
[c2-l015 >] *beça. ¶* Sacazzuqiuo itadaqu. *Aleuã*
[c2-l015 note] Beça completes cabeça. Sakazuki wo itadaku illustrates raising a drinking cup; Aleuã begins the Portuguese infinitive across the line break without an added hyphen.
[c2-l016 >] *tar o Sacazzuqui. ¶* Xiraga, yuqi, l.
[c2-l016 note] Tar completes levantar and the Portuguese embeds Sacazzuqui as the object being raised. The word is contiguous in the scan, not Sacazzu qui; the next Japanese example begins with white hair, snow, or frost.
[c2-l017 >] ximouo itadaqu. *Ter muitas cãs na-*
[c2-l017 note] Shimo wo itadaku completes the snow-and-frost imagery for having gray hair. The Portuguese gloss specifies many gray hairs on the head; the visible mark after na is retained even though na itself is not a divided word.
[c2-l018 >] *cabeça.*
[c2-l018 note] Cabeça completes the gray-hair expression. Its cedilla and separate indented row are visible.
[c2-l019] Itade. *Ferida mortal. Vt,* Itadeuo vô.
[c2-l019 note] Itade is a mortal or grave wound; itade wo ou means suffering such a wound. Both occurrences of the Japanese word are entirely roman, so the draft’s isolated italic d spans are removed.
[c2-l020 >] *Receber ferida mortal.*
[c2-l020 note] The Portuguese translation means receiving a mortal wound and completes the preceding Japanese example. It is not an additional headword.
[c2-l021] Itado. *Porta de taboas.*
[c2-l021 note] Itado is a door made of boards. The brief gloss directly matches the board-door compound.
[c2-l022] Itadori. *Erua aßi chamada.*
[c2-l022 note] Itadori is identified simply as an herb of that name. The source does not supply further botanical detail, and the historical spelling and provisional ß form are retained.
[c2-l023] Itai. *Couſa que doe, ou ter dor.*
[c2-l023 note] Itai describes something painful or being in pain. The two short forms below belong to this adjective rather than to the following well entry.
[c2-l024 >] Itaſa.
[c2-l024 note] Itasa is the corresponding noun for pain or painfulness. It occupies its own indented physical line and has no separate Portuguese gloss here.
[c2-l025 >] Itô.
[c2-l025 note] Itō is the adjective’s contracted adverbial form. The Japanese form and its terminal period are roman; the compact, ink-filled vowel mark is retained as the draft circumflex.
[c2-l026] Itai. *Poço feito, & fortificado à roda de ta-*
[c2-l026 note] This homograph means a well constructed and reinforced around its sides with boards. It is unrelated to the preceding adjective for pain; ta- begins taboas across the line break.
[c2-l027 >] *boas. ¶* Itaino mizzuuo cumu. *Tirar*
[c2-l027 note] Boas completes taboas. Itai no mizu wo kumu illustrates drawing water from such a well, with the Portuguese translation beginning Tirar.
[c2-l028 >] *agoa deſte poço, ou fonte que tem à roda estas*

[c2-l030] Itagami. *Certo papel com que o trinchante a-*

[c2-l031 >] *limpa as facas.*
[c2-l031 note] Limpa completes alimpa from the preceding row, meaning cleans the knives. The line division is preserved rather than rewriting this as the modern standalone limpa.
[c2-l032] Itajiqi. *Solhado de taboas. Vt,* Itajiqiuo

[c2-l034] Itaiqena. *Couſa bella, airoſa, gracioſa, &c.*
[c2-l034 note] Itaikena is glossed as beautiful, graceful, or charming. The scan has an uppercase initial I, correcting the OCR lowercase without changing the historical word.

[c2-l037] Itaitaxij. *Couſa que magoa, ou faz compaixão.*

[c2-l038 >] Itaitaxiſa.

[c2-l039 >] Itaitaxǔ. *Adu. ¶* Itaitaxǔ xeccã

[c2-l040 >] ſuru. *Caſtigar magoando muito, ou cauſan-*

[c2-l041 >] *do dor.*
[c2-l041 note] Do completes causando and ends the painful-punishment example with causing pain. The brief indented continuation remains a separate line.
[c2-l042] Itama. *Gretas, ou aberturas das taboas.*

[c2-l043] Itamaxime, uru, eta. *Tomar pena, ou dor.*
[c2-l043 note] Itamashime supplies the verb’s principal forms and is explained as taking grief or pain. The example below specifies affliction of the heart; the historical principal-form notation is left intact.
[c2-l044 >] *Vt,* Cocorouo itamaximuru. *Afligirſe,*
[c2-l044 note] Kokoro wo itamashimuru illustrates afflicting one’s heart or grieving. The introductory Vt and Portuguese Afligirſe are italic, while the Japanese example is roman.
[c2-l045 >] *& tomar pena, & dor no coração. S.*
[c2-l045 note] The Portuguese completes the explanation as taking grief and pain in the heart. The terminal S. is an upright written-language label, not part of the italic gloss or the catchword below.
