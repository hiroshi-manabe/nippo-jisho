[c1-l001] Fiban. Banni arazu. *Não vigiar, ou não lhe*
[c1-l001 note] Hiban, ban ni arazu, means being off watch or not having one's turn; the negative Japanese matches não vigiar.
[c1-l002 >] *caber ſua vez de vigiar. ¶ Item, O vigiar*
[c1-l002 note] The gloss first says the watch is not one's turn, then introduces voluntarily watching nevertheless; this entire row is Portuguese.
[c1-l003 >] *ſem lhe caber ſua vez. ¶* Tǒbã. *He vigia q̃*
[c1-l003 note] Tōban contrasts the assigned watch with hiban; the rightmost abbreviated que continues the Portuguese definition below.
[c1-l004 >] *cabe, ou pertence a alguem. Vt,* Fiban
[c1-l004 note] The watch belongs to someone by turn; Fiban starts an example combining both statuses.
[c1-l005 >] tǒban nacu, tçumete iru. *Eſtar sẽpre vi*
[c1-l005 note] Hiban tōban naku tsumete iru means remaining on duty regardless of turn; the Portuguese always-watching explanation agrees.
[c1-l006 >] *giando ſem ter deuer com lhe caber, ou não*
[c1-l006 note] The watch continues whether or not the turn belongs to the watcher; retain deuer as the printed wording.
[c1-l007 >] *caber ſua vez.*
[c1-l007 note] Caber sua vez closes the turn-taking condition; it is not a new capitalized Laber word.
[c1-l008] Fibana. *Roſa de fogo, ou foguete.* Fi
[c1-l008 note] Hibana is a fire-flower or spark/firework; Fi at the end begins the example continued below.
[c1-l009 >] banauo chiraite tatacǒ. *Pellejar rija mente*
[c1-l009 note] Hibana wo chiraite tatakō describes fierce fighting; chiraite is retained as printed rather than modernized to chirashite.
[c1-l010] F bara. *Lugar de certas aruores como aci*
[c1-l010 note] Hibara is a place of certain cypress-like trees; its initial i is faint but the headword is visible.
[c1-l011 >] *preſtes.*
[c1-l011 note] Prestes completes aciprestes across the physical break, finishing the tree comparison.
[c1-l012] Fibari. *Calhandra.*
[c1-l012 note] Hibari is the lark, glossed Calhandra; the headword's initial F was lost by OCR.
[c1-l013] Fibarigue. *Caualo de cor de calhandra.*
[c1-l013 note] Hibarige is a horse coat resembling a lark's color; cavalo and cor clarify the two damaged Portuguese words.
[c1-l014] Fibaſami. *Serpe da eſpingarda.*
[c1-l014 note] Hibasami is the serpentine or match-holding part of a firearm, not an animal despite Serpe.
[c1-l015] Fibaxi. *Dous ferros compridos como eſpetinhos*
[c1-l015 note] Hibashi are two long iron rods like small skewers; the pair is used as fire tongs.
[c1-l016 >] *com que tomaõ o fogo, ou o atiçaõ.*
[c1-l016 note] The rods take up or stir fire; preserve the printed conjunction and independent tilde positions rather than normalizing them.
[c1-l017] Fibaxita. Ichinichi taranu. *Dia imperfeito,*
[c1-l017 note] Hibashita is an incomplete day or the portion lacking to make a whole day, as the Portuguese below states. Ichinichi is clear; the worn tararu is retained provisionally, although a negative tarazu would fit the gloss better. The surviving ink does not securely establish the missing internal letters.
[c1-l018 >] *ou que lhe falta pera ſer inteiro.*
[c1-l018 note] The day lacks something of being whole, supporting the incomplete-day interpretation of hibashita.
[c1-l019] Fibi. i, *Gretas de frio. Vt* Fibiga qiruru.
[c1-l019 note] Hibi are cold-induced cracks; hibi ga kiruru is the example, and frio restores the final vowel of the gloss.
[c1-l020 >] *Grearem as maõs, pès, &c. com frio.*
[c1-l020 note] Hands and feet crack from cold; Gretarem is the relevant Portuguese verb, not OCR Orearem.
[c1-l021] Fibicaxe, ſuru, xeta. *Fazer retumbar, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Hibikase is causative resonance, 'make resound'; its inflection and the thunder example guide the damaged vowel reading.
[c1-l022 >] *ſoar. Vt,* Raidenga cumouo fibicaſu.
[c1-l022 note] Raiden ga kumo wo hibikasu means thunder makes clouds resound; the Japanese syntax and next gloss support the damaged n and a.
[c1-l023 >] *O trouaõ faz retumbar as nuuens.*
[c1-l023 note] The Portuguese repeats thunder making clouds reverberate, confirming the causative interpretation rather than an unrelated action.
[c1-l024] Fibini. *Cada dia.*
[c1-l024 note] Hibi ni means every day; it is distinct from the neighboring hibi cracks and hibiki resonance.
[c1-l025] Fibiqi. *Soido. ¶ Item, Fenda, ou que*
[c1-l025 note] Hibiki is sound, but the entry also introduces a crack or break; both senses are kept rather than flattened into resonance alone.
[c1-l026 >] *bradura de batega, taboa. &c. ¶* Fibiqi-
[c1-l026 note] Quebradura of a vessel or plank completes the second meaning; Fibiqi- begins its example below.
[c1-l027 >] ga iru, iqu, l, ita. *Eſtar feita, ou auer*
[c1-l027 note] Hibiki ga iru, iku, ita gives the Japanese forms for a crack occurring; these forms were wrongly italicized as Portuguese in the draft.
[c1-l028 >] *esta fenda, ou quebradura.*
[c1-l028 note] The gloss says the crack or break exists or has formed, closing the example.
[c1-l029] Fibiqi, u. ijta. *Retumbar, ou ſoar muito a*
[c1-l029 note] Hibiki, -u, iita is resounding; the printed ijta is retained rather than silently replacing its historical i/j appearance.
[c1-l030 >] *voz. Vt,* Toqiuo dotto aguru coye, ma-
[c1-l030 note] Toki wo dotto aguru koe is the raised battle cry; ma- starts makoto ni in the quotation.
[c1-l031 >] cotoni tenni fibiqi, ricugi xindô xite vo-
[c1-l031 note] Makoto ni ten ni hibiki, rikugi shindō shite describes sound echoing in heaven and shaking the earth; hibiki resolves OCR habiki.
[c1-l032 >] bitataxi. Tacax. *A voz da grita, &*
[c1-l032 note] Obitataxi completes the description of overwhelming sound; Tacax is the printed citation label, not automatically harmonized to another title.
[c1-l033 >] *alarido que ſe aleuantou, verdadeiramẽte que*
[c1-l033 note] The Portuguese identifies the raised shouting and clamour and introduces its truly thunderous effect.
[c1-l034 >] *retumbando no ceo, & abalandoſe a terra era*
[c1-l034 note] Echoing in heaven and shaking earth matches the quotation's ten and rikugi, strengthening the bilingual alignment.
[c1-l035 >] *couſa eſpantoſa.*
[c1-l035 note] Espantosa closes the description of an awe-inspiring or frightening event, not a separate headword.
[c1-l036] Fibiqiuatari, u, atta. *Retun bar, ou ſoar*
[c1-l036 note] Hibikiwatari means resound far and wide; retain the printed spacing in Retun bar pending finer typography review.
[c1-l037 >] *muito longe. Vt,* Micatato teqino coye
[c1-l037 note] Mikata to teki no koe contrasts our side's and the enemy's voices in the long quotation.
[c1-l038 >] xifǒ ſanbiacu yorini fibiqi vatatte, tẽmo
[c1-l038 note] Shiho (four directions) and sanbyaku yori (more than three hundred ri) describe the battle cries carrying widely. The scan supports xifǒ, not xitǒ: f represents the historical h in 四方. Tẽmo begins the image of heaven falling, continued below; the exact abbreviated mark is retained.
[c1-l039 >] tachimachi vochi, cogicumo vorete cata-
[c1-l039 note] Tachimachi vochi says “suddenly falling”; the following cogicu mo vorete describes the heavenly axes breaking, as the Portuguese exos do ceo confirms. The compound is worn, so cogicu remains provisional rather than being modernized or supplied with an unattested accent.
[c1-l040 >] muqucato ayaxiqi fodonizo qicoyetari.
[c1-l040 note] Katamuku ka to ayashiki hodo ni zo kikoyetari conveys seeming to hear the world tilt; the damaged muqucato is read as that sequence, with the printed q retained.
[c1-l041 >] Taif*. Lib. 16. A grita dos noſſos, &*
[c1-l041 note] Taif is the source label; the scan supports book 15, not OCR 36. The translation begins the voices of both sides.
[c1-l042 >] *dos inimigos ſoando por todas as partes, mais*
[c1-l042 note] The enemies' voices sound on all sides, matching the quotation's directional language.
[c1-l043 >] *de 300. legoas, parecia duuidarmos ſe as nu-*
[c1-l043 note] The gloss gives more than 300 leagues and uncertainty whether the clouds were falling; this is literary scale, not a measured distance claim.
[c1-l044 >] *uẽs cahião, & os exos do ceo quebrandoſe ſe*
[c1-l044 note] The heavenly axes break and incline in the translation; retain the printed cabião rather than modernizing the falling-cloud wording.
[c1-l045 >] *inclinauão com o grande eſtrondo.*
[c1-l045 note] The great estrondo explains the imagined collapse; the surviving strokes support inclinauão and estrondo over OCR garbling.
[c1-l046] Fibiraqi, u. l, fibiraqi itami, u. *Arder, ou*
[c1-l046 note] Hibiraki or hibiraki itami concerns stinging pain; the second form combines the same stem with itami rather than the OCR-separated fragments.
[c1-l047 >] *eſcozer algũa ferida com dor.*
[c1-l047 note] A wound burns or stings with pain; ferida and dor define the physical sensation.

[c2-l001] Fibiǔ. *Vt*, Cotobano fibiǔ. *Falta, ou er-*
[c2-l001 note] Hibiǔ in kotoba no hibiǔ concerns a flaw in speech; the final marked u is faint, so this remains a useful human-review point rather than an assured lexical restoration.
[c2-l002 >] *ro no falar. ¶ Item, Modo, ou geito do*
[c2-l002 note] A second sense is manner or turn of speaking, extending beyond an outright error.
[c2-l003 >] *falar, pollo qual ſe entende algũa couſa de que*
[c2-l003 note] The manner of speaking allows an uncertain matter to be understood; falar and entende restore the coherent Portuguese clause.
[c2-l004 >] *ſe duuida.*
[c2-l004 note] Se duvida closes the doubtful matter inferred from speech; it is not a separate uncertainty annotation.
[c2-l005] Fibiuare, uru, eta. *Gretar muito, ou abrir*
[c2-l005 note] Hibiware, -uru, -eta is cracking open; the visible final r of abrir belongs to this row.
[c2-l006 >] *como taboas ao ſol, &c.*
[c2-l006 note] Boards exposed to sun are the example of cracking, giving physical context to hibiware.
[c2-l007] Fibo. *Cordoẽs, ou correas pera atar.*
[c2-l007 note] Hibo means cords or straps for tying; the b is visible and matches the following compound.
[c2-l008] Fibo. Canaximu faua. *Lastimoſa mãy.*
[c2-l008 note] A different hibo is a sorrowing mother, kanashimu hawa; the Portuguese mãy separates it from the cord sense.
[c2-l009] Fibocaxi, ſu, aita. *Secar, ou enxugar algũa*
[c2-l009 note] Hibokashi is drying something, with algũa modifying the following cousa; the last a is present.
[c2-l010 >] *couſa ao fogo.*
[c2-l010 note] Ao fogo specifies drying by fire, completing the verb's definition.
[c2-l011] Fibo votoxi, l, vobinauoxi. *O deixar o mo*
[c2-l011 note] Hibo otoshi or obi naoshi concerns a change in fastening dress; vobi, not ubi, is supported by the belt context.
[c2-l012 >] *do de atar o cingidouro de tras como trazem*
[c2-l012 note] The old mode ties the girdle behind, and the next row identifies the children using it.
[c2-l013 >] *os mininos atè os noue annos, & cingirſe por*
[c2-l013 note] The text specifies children up to nine years and a change to fastening in front; preserve that historical age statement.
[c2-l014 >] *diante.*
[c2-l014 note] Diante completes por diante, the front-fastening alternative.
[c2-l015] Fibucure, uru. eta. *Empolar mão, ou outra*
[c2-l015 note] Hibukure, -uru, -eta is blistering of the hand or another body part; uru restores the infinitive ending.
[c2-l016 >] *parte do corpo queimada, ou eſcaldada.*
[c2-l016 note] The blistered part was burned or scalded, explaining the fire-related compound.
[c2-l017 >] *¶* Fibucurega xita. *Fazerſe algũa empo-*
[c2-l017 note] Hibukure ga shita says a blister formed; the visible t corrects the OCR gap in xi.a.
[c2-l018 >] *la com fogo.*
[c2-l018 note] Empo-la completes the blister caused by fire, not a separate noun entry.
[c2-l019] Fibucuro. *Cobertura de papel pera as lanter-*
[c2-l019 note] Hibukuro is a paper covering for lanterns; papel pera restores the damaged material-and-purpose phrase.
[c2-l020 >] *nas, ou que poem ſobre os candieiros.*
[c2-l020 note] The covering is also put over lamps; poem is the printed verb, not porm.
[c2-l021] Fibun. *Sem razão, ou injustiça.*
[c2-l021 note] Hibun is unreason or injustice; the Portuguese noun identifies the damaged letters without imposing a new sense.
[c2-l022] Ficague. *Reſplandor, ou rayo do ſol. Vt,*
[c2-l022 note] Hikage here is sunlight or a ray, not shade yet; the Portuguese definition establishes the first sense.
[c2-l023 >] F cague ſaſu. *Entrar, ou dar o rayo do ſol*
[c2-l023 note] Hikage sasu means sunlight falls or enters; the damaged letters are supported by both the headword and rayo do sol.
[c2-l024 >] *em algũa parte. ¶* Ficague nodocani na-
[c2-l024 note] Hikage nodoka ni na- begins the sun becoming calm and clear; nodocani supplies the adverbial ni rather than OCR vi.
[c2-l025 >] riyuqu Feiq*. Lib. 4. Irſe fazendo*
[c2-l025 note] Nariyuku completes the gradual change; Feiq is a citation label preceding Lib. 4 and the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l026 >] *o dia claro, & ſereno como no tempo da pri-*
[c2-l026 note] The day becomes clear and serene like spring, translating nodoka ni nariyuku.
[c2-l027 >] *ma vera. ¶ Item, Sombra contra os rayos*
[c2-l027 note] Primavera completes before the second hikage sense, shade against sunlight; both meanings are explicitly present.
[c2-l028 >] *do ſol. Vt.* Ficagueuo ſuru, l, coxirayu-
[c2-l028 note] Hikage wo suru or koshirayuru means make shade; coxirayu- is the visible continuation stem, not cexirayu.
[c2-l029 >] ru. *Fazer ſombra, ou toldo pera ſe defender*
[c2-l029 note] Ru completes koshirayuru; a canopy is made to protect oneself, with the printed Portuguese spacing retained.
[c2-l030 >] *do ſol.*
[c2-l030 note] Do sol finishes the protection from sun, completing the shade sense.
[c2-l031] Ficai. i, Tocoro. *Hũas raizes amargoſas*
[c2-l031 note] Hikai, equated with tokoro, names bitter roots; Ficai is supported by the visible c and the botanical synonym.
[c2-l032 >] *como cinouraſinhas amarelas que ſeruem de*
[c2-l032 note] The roots resemble small yellow carrots and are medicinal; the comparison need not imply botanical identity.
[c2-l033 >] *mezinha.*
[c2-l033 note] Mezinha closes servem de mezinha, medicinal use of the roots.
[c2-l034] Ficare, uru, eta. *Ser leuado, ou guiado.*
[c2-l034 note] Hikare, -uru, -eta is the passive of drawing or leading, as the Portuguese ser levado indicates.
[c2-l035 >] *Vt,* Teuo ficaruru. *Ser leuado polla mão*
[c2-l035 note] Te wo hikaruru means being led by the hand; the object construction is preserved as printed.
[c2-l036 >] *¶* Teqini ficaruru. *Ser tomado, ou leua-*
[c2-l036 note] Teki ni hikaruru means being taken by enemies, with ni marking the agent.
[c2-l037 >] *do dos inimigos. ¶* Vmani ficaruru. *Ser*
[c2-l037 note] The enemy gloss ends before uma ni hikaruru, being carried or dragged by a horse.
[c2-l038 >] *leuado do caualo deſenfreado, ou q̃ não obedece*
[c2-l038 note] The horse is unbridled or disobedient; desenfreado is the adjective, not the OCR final e form.
[c2-l039 >] *ao freo. ¶* Yocuni ficaruru. *Ser leua-*
[c2-l039 note] Yoku ni hikaruru shifts to being led by desire, after the horse's bridle explanation.
[c2-l040 >] *do da cubiça, &c.*
[c2-l040 note] Cubiҫa completes the figurative pull of greed or desire.
[c2-l041] Ficari. *Claridade. ¶* Ficariuo fanatçu.
[c2-l041 note] Hikari is light; hikari wo hanatsu is emitting light, with the complete hanatsu word visible.
[c2-l042 >] *Dar, ou deitar de ſi claridade. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l042 note] Giving off light completes the literal example; Item introduces a metaphorical extension.
[c2-l043 >] *Per met. Reſplandecer, ou dar bom exemplo*
[c2-l043 note] Per met. explains shining as giving a good example; exemplo is the relevant Portuguese noun.
[c2-l044 >] *em algũa obra boa. ¶* Ficariuo tçutçu-
[c2-l044 note] A good deed completes the moral example; hikari wo tsutsu- begins concealing one's light.
[c2-l045 >] mu. *Encubrir a claridade, & as vezes*
[c2-l045 note] Mu completes tsutsumu; concealing brightness can carry a figurative sense as explained below.
[c2-l046 >] *ſe toma por encubrir as virtudes, nome,*
[c2-l046 note] Concealing virtues or one's name explicates modest concealment, not loss of physical light alone.
[c2-l047 >] *&c.*
[c2-l047 note] The final &c. belongs to that explanation; the separate Fica- is already correctly recorded as catchword.
