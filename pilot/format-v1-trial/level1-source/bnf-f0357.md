[c1-l004 note] Das mãos completes entre as palmas: the cord is twisted between the palms of the hands. This short continuation is a complete physical row.
[c1-l005] Naua ami. *Rede feita de cordas.*
[c1-l005 note] Naua ami is a net made from ropes, as the Portuguese directly states. The visible separation between Naua and ami is retained.
[c1-l006] Nauadori. *O que tẽ a corda na mão do que eſ-*
[c1-l006 note] Nauadori denotes the person holding the rope attached to someone who is bound. The definition continues after the divided eſ-; tẽ retains its abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l007 >] *tà amarrado.*
[c1-l007 note] Tà amarrado completes eſtà amarrado, identifying the bound person whose rope the nauadori holds. The grave accent belongs to the printed continuation.
[c1-l008] Nauaguire. *Hum pedaço de corda.*
[c1-l008 note] Nauaguire is a piece or length of rope. The Japanese compound and the Portuguese pedaço de corda support the reading without supplying a more specific length.
[c1-l009] Nauaſaqi. *Ponta da corda.*
[c1-l009 note] Nauaſaqi is the end of a rope; the tall medial letter is long ſ, not f. Ponta da corda supplies the direct gloss.
[c1-l010] Nauate. *Caminho dantre as varzeas.*
[c1-l010 note] Nauate is a path between the cultivated low-lying fields. Dantre and varzeas are retained as printed rather than modernized.
[c1-l011] Nauatemichi. *Idem.*
[c1-l011 note] Nauatemichi repeats the preceding path-between-fields meaning through Idem. The extra michi element does not require an additional Portuguese definition.
[c1-l012] Nauaxiro. *Varzea, ou lugar onde ſe ſemea a*
[c1-l012 note] Nauaxiro names a rice nursery or seedbed. The definition starts with a plot where rice is first sown and continues with the later transplanting operation.
[c1-l013 >] *primeira vez o arroz pera depois em erua ſe*
[c1-l013 note] Rice is first sown here so that it can subsequently be transplanted while still green. The dotted i and the surrounding letters support depois, correcting OCR depots.
[c1-l014 >] *deſpor.*
[c1-l014 note] Deſpor completes the nursery-bed definition: setting out or transplanting the young rice. It is not an omitted new entry despite occupying a short row.
[c1-l015] Nauo. *Adu. Mais.*
[c1-l015 note] Nauo is the adverb meaning more or further. Adu. belongs to the italic grammatical apparatus, not the Japanese headword.
[c1-l016] Nauomotte. *Adu. Idem.*
[c1-l016 note] Nauomotte is another adverb with the same meaning as Nauo. Idem explicitly refers back to the preceding entry.
[c1-l017] Nauonauo. *Adu. Mais, & mais.*
[c1-l017 note] Nauonauo intensifies the preceding adverb: more and more. The repeated Japanese element corresponds to the repeated Portuguese mais.
[c1-l018] Nauori, u, otta. *Sàrar. Vt,* Yamaiga
[c1-l018 note] Nauori here means recovering from illness, illustrated by Yamaiga nauotta on the following row. Reinspection against the nearby nauotta and otta types shows a badly abraded first t between o and the clear second t; otta is adopted instead of the OCR ota. This restoration remains a damaged-glyph caveat, not an inferred modernization. The printed grave in Sàrar is preserved.
[c1-l019 >] nauotta. *Eſtà ja bẽ, ou são da infirmidade.*
[c1-l019 note] Yamaiga nauotta states that the illness has healed, rendered as being well or free from the illness. The example does print doubled tt, in contrast to the preceding abbreviated ota.
[c1-l020] Nauori, u, otta. *Emendarſe. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l020 note] The second Nauori entry concerns correction or restoration rather than healing. It prints otta and introduces additional applications after Item.
[c1-l021 >] *Endereitarſe como caſa inclinada, &c.*
[c1-l021 note] The correction can be physical: straightening a leaning house. The scan reads inclinada, restoring the l omitted in the OCR's incinada.
[c1-l022 >] *¶* Cajega nauoru. *Mudarſe o vento.*
[c1-l022 note] Cajega nauoru describes the wind changing. The printed j is retained; the Portuguese vento establishes the referent without normalizing the Japanese spelling.
[c1-l023 >] *¶* Zani nauoru. *Porſe, ou paſſarſe a ſeu*
[c1-l023 note] Zani nauoru means moving into one's proper place or seat. Porſe is printed as one word, and the Portuguese phrase continues with the place in the room below.
[c1-l024 >] *lugar no Zaxiqui. ¶* Fonreǒni nauoru.
[c1-l024 note] The room is called Zaxiqui within the Portuguese explanation. Fonreǒni nauoru then begins a new example concerning restoration to one's original lands or income.
[c1-l025 >] *Ser reſtituido a ſuas terras, ou renda.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes Fonreǒni nauoru: being restored to one's lands or revenue. The gloss concerns restitution, not merely recovery from disease.
[c1-l026] Nauoxi. i, Fitatare. *Certalaya de veſtido q̃*
[c1-l026 note] The noun Nauoxi is equated with Fitatare and described as a kind of garment. It is distinct from the correcting verb that follows; preserve the printed compact Certalaya and the unexpanded q̃.
[c1-l027 >] *ſe traz por cima.*
[c1-l027 note] The garment is worn over other clothing. Por cima completes the preceding description rather than naming a further garment.
[c1-l028] Nauoxi, ſu, oita. *Emendar. Vt,* Aya-
[c1-l028 note] The verb Nauoxi means correcting or putting right. Aya- begins Ayamari, the fault or error corrected in the example; the principal part oita is retained as historical morphology.
[c1-l029 >] mariuo nauoſu. *Emendar a falta. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l029 note] Ayamariuo nauoſu means correcting a fault. The next sense begins with abbreviated Itẽ; its tilde is retained without expansion.
[c1-l030 >] *Mudar a couſa a outro lugar. ¶* Nacauo
[c1-l030 note] An additional sense is moving something to another place. Nacauo starts a separate example about restoring friendly relations, continued below.
[c1-l031 >] nauoſu. *Fazer as amizades. ¶* Rǒnin
[c1-l031 note] Nacauo nauoſu is making peace or restoring friendship. Rǒnin begins the next example, concerning the return of an exile or displaced person.
[c1-l032 >] uo nauoſu. *Reſtituir, ou reduzir o deſter-*
[c1-l032 note] Rǒnin uo nauoſu is glossed as restoring or bringing back the exile. The scan supports reduzir, not OCR redugir; deſter- continues into rado below.
[c1-l033 >] *rado. ¶* Iyeuo nauoſu. *Endereitar a ca-*
[c1-l033 note] Rado completes deſterrado. Iyeuo nauoſu starts the house-straightening example, with Portuguese ca- divided across the next physical boundary.
[c1-l034 >] *ſa que eſtaua torta, ou inclinada.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes caſa: the house had been crooked or leaning. This is the transitive counterpart of the leaning-house sense under Nauori.
[c1-l035] Nauozari. l, nauozarini. *Pola mòr parte,*
[c1-l035 note] Nauozari and nauozarini are glossed as for the most part or approximately. The alternative marker l is Latin editorial apparatus, not a Japanese syllable.
[c1-l036 >] *ou pouco mais, ou menos.*
[c1-l036 note] Ou pouco mais, ou menos completes the approximate or general sense. It explains the preceding adverbial alternatives rather than beginning a new entry.
[c1-l037] Nauozarina. l, nauozarino. *Couſa medi-*
[c1-l037 note] Nauozarina and nauozarino describe something ordinary or middling. The Portuguese medi- continues into mediocre on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] *ocre, ou de pouco mais, ou menos.*
[c1-l038 note] Ocre completes mediocre; the rest gives the approximate, neither particularly good nor bad sense. Preserve the physical division instead of joining the Portuguese word in the transcription.
[c1-l039] Nauozari narazarugui. *Couſa não das ordi-*
[c1-l039 note] Nauozari narazarugui describes something not ordinary. The negative narazaru is consistent with Portuguese não; the final gui is retained as printed.
[c1-l040 >] *narias, mas muito grande, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] Completes the contrast: not an ordinary thing, but a very great one. The line belongs to the preceding negative expression, not the following adverbial entry.
[c1-l041] Nauozari narazu. *Grandemente, ou fora do*
[c1-l041 note] Nauozari narazu is glossed as greatly or beyond the ordinary. It is a separate expression from the preceding attributive narazaru construction.
[c1-l042 >] *ordinario.*
[c1-l042 note] Ordinario completes fora do ordinario. This short line is retained as running text rather than folded into the next pear entry.
[c1-l043] Naxi. *Peras.*
[c1-l043 note] Naxi here names pears. The following identical-looking headword is a verb, so the separate entry and full stop matter.
[c1-l044] Naxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer. ¶* Atauo naſu.
[c1-l044 note] The verb Naxi means doing or making. Atauo naſu is the first example, explained below as doing harm; aita is the printed historical principal part.
[c1-l045 >] *Fazer mal. ¶* Fufuno chiguiri. l, ca-
[c1-l045 note] Fazer mal translates Atauo naſu. The next example joins the matrimonial bond, Fufuno chiguiri, with the alternative catarai split after ca-.
[c1-l046 >] taraiuo naſu. *Ter copula matrimonial.*
[c1-l046 note] Taraiuo completes cataraiuo, and naſu governs the preceding matrimonial expressions. The Portuguese gloss explicitly concerns matrimonial intercourse.
[c1-l047 >] *¶* Guegiuo naſu. *Mandar como ſenhor*
[c1-l047 note] Guegiuo naſu concerns giving orders as a master to a servant, with the servant on the next column's first row. The worn initial retains the inner feature of G, supporting Guegiuo rather than OCR Cuegiuo.

[c2-l007] Naxicudaſare, uru, eta. *Dar peßoa nobre.*

[c2-l008 note] Xenjiuo naxi cudaſareta concerns a superior issuing an order or decree. Feiq. is the upright citation label; the Portuguese translation begins with the divided Mã-.

[c2-l010] Naxigi. *Ouro ſalpicado ſobre Vruxi. ¶* Naxi-
[c2-l010 note] Naxigi is gold sprinkled over lacquer, named Vruxi inside the Portuguese explanation. Naxi- begins the repeated term in the next example.
[c2-l011 >] giuo maqu. *Dourar deſta maneira.*
[c2-l011 note] Naxigiuo maqu describes applying the speckled gold finish. Dourar deſta maneira refers to the technique just defined, not a generic unrelated gilding operation.
[c2-l012] Naximono. *Certo comer de tripas, & outras*
[c2-l012 note] Naximono is a prepared food made from entrails and other small fish parts. The definition continues below with the salting qualification.
[c2-l013 >] *partes miudas de peixe ſalgadas.*
[c2-l013 note] The small parts of the fish are salted. This completes the food definition without identifying a particular species or modern recipe.
[c2-l014] Naxino qi. *Pereira.*
[c2-l014 note] Naxino qi is the pear tree, distinguished from Naxi, the fruit, in the preceding column. Pereira is the direct Portuguese equivalent.
[c2-l015] Naxivchi yeboxi. *Hũa maneira de barretes*
[c2-l015 note] Naxivchi yeboxi names a kind of cap. The Portuguese compares its shape and material on the following rows rather than giving a modern costume classification.
[c2-l016 >] *como caraminholas de papel vruxado que v-*
[c2-l016 note] The caps resemble caraminholas and are made of lacquered paper. The scan reads vruxado, with r after v, not OCR vxuxado; the final v- begins vſão below.
[c2-l017 >] *ſão nos autos.*
[c2-l017 note] São completes vſão: these caps are used in plays or performances. The reference is to use in autos, not to a separate object named São.
[c2-l018] Naxxo. Voſamuru tocoro. *Certo officio de*
[c2-l018 note] This Naxxo is explained by Voſamuru tocoro and introduces a monastic office. Voſamuru is worn but the surviving letters and the following steward gloss support the supplied reading.
[c2-l019 >] *ſeruir entre os Bonzos como deſpenſeiro, &c.*
[c2-l019 note] The office serves the Buddhist clergy in the manner of a steward or storekeeper. Bonzos belongs to the Portuguese description, not to an added Japanese headword.
[c2-l020] Naxxo. *O pagarem os lauradores o rendimẽ-*
[c2-l020 note] The second Naxxo concerns cultivators paying the revenue from their land. The abbreviated rendimẽ- continues into the next row.

[c2-l027] Nayami, u, ǒda. *Eſtar debilitado, atribulado,*
[c2-l027 note] Nayami means being weakened or afflicted. Enlargement shows ǒda, with a d ascender, rather than OCR ǒta; preserve the printed contracted historical principal part.
[c2-l028 >] *& moido como doente, &c.*
[c2-l028 note] The person is worn out as though ill. This continuation completes the debilitated-state definition and is distinct from the ceremonial term below.
[c2-l029] Nayarai. l, Voniyarai. *Ceremonia que fazẽ*
[c2-l029 note] Nayarai, alternatively Voniyarai, introduces the ceremony for driving away demons. The description continues across five rows; l remains the printed alternative marker.
[c2-l030 >] *os gentios no principio do anno botando graõs*
[c2-l030 note] The ceremony occurs at the beginning of the year and includes scattering grain. The enlarged final word reads graõs, with the tilde over o, unlike grãos two rows below.
[c2-l031 >] *torrados pera o àr dentro de caſa, & depois*
[c2-l031 note] The grains are roasted and thrown into the air inside the house. The printed grave in àr is preserved rather than changed to modern unaccented ar.
[c2-l032 >] *comendo cada hum da quelles grãos tantos,*
[c2-l032 note] Afterwards each participant eats some of those grains. Da quelles retains the printed separation, and grãos here carries its tilde on a.
[c2-l033 >] *quantos annos cada hum tem, & iſto pera que*
[c2-l033 note] The number eaten matches each person's years of age. The final clause introduces the protective purpose explained on the next line.
[c2-l034 >] *não chegue alli o demonio, &c.*
[c2-l034 note] The stated purpose is to prevent the demon from approaching the house. This reports the dictionary's account of the ceremony, not an endorsement of its effect.
[c2-l035] Naye. *Arroz em erua pouco crecido q̃ ſe despoẽ.*

[c2-l038 note] Tremer a terra translates Nayega yuru: the earth trembles. The short translation occupies its own physical row.
[c2-l039] Naye, uru, eta. *Eſtar paralitico do corpo,*
[c2-l039 note] The verb Naye concerns bodily paralysis or loss of function. The initial Portuguese word has a long ſ in Eſtar; the Japanese uru and eta principal forms are retained.
[c2-l040 >] *ou aleijado, & ſem ſentir algum membro.*
[c2-l040 note] The definition includes being disabled and having no sensation in a limb. This continues the bodily sense rather than the later figurative comparison.
[c2-l041 >] *¶* Axi, tega nayuru. *Ter algũa parle-*
[c2-l041 note] Axi, tega nayuru applies the condition to feet and hands. The Portuguese noun for paralysis is divided after parle-.
[c2-l042 >] *ſia, ou aleijão nos pees, ou nas mãos. ¶ I-*
[c2-l042 note] ſia completes parleſia; the feet and hands are explicitly named. I- begins Item introducing the figurative extension below.
[c2-l043 >] *tem, per met. Eſtar como morto, ou ſem*
[c2-l043 note] The figurative sense is being as though dead or motionless. Per met. belongs to the italic explanatory apparatus and is not expanded in the transcription.
[c2-l044 >] *ſe bulir. Vt,* Deini nayetaru vuono go-
[c2-l044 note] Bulir means moving, completing the motionless-state explanation; the initial b corrects OCR h. The quotation begins Deini nayetaru vuono go-, comparing the captives to enfeebled fish in mud; the fish word begins with printed v, not OCR u.
[c2-l045 >] tocunite iqedoraruru mono ſanbiacu yo-
[c2-l045 note] Tocunite completes gotocunite, the comparison, followed by being captured alive. Sanbiacu yo- begins the number of more than three hundred people.
[c2-l046 >] nin. Taif. *Lib. 18. Mais de trezen-*
[c2-l046 note] Nin completes the human numeral phrase. Taif. is the citation label and Lib. 18 gives the book number; the translation begins Mais de trezen-.
[c2-l047 >] *tos, que forão tomados viuos eſtauão como pei*
[c2-l047 note] Tos completes trezentos: more than three hundred taken alive were like fish. The scan supports eſtauão, restoring the omitted u in OCR eſtaão. Pei continues as xes at the next page opening; the lower repeated xes is the catchword, not an extra body row.
