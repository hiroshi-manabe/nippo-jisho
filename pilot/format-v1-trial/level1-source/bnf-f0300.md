[c1-l001 >] *porta aquem primeiro chegaria, eſperauão que*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding entry: waiting at the door for whoever would arrive first. The conditional chegaria and the plural eſperauão belong to this unfinished Portuguese sentence, not to a new entry.
[c1-l002 >] *chegaßem, &c.*
[c1-l002 note] Chegaßem completes the preceding expectation of arrival; the abbreviation &c. closes the carried-over explanation.
[c1-l003] Machicôgi. *Ruas, & traueßas.*
[c1-l003 note] Machikooji denotes streets and side streets, matching ruas and traueßas. The enlarged accent is a circumflex, so Machicôgi replaces the provisional caron spelling.
[c1-l004] Machigauo. *Sembrante de quem eſpera.*
[c1-l004 note] Machigao is the expectant expression of someone waiting. Sembrante describes the face or expression, not a town-related sense of machi.
[c1-l005] Machijin, *l.* Machǔdo. *Homem da rua, ou*
[c1-l005 note] Machijin and Machǔdo are alternative names for a townsman. The printed l. introduces the alternative; the gloss continues with a resident of a settlement.
[c1-l006 >] *morador de pouoação.*
[c1-l006 note] Morador de pouoação completes the definition of the town-dweller. The historical u/v spelling in pouoação is retained.
[c1-l007] Machimachi. *Couſa diuerſa, ou differente.*
[c1-l007 note] Machimachi means diverse or differing. Enlargement establishes the missing i in differente; the two f types remain distinct from the long s in diuerſa.
[c1-l008 >] *Vt,* Qigui machimachini yǔ. *Dizer ca-*
[c1-l008 note] The example illustrates people expressing differing opinions, as the next row explains. Qigui is retained as printed rather than altered to a more familiar Japanese form; machimachini modifies yǔ. The final ca- begins Portuguese cada.
[c1-l009 >] *da hum ſeu parecer differente.*
[c1-l009 note] Da joins ca- above to form cada: each person states a different opinion. This closes the machimachi example.
[c1-l010] Machimono. *Couſa que ſe vende na rua, ou*
[c1-l010 note] Machimono is merchandise sold in the street or a shop. The visible word gap gives na rua, not the OCR joining narua.
[c1-l011 >] *tenda. ¶ Item, Couſa de fancaria, ou de*
[c1-l011 note] Tenda completes the place of sale. The additional fancaria sense concerns ordinary stock or ready-made work and continues with corja on the next row.
[c1-l012 >] *corja*: *o contrario de* Atçurayemono.
[c1-l012 note] The stock-made sense is explicitly contrasted with Atçurayemono, something made to order. This Japanese term is embedded in Portuguese explanatory prose and remains upright.
[c1-l013] Machimǒqe, uru, eta. *Eſperando alcançar,*
[c1-l013 note] Machimǒqe, with the principal forms uru and eta, means obtaining or receiving something after waiting. The caron is visible here and is not assimilated to the different accent in Machicôgi.
[c1-l014 >] *ou receber. Vt,* Qiacujinuo machimǒqe
[c1-l014 note] The example begins receiving an expected guest: Qiacujinuo is the object, and machimǒqe continues with ta below. No terminal hyphen is supplied where none is visible.
[c1-l015 >] ta. *Recebi hum hoſpede que eſperaua.*
[c1-l015 note] Ta completes the past-tense example. Recebi hum hoſpede que eſperaua gives its first-person sense: receiving the guest one had been expecting.
[c1-l016] Machitçucuxi, ſu, uita. *Eſperar muito, &*
[c1-l016 note] Machitçucuxi means waiting exhaustively or for a long time. The printed principal part uita is a historical inflection, not an error to be modernized to ushita.
[c1-l017 >] *por muitas vias. Vt,* Cocorouo tçucuite
[c1-l017 note] Cocorouo tçucuite describes applying oneself wholeheartedly; together with the following row it illustrates prolonged, careful waiting. Tçucuite has the same historical i-onbin pattern as the entry forms.
[c1-l018 >] ſujit machitçucuita. Tog. *Cõ todo cui-*
[c1-l018 note] Sujit specifies several days, and machitçucuita completes the past-tense example. Tog. is an upright citation label. The Portuguese begins Cõ todo cui-, with a visible gap after Cõ and cuidado divided across the line.
[c1-l019 >] *dado eſtiue eſperando muitos dias.*
[c1-l019 note] Dado completes cuidado: the speaker waited many days with every care. The first-person eſtiue confirms that this is the translation of the preceding example.
[c1-l020] Machitçuqe, uru, eta. *Eſperar alguem atè*
[c1-l020 note] Machitçuqe means waiting for someone until that person arrives. The infinitive explanation is completed by chegar on the next physical row.
[c1-l021 >] *chegar. Vt,* Pedrouo machitçuqeta.
[c1-l021 note] Pedrouo machitçuqeta illustrates waiting until Pedro arrived. The complete name and attached Japanese object particle belong to the upright example, not partly to the italic Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l022] Machitçure, ruru, eta. *Eſperando leuar*
[c1-l022 note] Machitçure describes waiting for someone and then taking that person along. The visibly printed ruru is preserved, rather than standardized to the more usual abbreviated uru.
[c1-l023 >] *conſigo.*
[c1-l023 note] Conſigo completes leuar: taking the awaited person with oneself. This short line is genuine continuation text.
[c1-l024] Machitotonoye, uru, eta. *Eſperando apa-*
[c1-l024 note] Machitotonoye concerns preparing things while awaiting someone. Enlargement supports eta, not OCR cta; apa- begins Portuguese aparelhar on the next row.
[c1-l025 >] *relhar, como quem vai concertando comer, &*
[c1-l025 note] Relhar completes aparelhar. The gloss compares this preparation with arranging food while expecting a guest; concertando is retained in its historical sense of putting things in order.
[c1-l026 >] *o de mais para conuidar a alguem.*
[c1-l026 note] The preparation includes everything else needed to entertain someone. The spaced de mais is preserved rather than modernized to demais.
[c1-l027] Machitto. *Adu. Mais hum pouco.*
[c1-l027 note] Machitto is an adverb meaning a little more. Adu. is italic grammatical apparatus, not part of the Japanese headword.
[c1-l028] Machivqe, uru, eta. *Eſperando receber a al-*
[c1-l028 note] Machivqe means waiting to receive someone. The initial v of the second element represents the historical spelling of uke; al- divides Portuguese alguem.
[c1-l029 >] *guem. Vt,* Xoxono tçumarizzumari
[c1-l029 note] The quoted example begins with confined places at various locations. No full stop is established after Xoxono; removing the OCR stop restores the visible continuous Japanese phrase.
[c1-l030 >] machi vqete vchitomeqeru aida, *&c.*
[c1-l030 note] Machi vqete vchitomeqeru describes waiting in ambush and striking someone down; aida continues the cited narrative construction. The printed internal gap in machi vqete is retained, and &c. is the closing apparatus.
[c1-l031 >] Taif. *Lib. 7. Eſperandoo em lugares eſtre-*
[c1-l031 note] Taif. is the upright source label, followed by italic Lib. 7. The Portuguese translation begins waiting for him in narrow places; eſtre- continues as eſtreitos below.
[c1-l032 >] *itos, & apertados o matou, ou fez ficar morto.*
[c1-l032 note] Itos completes eſtreitos. The rest explains killing him, or making him remain dead, in those restricted places; it closes the ambush example.
[c1-l033] Machiya. *Caſas da rua.*
[c1-l033 note] Machiya is simply glossed as houses in the street. The definition supports the town-house sense without adding a modern architectural classification.
[c1-l034] Machiye, uru, eta. *Eſperando alcançar. Vt,*
[c1-l034 note] Machiye is obtaining something after waiting. The following wind example distinguishes this verb from the preceding noun Machiya.
[c1-l035 >] Sono cajeuo machiyete. *Vindo lhe o vento*
[c1-l035 note] Sono cajeuo machiyete describes obtaining the awaited wind. The damaged a in cajeuo is compatible with the remaining strokes and the Portuguese vento; the historical caje spelling is not normalized.
[c1-l036 >] *que eſperaua.*
[c1-l036 note] Que eſperaua completes the wind example: the wind that the person had been waiting for. The short line remains separate body text.
[c1-l037] Machǔdo. *Homem da rua, ou morador de*
[c1-l037 note] Machǔdo repeats the townsman term already offered as an alternative under Machijin. The gloss again defines a resident, rather than a person who is waiting.
[c1-l038 >] *villa, ou cidade.*
[c1-l038 note] Villa or cidade supplies the settlement in which the townsman lives. Both alternatives complete morador de on the preceding row.
[c1-l039] Macocoro. Macotono cocoro, l, magocoro.
[c1-l039 note] Macocoro is explained as a true heart, with magocoro as an alternative. Enlargement supports damaged cocoro: the apparent cedilla in the OCR crosses a paper crease and is not a secure printed mark. This local reading remains mildly uncertain because the first two letters are abraded.
[c1-l040 >] *Coração verdadeiro, & ſingelo. ¶* Ma-
[c1-l040 note] The Portuguese gives a true and straightforward heart. The paragraph mark then introduces the horse example, whose Macocorona is divided after Ma-.
[c1-l041 >] cocorona vma. *Caualo manſo, & de bo-*
[c1-l041 note] Macocorona vma describes a gentle, well-disposed horse. The scan supports manſo instead of OCR iranſo; bo- begins boas on the following row.
[c1-l042 >] *as manhas.*
[c1-l042 note] As completes boas: the horse has good habits or qualities. It belongs to the horse example, not to the following plant entry.
[c1-l043] Macomo. *Hũa certa erua de agua doce como*
[c1-l043 note] Macomo is described as a freshwater plant resembling a rush. The readable phrase erua de agua doce corrects the garbled OCR; no precise botanical identification is imported into the text.
[c1-l044 >] *bunho.*
[c1-l044 note] Bunho completes the comparison with a rush. This one-word botanical continuation is not a new headword.
[c1-l045] Macomo. Muxiro. *Esteira feita deſta erua.*
[c1-l045 note] Macomo. Muxiro names a mat made from the preceding plant. Both printed stops and the capital Muxiro are preserved; enlargement supports the t in deſta, rather than OCR deſa.
[c1-l046] Macoto. *Verdade.*
[c1-l046 note] Macoto is truth, directly matched by Verdade. The spelling is kept distinct from the following adverbial form.
[c1-l047] Macotoni. *Adu. Em verdade, certamente.*
[c1-l047 note] Macotoni means truly or certainly. The damaged initial is M, not the OCR sequence Du, and the Portuguese reads certamente, not centamente.

[c2-l001] Macotono. *Couſa verdadeira.*
[c2-l001 note] Macotono is the attributive expression for something true. The scan has an initial M, continuing the macoto family, rather than OCR Nacotono.
[c2-l002] Macotoraxij. *Couſa que parece ſer verdadeira.*
[c2-l002 note] Macotoraxij describes something that appears true. The gloss concerns the appearance of truth, not necessarily its actuality.
[c2-l003] Macotoxij. *Idem.*
[c2-l003 note] Macotoxij receives Idem, explicitly referring back to the preceding appearance-of-truth definition.
[c2-l004] Macotoxiyacani. *Adu. Como de verdade.*
[c2-l004 note] Macotoxiyacani is an adverb meaning as though something were true. The final ni and the italic label Adu. identify its adverbial use.
[c2-l005 >] *Vt,* Macoto xiyacani tabacaru. *Enganar*
[c2-l005 note] Macoto xiyacani tabacaru means deceiving in a plausible or truth-like manner. The printed gap within Macoto xiyacani is preserved even though the headword is unspaced.
[c2-l006 >] *a alguẽ fazendo o crèr mentira por verdade.*
[c2-l006 note] The Portuguese explains making someone believe a lie as truth. The visibly printed crir is retained rather than normalized to crer, and alguẽ keeps its abbreviation tilde.
[c2-l007] Macu. *Cortinas compridas que ſeruem nas em-*
[c2-l007 note] Macu denotes long curtains or screens. The explanation begins listing their uses, with em- continuing as embarcações.
[c2-l008 >] *barcações, exercitos, autos, &c. ¶* Ma-
[c2-l008 note] The curtains are used on vessels, with armies, and in performances. Autos is theatrical here; Ma- begins the first curtain-setting example.
[c2-l009 >] cuuo faru. *Eſtender as cortinas nos teatros,*
[c2-l009 note] Macuuo faru means extending or putting up curtains in theatres. The initial cuuo completes Ma- from the preceding row.
[c2-l010 >] *&c. ¶* Macuuo faxiracaſu. *Por, ou*
[c2-l010 note] Macuuo faxiracaſu is the expression for setting curtains on a ship. Por is the historical infinitive meaning put, not the Portuguese preposition in this construction.
[c2-l011 >] *eſtender cortinas no nauio. ¶* Macuuo v.
[c2-l011 note] The shipboard explanation ends before the next example, whose vtçu is divided between this row and the next. A separate second-pass enlargement shows a round point after v, not a secure hyphen; the original v. is therefore retained despite the continuation.
[c2-l012 >] tçu. *Eſtendelas no exercito.*
[c2-l012 note] Tçu completes vtçu, the verb used for erecting the screens in an army setting. Eſtendelas refers back to the curtains.
[c2-l013] Macubo. *Coua do olho muito metido pera*
[c2-l013 note] Macubo denotes a deeply hollowed eye socket. The Portuguese locates the eye as very far inward, with the direction completed below.
[c2-l014 >] *dentro. B.*
[c2-l014 note] Dentro completes pera dentro. The final B. is an upright low-register label, distinct from the italic gloss.
[c2-l015] Macubari, u, atta. *Repartir com proporção.*
[c2-l015 note] Macubari means distributing in proportion. The abbreviated principal forms u and atta are retained exactly as printed, without silently supplying r.
[c2-l016 >] *Vt,* Ninjuuo macubari auaſuru. *Repar-*
[c2-l016 note] Ninjuuo macubari auaſuru describes distributing people in balanced proportions. Repar- begins the Portuguese restatement on the next row.
[c2-l017 >] *tir a gente de maneira que fiquem igualmente*
[c2-l017 note] Tir completes repartir. The distribution is arranged so that the people are placed equally, with the participial phrase continuing below.
[c2-l018 >] *repartidos por ſuas eſtancias. ¶* Faxirauo
[c2-l018 note] The people are assigned to their respective stations. Faxirauo begins a second example, this time about the spacing of pillars rather than personnel.
[c2-l019 >] macubaru. *Repartir as columnas por ſeus*
[c2-l019 note] Faxirauo macubaru means distributing the pillars at appropriate intervals. The Portuguese columnas confirms the architectural object.
[c2-l020 >] *eſpaços.*
[c2-l020 note] Eſpaços completes the allocation of the columns to their spaces or intervals. It is an ordinary continuation, not a heading.
[c2-l021] Macuguxi. l, macuzucuxi. *Eſtacas, ou pa-*
[c2-l021 note] Macuguxi and macuzucuxi name the stakes or poles that support military curtains. The alternative marker l remains literal, and pa- continues as paos.
[c2-l022 >] *os de que ſe dependurão as cortinas no exercito.*
[c2-l022 note] Os completes paos. These are the poles from which the curtains are suspended in an army setting; the historical dependurão is retained.
[c2-l023] Macujen. *Adu. Modo de eſtar quieto, &*
[c2-l023 note] Macujen is explained as a manner of remaining quiet and inactive. The crop clearly reads Modo, not OCR Medo; this is not a definition involving fear.
[c2-l024 >] *ſẽ fazer nada. Vt,* Macujento xite iru.
[c2-l024 note] Macujento xite iru illustrates staying quietly without doing anything. The abbreviated ſẽ belongs to the Portuguese explanation, while the Japanese example remains upright.
[c2-l025] Macumǒjǒ. Xizzucanari midarini vomô.
[c2-l025 note] Macumǒjǒ is paraphrased as being quiet and thinking idly or without discipline. The following Portuguese gloss characterizes the thoughts negatively; the historical Xizzucanari is retained without syntactic modernization.
[c2-l026 >] i, mǒnen. *Ruins penſamentos, ou conſi-*
[c2-l026 note] The synonym mǒnen reinforces the sense of idle or misguided thoughts. Ruins penſamentos begins the Portuguese definition, followed by divided conſi-deração.
[c2-l027 >] *deração de couſas vaans.*
[c2-l027 note] Deração completes conſideração: reflection on vain things. This closes the thought-related entry before Macura begins the pillow vocabulary.
[c2-l028] Macura. *Almofada. ¶* Macurauo cata-
[c2-l028 note] Macura means a pillow or headrest. The gloss reads Almofada, with f rather than the OCR long s; Macurauo cata- begins the first example.
[c2-l029 >] muquru. *Cõcertar a almofada pera dormir.*
[c2-l029 note] Muquru completes catamuquru. The dictionary explains the expression as arranging the pillow for sleep, a contextual gloss retained rather than replaced by a literal modern translation.
[c2-l030 >] *¶* Macurauo naraburu. *Concertar a almo-*
[c2-l030 note] Macurauo naraburu is placing pillows side by side. The Portuguese explanation begins arranging the pillow and continues with the purpose below.
[c2-l031 >] *fada pera dormirem dous juntos.*
[c2-l031 note] Fada completes almofada: the arrangement is for two people to sleep together. The continuation supplies the point of the preceding Japanese example.
[c2-l032] Macurabaco. *Almofada de pao com algũs bu-*
[c2-l032 note] Macurabaco is first described as a wooden headrest with openings. The line ends algũs bu-, not the joined OCR algũsba-; bu- continues as buracos on the next row.
[c2-l033 >] *racos, ou aberturas polas quaes as molheres ſe*
[c2-l033 note] Racos completes buracos. The holes or openings allow women to perfume themselves, with the action and means continued on the next row.
[c2-l034 >] *perfumão com cheiro q̃ metẽ dentro. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l034 note] Perfume placed inside the headrest provides the scent. The final e of metẽ carries a visible abbreviation tilde; the OCR omitted it. Itẽ introduces a second sense.
[c2-l035 >] *Aliquando, Caixa em que metem a almofada.*
[c2-l035 note] Aliquando marks the occasional sense: a box into which the pillow is put. This is distinguished from the perforated wooden headrest just described.
[c2-l036] Macurabeǒbu. *Beòbus pequenos, & curtos.*
[c2-l036 note] Macurabeǒbu denotes small, low screens beside the pillow. The Portuguese plural reads Beòbus, with a grave over o, not OCR Bedbus; the different caron in the upright headword is preserved independently.
[c2-l037] Macuracotoba. *Palauras como ſinonimos de q̃*
[c2-l037 note] Macuracotoba introduces poetic expressions, described here as words like synonyms. This is the dictionary's explanation of their poetic function, not a reason to replace it with a modern technical definition.
[c2-l038 >] *vſão os poetas com artificio do verſo como pera*
[c2-l038 note] The poets use such expressions as a device of verse. The line leads into two examples, beginning with the expression associated with earth.
[c2-l039 >] *dizer terra, vſar deſta palaura,* Aracane.
[c2-l039 note] Aracane is the example connected with terra. Enlargement establishes the arch of n rather than OCR u in Aracaue; the term occurs within Portuguese explanation and remains upright.
[c2-l040 >] *& pera dizer caminho,* Tamaboco, &c.
[c2-l040 note] Tamaboco supplies the example associated with a road. The final &c. is upright here, unlike the italic Portuguese connective opening the row.
[c2-l041] Macuragami. *Cabeceira da cama. Vt,* Ma-
[c2-l041 note] Macuragami is the head of the bed. Ma- begins the example of standing at the sleeping person's bedside.
[c2-l042 >] curagamini tatçu. *Porſe em pè à cabecei-*
[c2-l042 note] Macuragamini tatçu means standing at the head of the bed. Portuguese Porſe em pè expresses taking up a standing position; cabecei- continues below.
[c2-l043 >] *ra do que dorme.*
[c2-l043 note] Ra completes cabeceira, specified as that of the person sleeping. It closes the position example.
[c2-l044] Macurayari. *Hũa lança curta que ſe poem pe-*
[c2-l044 note] Macurayari is a short spear kept beside the pillow. The headword has y, not OCR j; the description continues after pe-.
[c2-l045 >] *ra vigia à cabeceira.*
[c2-l045 note] Ra completes pera: the spear is kept for watch or protection at the head of the bed. The crop reads cabeceira, not the OCR cabeceita.
[c2-l046] Macuri, u, utta. *Deſcobrir enrolando, ou*
[c2-l046 note] Macuri means uncovering by rolling up or gathering a covering. The full Portuguese verb is Deſcobrir, rather than the truncated OCR Deſchrir; the printed principal part u remains unexpanded.
[c2-l047 >] *apanhando a couſa. ¶* Yaneuo macuru,
[c2-l047 note] The covering is gathered up. Yaneuo macuru introduces removing a roof covering, whose Portuguese explanation starts on f301; the printed closing mark after macuru is a comma. The lower isolated deſtelhar is a catchword, not a second body continuation.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *deſtelhar*
