## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001 >] ¶ Fanauo cǔzuru. *Offerecer roſas ao Ido-*
[c1-l001 note] `Fanauo cǔzuru` means offering flowers, here to an idol. The Japanese flower expression continues the examples on f167 rather than beginning a new headword.
[c1-l002 >] *lo. ¶* Fanauo chiguiru. *Colher roſas,*
[c1-l002 note] `Ido- / lo` finishes the recipient. `Fanauo chiguiru` then means plucking flowers; the method is specified below.
[c1-l003 >] *ou flores ſem pè. ¶* Fanaga firaqu. *A-*
[c1-l003 note] The flowers are picked without their stalks. `Fanaga firaqu` switches to the intransitive opening of a flower.
[c1-l004 >] *brir a roſa. ¶* Fanaga focoroburu. *Co-*
[c1-l004 note] The opening gloss ends, then `Fanaga focoroburu` describes beginning to open. The two verbs are related but not interchangeable stages in this definition.
[c1-l005 >] *meçar abrir a roſa.*
[c1-l005 note] `Começar abrir a roſa` completes the beginning-to-open sense. It is not an instruction to force a blossom open.
[c1-l006] Fanamezzuraxij. *Couſa noua, ou viſtoſa.*
[c1-l006 note] `Fanamezzuraxij` is something novel or striking. The spelling `zz` belongs to the historical ヅ series; modern めずらしい should not overwrite it.
[c1-l007 >] ¶ Fana icuſa. *Certo jogo, ou folguedo,*
[c1-l007 note] `Fana icuſa`, literally flower warfare, names a game. The following physical description prevents taking `icuſa` as an actual battle here.
[c1-l008 >] *que fazem cõ roſas amarradas nas põtas de*
[c1-l008 note] Flowers are tied to the ends of objects named below. This describes the game's equipment rather than an ordinary flower arrangement.
[c1-l009 >] *bambus.*
[c1-l009 note] `bambus` completes the bamboo supports for the flowers. The material is Portuguese vocabulary, not a Japanese example.
[c1-l010] Fanabaxira. *Lombo do nariz.*
[c1-l010 note] `Fanabaxira` is the ridge or bridge of the nose. Portuguese `Lombo do nariz` confirms 鼻柱 rather than a floral pillar.
[c1-l011] Fanabira. *Hũas roſas feitas de papel de vari-*
[c1-l011 note] `Fanabira` here includes paper flowers or petals, not only natural petals. They are made in several colors, completed below.
[c1-l012 >] *as cores que os Bõzos eſpalhão em certos offici-*
[c1-l012 note] Buddhist monks scatter the colored paper flowers during certain services. `Bõzos` belongs to the Portuguese explanatory sentence.
[c1-l013 >] *os. ¶ Item, Hũa certa laya de Mochis.*
[c1-l013 note] The first sense ends with religious services. A second sense is a kind of `Mochis`, so both ritual paper and a confection are included under the same spelling.
[c1-l014] Fanabirayuqi. *Neue que cae em paſta como*
[c1-l014 note] `Fanabirayuqi` describes snow falling in broad flakes or clumps like petals. The Portuguese comparison continues with soft fibrous material.
[c1-l015 >] *lãa, ou borra. Vt,* Fanabira yuqiga furu.
[c1-l015 note] Wool or fluff supplies the comparison. `Fanabira yuqiga furu` is the snow-falling example, with `ga` marking snow as subject.
[c1-l016 >] *Chouer eſta neue.*
[c1-l016 note] `Chouer eſta neue` means this kind of snow falling. The Portuguese verb need not imply rain rather than snow in this construction.
[c1-l017] Fanabuſa. *Roſa, ou flor como borla.*
[c1-l017 note] `Fanabuſa` denotes a tassel-like flower or cluster. The compiler explains its shape, so no particular botanical species is established.
[c1-l018] Fanabuye. *Cano por õde reſfolegão os narizes.*
[c1-l018 note] `Fanabuye` names the passage through which the nose breathes. The gloss is anatomical, not necessarily a musical nose flute.
[c1-l019] Fanacago. *Ceſto de roſas, ou flores.*
[c1-l019 note] `Fanacago` is a basket for flowers. This is a container compound, distinguished from the nasal vocabulary surrounding it.
[c1-l020] Fanachi. i, Couan, l, mitçuname. *Cana meãa*
[c1-l020 note] The enlarged scan supports `mitçuname`, not `mitçume`. Together with `Couan`, it names a hollow drinking tube; the exact lexical analysis remains less secure than the visible missing syllable.
[c1-l021 >] *por onde ſe bebe.*
[c1-l021 note] The tube is used for drinking through. This completes the functional definition and supports interpreting the preceding object as a drinking implement.
[c1-l022] Fanachi, tçu, aita. *Deſparar, ou atirar com*
[c1-l022 note] `Fanachi, tçu, aita` is releasing or shooting. The `aita` ending is unusual for 放つ; NINJAL also records this printed anomaly and suggests possible confusion with 放す. Preserve it rather than normalizing the paradigm. Portuguese first concerns an arrow.
[c1-l023 >] *ſeta. ¶ Item, Largar, ou ſoltar paſſaros,*
[c1-l023 note] After shooting an arrow, the definition adds letting birds go free. The common semantic element is release, not necessarily combat.
[c1-l024 >] *& animaes. Vt,* Reǒginno inudomouo
[c1-l024 note] The enlarged reading `Reǒginno` is compatible with 両陣の, “of both armies,” matching Portuguese `de cada exercito` below. It is not the previous `Rǒginno` reading.
[c1-l025 >] ichinifiacqizzutçu fanachi auaxtareba,
[c1-l025 note] The dogs are released in groups of one or two hundred. `fanachi auaxtareba` is damaged and morphologically awkward; an expected joining verb does not by itself authorize adding a faint or absent letter.
[c1-l026 >] *&c.* Taif*. Lib. 5. Soltando, & fa-*
[c1-l026 note] `Taif. Lib. 5` cites the Taiheiki. Portuguese begins translating the dogs being released and made to fight; the citation is not part of the Japanese example.
[c1-l027 >] *zendo pellejar de cada exercito cento, ou duzẽ*
[c1-l027 note] Each army contributes a hundred or two hundred. This numerical comparison supports reading the Japanese opening as “both armies.”
[c1-l028 >] *tos caẽs, &c. ¶* Ficariuo fanatçu. *Reſplã-*
[c1-l028 note] The dogs complete the battle example. `Ficariuo fanatçu`, 光を放つ, introduces the separate sense of emitting light.
[c1-l029 >] *decer, ou dar claridade, & reſplandor.*
[c1-l029 note] Shining or giving brightness completes the light-emission definition. The verb's release sense extends from concrete projectiles to radiance.
[c1-l030] Fanachicaqe, ru, eta. *Atirar com frecha.*
[c1-l030 note] `Fanachicaqe` describes shooting an arrow toward something. It combines release with a directional or applicative second verb.
[c1-l031 >] ¶ Inuuo fanachi caquru. *Soltar, & açular cães.*
[c1-l031 note] `Inuuo fanachi caquru` means letting dogs loose upon someone or setting them on. Portuguese `açular` confirms incitement, not simply allowing dogs to wander.
[c1-l032] Fanachigai, l, fanaxigai. *Paſſaro, ou ani-*
[c1-l032 note] `Fanachigai` and `fanaxigai` refer to keeping animals loose. The spelling variants should remain separate alternatives, not be harmonized.
[c1-l033 >] *mal que ſe cria ſolto, & fora da gayola, &c.*
[c1-l033 note] The animal is raised free and outside a cage. This completes the husbandry sense of 放ち飼い or 放し飼い.
[c1-l034 >] *Vide* fanaxigai.
[c1-l034 note] `Vide` is the Portuguese/Latin cross-reference instruction, now italic; `fanaxigai` is its Japanese target. It should not generate a kana reading as one Japanese phrase.
[c1-l035] Fanada. *Papel tinto de azul com hũa fulla*
[c1-l035 note] `Fanada` is paper dyed blue. The plant-derived coloring material is named in the following line.
[c1-l036 >] *que ſe chama Fanagara. No* cami *ſe diz.* A-
[c1-l036 note] `Fanagara` names the coloring plant or leaf; `cami` introduces a regional name. Both are embedded in Portuguese, while `A-` starts the alternative Japanese term.
[c1-l037 >] uobana.
[c1-l037 note] `A- / uobana` gives アヲバナ. Its spelling should not be split into an unrelated initial vowel and a flower name in interpretation.
[c1-l038] Fanafadaxij. *Couſa grande, & terribel, como*
[c1-l038 note] `Fanafadaxij` is はなはだしい, describing something extreme or formidable. The Portuguese examples below establish degree rather than physical size alone.
[c1-l039 >] *ira, luxo, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] Anger and luxury/excess exemplify the adjective. These are abstract qualities that can be extreme, not merely large objects.
[c1-l040 >] Fanafadaxijſa.
[c1-l040 note] The `-ſa` form is the adjective's nominal derivative, “extremeness.” It belongs to the preceding entry despite occupying its own physical line.
[c1-l041 >] Fanafadaxǔ.
[c1-l041 note] The scan supports `Fanafadaxǔ`, the contracted adverb corresponding to はなはだしく, rather than another copy of the adjective ending `-xij`.
[c1-l042] Fanaga. *Bom cheiro, particularmente de Cha.*
[c1-l042 note] `Fanaga` denotes a pleasant aroma, particularly of `Cha`, tea. This is not the phrase 花が with a subject particle.
[c1-l043 >] ¶ *Per met.* Ano fitono fanaga ga nuqe-
[c1-l043 note] `Ano fitono fanaga ga nuqe-` applies the loss of fragrance metaphorically to a person. The two `ga` sequences have different roles: one ends the noun, the other is a particle.
[c1-l044 >] ta. *Ia eſte homem não contenta como primei-*
[c1-l044 note] `nuqe- / ta` completes “has gone out.” Portuguese explains that the person no longer pleases as before, rather than a literal bodily smell.
[c1-l045 >] *ro, nem he tam eſtimado, &c.*
[c1-l045 note] The person is no longer as esteemed. This finishes the social metaphor underlying the loss-of-aroma expression.
[c1-l046] Fanagami. *Papel pera ſe aſoar, &c.*
[c1-l046 note] `Fanagami` is paper for blowing one's nose. The nasal sense, 鼻紙, distinguishes it from the adjacent flower-coloring material.
[c1-l047] Fanagara. *Erua aſi chamada que da hũa fulla*
[c1-l047 note] `Fanagara` is a plant said to produce a blue leaf. Compare the earlier dye-paper entry, but avoid assigning a modern species without further evidence.
[c1-l048 >] *azul.*
[c1-l048 note] `azul` completes the blue color description. This is body text, distinct from any catchword at the page foot.

[c2-l001] Fanagaſa. *Chapeo feito de roſas, ou fullas.*
[c2-l001 note] `Fanagaſa` is a hat made of flowers or leaves. The floral sense is confirmed by the materials, not by the syllable `fana` alone.
[c2-l002 >] *¶ Item, Roſas de baxo das quais ſe hum*
[c2-l002 note] Another sense is flowers beneath which someone places themselves. The compiler describes a floral covering or canopy-like situation, not necessarily the same wearable hat.
[c2-l003 >] *poem.*
[c2-l003 note] `poem` completes the reflexive placement expression. The line closes the second sense of `Fanagaſa`.
[c2-l004] Fanagatami. *Ceſtinho de roſas.*
[c2-l004 note] `Fanagatami` is a small flower basket. It belongs to the floral container vocabulary, like `Fanacago` in column 1.
[c2-l005] Fanagaua. *Cabreſto do cauallo com que eſta pre-*
[c2-l005 note] `Fanagaua` names a horse halter used to keep the horse tied. The location is specified below.
[c2-l006 >] *ſo na eſtrebaria.*
[c2-l006 note] The horse is held in the stable. This distinguishes the halter's tethering use from the separate bit and bridle entries.
[c2-l007] Fanagayexi. *Certo calçado como çapatos que*
[c2-l007 note] `Fanagayexi` denotes footwear resembling shoes. Its particular occasion of use follows on the next line.
[c2-l008 >] *ſe calção indo à caça.*
[c2-l008 note] The shoes are worn for hunting. That contextual detail is more secure than inferring a modern footwear type from the headword alone.
[c2-l009] Fanague. *Cabelinhos das ventas dos narizes.*
[c2-l009 note] `Fanague` is nose hair, 鼻毛. `gue` supplies hard ゲ; it is not the voiced palatal sequence represented by bare `gi`.
[c2-l010] Fanagi. *Sangue dos narizes. Vt,* Fanagi
[c2-l010 note] `Fanagi` is 鼻血, with historical ヂ. The example repeats the noun before its subject particle on the following line.
[c2-l011 >] ga taru. *Sair, ou correr ſãgue dos narizes.*
[c2-l011 note] `Fanagi ga taru` is blood dripping from the nose. Portuguese describes nasal bleeding, not mucus as in several preceding examples.
[c2-l012] Fanagoye. *Voz fanhoſa.*
[c2-l012 note] `Fanagoye` is a nasal voice. Portuguese `fanhoſa` characterizes the sound rather than naming an anatomical object.
[c2-l013] Fanaguri, l, potiùs, Fanazzuru. *Hũ cir-*
[c2-l013 note] `potiùs` introduces `Fanazzuru` as the preferred alternative to `Fanaguri`. Both refer to the ring described below, not two unrelated entries.
[c2-l014 >] *culo de pao, de era, ou vimes que mete pell-*
[c2-l014 note] The scan reads `de era`, ivy, rather than `de cera`, wax. Wood, ivy, and withies are plausible ring materials; the visible letters govern the correction.
[c2-l015 >] *as ventas dos narizes dos bois em Iapão furã-*
[c2-l015 note] The ring passes through the nostrils of cattle in Japan. The final verb continues below and explains how it is inserted.
[c2-l016 >] *do lhas.*
[c2-l016 note] `furã- / do lhas` describes piercing the nostrils. This completes the practical nose-ring description, not another lexical definition.
[c2-l017] Fanai. *Vt,* Fanai fito. *Homem que logo*
[c2-l017 note] `Fanai fito` describes a person who quickly becomes angry. The adjective's meaning is supplied by the next line, not a literal flower or nose interpretation.
[c2-l018 >] *ſe agaſta.*
[c2-l018 note] `ſe agaſta` completes becoming irritated. The phrase gives a temperament, not a momentary movement of the face.
[c2-l019] Fanaiqe. *Hum vaſo com agoa onde ſe poem*
[c2-l019 note] `Fanaiqe` is a vessel holding water for flowers. The purpose of the water is explained in the continuation.
[c2-l020 >] *flores, ou roſas pera eſtarem freſcas.*
[c2-l020 note] The flowers are kept fresh in it. This confirms the vase sense of 花生け rather than an act of planting in soil.
[c2-l021] Fanaiqi. *Folego que ſae pellos narizes. Vt,*
[c2-l021 note] `Fanaiqi` is breath from the nose, 鼻息. The following example makes it into an action with `suru`.
[c2-l022 >] Fanaiqiuo ſuru. *Reſfolegar pollos narizes.*
[c2-l022 note] `Fanaiqiuo ſuru` means breathing through the nose. The gloss directly identifies the route of breathing.
[c2-l023] Fanaire. *Vaſo em que ſe metem flores como no ra-*
[c2-l023 note] `Fanaire` is a vessel into which flowers are put. The bouquet comparison follows below.
[c2-l024 >] *malhete.*
[c2-l024 note] `ra- / malhete` completes the bouquet comparison. This line belongs to the container definition rather than a separate arranging verb.
[c2-l025] Fanameqi, u, eita. *Andar luſtroſamente veſ-*
[c2-l025 note] `Fanameqi` describes appearing splendidly dressed. The Portuguese continuation adds the festive manner, supporting a showy or flourishing appearance.
[c2-l026 >] *tido, ou de feſta.*
[c2-l026 note] Being dressed as for a celebration completes the sense. The verb is not merely the growth of literal flowers.
[c2-l027] Fanami. *Ver flores por recreação. Vt,* Fa-
[c2-l027 note] `Fanami` is flower-viewing for recreation. The Japanese example begins with `Fa-` and continues below.
[c2-l028 >] namini mairu. *Ir ver as flores, ou roſas.*
[c2-l028 note] `Fanamini mairu` means going to view flowers. The polite movement verb belongs to the example, not the headword itself.
[c2-l029 >] ¶ Fanamigaterani quru fito. *Homem q̃*
[c2-l029 note] `Fanamigaterani quru fito` is someone coming while taking the opportunity to view flowers. `-gatera ni` connects the visit with that accompanying purpose.
[c2-l030 >] *vem com ocaſião de ir ver as flores, ou como*
[c2-l030 note] Portuguese explains coming on the occasion of flower-viewing. It presents a combined motive, not necessarily an exclusive purpose.
[c2-l031 >] *quem vai ver as flores.*
[c2-l031 note] The person comes as one going to see flowers. This completes the explanation of the opportunistic or accompanying visit.
[c2-l032] Fanamuqe. *Dadiua que ſe da ao que vai pera*
[c2-l032 note] `Fanamuqe` is a gift to someone departing. The following line specifies a distant journey rather than an ordinary daily departure.
[c2-l033 >] *longe.*
[c2-l033 note] `longe` completes the journey away. The definition supports the farewell-gift sense of はなむけ without requiring an etymological reconstruction.
[c2-l034] Fananegi. *Aziar com que apertão os beiços às*
[c2-l034 note] The enlarged scan reads `Aziar`, an animal-restraint device, not `Argola`. The lip-compressing action described here fits 鼻捻じ and explains its purpose below.
[c2-l035 >] *beſtas pera ter mão nellas, & as ſujeitar.*
[c2-l035 note] The device is used to control and restrain beasts. This closes the practical definition rather than recommending the procedure.
[c2-l036] Fanano aca. *Sugidade, ou ranho dos narizes.*
[c2-l036 note] `Fanano aca` is dirt or mucus of the nose. Unlike bare `fana`, this compound can name the material, consistent with the warning on f167.
[c2-l037] Fanano ana. *Ventas dos narizes: no* Ximo *ſe*
[c2-l037 note] `Fanano ana` is nostrils. `Ximo` introduces the lower-region alternative, not a word inside the Japanese example.
[c2-l038 >] *diz.* Fanano ſu.
[c2-l038 note] The regional form is `Fanano ſu`. Preserve the vocabulary variant instead of regularizing it to the preceding `ana`.
[c2-l039] Fanano boxi. *Certos veos, ou mantilhas que*
[c2-l039 note] `Fanano boxi` denotes a veil or mantilla-like head covering. Its wearers in the next line define the religious context.
[c2-l040 >] *trazẽ as Freiras de Iapão, & Bõzos na cabeça.*
[c2-l040 note] Japanese nuns and Buddhist monks wear it on the head. This supports interpreting the item as a religious covering, not an ordinary hat inferred from `boxi`.
[c2-l041] Fanano ca. *Cheiro das roſas, ou flores.*
[c2-l041 note] `Fanano ca` is the fragrance of flowers. Here `fana` is floral, unlike the nostril entries surrounding it.
[c2-l042] Fanano ſu. *Ventas dos narizes.* X*.*
[c2-l042 note] `Fanano ſu` repeats the nostril term, with an editorial regional label. It cross-checks the variant given just above.
[c2-l043] Fanaqire vji. *Vide,* Fana.
[c2-l043 note] The cross-reference repeats the questionable `Fanaqire vji` spelling from f167. Expected 牛 would suggest `vxi`, but recurrence is context, not proof that the unusual printed letter is linguistically correct.
[c2-l044] Fanare, uru, eta. *Apartaſe, ou deſpegarſe.*
[c2-l044 note] `Fanare` is separating or becoming detached. Portuguese `Apartaſe` is retained as transcribed rather than expanded to a modern infinitive form.
[c2-l045 >] ¶ Acuuo fanaruru. *Apartarſe dos pec-*
[c2-l045 note] `Acuuo fanaruru` means separating oneself from evil. Its Portuguese translation uses the moral vocabulary of sins.
[c2-l046 >] *cados. ¶* Suuo fanaruru. *Sair o paſſaro*
[c2-l046 note] The sin gloss finishes, then `Suuo fanaruru` describes a bird leaving its nest. This supplies a concrete counterpart to moral separation.
[c2-l047 >] *do ninho. ¶* Fitoni fanaruru. *Apartar*
[c2-l047 note] The bird leaves the nest. `Fitoni fanaruru` next concerns separation from a person; retain the printed particle `ni` rather than modernizing it.
[c2-l048 >] *ſe de alguem.*
[c2-l048 note] `Apartar / ſe de alguem` completes separation from someone. The nearby page-foot catchword is not part of this sentence.
