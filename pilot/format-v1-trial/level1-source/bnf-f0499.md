[c1-l001 >] *¶ Per met.* Ano fitoua temochi varui.
[c1-l001 note] Continues temochi from the preceding page with a figurative use: ano hito wa temochi warui describes someone whose standing or relations with his master are poor. Per met. marks the figurative explanation.
[c1-l002 >] *Aquele homem não anda bem, ou corrente cõ*
[c1-l002 note] The Portuguese says that the person is not on good terms with someone; corrente, rather than OCR cotrente, belongs to the paired expression anda bem, ou corrente. Aquele homem is restored from the two words visible in the scan.
[c1-l003 >] *o ſenhor, ou amo.*
[c1-l003 note] O senhor, ou amo completes the preceding com: the troubled relationship is with the lord or master, rather than a general description of bad conduct.
[c1-l004] Temoto. *Lugar perto das mãos. ¶* Temo-
[c1-l004 note] Temoto denotes the space close to the hands or immediately at hand. Temo- begins the repeated form temoto ni in the martial example below.
[c1-l005 >] toni ſuſumu tçuuamonouo xichi fachi-
[c1-l005 note] Temoto ni susumu tsuwamono wo shichi hachi-ki describes seven or eight mounted warriors advancing close at hand. The mounted-person counter ki begins on the following row.
[c1-l006 >] qi qitte votoita. *Cortou, & derrubou ſete,*
[c1-l006 note] Ki completes the counter; kitte otoita describes cutting down and felling the warriors. The Portuguese past-tense Cortou is visible and corrects OCR Corton; votoita retains the historical s-row i-onbin form rather than modern otoshita.
[c1-l007 >] *ou oito de caualo, q̃ ſe lhe chegarão perto ata-*
[c1-l007 note] The warriors are seven or eight horsemen who came close enough to strike. The printed ata- continues into lho, completing the Portuguese expression a talho de mão.
[c1-l008 >] *lho de mão.*
[c1-l008 note] Lho de mão completes the close-reach expression begun on the previous row. This short continuation belongs to the mounted-combat example, not to the next headword.
[c1-l009] Temuqi. *Modo, ou feição. ¶* Cano iye-
[c1-l009 note] Temuki is the manner, form, or arrangement of something. The example begins kano ie, referring to the particular house whose design is assessed in the next two rows.
[c1-l010 >] no temuqi qini auanu. *O modo, ou traça*
[c1-l010 note] Kano ie no temuki ki ni awanu means that the house’s arrangement does not suit the speaker. The Portuguese modo, ou traça identifies layout or design as the relevant sense.
[c1-l011 >] *daquella caſa não me contenta.*
[c1-l011 note] The house’s design does not please me completes the example’s translation. The printed contenta belongs to the verb contentar and is retained despite weak ink in the middle.
[c1-l012] Temuſai. *Couſa ſuja, ou mallimpa, & com-*
[c1-l012 note] Temusai describes something dirty or ill kept. The printed mallimpa is retained as one word; mal also bears on the following com-poſta in the description of poor arrangement.
[c1-l013 >] *poſta.*
[c1-l013 note] Poſta completes composta across the line break, finishing the description of something badly cleaned and arranged. It is a continuation of Temuſai.
[c1-l014] Ten. *Ceo. ¶ Item, Nos liuros he o meſ-*
[c1-l014 note] Ten is first glossed as heaven or sky. A second, bookish use begins here and is equated with Tentǒ on the next row.
[c1-l015 >] *mo que* Tentǒ. *Ordem, ou reuolução, & go*
[c1-l015 note] Tentǒ names heaven’s order or rule; the Portuguese lists order and revolution before continuing into governo. The damaged initial sequence supports Ordem rather than OCR O dem; the line ends visibly in go without supplying a hyphen.
[c1-l016 >] *uerno do ceo, pollo qual algũs parece q̃entendẽ*
[c1-l016 note] Verno completes governo, the government or ordering of heaven. The explanation then says that some people apparently understand this as referring to a divine governor; abbreviation tildes are retained unexpanded.
[c1-l017 >] *a Deos, ou o gouernador do ceo.*
[c1-l017 note] The previous description concludes with God or the governor of heaven. This reports the dictionary’s interpretation of the literary use of ten, rather than identifying every occurrence of the Japanese word with the Christian God.
[c1-l018] Ten. *Hũ animalinho como doninha, ou mayor.*
[c1-l018 note] This separate ten is the animal name, glossed as a small creature resembling a weasel or a larger one. The visible a restores animalinho from OCR tnimalinho without imposing a precise species identification.
[c1-l019] Ten. *Pontinho, ou riſca, ou maneira de til q̃*
[c1-l019 note] Another ten means a small point, stroke, or tilde-like written mark. The definition concerns annotation of writing and accounts, with several applications below.
[c1-l020 >] *ſe poem nas letras, ou junto dellas, ou nos ro-*
[c1-l020 note] The mark can be placed on letters, beside them, or in lists. Ro- begins the historically spelt plural rois on the next physical row.
[c1-l021 >] *is. ¶* Tenuo vtçu. *Por eſte pontinho nas*
[c1-l021 note] Is completes rois; ten wo utsu is putting this small mark on writing. The Japanese example and its Portuguese explanation continue into letras de Iapão.
[c1-l022 >] *letras de Iapão. ¶* Tenuo caquru. *Por,*
[c1-l022 note] Letras de Iapão completes the first annotation example. Ten wo kakuru begins another use, putting a longer mark alongside text; letras corrects the OCR leiras.
[c1-l023 >] *ou fazer hũa riſca comprida, ao longo das le-*
[c1-l023 note] The second marking action makes a long stroke beside the written characters. The final le- is the printed division of letras, continued below.
[c1-l024 >] *tras da china, ou Iapão porſinal de ſerem boas,*
[c1-l024 note] The stroke alongside Chinese or Japanese writing indicates whether it is good or bad. Porſinal is retained with the close printed joining rather than supplied with a modernized separation.
[c1-l025 >] *ou ruins. ¶ Item, Riſcas que ſe dão em al-*
[c1-l025 note] The mark can show that writing is poor, completing the good-or-bad distinction. A further application concerns strokes drawn through settled accounts or lists.
[c1-l026 >] *gũas contas, ou roes que eſtão ja liquidados.*
[c1-l026 note] Gũas completes algũas. The accounts or lists have already been settled, so the marks register completion or cancellation rather than being pronunciation signs.
[c1-l027 >] *¶ Item, Sinal, ou letra chamada Catacana que*
[c1-l027 note] Ten also denotes an annotation sign or a character called Catacana. Here Catacana is a Japanese script name embedded in a Portuguese explanation, not an independent headword.
[c1-l028 >] *ſe poem ao pè das letras Chinas pera denotar o*
[c1-l028 note] These signs are placed beside Chinese characters to indicate how to read them. The visible ao pè and Chinas correct OCR to pè and Chenas; pè retains its printed accent.
[c1-l029 >] *modo de ler. Vt,* Cono goua reǒtenni yo.
[c1-l029 note] Cono go wa reǒten ni yomu says that the phrase can be read with two sets of reading marks. The Portuguese modo de ler corrects the garbled daler; the weak terminal point after yo is retained, rather than converting it to an inferred hyphen.
[c1-l030 >] mu, l, riǒtengaara. *Eſta ſentença leeſe*
[c1-l030 note] Mu completes yomu. The alternative riǒtengaara is retained diplomatically: the gloss clearly implies “has two reading marks,” for which aru is expected, but the abraded last vowel is not securely distinguishable from the OCR a. The riǒ/reǒ spelling difference is preserved.
[c1-l031 >] *de duas maneiras, ou tem dous ſinais pollos qua-*
[c1-l031 note] The Portuguese explains that the phrase is read in two ways, or has two signs enabling alternative readings. Duas maneiras is supported by the surviving letter parts and corrects OCR duis meneiras; both words are locally abraded.
[c1-l032 >] *es ſe pode ler de hũa maneira, & doutra.*
[c1-l032 note] The two signs permit one reading and another, completing the explanation of alternative annotation. The sentence ends before the separate Tenaga entry below.
[c1-l033] Tenaga. *O ſeruir à meſa leuando as meſas a-*
[c1-l033 note] Tenaga is a table-service duty involving carrying the individual tables. The capital T is visible and corrects OCR Fenaga; a- starts the expression ate on the following row.
[c1-l034 >] *te o caminho ſem entrar com ellas no Zaxiqui.*
[c1-l034 note] The carrier takes the tables as far as the passage and does not enter the zashiki with them. Zaxiqui occurs as a Japanese room-name within the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l035 >] *¶* Tenaga tçucamatçuru. *Fazer este of-*
[c1-l035 note] Tenaga tsukamatsuru means performing this table-carrying duty. The final uru is supported by the visible letter strokes, correcting OCR ura; the gloss’s officio is divided of-/ficio.
[c1-l036 >] *ficio de leuar as meſas.*
[c1-l036 note] Ficio de leuar as mesas completes the account of the attendant’s duty. The line concerns carrying tables, rather than the food or utensils themselves.
[c1-l037] Tenaguſami. *Folguedo, ou brinco de mãos.*
[c1-l037 note] Tenagusami denotes play or amusement with the hands. The entry begins with a T matching Tenaga and Tenami, not the OCR F; the gloss does not establish a more specific pastime.
[c1-l038] Tenami. *Força, ou valentia com q̃ hum joga*
[c1-l038 note] Tenami refers to strength or prowess in wielding weapons and performing feats. Tenami and Força correct the two OCR initial-letter confusions.
[c1-l039 >] *as armas, ou faz façanhas. ¶* Tenamiuo vo-
[c1-l039 note] The prowess is expressed through weapon use or feats. Façanhas is visible despite abrasion, rather than OCR façaxhas; vo- begins oboyeta in the first-person example.
[c1-l040 >] boyeta. *Ia ſei as forças que tendes, ou que*
[c1-l040 note] Tenami wo oboyeta means having learned or experienced the extent of someone’s prowess. Ia ſei, “I now know,” corrects OCR Laſei, and the translation explicitly allows either your strength or my own.
[c1-l041 >] *eu tenho. ¶* Tenamino rodouo mixôzo.
[c1-l041 note] Eu tenho completes the alternative “the strength that I have.” The next example promises to show the extent of one’s prowess. Rodouo is retained because the initial type looks like r despite the expected hodo/fodo; mixôzo has a visible circumflex, not the OCR caron.
[c1-l042 >] *Moſtrar vos ei quanto poßo com meu braço.*
[c1-l042 note] The speaker promises to show how much he can do with his arm. This Portuguese translation supplies the martial meaning of the preceding Japanese example without licensing correction of its anomalous rodouo.
[c1-l043] Tenare, ru, eta. *Trazer ordinariamente al*
[c1-l043 note] Tenare describes habitual handling: carrying or having an object regularly in the hand. Al begins algũa across the physical line break; no absent hyphen is supplied.
[c1-l044 >] *gũa couſa na mão. ¶* Cono monodomo-
[c1-l044 note] Gũa completes algũa. Cono monodomo begins an example about the objects these youngsters habitually handle, with the possessive no on the next row.
[c1-l045 >] no tenare motta aſobi dǒgu. *Brincos que*
[c1-l045 note] No tenare motta asobi dǒgu refers to the playthings habitually held by these youngsters. Dǒgu retains the printed caron, and the Portuguese brincos identifies the objects as toys.
[c1-l046 >] *costumão trazer nas mãos eſtes moços. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l046 note] The youngsters habitually carry these things in their hands. The abraded first word is read costumão from its surviving letters and the habitual-action context; the next Item begins a further sense of tenare.
[c1-l047 >] *Estar manſo, & domesticado algum animal,*
[c1-l047 note] The further sense is an animal being tame and domesticated, continuing into bird and dog examples in column 2. Manſo, domesticado, and animal recover the words obscured in OCR; the st cluster in the damaged domesticado is retained as a provisional short-s reading.

[c2-l001 >] *paſſaro, &c. ¶* Tenareta tori, l, inu.
[c2-l001 note] Continues the tameness sense of tenare from the foot of column 1. Tenareta tori, alternatively inu, gives a tame bird or dog; the Latin l marks the alternative animal.
[c2-l002 >] *Paßaro, ou cão manſo, & domestico.*
[c2-l002 note] The Portuguese explicitly calls the bird or dog tame and domestic. Paßaro preserves the printed ligature, distinct from the two long-s forms in paſſaro above.
[c2-l003] Tenaye. *Aleijão das mãos.*
[c2-l003 note] Tenaye denotes impairment or disability of the hands. The gloss is simply aleijão das mãos, without specifying a particular cause or modern diagnosis.
[c2-l004] Tenba. Dairino vma. *Caualo do Rey.*
[c2-l004 note] Tenba is explained as dairi no uma, the sovereign’s horse. The whole Portuguese Caualo do Rey is italic; the OCR’s isolated roman e within Rey is spurious.
[c2-l005] Tenbachi. *Melius,* Tenbat. *Caſtigo do ceo.*
[c2-l005 note] Tenbachi is redirected by Melius to the preferred form Tenbat, heavenly punishment. The scan supplies the missing u in Melius and the final o in ceo; the editorial Melius is italic.
[c2-l006] Tenben. i, Vtçuri cauaru. *Mudança. Vt,*
[c2-l006 note] Tenben means change, explained by utsuri kawaru. The synonym marker is a printed i followed by a comma; Vt introduces the examples of changing seasons and world.
[c2-l007 >] Xiqino tenben. *Mudança, ou variedade*
[c2-l007 note] Shiki no tenben is the change or variation of the seasons. The example repeats tenben, not OCR fenben, and its Portuguese plural phrase continues below.
[c2-l008 >] *dos 4. tempos. ¶* Xejǒno tenben.
[c2-l008 note] Dos 4. tempos identifies the four seasons, with a visible numeral 4 rather than OCR a. The new example, sejǒ no tenben, concerns change in the world or human affairs.
[c2-l009 >] *Mudança, & variedade do mundo.*
[c2-l009 note] Change and variety of the world completes sejǒ no tenben. The printed variedade corrects the OCR vartedade; the definition moves from seasonal change to worldly vicissitudes.
[c2-l010] Tenbin. *Balança.*
[c2-l010 note] Tenbin is a balance or weighing scale. The one-word Portuguese gloss is complete and does not belong to the preceding change entry.
[c2-l011] Tenbô. Tenguno fô. *Ley, & ceremonia*
[c2-l011 note] Tenbô is explained by tengu no fô, the law or ritual associated with tengu. The dictionary’s Portuguese characterizes it polemically as the devil’s law and ceremony; Ley corrects OCR Lcy.
[c2-l012 >] *do diabo. Vt,* Tenbôuo voconǒ. *Fazer*
[c2-l012 note] Tenbô wo okonǒ illustrates performing those rites. The damaged word before Vt is read diabo, supported by the repeated diabo below; OCR mistook the abraded a for a hyphen.
[c2-l013 >] *eſtas ceremonias, & ſuperſtiçoens do diabo.*
[c2-l013 note] The Portuguese completes fazer: performing these ceremonies and superstitions of the devil. Ceremonias is continuous in the scan and diabo has b rather than OCR h; the wording records the dictionary’s characterization.
[c2-l014] Tenbon nixxǒ. *Demao fazerſe bem. S.*
[c2-l014 note] Tenbon nixxǒ is glossed as becoming good from being bad. Demao retains the printed joining, while fazerſe restores the long s mistaken for f by OCR; the final S is an upright written-usage label.
[c2-l015] Tenbôrin. *Doutrina que Xaca prègou. S.*
[c2-l015 note] Tenbôrin is explained as the doctrine preached by Shaka. Xaca is a Japanese name inside the Portuguese explanation and the final S is an upright usage label; the printed grave in prègou is retained.
[c2-l016] Tenbu. *Hũs como Fotoques como Bixamõ, &c.*
[c2-l016 note] Tenbu denotes beings compared with Fotoques, with Bixamõ given as an example. Both complete Japanese names belong to the Portuguese explanation; the OCR’s isolated roman B is not a separate word or fragment to mark.
[c2-l017] Tenca. Amega xita. *Monarchia, ou impe-*
[c2-l017 note] Tenka is explained as ame ga shita, “under heaven,” and glossed as monarchy or empire. The surviving rounded second letter supports Monarchia, correcting OCR Menarchia.
[c2-l018 >] *rio. ¶* Tencauo voſamuru. *Gouernar*
[c2-l018 note] Rio completes imperio. Tenka wo osamuru means governing the realm; the scan supports voſamuru with a, not OCR voſumuru, and retains the historical verb form.
[c2-l019 >] *o imperio. ¶* Tencauo toru. *Tomar,*
[c2-l019 note] O imperio completes governar. The next expression, tenka wo toru, means taking the realm and begins the explanation of gaining possession of it.
[c2-l020 >] *ou apoderarſe do imperio. ¶* Tencauo xiz-
[c2-l020 note] Apoderarſe do imperio completes the taking-possession gloss. The following shizzumuru example is physically divided xiz-/zumuru; both printed z letters are retained.
[c2-l021 >] zumuru. *Aquietar o imperio. ¶* Ten-
[c2-l021 note] Zumuru completes xizzumuru, quieting or pacifying the realm. Ten- begins another example, whose repeated tenka is divided across the row boundary.
[c2-l022 >] cauo cutçugayeſu. *Reuoluer, & pertur-*
[c2-l022 note] Tenka wo kutsugayesu describes overturning the realm. The Portuguese reuoluer and perturbar express disruption, with pertur- continued below.
[c2-l023 >] *bar o imperio. ¶* Tencauo midaſu. *Per-*
[c2-l023 note] Bar completes perturbar. A separate example, tenka wo midasu, means throwing the realm into disorder; its gloss is again divided per-/turbar.
[c2-l024 >] *turbar o imperio. ¶* Tencauo araſô. *Cõ-*
[c2-l024 note] Tenka wo arasô means contending for the realm. The tall letter is read long ſ, correcting OCR aratô; the visible circumflex and abbreviated Portuguese Cõ- are retained.
[c2-l025 >] *tender ſobre o imperio. ¶* Tencauo xiru.
[c2-l025 note] Tender completes contender, contending over the realm. Tenka wo shiru begins the next expression, where shiru means governing or possessing dominion rather than merely knowing information.
[c2-l026 >] l, Tencauo tanagocoroni niguiru. *Poſ-*
[c2-l026 note] The alternative tenka wo tanagokoro ni nigiru pictures holding the realm in one’s palm. Latin l links the alternatives, and Poſ- starts the Portuguese possuir across the row boundary.
[c2-l027 >] *ſuir, ou ter na mão o imperio, ou monarchia.*
[c2-l027 note] ſuir completes possuir; the translation explains both expressions as possessing or holding the empire or monarchy in the hand. The imagery therefore agrees with the palm-and-grasp Japanese wording.
[c2-l028 >] *¶* Tenca dai ichi. *Couſa vnica, & ſin-*
[c2-l028 note] Tenka dai ichi describes something foremost or unparalleled in the world. The Portuguese gloss calls it unique and singular and continues into the following row.
[c2-l029 >] *gular no mundo. ¶* Tencabuſǒ. *Idem.*
[c2-l029 note] The unique-in-the-world gloss is completed and Tencabuſǒ is given with Idem, the same meaning. The damaged vowel mark in this repeated form retains the supplied caron rather than being normalized from another occurrence.
[c2-l030] Tenca busǒna. l, Tenca dai ichina. *Couſa*
[c2-l030 note] Tenca busǒna and Tenca dai ichina give adjectival forms of the preceding unparalleled expressions. The printed space in Tenca busǒna and the differing s form are preserved.
[c2-l031 >] *vnica, & ſingular no mundo.*
[c2-l031 note] Unique and singular in the world completes the adjectival gloss. The short continuation belongs to both alternatives in the preceding physical row.
[c2-l032] Tencai. Tenno imaxime. *Ley, ou prohi-*
[c2-l032 note] Tencai is explained as ten no imashime, heaven’s commandment or prohibition. The abraded Portuguese word is Ley, with the surviving e trace restoring OCR Ly; prohi- continues below.
[c2-l033 >] *bição de Deos, ou do ceo.*
[c2-l033 note] Bição completes prohibição, a prohibition of God or heaven. The paired Portuguese terms preserve the dictionary’s explanation of tencai without imposing a narrower doctrinal identification.
[c2-l034] Tencan. *Olhos de Deos. ¶* Tencan vata-
[c2-l034 note] Tencan is glossed as the eyes of God. The example begins tencan watakushi nashi, with watakushi divided vata-/cuxi across the physical rows.
[c2-l035 >] cuxinaxi. *Deos tudo vè rectamente, & ſem*
[c2-l035 note] Cuxinaxi completes watakushi nashi, without partiality or private interest. The Portuguese says God sees everything rightly, with the qualification completed on the next row.
[c2-l036 >] *aceptação de peßoas.*
[c2-l036 note] Sem aceptação de peßoas completes the statement of impartial judgment: without favouring persons. The historical aceptação and printed ligature are retained.
[c2-l037] Tencan. i, Cutçuchi. *Gota coral.*
[c2-l037 note] A separate medical tencan is equated with Cutçuchi and glossed by the traditional disease name gota coral, corresponding to epilepsy. This is a homophone entry distinct from the preceding eyes-of-God sense.
[c2-l038] Tenchi. Ame, tçuchi. *Ceo, & terra.*
[c2-l038 note] Tenchi is explained by ame and tsuchi, heaven and earth. The comma separates the paired Japanese nouns rather than marking a new entry.
[c2-l039 >] *¶* Tenchi caifiacu yori conocata. *Deſ-*
[c2-l039 note] Tenchi caifiacu yori konokata describes the period since the opening or beginning of heaven and earth. The historical caifiacu spelling is preserved; Deſ- begins the Portuguese temporal expression below.
[c2-l040 >] *do principio, ou começo do ceo, & da terra a-*
[c2-l040 note] Do principio, ou começo do ceo, & da terra completes the “since the beginning” construction. The concluding a- carries the expression onward to ate agora in the next row.
[c2-l041 >] *tè agora.*
[c2-l041 note] Tè agora completes até agora, up to the present. The grave accent is retained on this short final piece of the heaven-and-earth example.
[c2-l042] Tenchocu. *Mandado, ou recado do Rey.*
[c2-l042 note] Tenchocu is a sovereign’s command or message. The abraded final word retains enough of the y to read Rey, correcting OCR Re.
[c2-l043 >] *¶* Tenchocuni macaxete. *Conforme ao*
[c2-l043 note] Tenchocu ni makasete means following the sovereign’s command. The Portuguese conforme ao is completed by the next physical row rather than ending a separate definition here.
[c2-l044 >] *que o Rey manda. ¶ Item, Mandado, ou or-*
[c2-l044 note] The action conforms to what the sovereign orders. A further sense then begins: a command or ordinance of God. Rey restores the damaged e omitted in OCR Ry.
[c2-l045 >] *denação de Deos.*
[c2-l045 note] Denação completes ordenação, finishing the divine-command sense. This is the end of Tenchocu and is separate from the two Tenchǔ entries below.
[c2-l046] Tenchǔ. *i, Paços do Cubǒ, ou Yacata.*
[c2-l046 note] This Tenchǔ means the palace of the Cubǒ or Yacata. Cubǒ and Yacata are Japanese titles inside Portuguese prose; the scan’s mark over Cubǒ is caron-shaped, not the OCR tilde, and is preserved despite the expected conventional Kubô reading.
[c2-l047] Tenchǔ. *Seruiços feitos a Deos, ou ao Rey.*
[c2-l047 note] A separate Tenchǔ denotes services rendered to God or to the sovereign. The entire Portuguese gloss, including Rey, is italic; the OCR’s roman ey fragment was not an embedded Japanese form.
