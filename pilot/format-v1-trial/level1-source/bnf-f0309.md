[c1-l001 >] *& fechão os olhos. i, Em breuißimo eſpaço.*
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding page's comparison with closing the eyes: the explanatory i introduces an extremely brief interval. The row begins mid-definition, not with a new headword.
[c1-l002] Mataua. *Ou, l, & tambem, & mais iſto, &c.*
[c1-l002 note] Matawa is explained both as an alternative, 'or', and additively, 'also' or 'and this too'. Preserve the range of conjunctions given by the Portuguese rather than restricting it to its usual modern sense.
[c1-l003] Matçu. *Pinheiro.*
[c1-l003 note] Matsu is a pine tree; the one-word Portuguese gloss is complete. The cedilla belongs to the historical tç spelling.
[c1-l004] Matçuba. *Folhas de pinheiro.*
[c1-l004 note] Matsuba denotes pine leaves or needles, literally the leaves of the tree named in the preceding entry.
[c1-l005] Matçubara. *Pinheiral.*
[c1-l005 note] Matsubara is a stand or grove of pines, matched by the collective Portuguese pinheiral.
[c1-l006] Matçubune, l, inocozzuchi. *Erua aßi cha-*
[c1-l006 note] Matsubune is equated with inokozuchi through the alternative marker l. The gloss merely calls it an herb of that name; it does not by itself establish a modern botanical species.
[c1-l007 >] *mada.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes cha-mada, 'called', in the preceding plant definition. This is a genuine short continuation, not an omitted entry.
[c1-l008] Matçucaje. *Vento que dà nos pinheiros.*
[c1-l008 note] Matsukaze is wind blowing against or through pines. Caje is the dictionary's historical spelling of kaze, not an anomalous personal name.
[c1-l009] Matçudai. l, Matdai. i, Suyeno yo. *Idade*
[c1-l009 note] The paired forms denote the last age or later generations, explained by sue no yo. Renewed enlargement confirms a descending cedilla on the c of Matçudai, agreeing with the marked spelling in the example below.
[c1-l010 >] *derradeira, ou couſa perpetua. ¶* Matçu-
[c1-l010 note] Completes idade derradeira and adds perpetuity as a second interpretation. Matçu- begins the example whose sense is preserving a name for posterity.
[c1-l011 >] daini nauo todomeyoto nanotte, *&c.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes Matsudai ni na wo todomeyo to nanotte: declaring one's name so that it will remain for later generations. Keep the printed spacing and the line break inside Matçudai.
[c1-l012 >] *Nomeandoſe que queria deixar nome pera os*
[c1-l012 note] The Portuguese begins 'naming himself, wishing to leave a name for ...'. The scan supports Nomeandoſe, not OCR Nomcandoſe; the intended posterity appears on the following row.
[c1-l013 >] *vindouros, ou pera ſempre.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the translation with future generations, or forever. The rounded vowel supports vindouros rather than the OCR's vindauros.
[c1-l014] Matçudaqe. *Hũa laya de cugumelos.*
[c1-l014 note] Matsudake is defined simply as a kind of mushroom. Preserve the historical voiced spelling daqe and Portuguese cugumelos rather than replacing them with modern forms.
[c1-l015] Matçugue. *Peſtanas dos olhos.*
[c1-l015 note] Matsuge means eyelashes, directly matched by pestanas dos olhos. The gue sequence represents the hard g of the Japanese word.
[c1-l016] Matçume, uru, eta. *Ajuntar couſas eſpa-*
[c1-l016 note] Matsume, with the stated uru and eta principal parts, means gathering things together. The gloss continues with things scattered or out of order, so the historical form should not be replaced by modern matomeru merely for familiarity.
[c1-l017 >] *lhadas, ou ſem ordem. ¶* Mimatçume-
[c1-l017 note] Completes espalhadas, 'scattered', and adds 'without order'. Mimatçume- begins the nominal example of gathering or arranging, continued by the object particle below.
[c1-l018 >] uo ſuru. *Idem.*
[c1-l018 note] The preceding Mimatçume joins uo suru, 'perform the gathering/arranging'. Idem explicitly gives it the same meaning as the headword; uo is not part of the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l019] Matçumuxi. *Hum genero de bicho que can-*
[c1-l019 note] Matsumushi is introduced as a kind of insect that sings. The definition continues below and should not be forced into a precise modern species identification.
[c1-l020 >] *ta como grilo. ¶ Item, Hum bichinho*
[c1-l020 note] Completes canta como grilo, 'sings like a cricket', then begins an additional small-insect sense. The second description continues on the next row.
[c1-l021 >] *que ſe cria nos pinheiros, & canta como grillo*
[c1-l021 note] The additional insect breeds or lives among pines and sings like a cricket. The scan supports grillo, including r and two l types, rather than OCR gillo; the earlier grilo remains singly spelled.
[c1-l022 >] *ſaudoſamente.*
[c1-l022 note] Saudosamente qualifies the insect's song as plaintive or wistful. It completes the preceding sentence rather than beginning a new botanical entry.
[c1-l023] Matçunoqi. *Pinheiro.*
[c1-l023 note] Matsu no ki is another expression for a pine tree, repeating the meaning of Matçu with the explicit word for tree.
[c1-l024] Matçuno to. *P. Porta feita toſcamente de ra-*
[c1-l024 note] Matsu no to is a roughly made pine-branch door. Initial P. marks poetic usage; the Portuguese explanation divides ramos across the line boundary.
[c1-l025 >] *mos de pinheiros, &c.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes ra-mos, specifying branches of pine as the door's material. The short row closes the poetic expression above.
[c1-l026] Matçuri. *Feſta, ou ceremonias que ſe fazem,*
[c1-l026 note] Matsuri is a festival or ceremonies performed for the kami named below. The terminal curved mark is a comma, comparable to the comma after ceremonias two rows below, not a word-division hyphen; the sentence continues aos Camis.
[c1-l027 >] *aos Camis.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes the definition: ceremonies offered to the kami. Camis is the Japanese religious term with the gloss's plural ending, embedded in the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l028] Matçuri, u, utta. *Fazer feſta, ceremonias,*
[c1-l028 note] The verb matsuri denotes holding festivals or rites. Preserve the printed abbreviated principal part u, without silently expanding it to an expected ru.
[c1-l029 >] *ſacrificios, & de precaçoens aos Camis. ¶* I-
[c1-l029 note] The rites include sacrifices and supplications to the kami. The spaced de precaçoens is retained; I- begins ikusagami, the war deity, in the following example.
[c1-l030 >] cuſagamini chiuo matçuru. *Offerecer, ou*
[c1-l030 note] Completes ikusagami ni chi wo matsuru, offering blood to the war deity. The Portuguese begins the equivalent 'offer, or ...', continued by sacrificar below.
[c1-l031 >] *ſacrificar ao Cami da guerra oprimeiro ſangue q̃*
[c1-l031 note] The offering is the first enemy blood shed, sacrificed to the deity of war. The scan has guerra with two r types; preserve the printed oprimeiro spacing and the unexpanded abbreviation q̃.
[c1-l032 >] *hum derrama dos inimigos. ¶* Tenguuo
[c1-l032 note] Completes the first-blood translation and begins a separate example with tengu as object. This Japanese example is distinct from a Japanese term embedded inside Portuguese prose.
[c1-l033 >] matçuri tçuquru. *Fazer entrar ao demo-*
[c1-l033 note] Completes tengu wo matsuri tsukuru, explained as ritually causing a spirit to enter someone. The Portuguese calls the spirit a demon; that is the dictionary's Christian framing, not an independent identification supplied by the reviewer.
[c1-l034 >] *nio no corpo dalguem com ceremonias, ou*
[c1-l034 note] Completes demo-nio and specifies entry into someone's body through ceremonies. The means of invocation continues with supplications on the next row.
[c1-l035 >] *deprecações, &c.*
[c1-l035 note] Supplications complete the description of the invocation. The printed spelling here is not automatically assimilated to the spaced de precaçoens above.
[c1-l036] Matçurigoto. *Gouerno, ou ordenaçoẽs. ¶* Ma-
[c1-l036 note] Matsurigoto means government or ordinances. The initial of Gouerno is a damaged G, not OCR C; the Japanese example starts with divided Ma-.
[c1-l037 >] tçurigotouo yaburu. *Quebrar as ordena-*
[c1-l037 note] Completes matsurigoto wo yaburu, breaking or violating ordinances. The Portuguese equivalent continues across ordena-coẽs on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] *coẽs do Reino.*
[c1-l038 note] Completes the phrase 'the ordinances of the kingdom'. Preserve the visible uncedilled c and the tilde on e, rather than silently regularizing the Portuguese ending.
[c1-l039] Matçuuari, ru, atta. *Emburulharſe, ou en-*
[c1-l039 note] Matsuwari is becoming entangled or entwined. The ru/atta principal parts belong to the listed verb; the metaphorical applications follow the physical comparison.
[c1-l040 >] *laçarſe como era naruore. &c. ¶ Per-*
[c1-l040 note] Completes enlaçar-se with ivy entwining a tree. Era is the historical spelling for ivy and naruore represents the tree phrase as printed; Per- begins the figurative-use label on the following row.
[c1-l041 >] *met.* Matçuuatta fito. *Homem de emba-*
[c1-l041 note] Met. completes the wholly italic Per-met. figurative-use marker. Matsuwatta hito describes an entangling or troublesome person; the Portuguese continues with embarrassments and tricks.
[c1-l042 >] *raços, & trapaças. ¶* Matçuuatta co-
[c1-l042 note] Completes embaraços and adds trapaças, 'tricks' or 'frauds'. The next example begins a description of tangled or misleading statements.
[c1-l043 >] touo yǔ. *Dizer couſas que não ſe entendẽ,*
[c1-l043 note] Completes matsuwatta koto wo yuu, saying things that cannot be understood. The damaged final Japanese form is yǔ, with a visible caron, rather than the OCR's yfi; the Portuguese identifies it as the verb 'say'.
[c1-l044 >] *ou impor a alguem o que não he, &c.*
[c1-l044 note] Adds the alternative of attributing something untrue to another person. This completes the misleading-speech example rather than the next pine-related noun.
[c1-l045] Matçuyama. *Mato de pinheiros.*
[c1-l045 note] Matsuyama is glossed as a pine wood or thicket. Although yama can suggest a mountain, the Portuguese here specifies vegetation, not a place name.
[c1-l046] Matçuyani. *Reſina de pinheiro, ou breu.*
[c1-l046 note] Matsuyani is pine resin or pitch. The headword's damaged vowel is compatible with yani; there is no basis for replacing the historical spelling with a modernized form.
[c1-l047] Matdai. Suyenoyo. *Derradeira idade, ou pe-*
[c1-l047 note] Matdai repeats the later-age or perpetual sense, explained by sue no yo. The scan supports t in Matdai and the separate words Derradeira idade; the Portuguese phrase continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *ra ſempre. Vt,* Fitoua ichidai, naua mat-
[c2-l001 note] Completes pe-ra sempre, 'forever', then begins the saying 'a person lasts one lifetime; a name ...'. The divided mat- belongs to matdai on the next row.
[c2-l002 >] daizo. Yax. *O homẽ dura hũa idade, o no-*
[c2-l002 note] Completes the saying 'a person has one lifetime, a name endures for later ages'. Yax. is the upright printed citation label; the Portuguese contrasts a human lifespan with the persistence of a name.
[c2-l003 >] *me fica pera ſempre.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes no-me, stating that the name remains forever. This closes the cross-column Matdai entry.
[c2-l004] Mate. *Hum genero de mariſco.* Mategai.
[c2-l004 note] Mate is a kind of shellfish, also called mategai. The alternative Japanese name belongs to the same definition and remains upright.
[c2-l005 >] *Idem.*
[c2-l005 note] Idem explicitly assigns Mategai the same shellfish meaning. It is a short continuation of the preceding row, not an additional missing headword.
[c2-l006] Matgo. *Hora da morte. ¶* Matgono ic-
[c2-l006 note] Matgo denotes the hour of death. The example begins matgo no iccu, a deathbed utterance; the scan supports ic-, not the OCR's ie-, with the doubled consonant completed below.
[c2-l007 >] cu. *Sentença, ou outras palauras que hum*
[c2-l007 note] Cu completes iccu, a verse or utterance. The Portuguese describes a sentence or other words spoken by someone, with the deathbed circumstance supplied on the next row.
[c2-l008 >] *diz na hora da morte.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes the definition of the deathbed utterance: words said at the hour of death. It belongs to Matgono iccu rather than the following temple entry.
[c2-l009] Matji. Suyeno tera. Tera, *ou varela ſufra-*
[c2-l009 note] Matsuji is a dependent temple, explained by sue no tera. Tera within the Portuguese definition is visibly upright as a whole word; the former mixed T/era styling did not match the printed type.
[c2-l010 >] *ganea a outra principal como fregueſia em com-*
[c2-l010 note] Completes sufraganea, describing a temple subordinate to a principal one. The missionary analogy compares that relationship with a parish and its cathedral.
[c2-l011 >] *paração da ſee.*
[c2-l011 note] Completes comparação da see, the cathedral comparison. The scan supports two e types in the historical see, not OCR sce; no accent is supplied from modern sé.
[c2-l012] Matji. Suyeno toqi. *Derradeira hora.*
[c2-l012 note] This separate homophonous Matji is explained by sue no toki, the last time or hour. It must not be merged with the preceding dependent-temple entry.
[c2-l013] Mato. *Aluo que ſe poem na barreira. ¶* Ma-
[c2-l013 note] Mato is the target set up at a shooting range. Ma- begins the example of shooting at that target.
[c2-l014 >] touo iru. *Atirar a o aluo. ¶* Matoni
[c2-l014 note] Completes mato wo iru, to shoot at the target, and starts mato ni ataru below. Preserve the visibly separated a o in the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l015 >] ataru. *Acertarno aluo. ¶* Matouo ifaz-
[c2-l015 note] Mato ni ataru means hitting the target. The next example, mato wo ihazusu, contrasts missing it; the scan retains the close Acertarno word spacing and the divided Japanese spelling.
[c2-l016 >] zuſu. *Errar o aluo.*
[c2-l016 note] Completes ifaz-zuſu, missing the target. The long s is visible in the final syllable; the two z letters straddle the physical line break.
[c2-l017] Matoba. *Lugar onde atirão à barreira.*
[c2-l017 note] Matoba is the place where people shoot at targets, an archery or shooting range. This names the location rather than another kind of target.
[c2-l018] Matocana. *Couſa redonda, & ſolida como glo-*
[c2-l018 note] Matocana describes a rounded, solid thing, compared with a globe. The following example extends solidity or wholeness to a person's character.
[c2-l019 >] *bo, &c. ¶* Matocana fito. *Homem*
[c2-l019 note] Completes glo-bo and introduces matocana hito, a person characterized by solidity or integrity. The Portuguese noun homem continues with its qualifications below.
[c2-l020 >] *ſolido, & inteiro.*
[c2-l020 note] The person is 'solid and whole', here expressing dependable integrity. The two adjectives complete the human example rather than the preceding physical comparison alone.
[c2-l021] Matocani. *Adu.*
[c2-l021 note] Matocani is identified as the adverb corresponding to Matocana. Adu. supplies the grammatical relationship even though no separate Portuguese paraphrase follows.
[c2-l022] Matǒdo. *Homem ſimples, & recto.*
[c2-l022 note] Matǒdo is a simple, upright person. The caron is visible; the Portuguese includes the i of ſimples, lost in OCR ſmples.
[c2-l023] Matoi, ô, ôta. *Emburulhar, ou enuoluer.*
[c2-l023 note] Matoi means wrapping or enveloping. Both abbreviated long-vowel forms have circumflexes, in contrast with the caron of Matǒdo immediately above.
[c2-l024 >] *¶ Item, Veſtir. Vt,* Tçuri ſuru ama-
[c2-l024 note] The added sense is putting on clothing. The t is present in Veſtir; Tçuri ſuru ama- begins an example involving people fishing.
[c2-l025 >] bitoni maguirete yaburetaru minouo mi-
[c2-l025 note] Completes amabito ni magirete, mingling or disguising oneself among fishermen, and begins wearing a torn straw cloak on one's body. The final mi- continues as mini below.
[c2-l026 >] ni matoi, &c. Taif. *Lib. 29. Diſ-*
[c2-l026 note] Completes mi ni matoi, putting the cloak on. Taif. is the upright source label followed by italic Lib. 29; the Portuguese translation begins the divided Disfraçado.
[c2-l027 >] *fraçado com os peſcadores viſtio hũa capa de pa*
[c2-l027 note] The translation describes disguising oneself among fishermen and putting on a straw cloak. Preserve the printed Dis-fraçado order and the unhyphenated pa at the physical edge rather than supplying an expected line-end mark.
[c2-l028 >] *lha rota.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes pa-lha rota, a torn straw cloak. The absence of a printed hyphen on the preceding row does not alter the continuity of the word.
[c2-l029] Matouari, u, atta. *Emburulharſe, ou trauar-*
[c2-l029 note] Matowari denotes becoming wrapped or entangled. The Portuguese continues the reflexive trauar-se on the next physical line.
[c2-l030 >] *ſe hũa couſa cõ outra. Vide,* Matçuuari, u.
[c2-l030 note] Completes the sense of one thing becoming entangled with another and refers the reader to Matçuuari. Preserve the abbreviated cõ and the cross-reference's printed principal part u.
[c2-l031] Matouono. l, madouono coromo. Meno a-
[c2-l031 note] Matouono and madouono coromo name the garment explained by me no arai nuno, cloth with a coarse or open weave. Preserve the different t/d spellings in the paired forms.
[c2-l032 >] rai nuno. *Veſtido de linho canemo großo,*
[c2-l032 note] Completes me no arai nuno and gives the material as coarse linen or hemp cloth. The historical canemo and the printed double-s sort in großo are retained.
[c2-l033 >] *& ralo. P.*
[c2-l033 note] Ralo completes the coarse, loosely woven garment description. The closing P. marks poetic usage, not another Japanese word.
[c2-l034] Matoya. *Frecha com que ſe atira à barreira.*
[c2-l034 note] Matoya is an arrow used for target shooting. Preserve the historical Portuguese Frecha rather than modernizing it to flecha.
[c2-l035] Mattacu. *Adu. Certamente, ou totalmen-*
[c2-l035 note] Mattaku means certainly or completely. The initial t of totalmen- is abraded but has compatible surviving ink; the OCR's eotalmen- does not represent the printed word.
[c2-l036 >] *te, & perfeitamente.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes totalmente and adds perfeitamente, 'perfectly'. These are the adverbial meanings of Mattacu, not a fresh headword.
[c2-l037] Mattai. *Couſa inteira. ¶* Qizzu tçucanu
[c2-l037 note] Mattai describes something whole or intact. Kizu tsukanu begins the example of a pear without damage, continued below.
[c2-l038 >] mattai naxiua fitotçumo nai. *Nem hũa*
[c2-l038 note] Completes the negative example: there is not even one whole, unblemished pear. Naxi is the pear noun here, distinct from the final negative nai.
[c2-l039 >] *ſo pera inteira ſem lesão ficou.*
[c2-l039 note] The translation confirms that not a single intact, undamaged pear remained. The apparently repetitive inteira and sem lesão both belong to the printed explanation.
[c2-l040] Mattô. B. Atama. *Cabeça. ¶* Acu-
[c2-l040 note] Mattô is equated with atama, 'head', with the upright B. usage label. The entire label is roman; Acu- begins the example about dirty water.
[c2-l041 >] ſui mattôni ſoſoqu. *Botar agoa ſuja ſobre*
[c2-l041 note] Completes akusui mattō ni sosogu, pouring dirty water over the head. Both long-s types in ſoſoqu are retained and the translation continues below.
[c2-l042 >] *a cabeça.*
[c2-l042 note] Completes sobre a cabeça, 'over the head'. This closes the example of Mattô as a noun.
[c2-l043] Mattô. *Adu. Inteiramente.*
[c2-l043 note] A separate Mattô is the adverb 'entirely'. The repeated spelling does not license merging it with the preceding head noun.
[c2-l044] Matto. *Adu. Hum pouco mais.*
[c2-l044 note] Unaccented Matto means 'a little more'. The contrast with Mattô above is visibly printed and semantically meaningful.
[c2-l045] Mat yô. *Deſendentes. Meliùs,* Bat yô.
[c2-l045 note] Mat yô means descendants, with Bat yô recommended as the preferable form. Preserve the source's Deſendentes without inserting a c and the visible grave accent in Meliùs.
[c2-l046] Matza. *Melius,* Batza. Suyeno za. *Der-*
[c2-l046 note] Matza, preferably Batza, is explained by sue no za, the last or lowest seat. The damaged label supports Melius rather than OCR Mecius; its accent area is too abraded to add a grave securely, so the unaccented baseline is retained.
[c2-l047 >] *radeiro aßento do Zaxiqui.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes derradeiro assento, the lowest seat in the zashiki or formal room. Zaxiqui occurs within the Portuguese explanation and is retained in its observed typeface for optional human whole-word review.
