[c1-l001] Vanare, *meliùs,* Vuanari. *Manceba de ho-*
[c1-l001 note] Vanare names a married man's mistress; melius recommends the form Vuanari. The latter is a Japanese alternative in upright type, and ho- continues as homem below.
[c1-l002 >] *mem caſado. Palaura de que vſa algũa gente*
[c1-l002 note] Completes homem casado, a married man, and begins the usage qualification. The tilde is over the u of algũa; the word is not expanded or modernized.
[c1-l003 >] *baixa de Ximo: a propria palaura he Vuanari.*
[c1-l003 note] The entry attributes Vanare to low-status usage in Ximo and calls Vuanari the proper expression. Both Ximo and the final Vuanari occur within the italic Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l004] Vanbaco. *Caixa em q̃ metem eſcudelas de arroz.*
[c1-l004 note] Wanbako is a box holding bowls of rice. The abbreviation q̃ is visible over q and remains unexpanded, while eſcudelas denotes the bowls rather than loose rice.
[c1-l005] Vancot. Vdebone. *Oßo dos braços.*
[c1-l005 note] Wankot is equated with udebone, the arm bone. The checked final t in Vancot belongs to the historical Sino-Japanese form and is not expanded to a modern syllable.
[c1-l006] Vani. *Peixe aßi chamado que dizẽ come gente.*
[c1-l006 note] Wani is described as a fish said to eat people. This wording is preserved without imposing a modern zoological identification; the abbreviated dizẽ means 'they say'.
[c1-l007] Vaniaxi. *Pes zambros.*
[c1-l007 note] Waniashi denotes crooked or splayed feet, matched by Portuguese pes zambros. No accent is supplied to Pes where none is established in the print.
[c1-l008] Vaniguchi. *Sino pequeno que tangem diante*
[c1-l008 note] Waniguchi is described as a small bell rung before sacred figures. The sentence continues on the next physical line with the objects before which it is sounded.
[c1-l009 >] *dos Camis, & Fotoques.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes the bell's setting: before the kami and Buddhist images. Camis and Fotoques are Japanese religious terms embedded in the Portuguese explanation, with the printed plural endings preserved.
[c1-l010] Vanrei. Reiuo cayeſu. l, Fenreiuo ſuru. *Tor-*
[c1-l010 note] Wanrei means returning a courtesy, explained by rei wo kaesu or henrei wo suru. Tor- begins the Portuguese tornar on the next row; the alternatives remain separate printed expressions.
[c1-l011 >] *nar a viſitar com algum preſente, ou retorno.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes tornar a visitar: paying a return visit with a present or reciprocal offering. This is the social-return sense of wanrei, not simply turning around.
[c1-l012] Vãriqi. Cainano chicara. *Forças. ¶* Deos no
[c1-l012 note] Wanriki is arm strength, paraphrased as kaina no chikara. The small damaged mark over a is retained as an abbreviation tilde in Vãriqi; the following govanriqi also supports the nasal reading. NINJAL's Entry Words Data of Nippojisho, version 202510 (Ohshima and Aida, CC BY 4.0; https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/textdb_dataset/en/nipp/index.html), corroborates the lexical identification after scan inspection. The worn pilcrow introduces the example Deos no govanriqi.
[c1-l013 >] govanriqi. *Forças, & poder de Deos.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes Deos no gowanriki, here God's strength and power. Deos is used within the Japanese example, and the respectful go- form supplies the unabbreviated vanriqi sequence.
[c1-l014] Vappa. *Moço do ſeruiço.*
[c1-l014 note] Wappa is a servant boy or young attendant. The short Portuguese definition identifies the service role without requiring a modern slang interpretation.
[c1-l015] Vappato. *Adu. Modode dar grita, ou fazer*
[c1-l015 note] Wappato is introduced as an adverb describing a shout or noisy outburst. The printed Modode is kept together; the second coordinated action continues below.
[c1-l016 >] *traquinada, &c. Vt,* Vappato vamequ.
[c1-l016 note] Traquinada completes the noisy-commotion gloss; wappato wameku illustrates shouting or clamouring. The example retains the initial v spelling of wameku.
[c1-l017] Vaqe. Amaru iy. *Arroz que ſobeja. ¶ I-*
[c1-l017 note] Wake is leftover rice, explained by amaru ii. The terminal I- begins Portuguese Item below and belongs to the italic explanatory text, not to the Japanese headword.
[c1-l018 >] *tem, Outra qualquer couſa de comer q̃ ſobeja.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes Item and broadens the sense to any leftover food. The abbreviated q̃ is retained exactly rather than expanded to que.
[c1-l019] Vaqe, ru, eta. *Diuidir, ou apartar. Vt,*
[c1-l019 note] Wake, wakuru, waketa is the verb 'divide' or 'separate'. The printed abbreviated principal parts are preserved, and Vt introduces the group-dividing example below.
[c1-l020 >] Ninjuuo vaquru. *Diuidir a gente. ¶ Cõ-*
[c1-l020 note] Ninjuu wo wakuru means dividing the people into groups. The Portuguese prints a space in a gente; Cõ- begins the following grammatical observation across the line break.
[c1-l021 >] *munmente ſe ajunta eſte verbo às raizes de al-*
[c1-l021 note] The note says this verb is commonly attached to the stems of other verbs. The scan supports raizes, with an i, rather than the OCR rarzes; the final al- continues into alguns.
[c1-l022 >] *guns outros. Vt,* Mǒxi vaquru. *Dizer*
[c1-l022 note] Completes alguns outros and supplies mooshi wakuru, to explain with distinctions. The initial letter of vaquru is v, matching the preceding verb and the visible type, rather than OCR y.
[c1-l023 >] *as couſas com diſtinção, & clareza. ¶* Qi-
[c1-l023 note] The translation specifies speaking with distinction and clarity. Qi- begins the next Japanese compound, qiqivaquru, divided at the physical line end.
[c1-l024 >] qivaquru. *Ouuir, & perceber, ou diſcernir.*
[c1-l024 note] With Qi- above, this is kikiwakuru: hearing and understanding or distinguishing what is heard. The divided Japanese word remains on its two physical rows.
[c1-l025] Vaqe. *Diuiſão, ou ſeparação. Vt,* Vaqemo-
[c1-l025 note] The noun wake is division or separation. Vaqemo- begins wake mo nai, the following expression about speech whose distinctions or meaning cannot be understood.
[c1-l026 >] nai cotouo yǔ. *Dizer couſas que ſe não*
[c1-l026 note] Completes wake mo nai koto wo yuu, saying things without an intelligible distinction or sense. The caron on yǔ is visible and is retained.
[c1-l027 >] *entendem, ou diſtinguem bem. ¶* Vaqega
[c1-l027 note] Completes the explanation as things not well understood or distinguished. A new example begins wake ga and continues with contrasting negative and affirmative predicates below.
[c1-l028 >] qicoyenu. l, Qicoyeta. *Não ſe entender*
[c1-l028 note] Wake ga kikoyenu and kikoyeta contrast not being understood with having become clear. The capital Q has a broad swash but belongs to Qicoyeta; the OCR inserted an internal word space after it.
[c1-l029 >] *bẽ a couſa, ou terſe bem entẽdida, & diſtincta.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes the two interpretations: the matter is not well understood, or is understood distinctly. The scan retains the abbreviation bẽ and separates bem entẽdida; neither form is modernized.
[c1-l030] Vaqebun. *Quinhão, ou porção de qualquer couſa.*
[c1-l030 note] Wakebun is a share or portion of something. The paired Portuguese nouns specify the allotted-part sense rather than an abstract reason.
[c1-l031] Vaqecuchi. *O que pertence acada hum na repar-*
[c1-l031 note] Wakekuchi is the amount belonging to each person in a distribution. The close printed acada remains joined, and repar- continues as repartição below.
[c1-l032 >] *tição de algũa couſa como deſpojos, prata do*
[c1-l032 note] Completes repartição and gives spoil and money from a game as examples of distributed property. Prata do continues into jogo on the next row.
[c1-l033 >] *jogo, &c. ¶* Vaqecuchiuo toru. *To-*
[c1-l033 note] Jogo completes the example of game money. Wakekuchi wo toru introduces taking one's share, and To- continues as tomar on the following row.
[c1-l034 >] *mar cada hum ſua parte ſeparada, ou repar-*
[c1-l034 note] Completes tomar: each person takes a separate part, or one distributed by someone else. Repar- continues into repartida rather than beginning a new entry.
[c1-l035 >] *tida por outro.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes repartida por outro, allocated by another person. The brief continuation remains an independent physical row.
[c1-l036] Vaqedori. *Quinhão que cada hum toma porſi.*
[c1-l036 note] Wakedori is the share each person takes for himself. The abraded middle of the headword is confirmed by the repeated Vaqedoriuo on the next row.
[c1-l037 >] *¶* Vaqedoriuo ſuru. *Repartir, ou tomar*
[c1-l037 note] Wakedori wo suru is dividing shares or each taking his own share. The Portuguese coordination continues below and belongs with this example.
[c1-l038 >] *cada hum porſi ſeu quinhão.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*eruas.*
[c1-l038 note] Completes taking one's own share; enlargement supports quinhão rather than OCR quinbão. At the far right, the isolated parenthesis and eruas are a displaced completion of the next entry's matos, ou. Their physical position on this row is preserved rather than moved into Vaqeide.
[c1-l039] Vaqeide, uru, eta. *Sair como dãtre matos, ou*
[c1-l039 note] Wakeide means emerging from among thickets or herbs. Eruas is printed at the far right of the preceding physical row; the text is retained there with an explicit placement marker. The abbreviation dãtre is not expanded.
[c1-l040] Vaqeiri, ru, itta. *Entrar, ou romper por en-*
[c1-l040 note] Wakeiri means entering or forcing a way through something. The final en- continues as entre, specifying what one passes through below.
[c1-l041 >] *tre gente, matos, &c. ¶* Teqino naca-
[c1-l041 note] Completes entre gente, matos: through people, thickets, and similar obstacles. Teki no naka- begins the example of breaking through the midst of enemies.
[c1-l042 >] ye vaqeiru. *Rõper pollo meyo dos inimigos.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes teki no naka ye wakeiru, forcing a way into the enemy's midst. The Portuguese Rõper retains its printed abbreviation tilde and historical pollo meyo spelling.
[c1-l043] Vaqemayoi, ô, ôta. *Andar por montes, ou*
[c1-l043 note] Wakemayoi describes wandering astray through mountains or difficult routes. The two abbreviated forms visibly use circumflex o, not a caron.
[c1-l044 >] *runs caminhos errado.*
[c1-l044 note] Completes the definition as wandering lost along bad roads. The scan shows the anomalous four-letter runs, with no secure i or tilde; the clear ruins on c2-l001 supplies a same-page comparison. The missing vowel is not silently supplied.
[c1-l045] Vaqeme. *Eſtremadura da couſa que ſe parte,*
[c1-l045 note] Wakeme is the dividing boundary or parting in something. Eſtremadura is one printed word, not the OCR's Eſtrema dura; the following hair example clarifies the sense.
[c1-l046 >] *ou diuide, como dos cabellos que eſtão diuididos*
[c1-l046 note] The gloss continues with a thing divided, such as parted hair. The scan supports diuide, with u, rather than OCR dicide, while retaining the historical u/v spelling.
[c1-l047 >] *em duas partes, &c.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes the example as hair separated into two parts. This is the final body row of column 1, with the lower rule visible beneath it.
