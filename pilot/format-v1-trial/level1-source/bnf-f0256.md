[c1-l007 note] Juubako is a box divided into three or more sections. The printed abbreviation ẽ and the division pe- / ças are retained.

[c1-l016 >] *te dalguem.*

[c1-l017] Giǔcocu. Cunini ſumu. *O estar de aßento*

[c1-l019] Giǔdai. Caſanuru yo. *Muitas idades. Vt,*

[c1-l022] Giǔden. *Couſa repetida por eſcrito, ou eſcri-*

[c1-l024] Giǔgi. *Superior do moeſteiro, ou varela.*

[c1-l027] Giǔgiǔ. Caſanegaſane. *Hũa vez, & ou-*

[c1-l030] Giǔ-i. Curaiuo caſanuru. *Dignidade, ou*

[c2-l001] Giǔſô. Sumu fijiri. *Bonzos que reſidem em*
[c2-l001 note] Juusoo names resident Buddhist monks, paraphrased as sumu hijiri. The scan supports the worn Giǔſô, not OCR Giiſô; the gloss continues with their place of residence.
[c2-l002 >] *algum moeſteiro.*

[c2-l003] Giǔtacu. Sumu iye. *Habitar, ou morar da-*
[c2-l003 note] Juutaku concerns inhabiting a house or dwelling, paraphrased by sumu iye. The Portuguese da- starts the contracted expression for settled residence, continued below.
[c2-l004 >] *ſẽto em algũa parte.*
[c2-l004 note] The continuation reads ſẽto, completing the settled-residence expression. Its faint nasal abbreviation is retained, and the gloss allows residence in any place.
[c2-l005] Giǔuon. Caſanaru megumi. *Grandes, &*
[c2-l005 note] Juuon denotes accumulated or repeated benefactions, paraphrased by kasanaru megumi. The damaged headword reads Giǔuon, not OCR Gifuon; the Portuguese adjective continues below.
[c2-l006 >] *muitos beneficios.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes the gloss as great and numerous benefits. This closes the benefaction entry rather than the subsequent mountain-residence entry.
[c2-l007] Giǔxen. Yamani ſumu. *Morar nos mõtes.*
[c2-l007 note] Juusen is glossed by yama ni sumu, living in the mountains. Portuguese mõtes retains the nasal abbreviation rather than spelling out the missing n.
[c2-l008] Giǔxi, uru, ita. *Habitar.*
[c2-l008 note] Juushi, with the printed principal forms uru and ita, is to inhabit or reside. The opening capital is G, not OCR C; preserve the supplied abbreviated inflection pattern.
[c2-l009] Giǔxo. Sumidocoro. *Morada, ou lugar*
[c2-l009 note] Juusho is a dwelling place, paraphrased by sumidokoro. The Portuguese noun is lugar, not the OCR's lagar, which would change the meaning.
[c2-l010 >] *onde ſe habita.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes the definition as the place where one lives. This is the first, dwelling-place Juusho entry.
[c2-l011] Giǔxo. i, Iyeni tçutauaru xo. *Liuro que*
[c2-l011 note] A second Juusho is a book passed down within a household, paraphrased by ie ni tsutawaru sho. The repeated headword is a distinct entry, not accidental duplication.
[c2-l012 >] *vem por deſcendencia em algũa familia a cer-*
[c2-l012 note] The book descends through a family. The phrase a cer- continues into the subject matter recorded in it; its physical division is retained.
[c2-l013 >] *ca da gèração, ou rendas, &c.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes a cerca and identifies family lineage, rents, or similar matters as the book's contents. The printed mark in gèração is retained.
[c2-l014] Giǔxocu. *Officio, cargo, ou dignidade que*
[c2-l014 note] Juushoku is an office, charge, or dignity transmitted through generations. The headword has no extra i before x, and Portuguese ou is a single word.
[c2-l015 >] *vem continuandoſe por idades nos pays, fi-*
[c2-l015 note] The office continues through fathers and descendants. The visible line-end fi- begins filhos; OCR lost the i, while the worn nos is still recoverable.
[c2-l016 >] *lhos, netos, &c. ¶ Item, Officio, ou car-*
[c2-l016 note] Lhos completes filhos, followed by grandchildren. An additional sense begins with an office or charge, divided after car-.
[c2-l017 >] *go annexo a algũa dignidade ẽ caſa do* Dairi.
[c2-l017 note] Go completes cargo: an office attached to a dignity in the Dairi's household or court. Dairi is a Japanese institutional term embedded in the Portuguese sentence and is set apart in roman type.
[c2-l018 >] *¶ Itẽ,* Giǔxocu. i, Vomoi curai. *Grã-*
[c2-l018 note] A visible paragraph sign introduces another Juushoku sense, paraphrased by omoi kurai. The headword is Giǔxocu, not OCR Ciixocu; Grã- starts the Portuguese grande.
[c2-l019 >] *de, ou auentajada dignidade, ou cargo.*
[c2-l019 note] De completes grande, describing a high or eminent dignity or office. This closes the set of Juushoku explanations.
[c2-l020] Giǔyacu. Caſanaru yacu. *Officio acrecen-*
[c2-l020 note] Juuyaku concerns additional or multiple duties, paraphrased as kasanaru yaku. Officio is one word; acrecen- continues the description below.
[c2-l021 >] *tado, ou muitos officios.*
[c2-l021 note] Tado completes acrecentado, with many offices given as an alternative formulation. This belongs to the cumulative-duty entry.
[c2-l022] Giǔyacu. *Erua aßi chamada.*
[c2-l022 note] Another Juuyaku is simply identified as the name of a plant. No species identification is supplied in the line, so none is inserted into the transcription or interpretation.
[c2-l023] Giǔyen. *Parentesco por muitas vias, como*
[c2-l023 note] Juuyen is kinship through multiple connections. The source reads muitas vias, not OCR muitar; the example follows on the next row.
[c2-l024 >] *o que caſa hum filho e filha com dous irmãos*
[c2-l024 note] The example marries a son and a daughter to two siblings. Irmãos restores a weak r rather than treating the OCR's i mãos as separate words.
[c2-l025 >] *ficando conſogro do outro por duas vias.*
[c2-l025 note] The two parents thereby become co-parents-in-law through two marriages. This completes the example of multiple kinship links and does not refer to the married pair becoming siblings.
[c2-l026] Giǔzai. Vomoi tçumi. *Graue peccado.*
[c2-l026 note] Juuzai is a grave sin, paraphrased by omoi tsumi. The visible headword is Giǔzai, with worn marked u, not OCR Gibzai.
[c2-l027] Giǔzainin. *Grande peccador.*
[c2-l027 note] Juuzainin is a person guilty of grave sin. This closes the G-before-I section before the independent letter-I heading and decorated initial.
