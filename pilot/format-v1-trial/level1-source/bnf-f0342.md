[c1-l001] Muſai. *Couſa ſuja, ou que faz aſco.*
[c1-l001 note] Musai is dirty or repulsive; the Portuguese distinguishes physical dirt from something that provokes disgust.
[c1-l002] Muſamuſato. *Adu. Modo de fazer as*
[c1-l002 note] Musamusato is labelled an adverb. The following definition concerns an imperfect, unenthusiastic manner of doing things.
[c1-l003 >] *couſas imperfectamente, ou ſem goſto, nem a-*
[c1-l003 note] The gloss specifies doing things imperfectly, without pleasure or application. The final a- belongs to aplicação on the next row.
[c1-l004 >] *plicação. ¶* Muſamuſato fiuo curaſu.
[c1-l004 note] Musamusato hi wo kurasu illustrates spending one’s days listlessly. The initial plicação completes the preceding Portuguese definition.
[c1-l005 >] *Paßar o tempo ſem fazer nada, ou ainda q̃*
[c1-l005 note] The example is translated as passing time doing nothing; the concessive clause beginning ainda q̃ continues below. Keep the abbreviation unexpanded.
[c1-l006 >] *faça fazer com imperfeição.*
[c1-l006 note] The concessive clause adds that even when one does act, the work is imperfect. This is a continuation, not another entry.
[c1-l007] Muſaraxij. *Couſa ſuja, ou que faz aſco, ou*
[c1-l007 note] Musarashii is again something dirty or repulsive. The final ou introduces the additional description on the following row.
[c1-l008 >] *mal compoſta, &c.*
[c1-l008 note] Mal composta adds the sense of being ill-arranged or untidy to musarashii; the short row completes that entry.
[c1-l009] Muſaſabi. i, Fucurô. *Curuja.*
[c1-l009 note] Musasabi is explicitly equated with Fucurô and glossed Curuja, an owl. Preserve that printed identification rather than substituting the animal associated with modern musasabi.
[c1-l010] Muſato. *Vt,* Muſato xita cotouo yǔ. *Di-*
[c1-l010 note] Musato shita koto wo yuu is saying things without consideration, as the divided Portuguese translation explains. Vt is printed italic.
[c1-l011 >] *zer couſas ſem conſideração.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes Di-zer: saying things without consideration. The physical division of the Portuguese verb is retained.
[c1-l012] Musǒ. i, Yumeno tçugue. *Reuelação, ou*
[c1-l012 note] Musǒ is explained by yume no tsuge, a message received in a dream, and by Portuguese revelation. The headword has round s and a caron.
[c1-l013 >] *viſta, & aparencia em ſonhos.*
[c1-l013 note] Vista and aparencia em sonhos complete the dream-vision definition. The gloss describes a manifestation in dreams rather than ordinary waking sight.
[c1-l014] Muſocu. *O não ter renda, ou ſeruir ſem ter*
[c1-l014 note] Musoku concerns lacking an income or serving without one. The next row specifies landed revenue or an equivalent provision.
[c1-l015 >] *renda de terras, ou couſa equiualente.*
[c1-l015 note] The absent income is revenue from land or something equivalent. This completes the definition of musoku.
[c1-l016] Muſocuna. l, muſocuni natta. *Ficar ſem*
[c1-l016 note] Musocuna and musocuni natta describe being, or becoming, without income. The Latin alternative marker l is not Japanese.
[c1-l017 >] *renda, ou terras que coſtumão ter os ſoldados.*
[c1-l017 note] The gloss specifies the income or lands customarily held by soldiers, narrowing the preceding general reference to support.
[c1-l018 >] *¶ Itẽ,* Muſocuna. l, muſocuno. *Cou-*
[c1-l018 note] A visible paragraph mark introduces another sense: musocuna or musocuno for something fruitless. The Portuguese Cou- continues below.
[c1-l019 >] *ſa baldada, ou feita em vão. Vt,* Muſo-
[c1-l019 note] The additional sense is something vain or wasted. Muſo- begins the illustrative phrase continued on the next row.
[c1-l020 >] cuna xinrǒuo itaita. *Trabalhei em vão.*
[c1-l020 note] Musocuna shinrǒ wo itaita is translated “I laboured in vain.” Preserve the printed itaita rather than substituting a modern past-tense form.
[c1-l021] Muſocunin. *Homem ſem renda nem comedia*
[c1-l021 note] Musokunin is a person without sufficient income or maintenance. Comedia here belongs to the provision-of-support gloss, not a theatrical genre.
[c1-l022 >] *baſtante que ſupra a falta do Chiguiǒ, ou rẽda.*
[c1-l022 note] The maintenance must make up for missing Chiguiǒ, explicitly explained as income. This Japanese term is embedded in italic Portuguese; the final o has a caron and rẽda abbreviates renda.
[c1-l023] Muſogi. *P. 60. annos.*
[c1-l023 note] Musogi denotes sixty years and is marked P. for poetic usage. The historical gi spelling is retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l024] Muſubi, u, unda. *Atar. ¶* Yẽuo mu-
[c1-l024 note] Musubi is the verb “tie.” The first example begins yen wo musubu, tying a bond; its Portuguese interpretation follows on the next row.
[c1-l025 >] ſubu. l, yẽpẽuo muſubu. *Caſar. ¶* Iuo-
[c1-l025 note] Yen wo musubu and yenpen wo musubu are glossed as marrying. The nasal abbreviations in Yẽ and yẽpẽ remain unexpanded.
[c1-l026 >] riuo muſubu. *Fazer choupana. ¶* Miuo
[c1-l026 note] Iuori wo musubu means making a hut. Mi wo then begins the separate example about a tree bearing fruit.
[c1-l027 >] muſubu. *Dar fruito a aruore, &c.* S *¶* In-
[c1-l027 note] Mi wo musubu is bearing fruit; the upright S qualifies that usage. In- begins the next expression, about forming ritual hand gestures.
[c1-l028 >] uo muſubu. *Fazer com as mãos varios*
[c1-l028 note] In wo musubu is described through making various movements with the hands. The Japanese phrase continues from the preceding divided In-.
[c1-l029 >] *meneos, ou ceremonias como cuſtumão os*
[c1-l029 note] The hand movements are compared with customary religious ceremonies. Cuſtumão is retained as the printed historical spelling.
[c1-l030 >] *gentios.*
[c1-l030 note] Gentios completes the explanation of whose ritual gestures are meant. This is the dictionary’s own religious terminology, not a separate lexical entry.
[c1-l031] Muſubi. *Nò, ou atadura. Cõmũmente ſe vſa*
[c1-l031 note] The noun musubi means a knot or binding. Cõmũmente introduces the statement that it is commonly used in compounds.
[c1-l032 >] *compoſto. Vt,* Fitomuſubi, futamuſubi.
[c1-l032 note] Hitomusubi and futamusubi illustrate one knot and two knots. The examples complete the preceding statement about compound use.
[c1-l033] Muſubiague, uru, eta. *Amarrando, ou a-*
[c1-l033 note] Musubiague means lifting something by tying it. The divided a- begins atando, whose continuation is on the next row.
[c1-l034 >] *tando aleuantar. ¶ Item, per met. Ti-*
[c1-l034 note] Atando aleuantar completes the literal definition. The paragraph mark introduces a transferred use, with Ti- starting tirar.
[c1-l035 >] *rar agoa de poço, ou rio. Vt,* Tomoni aca-
[c1-l035 note] The transferred sense is drawing water from a well or river. Tomoni aca- begins the Japanese example about drawing ritual water together.
[c1-l036 >] no mizzuuo muſubiague, fitotçu fachiſu-
[c1-l036 note] The example continues with aca no mizzu wo musubiague and hitotsu hachisu, drawing the water and sharing one lotus. Keep the physical split fachiſu-.
[c1-l037 >] no yẽto narǒ. Feiq. *Lib. 4. To-*
[c1-l037 note] No yen to narǒ completes the wish to share that bond. The light, partly broken mark over e is read as the nasal abbreviation in yẽ; Feiq. is an upright source label and Lib. 4 is italic.
[c1-l038 >] *mando agoa ( que era como benta, juntamente*
[c1-l038 note] The translation describes taking water regarded as blessed. The printed opening parenthesis has no matching closing mark here; none is supplied editorially.
[c1-l039 >] *ajuntar nos emos no meſmo golfão. i; Saluar*
[c1-l039 note] The lotus image is translated as joining together in the same golfão and then interpreted as salvation. Enlarged reinspection shows a semicolon after i, retained as printed rather than regularized.
[c1-l040 >] *nos emos.*
[c1-l040 note] Nos emos completes Saluar nos emos: “we shall be saved.” This short Portuguese continuation closes the cited example.
[c1-l041] Muſubiai, yǒta. *Vnirſe entre ſi.*
[c1-l041 note] Musubiai means joining one another. The gloss reads entre ſi, not OCR entreſio; no missing dictionary-form principal part is supplied.
[c1-l042] Muſubiauaxe, ſuru, eta. *Amarrar, ou atar*
[c1-l042 note] Musubiauaxe is tying things together. Preserve the printed principal form ſuru even though the modern verb suggests a different expansion.
[c1-l043 >] *hũas couſas com outras.*
[c1-l043 note] Hũas couſas com outras completes the meaning “some things with others.” The tilde belongs to u in hũas.
[c1-l044] Muſubime. *Nò, ou atadura.*
[c1-l044 note] Musubime denotes a knot or binding. Nò carries the printed grave accent, not a Japanese long-vowel mark.
[c1-l045] Muſubiſague, uru, eta. *Trazer dependura-*
[c1-l045 note] Musubisague means carrying something tied and hanging down. Dependura- has a terminal division mark and continues as dependurada below.
[c1-l046 >] *da, ou atada algũa couſa.*
[c1-l046 note] Da completes dependurada; the object is something hanging or tied. The preceding row’s terminal mark is not sentence punctuation.
[c1-l047] Muſubitçuqe, uru, eta. *Atando pegar.*
[c1-l047 note] Musubitsuke means fastening by tying. The scan reads Atando, with n, rather than OCR Atardo.

[c2-l001] Muſubôre, ôru, eta. S. l, Muſubore, uru.
[c2-l001 note] Musubôre and the alternative Musubore are forms for becoming tangled. Both circumflexes in the first form are visible; the S. label is upright.
[c2-l002 >] *Emburulharſe, ou embaraçarſe como linhas,*
[c2-l002 note] The literal comparison is becoming entangled like threads. The comma after linhas connects the following &c., rather than ending the entry.
[c2-l003 >] *&c. ¶* Cocoroga muſuboruru. i, Mo-
[c2-l003 note] Kokoro ga musuboruru applies the tangling image to the heart; Mo- begins the Japanese explanatory equivalent continued below.
[c2-l004 >] giruru. *Perturbarſe o coração.*
[c2-l004 note] Mogiruru completes the Japanese equivalent. The Portuguese explicitly explains the figurative sense as the heart becoming disturbed.
[c2-l005] Muſuco. *Filho.*
[c2-l005 note] Musuko is glossed directly as filho, a son. This is a separate short entry.
[c2-l006] Muſume. *Filha.*
[c2-l006 note] Musume is glossed directly as filha, a daughter, in parallel with the preceding entry.
[c2-l007] Mutai. Tai naxi. *Couſa deſarrezoada, ou*
[c2-l007 note] Mutai is explained by tai nashi and Portuguese “unreasonable.” The Japanese explanatory phrase is not part of the Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l008 >] *deſaproueitada.*
[c2-l008 note] Desaproueitada adds the sense of being unprofitable or useless to mutai. This short continuation closes the definition.
[c2-l009] Mutaina. *Couſa deſarrezoada, ou iniuſta.*
[c2-l009 note] Mutaina is the adjectival form, unreasonable or unjust. The scan prints iniuſta with consonantal i here, in contrast to the descending j in injuſta- on c2-l012.
[c2-l010] Mutaini. *Adu. Sem rezão. ¶* Mutaini
[c2-l010 note] Mutaini is marked as an adverb meaning without reason. Its illustrative phrase begins again at the end of this row.
[c2-l011 >] monouo toru. l, foxigaru. *Tomar, ou*
[c2-l011 note] Mutaini mono wo toru or hoshigaru means taking or wanting something without justification. The alternative marker separates the two verbs.
[c2-l012 >] *deſejar algũa couſa ſem razão, & injuſta-*
[c2-l012 note] The Portuguese describes taking or desiring something without reason and unjustly. The final injuſta- continues into -mente below.
[c2-l013 >] *mente.*
[c2-l013 note] Mente completes injustamente, “unjustly.” Its short physical row belongs to the preceding example.
[c2-l014] Mutçucari, u, atta. *Chorar. Palaura de*
[c2-l014 note] Mutsukari is glossed as crying. The usage note begins by assigning it to women’s speech; the following rows qualify that statement.
[c2-l015 >] *molheres posto que tambem a vſão homens fal-*
[c2-l015 note] The note says men also use the expression, despite its association with women’s speech. The divided fal- continues as fallando.
[c2-l016 >] *lando de mininos nobres.*
[c2-l016 note] The qualification concerns speaking about children of noble status. This completes the usage restriction on mutsukari.
[c2-l017] Mutçucarigoye. *Voz choroſa.*
[c2-l017 note] Mutsukarigoye denotes a tearful or crying voice. Goye is retained as the printed historical spelling.
[c2-l018] Mutçucaxij. *Couſa enfadonha, trabalhoſa,*
[c2-l018 note] Mutsukashii is explained as troublesome or laborious, with further detail below. The definition stresses difficulty and annoyance.
[c2-l019 >] *ou de embaraço, &c.*
[c2-l019 note] The gloss adds something that causes encumbrance or trouble. It completes the adjective before the separately printed derived forms.
[c2-l020 >] Mutçucaxiſa.
[c2-l020 note] Mutsukashisa is the nominal derivative, “difficulty” or “troublesomeness.” It has its own indented physical row.
[c2-l021 >] Mutçucaxǔ.
[c2-l021 note] Mutsukashǔ is the contracted adverbial form of the preceding adjective. Its final u carries a caron, not an ordinary unmarked vowel.
[c2-l022] Mutçugoto. *P. i,* Nengorona tei. l, mu-
[c2-l022 note] Mutsugoto is marked P. and explained as nengorona tei or affectionate words. The scan supports tei and the divided mu-, not OCR toi and mru-.
[c2-l023 >] tçumaxij cotoba nari. *Trato, ou prati-*
[c2-l023 note] Mutsumashii kotoba nari completes the affectionate-speech explanation. The Portuguese prati- continues as pratica, conversation, not OCR praſica.
[c2-l024 >] *ca familiar.*
[c2-l024 note] Ca familiar completes pratica familiar: intimate or familiar conversation. This closes the poetic mutsugoto entry.
[c2-l025] Mutçumaxii. *Couſa amigauel, ou de vnião,*
[c2-l025 note] The affectionate adjective is glossed as friendly or united. Enlargement confirms two final dotted i types in Mutçumaxii, rather than the more usual ij; the supplied NINJAL index independently agrees.
[c2-l026 >] *& amizade.*
[c2-l026 note] Amizade completes the gloss with friendship. The short continuation belongs to the affectionate adjective.
[c2-l027 >] Mutçumaxiſa.
[c2-l027 note] Mutsumashisa is the corresponding noun for affection or closeness. It is printed on a separate indented row.
[c2-l028 >] Mutçumaxǔ.
[c2-l028 note] Mutsumashǔ is the contracted adverbial form. The final u and caron are visible; OCR lost both the vowel identity and its mark.
[c2-l029] Mutçuqe, uru, eta. *Enfraquecer, & emma-*
[c2-l029 note] Mutsuke describes becoming weak or wasting away. The divided emma- is continued by -grecer on the following row.
[c2-l030 >] *grecer, ou definhar. Particularmente ſe diz*
[c2-l030 note] Emmagrecer and definhar specify becoming thin or wasting away. The next clause explains the expression’s typical application.
[c2-l031 >] *de caualos, & paſſaros que ſe crião em caſa,*
[c2-l031 note] The usage note particularly mentions horses and birds raised domestically. Paſſaros retains the printed long-s sequence.
[c2-l032 >] *tambem ſe diz de homẽ, maximè de mininos.*
[c2-l032 note] The same expression also applies to people, especially children. Maximè is a Latin qualifier within the Portuguese note.
[c2-l033] Mutçuqi. *Pano de linho, ou peça que ſe poẽ*
[c2-l033 note] Mutsuki is a linen cloth or piece of cloth placed on a child. The next rows describe its diaper-like function.
[c2-l034 >] *nas partes exteriores da criança pera que ſe*
[c2-l034 note] The cloth is placed about the child’s external parts, with its purpose completed on the next row. The physical line remains intact.
[c2-l035 >] *não çuje.*
[c2-l035 note] The purpose is to prevent soiling. The printed çuje has a cedilla and is not normalized to modern suje.
[c2-l036] Mute. *O não ſaber da arte de eſgrima, ou do*
[c2-l036 note] Mute here means lack of skill in fencing or a game. The negative phrase O não ſaber is central to the sense.
[c2-l037 >] *jogo.*
[c2-l037 note] Jogo completes the alternative to fencing. This short row is part of mute’s skill-related definition.
[c2-l038] Muten. Tennaxi. *O não terem as letras da*
[c2-l038 note] Muten is explained by ten nashi, lacking points or marks. Tennaxi remains printed as one word.
[c2-l039 >] *China, ou Iapão certos ſinaes, ou pontos por*
[c2-l039 note] The missing signs or points concern Chinese or Japanese writing. Iapão is a Portuguese place-name, not a Japanese term requiring special tagging.
[c2-l040 >] *onde ſe conheção.*
[c2-l040 note] The signs serve to make the written characters intelligible. The clause completes the definition of unpointed writing.
[c2-l041] Mutena. l, mutena fito. *Peßoa que não ſa-*
[c2-l041 note] Mutena or mutena hito is a person without the relevant skill. The adjective introduces an extended description of inexperience.
[c2-l042 >] *be bem da arte de jogo, nem eſgrima, nem lu-*
[c2-l042 note] The person lacks mastery of games, fencing, and wrestling. Lu- begins luta, divided across the following physical row.
[c2-l043 >] *ta, &c. ¶ Item, permet.* Mutena fito.
[c2-l043 note] Ta completes luta. A new transferred example, mutena hito, broadens the meaning to general incapacity; the whole row is indented continuation text.
[c2-l044 >] *Homem inhabil, & que não ſabe nenhũa arte,*
[c2-l044 note] The broader meaning describes an unskilled person who knows no art. The short relative construction continues below.
[c2-l045 >] *nem tem induſtria, &c.*
[c2-l045 note] The person also lacks practical resourcefulness or industry. This completes the skill-related mutena entry, before the separate unpointed-text adjective.
[c2-l046] Mutẽna. *Liuro, ou eſcritura ſem pontos, ou*
[c2-l046 note] Mutẽna concerns an unpointed book or text, corresponding to muten above. The headword has t and a nasal abbreviation over e, not OCR Mubẽna.
[c2-l047 >] *ſinais por õde ſe conhecẽ as letras. ¶ Item, per-*
[c2-l047 note] The missing signs aid recognition of the characters. The visible abbreviations are õde and conhecẽ; Item, per- introduces the transferred sense continued on f343.
