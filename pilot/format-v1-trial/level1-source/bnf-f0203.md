[c1-l001 >>] *¶ Item, Modo de estar no Zaxiqui muita gẽ*
[c1-l001 note] Continues hishito from f202: many people sitting closely in a zashiki. This actual opening confirms the preceding bottom-right Item as an anticipatory catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *te apertada, &c.*
[c1-l002 note] Te completes gente, with apertada describing the crowded group. The crop contains the full short continuation, including its closing punctuation.
[c1-l003] Fixiuo. *Certo manjar q̃ ſe faz de grãos, trigo*
[c1-l003 note] Hishio is a preparation of pulses, wheat, and salt. The small first stroke of trigo is supported by the scan and the ingredient list, correcting OCR's rigo.
[c1-l004 >] *ſal, &c. muito piſado, & moido. ¶* Fo-
[c1-l004 note] The ingredients are thoroughly pounded and ground. Fo- starts hone, bones, in the metaphorical example that follows.
[c1-l005 >] neuo fixiuoni xerarurutomo, Chriſtãouo
[c1-l005 note] Hone wo hishio ni seraruru tomo imagines one's bones being reduced to paste. Christãouo is the Christian identity in the Japanese sentence, not a switch to a Portuguese explanatory clause.
[c1-l006 >] cayurucoto arumai. *Ainda que me moão*
[c1-l006 note] Kayuru koto arumai denies changing that identity. The concessive Portuguese begins even if they grind me; do not replace the historical kayuru with a modern verb form.
[c1-l007 >] *os oſſos, & desfação não eide deixar de ſer*
[c1-l007 note] The bones may be ground and destroyed, but the speaker will not cease to be Christian. Eide is retained as printed, and the s forms are not regularized from modern spelling.
[c1-l008 >] *Chriſtão.*
[c1-l008 note] Christão completes the declared identity. This italic word belongs to the Portuguese translation, unlike the upright borrowed term in the Japanese example.
[c1-l009] Fixiuo. *Marè que vaza, ou vazia. ¶* Fi-
[c1-l009 note] A homonymous hishio denotes an ebbing or low tide. The maritime gloss distinguishes it entirely from the food above.
[c1-l010 >] xiuoni naru. *Vazar a marè.*
[c1-l010 note] Hishio ni naru means the tide ebbing. The repeated accented marè is preserved without modernization.
[c1-l011] Fixizzuru. *Cana, ou grelo da erua que ſe cha*
[c1-l011 note] Hishizzuru is the stem or shoot of the hishi plant. The compound's second part corresponds to a plant stem, not a verb in suru.
[c1-l012 >] *ma* Fixi.
[c1-l012 note] Fixi supplies the named plant inside Portuguese prose and is upright. This short continuation is still part of the hishizzuru definition.
[c1-l013] Fixo. *Eſcritura, ou liuro de ſegredos. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l013 note] Hisho is a secret writing or book. Escritura here means writing or document, not necessarily Christian Scripture.
[c1-l014 >] *Segredo. ¶* Monouo fixoſuru. *Eſcon-*
[c1-l014 note] The entry adds secrecy itself and mono wo hisho suru, concealing something. The Japanese example agrees with the following Portuguese infinitive.
[c1-l015 >] *der algũa couſa.*
[c1-l015 note] Der completes esconder, and algũa couſa supplies its object. Keep the physical line division and nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l016] Fixǒ. Canaximi itamu. *Doerſe com tris-*
[c1-l016 note] Hishoo is kanashimi itamu, grieving painfully. The Portuguese describes sorrowful suffering rather than the secret-document sense of unaccented hisho.
[c1-l017 >] *teza, ou affligirſe.*
[c1-l017 note] The grief is further glossed affligir-se. Two f stems are visible in the ligature cluster, supporting affligirſe rather than OCR's single-f form.
[c1-l018] Fixǔ. Canaximi vreô. *Triſteza, ou en-*
[c1-l018 note] Hishuu is kanashimi ureo(o), sorrow or grieving. The long-u headword and the following Portuguese noun/verb alternatives are coherent.
[c1-l019 >] *triſtecerſe.*
[c1-l019 note] Tristecerſe completes entristecer-se. It is a continuation of the verb, not another independent entry.
[c1-l020] Fixxa. i, Fudetori. *Eſcriuão.*
[c1-l020 note] Hissha is a fude-tori, a scribe. The scan separates the explanatory i, from Fixxa; OCR had attached and misplaced it.
[c1-l021] Fixxei. Fudeno iqiuoi. *Feição de letra,*
[c1-l021 note] Hissei is fude no ikioi, the force or character of brushwork. The Portuguese covers handwriting and painting rather than simply the writer's social role.
[c1-l022 >] *ou pintura em que ſe hum eſmerou. ¶* Fix-
[c1-l022 note] The writing or painting is executed with care or mastery. The example repeats hissei, split after Fix-.
[c1-l023 >] xeiuo tçucuſu. *Eſmerarſe em eſcreuer, ou*
[c1-l023 note] Hissei wo tsukusu means exerting one's skill in writing or painting. The long s in tçucuſu remains the OCR reading because it matches the visible form.
[c1-l024 >] *pintar.*
[c1-l024 note] Pintar completes the alternative artistic activity. This short row closes the same expression.
[c1-l025] Fixxi. Canarazu xiſuru. *Morte irremedia-*
[c1-l025 note] Hisshi is kanarazu shi suru, certain death. The gloss is not the common modern adverbial sense of desperate effort.
[c1-l026 >] *uel, ou certa, & determinada.*
[c1-l026 note] Death is unavoidable, certain, and determined. These qualifications reinforce the literal interpretation of the Japanese compound.
[c1-l027] Fixxi. Fude, cami. *Pena, & papel.*
[c1-l027 note] Another hisshi is fude and kami, brush and paper. The identical sound represents a different compound from certain death.
[c1-l028 >] *¶* Fixxini tçucuxigataxi. *Não ſe po-*
[c1-l028 note] Hisshi ni tsukushigatashi means that writing cannot exhaust the matter. A conspicuous paragraph mark before the example was omitted by OCR and is restored.
[c1-l029 >] *de acabar de eſcreuer, ou explicar por pena.*
[c1-l029 note] The Portuguese spells out inability to finish writing or explaining with a pen. It confirms the idiomatic reading rather than physical shortage of paper.
[c1-l030] Fixxime, uru, eta. *Apertar muito como*
[c1-l030 note] Hisshime, hisshimuru, hisshimeta means tightening strongly. The next row supplies binding implements.
[c1-l031 >] *com corda, cordão, &c.*
[c1-l031 note] Rope or cord exemplifies how the tightening is done. Cordão remains accented on the printed vowel rather than standardized from neighbouring instances.
[c1-l032] Fixxiqi. *Pelle que trazem os ſoldados pera ſe*
[c1-l032 note] Hisshiki is a skin carried by soldiers for sitting. The gloss describes a portable seating hide, with additional users below.
[c1-l033 >] *aßentarem, ou de que vſão outras peßoas pera*
[c1-l033 note] Other people also use the hide as a seat. The two ß forms retain the ligature convention; the text does not restrict this object exclusively to soldiers.
[c1-l034 >] *ſe aßentar.*
[c1-l034 note] Se assentar completes the sitting purpose. It closes the hide entry rather than introducing a separate verb.
[c1-l035] Fixxito. *Adu. Modo de fazer algũa cou-*
[c1-l035 note] Hisshito is an adverb describing how something is done. Its doubled x records the stronger consonant form compared with hishito on f202.
[c1-l036 >] *ſa com efficacia, ou rijamente, ou modo de*
[c1-l036 note] The action is effective or forceful; the definition then turns to closeness. Efficacia is the source's spelling, without an added modern accent.
[c1-l037 >] *eſtar a couſa junta, & apertada. Vt,* Fix-
[c1-l037 note] The object can also be closely joined or pressed together. Vt introduces the divided hisshito example and is italic in the scan.
[c1-l038 >] xito vtçu, l, yamaſuru. *Dar pancadas ri*
[c1-l038 note] Hisshito utsu or yamasuru concerns hard blows. Retain the less familiar yamasuru, whose printed letters are clear, rather than invent a more familiar Japanese verb.
[c1-l039 >] *jamente.*
[c1-l039 note] Jamente completes rijamente, forcefully. No new hyphen is supplied to the previous ri merely because the word crosses a row.
[c1-l040] Fiya. *Frecha de fogo. ¶ Item, Foguete.*
[c1-l040 note] Hiya is a fire-arrow, with rocket added as another sense. This is not the cold-related hiya family that begins below.
[c1-l041 >] *¶* Fiyauo iru. *Tirar com frecha de fogo,*
[c1-l041 note] Hiya wo iru is shooting a fire-arrow. The scan has uo, agreeing with the object of iru, and corrects OCR's no.
[c1-l042 >] *ou com foguete pegado na frecha.*
[c1-l042 note] The alternative is a rocket attached to the arrow. The gloss thus specifies the projectile construction instead of a wholly unrelated meaning of fire.
[c1-l043] Fiyacu. *Mezinha muito estimada, & como*
[c1-l043 note] Hiyaku is a highly valued medicine, treated almost as a secret. This fits the secret-remedy compound rather than a flying or jumping verb.
[c1-l044 >] *eſcondida que logo obra. ¶ Item, Aliquã*
[c1-l044 note] The medicine works promptly. Aliquã begins Latin aliquando, sometimes, introducing a contrasting usage on the next row.
[c1-l045 >] *do, Mezinha leue, & de pouca ſustancia.*
[c1-l045 note] Sometimes it means a mild medicine of little substance. The apparent contrast is explicitly marked by aliquando and should not be harmonized away.
[c1-l046] Fiyai. *Couſa fria, ou fazer frio.* X.
[c1-l046 note] Hiyai is cold or being cold, marked as Ximo usage. The damaged capital belongs to the word; X. is a separate upright regional label.
[c1-l047 >] Fiyaſa.
[c1-l047 note] Hiyasa is the associated noun, coldness. Its indented isolated row is part of the adjective's entry, not a running label.
[c1-l048 >] Fiyǒ.
[c1-l048 note] Hiyoo is the associated adverbial form. It sits beneath hiyasa within the column; unlike a bottom-right next-page catchword, it belongs in the body.

[c2-l001] Fiyamugui. *Aletria de trigo fria, & freſca*
[c2-l001 note] Hiyamugi is cold wheat noodles. Portuguese aletria is the comparison used for the noodle form, not a reason to replace the Japanese name with a European dish.
[c2-l002 >] *aßi como ſe come.*
[c2-l002 note] The noodles are eaten in that cold, fresh state. Aßi retains the ligature transcription and completes the manner of serving.
[c2-l003] Fiyaqe. *Oqueimarſe, ou ſecarſe com aſeca,*
[c2-l003 note] Hiyake is scorching or drying in drought. The scan joins Oqueimarſe; OCR's internal break in queimar is removed without imposing modern spacing on the initial article.
[c2-l004 >] *ou muito ſol, como os campos, & varzeas.*
[c2-l004 note] Strong sun dries fields and lowland cultivated plots. This completes the agricultural sense before the bodily sunburn sense below.
[c2-l005 >] *¶ Item, Abraſarſe, ou queimarſe ao ſol*
[c2-l005 note] The entry additionally describes scorching or burning in sunlight. The reflexive Portuguese concerns the affected body, not deliberate setting of fire.
[c2-l006 >] *o corpo, roſto, &c. ¶* Fiyaqega ſuru.
[c2-l006 note] The body or face is sunburned; hiyake ga suru gives the Japanese construction. The particle ga agrees with that intransitive event.
[c2-l007 >] *Idem.*
[c2-l007 note] Idem explicitly equates the example with the sunburn definition. The short isolated row is not furniture.
[c2-l008] Fiyaqeda. *Varzeas de ſequeiro.*
[c2-l008 note] Hiyaketa denotes dry-field plots. Portuguese de sequeiro distinguishes land lacking irrigation; retain the printed voiced da rather than modernize the compound.
[c2-l009] Fiyaxi, ſu, aita. *Eſfriar algũa couſa, me-*
[c2-l009 note] Hiyashi, hiyasu, hiya(i)ta is cooling something. The historical -aita past is compatible with the dictionary's s-verb onbin and is not automatically hiyashita.
[c2-l010 >] *tendoa na agoa. O eſfriar ao vento he,* Caza-
[c2-l010 note] Immersion in water is contrasted with cooling in wind. He, not OCR's be, is the copula; Caza- begins the Japanese alternative and is visibly upright.
[c2-l011 >] *ſame*, uru.
[c2-l011 note] Same completes kazasame, kazasamuru. Its first glyph is long s, not f; the first fragment is actually italic while uru is upright, an irregular typeface boundary preserved from the scan.
[c2-l012] Fiyaximono. *Couſa de comer fria, & que*
[c2-l012 note] Hiyashimono is cold food that refreshes. This is a general class before the more specific noodle sense on the next row.
[c2-l013 >] *refreſca.* X. *¶ Item, Aletria cozida fria.*
[c2-l013 note] The regional label X. closes the first sense. A second sense is cooked noodles served cold; the label is upright while the surrounding prose is italic.
[c2-l014] Fiyaxiru. *Certo Xiru frio com algũa miſtura*
[c2-l014 note] Hiyashiru is a cold shiru with an admixture. Xiru is an embedded Japanese food term and appears italic, which should not be overridden by its usual upright treatment elsewhere.
[c2-l015 >] *de eruas que ſe come no verão.*
[c2-l015 note] Herbs are mixed into the dish, which is eaten in summer. The season reinforces the cold-serving interpretation.
[c2-l016] Fiyayacana. *Couſa fria, & freſca.*
[c2-l016 note] Hiyayakana describes something cold and fresh. The printed adjective is retained rather than replaced with a modern preferred spelling.
[c2-l017] Fiyazaqe. *Vinho frio.*
[c2-l017 note] Hiyazake is cold sake, rendered vinho frio. The European beverage category is the dictionary's gloss, not a botanical claim about grapes.
[c2-l018] Fiye, uru, eta. *Effriarſe, ou ſentir frio.*
[c2-l018 note] Hiye, hiyuru, hiyeta is becoming cold or feeling cold. It is intransitive, in contrast to hiyasu above.
[c2-l019] Fiye. *Doença de frio. Vt,* Fiyeuo vaz-
[c2-l019 note] Hiye also denotes an illness associated with cold. Hiye wo waz- introduces the example, with the word divided between the two z characters.
[c2-l020 >] zurǒ. *Adoeçer de frio.* X.
[c2-l020 note] Wazzuroo completes falling ill from cold. X. is an upright regional marker, not a Japanese syllable or part of adoecer.
[c2-l021] Fiye. *Hum legume, ou ſemente de cor preta*
[c2-l021 note] Another hie is a dark-coloured grain or seed. This is the cereal name, unrelated to the preceding illness apart from pronunciation.
[c2-l022 >] *como milho.*
[c2-l022 note] Como milho compares the grain with millet-like cereal. The brief comparison does not establish a modern botanical species by itself.
[c2-l023] Fiye. *Carnegão de eſpinha do roſto que ſe pa-*
[c2-l023 note] Hie also names the core of a facial spot. Carnegão and espinha belong to that bodily description, not the cereal definition itself.
[c2-l024 >] *rece com eſta ſemente. ¶ Item, Ameſma*
[c2-l024 note] The core resembles the previously named seed. The entry then adds the spot itself as a further sense.
[c2-l025 >] *eſpinha do roſto.*
[c2-l025 note] Espinha do rosto completes the whole-spot sense. This is a semantic extension from the seed-like core, not another isolated headword.
[c2-l026] Fiyegai. *Meliùs*, fiyenocai. *Canja feita daquelle*
[c2-l026 note] Hiyegai is corrected by the dictionary itself to hiye no kai, a gruel. Meliùs is Latin better, and must not be included in the Japanese reading.
[c2-l027 >] *legume como milho preto.*
[c2-l027 note] The gruel is made from that dark millet-like grain. The demonstrative links it back to hie, not to cold rice in the following entry.
[c2-l028] Fiyeiy. *Arroz cozido frio.*
[c2-l028 note] Hiyeii is cold cooked rice. The iy ending is the dictionary's way of keeping the rice word's vowel sequence, not a ji sound.
[c2-l029] Fiyemono. *Couſa naturalmente fria como co*
[c2-l029 note] Hiyemono is something naturally cooling. The gloss begins an example involving food rather than merely an object chilled externally.
[c2-l030 >] *mer que eſfria o eſtamago, &c.*
[c2-l030 note] Such food cools the stomach. Eſtamago is visibly printed with a; retain it despite modern estômago and do not use dictionary familiarity to normalize the vowel.
[c2-l031] Fiyet. Firaqi miru. *Abrĩdo a carta, lela.*
[c2-l031 note] Hiyet, opening and reading a letter, is glossed hiraqi miru. Enlarging the worn gap after Ab reveals a small lower-stroke remnant compatible with r; Abrĩdo is inferred from this and the clear Abrindo in the next line. This is a damaged reading, not a fully legible r.
[c2-l032 >] *Vt,* Gojo fiyet xexime ſoro. *Abrindo a*
[c2-l032 note] Gojo hiyet seshime sooro is a formal letter-reading expression. The weak final o of Gojo is not supplied with an accent merely because gojoo is expected.
[c2-l033 >] *voßa carta, ali, ou vi.*
[c2-l033 note] The translation says that on opening the addressee's letter the speaker read or saw it. Ali is a li, I read it, rather than the locative ali in this context.
[c2-l034] Fiyô. Tçuyaxi mochijru. i, Zǒſa. *Gaſto,*
[c2-l034 note] Hiyoo is expenditure: tsuyashi mochijru, with zoosa as an equivalent. The circumflex in the headword is preserved and not confused with the open-o mark in Zoosa.
[c2-l035 >] *ou cuſto. ¶* Fiyôga iru. *Auer gaſto,*
[c2-l035 note] Hiyoo ga iru means there is expense or something costs money. The repeated accented headword agrees with the cost sense.
[c2-l036 >] *& cuſto.*
[c2-l036 note] E custo completes the paired Portuguese gloss. It remains a body continuation despite its brevity.
[c2-l037] Fiyô. Cacuxi mochijru. *Couſa ſecreta, &*
[c2-l037 note] Another hiyoo is keeping something hidden for use. Cacuxi mochijru and the Portuguese secret and valued distinguish this from expense.
[c2-l038 >] *eſtimada. Vt,* Fiyôno fǒ. *Ley ſecreta,*
[c2-l038 note] Hiyoo no hoo exemplifies a secret law or teaching. Enlargement reveals a distinct shallow v above the final o, supporting fǒ rather than OCR's unaccented fo; this contrasts with the circumflex earlier in Fiyôno.
[c2-l039 >] *& deſtima.*
[c2-l039 note] Deſtima means de estima, of esteem or value. Its joined spelling is preserved rather than split editorially.
[c2-l040] Fiyoco. *Pintão, ou frangão.*
[c2-l040 note] Hiyoko is a chick or young chicken. The two Portuguese bird-age terms agree with the straightforward Japanese reading.
[c2-l041] Fiyocu. Tçubaſauo naraburu tori. *Certos*
[c2-l041 note] Hiyoku is explained as birds setting their wings alongside each other. This introduces the literary paired-bird image, not ordinary ornithological classification.
[c2-l042 >] *paſſaros que voam com hũa aſa ſoo que tem*
[c2-l042 note] Each bird has only one wing. The scan supports the separate long-s pair retained in paſſaros; no ligature normalization is imposed during this semantic review.
[c2-l043 >] *ajudandoſe da do companheiro. Vſaſe em cõ-*
[c2-l043 note] Each uses the companion's wing. The dictionary then says the expression is used comparatively, explicitly identifying the metaphorical extension.
[c2-l044 >] *paração dos que muito ſe amão, & não po-*
[c2-l044 note] The comparison concerns people who love one another intensely and cannot part. This matches the literary hiyoku image of inseparability.
[c2-l045 >] *dem apartar. ¶* Fiyocunotori. *Idem.*
[c2-l045 note] Hiyoku no tori restates the paired-bird expression. Idem refers to the preceding description and comparison.
[c2-l046] Fiyodori. *Eſtorninho.*
[c2-l046 note] Hiyodori is compared with a starling. Preserve that historical gloss rather than substitute the modern bulbul identification into Level 1.
[c2-l047] Fiyodorijǒgo. *Certa erua de latada.*
[c2-l047 note] Hiyodorijoogo is a climbing plant name. The scan supports y in Fiyodori, not OCR's j; the compound and preceding bird name independently flag the mistake.
