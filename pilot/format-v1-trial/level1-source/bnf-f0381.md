[c1-l001 >] ni coxite. *Eſtando humildemente.* S.
[c1-l001 note] This carries over an example from the preceding page: the Portuguese describes remaining humbly. The initial fragment is preserved without reconstructing the missing beginning; the final S. is a roman usage label.
[c1-l002] Qen. Tçurugui. *Eſpada. ¶* Qenuo nu-
[c1-l002 note] Ken is equated with tsurugi, a sword. The example begins ken wo nuki-, and the printed hyphen connects it to the following physical row.
[c1-l003 >] qi cutçuroguru. *Arrancar mea eſpada.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes nuki kutsuroguru, loosening or partly drawing the sword. The Portuguese specifies drawing half the sword; historical mea is retained rather than modernized to meia.
[c1-l004 >] *¶* Qenuo tai ſuru. *Trazer eſpada.*
[c1-l004 note] Ken wo tai suru means carrying or wearing a sword, matching trazer espada. The printed separation tai ſuru is retained.
[c1-l005 >] *¶* Qenuo nuqi fanaſu. *Arrancar de to-*
[c1-l005 note] Ken wo nuki hanasu means drawing the sword completely. The scan supports the object-particle sequence Qenuo rather than OCR Qenno; the Portuguese todo is divided here.
[c1-l006 >] *do. ¶* Qenuo ſaxicazaſu. *Por a eſpa-*
[c1-l006 note] Do completes de todo. The next sword example, sashi kazasu, is explained as placing or holding the sword upright; espada continues on the next row.
[c1-l007 >] *da direita diante do roſto. ¶* Qenuo furu,
[c1-l007 note] The sword is held upright before the face. Ken wo furu introduces swinging it; retain the source's roſto spelling pending the separate st-ligature audit.
[c1-l008 >] l, tçucǒ. *Iugar da eſpada.*
[c1-l008 note] The Latin alternative marker l introduces tsukau, another expression for wielding the sword. Iugar da espada gives the fencing sense, not ordinary playing with an object.
[c1-l009] Qenague. *Valentia, ou fortaleza.*
[c1-l009 note] Kenage denotes valour or strength here. The historical meaning is established by the paired Portuguese nouns, rather than the narrower associations of the modern Japanese adjective.
[c1-l010] Qenague date. *Moſtra de valentia. ¶* Qe-
[c1-l010 note] Kenage date is a display of bravery. The repeated expression starts at the right edge and continues after the visible Qe- division.
[c1-l011 >] nague dateuo ſuru. *Moſtrar, ou dar moſtras*
[c1-l011 note] Completes kenage date wo suru, making a show of bravery. The Portuguese offers two equivalent constructions for showing or giving signs of it.
[c1-l012 >] *de valente.*
[c1-l012 note] De valente completes the preceding Portuguese expression: giving signs of being brave. This short continuation belongs to the same example.
[c1-l013] Qenaguena. *Valente, & ſaõ.*
[c1-l013 note] Kenage na is glossed both as brave and as sound or healthy. Preserve the printed adjective and the tilde on the final vowel of ſaõ.
[c1-l014] Qenagueni. *Adu. Valentemente, ou com*
[c1-l014 note] Kenage ni is the corresponding adverb. Adu. belongs to the italic grammatical apparatus; the second Portuguese explanation continues below.
[c1-l015 >] *ſaude, & boa diſpoſiçaõ. Vt,* Qenagueni
[c1-l015 note] The adverb can describe health and good condition. Vt introduces kenage ni gozaru, rather than another headword.
[c1-l016 >] gozaru. *Eſtar ſaõ, & bem deſpoſto.*
[c1-l016 note] Gozaru completes the example, rendered as being healthy and well disposed. The historical Portuguese deſpoſto is retained rather than regularized to disposto.
[c1-l017] Qenai. Iyeno vchi. *Familia, ou gente de*
[c1-l017 note] Kenai is explained by ie no uchi, the household. The Portuguese describes a family or the people of a house, with the final noun continued below.
[c1-l018 >] *caſa. ¶* Qenaino mono. *Idem.*
[c1-l018 note] Casa completes the household gloss. Kenai no mono is given as an equivalent expression for members of that household.
[c1-l019] Qenarigue. *Couſa de que ſe tem enueja em boa*
[c1-l019 note] Kenarige concerns something regarded with envy or admiration. The qualification em boa parte continues below and makes this a favourable rather than malicious sense.
[c1-l020 >] *parte, ou que ſe deſeja imitar, ou auer. Vt,*
[c1-l020 note] The desirable thing is something one wishes to imitate or possess. This completes the favourable sense of envy and introduces the following example.
[c1-l021 >] Qenarigueni miyuru. *Parece que deſeja,*
[c1-l021 note] Kenarige ni miyuru is rendered as seeming to desire something. The Portuguese subject remains implicit; its alternative explanation continues on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *ou tem enueja in bonam partem.*
[c1-l022 note] The alternative is having envy in a good sense. The Latin phrase in bonam partem is printed within the italic explanatory passage, not as Japanese text.
[c1-l023] Qenarij. l, qenarij coto. *Idem.*
[c1-l023 note] Kenarii and kenarii koto are referred back to the same meaning. The lowercase alternative after l is preserved, as is the final ij spelling.
[c1-l024] Qenba. Inu, vma. *Cão, & caualo.*
[c1-l024 note] Kenba is explained as dog and horse, inu and uma. The visible descending stroke identifies the initial as Q, correcting OCR Cenba.
[c1-l025] Qenbeqi. *Doença dos ombros. No Ximo: ẽ*
[c1-l025 note] Kenbeki is a shoulder ailment. Ximo is an embedded regional name; the visibly slanted lowercase letters support retaining the italic treatment of the explanatory passage, rather than making the term uniformly roman. The following mark is read as a colon and the nasalized ẽ is retained; the compressed regional wording is not silently rewritten.
[c1-l026 >] *outras partes ſe diz,* Feqi.
[c1-l026 note] The regional explanation supplies heki as another name. Feqi is a Japanese word embedded in the Portuguese naming construction, not a new independent headword.
[c1-l027] Qenbin. *Certos bolos de farinha de trigo, ou*
[c1-l027 note] Kenbin names cakes made from wheat flour. The Portuguese continues with a comparison to another kind of cake rather than identifying a second Japanese term.
[c1-l028 >] *filhòs aſados de maneira queſe dobrão como o-*
[c1-l028 note] The cakes are described as baked and folded like thick wafers. The printed single s in aſados and the contiguous queſe are retained; o- begins obreas on the next row.
[c1-l029 >] *breas groſſas.*
[c1-l029 note] Breas completes obreas, wafers, qualified as thick. This short line finishes the description of kenbin.
[c1-l030] Qenbǒ. *Iustiça.*
[c1-l030 note] Kenbou is glossed as justice. The visible caron is preserved; the provisional st classification in Iustiça is left for the dedicated typography audit.
[c1-l031] Qenbǒna. *Couſa recta, ou juſta.*
[c1-l031 note] Kenbou na describes something upright or just. This is the adjectival member of the family continuing in the two indented rows below.
[c1-l032 >] Qenbǒni. *Adu.*
[c1-l032 note] Kenbou ni supplies the adverbial form, with the italic abbreviation Adu. Its indentation associates it with the preceding justice entry.
[c1-l033 >] Qenbǒſa.
[c1-l033 note] Kenbousa gives the corresponding quality noun. The row has no separate Portuguese gloss; its interpretation follows the adjacent adjective and adverb.
[c1-l034] Qenbut. *O ver algũ a couſa, ou vista de al-*
[c1-l034 note] Kenbutsu concerns seeing or viewing something. The printed space within algũ a is retained as source evidence, and the second alguma is divided at the line end.
[c1-l035 >] *gũa couſa por nouidade, ou recreação. Vt,*
[c1-l035 note] The viewing is undertaken for novelty or recreation. Vt introduces the example of an unprecedented sight on the following row.
[c1-l036 >] Iendaimimonno qenbut nari. *He hũa vi-*
[c1-l036 note] Zendai mimon no kenbutsu nari describes an unprecedented spectacle. The scan joins qenbut, correcting the OCR's internal space; initial Ien has the consonantal value explained in the supplied historical notes.
[c1-l037 >] *ſta que tègora ſe não vio, nem ouuio tal.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes vista: a sight whose like has not previously been seen or heard of. Tègora is retained as the printed historical expression for 'until now'.
[c1-l038] Qenbutxa. *Os que vem de nouo, ou por re-*
[c1-l038 note] Kenbutsusha denotes spectators. Here vem belongs to ver, seeing, in the description of people viewing something for novelty or recreation; it is not modernized with an accent.
[c1-l039 >] *creação algũa couſa.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes recreação and the spectators' act of viewing something. The nasal mark in algũa remains on u as printed.
[c1-l040] Qenca. *Gouernador principal.*
[c1-l040 note] Kenka is glossed as a chief governor. The damaged initial retains the visible Q outline; this is a separate office-title entry, not the later word for a quarrel.
[c1-l041] Qencacu. *Homem que traz eſpada. Vt,* Mi-
[c1-l041 note] Kenkaku is a man carrying a sword. Mi- begins michi in the following example; it is a genuine divided Japanese word.
[c1-l042 >] chini qencacuni vǒte, ſubecaracu maſu be
[c1-l042 note] The example describes meeting a swordsman on the road and what one should then do. The scan reads maſu be, although the Portuguese suggests readying or sharpening one's sword; no inferred Japanese replacement is supplied.
[c1-l043 >] xi. *Se encontrares com homem que traz eſ-*
[c1-l043 note] Xi completes bexi, the modal beshi. The Portuguese begins the condition 'if you meet a man carrying a sword', with espada divided across rows.
[c1-l044 >] *pida, tu tambem concerta, & afia tu eſpa-*
[c1-l044 note] The gloss advises likewise putting one's sword in order and sharpening it. The first vowel in the damaged pida fragment remains uncertain; the retained surface reading is contextually espada, not a different lexical item.
[c1-l045 >] *da. i, Preparate.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes espada and adds the explanatory imperative Preparate, 'prepare yourself'. The physical division and the source's unaccented joined form are preserved.
[c1-l046] Qenchi. Suzurino iqe. *Coua q̃ està no tinteiro*
[c1-l046 note] Kenchi is the 'pond' of an inkstone, suzuri no ike. The Portuguese identifies the hollow in the stone inkwell; q̃ is an abbreviation, not an expanded que.
[c1-l047 >] *de pedra da China com agoa pera moer a tin-*
[c1-l047 note] The hollow contains water for grinding ink on a Chinese stone inkstone. Historical agoa and pera are retained, and tinta continues below.
[c1-l048 >] *ta.* S.
[c1-l048 note] Ta completes tinta. The final upright S. labels this written usage and is not part of the italic Portuguese gloss.

[c2-l001] Qenchi. Caxicoi chiye. *Saber, & pruden*
[c2-l001 note] This kenchi is wise understanding, explained by kashikoi chie. The Portuguese prudencia is divided without a securely readable hyphen, so none is supplied.
[c2-l002 >] *cia. ¶* Qenchi naru fito. *Homem de*
[c2-l002 note] Cia completes prudencia. Kenchi naru hito supplies a person characterized by this wisdom; the Portuguese noun phrase continues below.
[c2-l003 >] *bom ſaber, & prudente.*
[c2-l003 note] Completes the description as a person of sound knowledge and prudence. This is the gloss of the preceding Japanese example, not a further headword.
[c2-l004] Qenchi. *O medir as terras. Vt,* Qenchiuo
[c2-l004 note] A distinct kenchi denotes land measurement or surveying. The example begins kenchi wo and is completed by suru on the next row.
[c2-l005 >] ſuru.
[c2-l005 note] Suru completes 'carry out a land survey'. It is a genuine short Japanese continuation, with no independent Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l006] Qencho. *Couſa manifesta, ou deſcuberta. Vt,*
[c2-l006 note] Kencho denotes something evident or revealed. The unmarked final o is preserved; no modern long-vowel spelling is imposed.
[c2-l007 >] Qencho nari. *He couſa manifeſta.* S.
[c2-l007 note] Kencho nari means that something is evident. The final S. is printed upright as the written-usage label.
[c2-l008] Qenchona. *Vt,* Qenchona fito. *Homem*
[c2-l008 note] Kencho na hito describes an open or straightforward person. The repeated Qenchona, confirmed by the scan and entry heading, corrects OCR Qeachona.
[c2-l009 >] *deſcuberto, & claro.*
[c2-l009 note] Describing the person as open and clear completes the example. The Portuguese qualities explain the figurative human application of the preceding adjective.
[c2-l010] Qenchôna. *Peßoa, q̃ ſe eſpanta facelmẽte.*
[c2-l010 note] This separately accented kenchouna describes someone easily frightened. Its circumflex distinguishes it from the preceding unaccented forms; the visibly abbreviated facelmẽte is retained.
[c2-l011] Qencǒ. *Couſa forte, & dura.* S.
[c2-l011 note] Kenkou denotes strength or hardness. The initial is a roman Q, not an italic O, and the caron contrasts with the circumflex in the next entry.
[c2-l012] Qencô. Tçurugui, cabuto. *Eſpada, & ca-*
[c2-l012 note] This kenkou combines sword and helmet, tsurugi and kabuto. The scan's line-end mark divides capacete; the OCR period is corrected to a hyphen.
[c2-l013 >] *pacete.* S.
[c2-l013 note] Pacete completes capacete, helmet. The final S. is a roman written-usage label for the sword-and-helmet entry.
[c2-l014] Qencon. i, Tenchi. *Ceo, & terra.* S.
[c2-l014 note] Kenkon is equated with tenchi, heaven and earth. The synonym after i is not an embedded word in a Portuguese sentence; the final S. remains upright.
[c2-l015] Qencun. Caxicoi qimi. *Senhor prudẽte.* S.
[c2-l015 note] Kenkun means a wise lord, kashikoi kimi. The Q tail corrects OCR Gencun, and the abbreviated prudẽte preserves the printed nasal mark.
[c2-l016] Qendai. *Certa estante que ſerue de pòr o liuro.*
[c2-l016 note] Kendai denotes a stand on which a book is placed. The initial is a roman Q rather than the OCR's italic O; the grave in pòr is preserved.
[c2-l017] Qendan. *Officio de gouernar, & julgar.*
[c2-l017 note] Kendan is the office or function of governing and judging. The Portuguese describes an administrative role rather than a physical object.
[c2-l018 >] *¶* Qendan ſuru. *Fazer este officio como*
[c2-l018 note] Kendan suru means carrying out that office. The visible paragraph mark omitted by OCR is restored; the comparison with a presiding official continues below.
[c2-l019 >] *de preſidente.*
[c2-l019 note] Completes the explanation as acting in the capacity of a president or presiding official. The scan supplies both the space and the missing i in de preſidente.
[c2-l020] Qendannin. *Preſidente, ou gouernador, ou*
[c2-l020 note] Kendannin names the person holding the office: a president, governor, or judge. The final alternative continues on the next physical row.
[c2-l021 >] *juiz dalgum lugar.*
[c2-l021 note] The third equivalent is a judge of some locality. Dalgum remains joined as printed and completes the preceding entry.
[c2-l022] Qendon. *Crueza, deſhumanidade.*
[c2-l022 note] Kendon is cruelty or inhumanity. The small but visible e restores Crueza from OCR Cruza without modernizing the historical noun.
[c2-l023] Qendon fǒit. *Idem.*
[c2-l023 note] Kendon houitsu is assigned the same sense. The initial Q, damaged but matching its neighbours, corrects OCR Dendon; the checked final t is preserved.
[c2-l024] Qendonna. *Couſa cruel, & deſhumana.*
[c2-l024 note] Kendon na is the adjectival form, describing something cruel and inhuman. Its doubled n corresponds to the source's stem-plus-na spelling.
[c2-l025] Qendonni. *Adu. Cruel, & deſhumana*
[c2-l025 note] Kendon ni is the adverbial form. The Portuguese uses the shared -mente construction, with deshumanamente divided after deſhumana and no added hyphen.
[c2-l026 >] *mente.*
[c2-l026 note] Mente completes the adverbial gloss 'cruelly and inhumanly'. Its shortness does not make it furniture or a separate entry.
[c2-l027] Qenen. *O vir, ou o correr ao penſamento.* S.
[c2-l027 note] Kenen is described as something coming or running into one's thoughts. The printed S. is the upright label for written usage.
[c2-l028] Qengacu. Farucani fedataru. *Differença.*
[c2-l028 note] Kengaku is a great difference or separation, explained by haruka ni hedataru. The following clouds-and-mud comparison illustrates the scale of that difference.
[c2-l029 >] *¶* Vndeino qengacu nari. *He differen-*
[c2-l029 note] Undei no kengaku nari describes a difference like that between clouds and mud. The scan contains a paragraph mark before the example, restored here from the OCR omission.
[c2-l030 >] *ça como de nuuẽs, & lama.* S.
[c2-l030 note] Completes differença and explicitly compares clouds with mud. The nasal abbreviation in nuuẽs and the roman S. are preserved.
[c2-l031] Qengacu. Canete manabu. *Eſtudo, ou ter*
[c2-l031 note] A separate kengaku is explained by kanete manabu, studying beforehand. The Portuguese offers both the noun study and a completed prior act.
[c2-l032 >] *estudado dantes.* S.
[c2-l032 note] Ter estudado dantes means having studied previously. The final S. belongs to the usage apparatus and is upright.
[c2-l033] Qengio. Caxicoqi vonna. *Molher prudẽ-*
[c2-l033 note] Kenjo is a wise woman, kashikoki onna. The historical vonna spelling and the visible division of abbreviated prudẽte remain unchanged.
[c2-l034 >] *te, & acautelada, & q̃ não ouue quaes quer*
[c2-l034 note] The woman is prudent and cautious and does not listen to harmful persuasion. Quaes quer remains two printed words; q̃ is not expanded.
[c2-l035 >] *perſuaſões ruins.*
[c2-l035 note] Bad persuasions or counsels complete the preceding relative clause. This qualification belongs to the definition of the prudent woman.
[c2-l036] Qengo. Cataxi, cataxi. i, Tçuyoi coto. *Cou*
[c2-l036 note] Kengo denotes firmness or strength. The repeated kataxi is printed intentionally as the Japanese explanatory sequence; Cou begins couſa below without a supplied hyphen.
[c2-l037 >] *ſa forte.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes couſa forte, a strong thing. This short continuation belongs to kengo and does not introduce an additional entry.
[c2-l038] Qengona. *Cauſa forte. ¶* Qengona fi-
[c2-l038 note] Kengo na is illustrated with a strong or brave person. The scan supports the anomalous Cauſa rather than expected Couſa, so that spelling is preserved and flagged rather than emended.
[c2-l039 >] to. *Homem forte, & valente.*
[c2-l039 note] To completes hito in kengo na hito. The Portuguese explicitly gives a strong and valiant man.
[c2-l040] Qengoni. *Adu. Fortemente. ¶* Qen-
[c2-l040 note] Kengo ni is the adverb, strongly or firmly. Qen- begins kenzoku in the following example about the household's condition.
[c2-l041 >] zocu izzuremo qengoni gozaru. *A gen-*
[c2-l041 note] Kenzoku izure mo kengo ni gozaru says that all the household are well. The printed doubled zz in izzuremo is retained, and gente is divided at the line end.
[c2-l042 >] *te de caſa toda està bem, & em paz, ou bem*
[c2-l042 note] Completes gente and explains that everyone in the house is well and at peace. The alternative bem deſpoſta continues on the next row.
[c2-l043 >] *deſpoſta.*
[c2-l043 note] Deſpoſta completes the statement that the household is in good condition. The feminine form agrees with gente, not with a new Japanese noun.
[c2-l044] Qengoxin. *Fortaleza, ou coração forte.*
[c2-l044 note] Kengoshin is fortitude or a strong heart. The compound's mental quality is explicit in the second Portuguese equivalent.
[c2-l045] Qengu. Caxicoqi mono, guchina mono.
[c2-l045 note] Kengu contrasts a wise person with a foolish one. The scan ends mono with a period, correcting the OCR hyphen; the whole Japanese explanation remains roman.
[c2-l046 >] *Homem prudente, & homem paruo, ou ig-*
[c2-l046 note] The Portuguese likewise contrasts a prudent man with a foolish or ignorant man. Ignorante is visibly divided after ig-.
[c2-l047 >] *norante.*
[c2-l047 note] Norante completes ignorante and the wise-versus-foolish entry. The signature and catchword below it are separate page furniture, not additional body text.
