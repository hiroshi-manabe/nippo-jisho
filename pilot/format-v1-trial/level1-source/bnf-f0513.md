[c1-l001] Tonjeija. *Homem que ſe rapa, & deixa o*
[c1-l001 note] Tonjeija denotes a person who shaves his head and leaves worldly life; the Portuguese relative clause continues on the next row.
[c1-l002 >] *mundo.*
[c1-l002 note] Mundo completes the renunciation of the world. The final rounded o corrects OCR munde; this short continuation belongs to Tonjeija.
[c1-l003] Tonji. l, tonxi. Niuacani xiſuru. *Morte*
[c1-l003 note] Tonji, alternatively tonxi, is sudden death, explained by niwaka ni shisuru. The worn n and j support Tonji rather than the OCR's Toiiji.
[c1-l004 >] *ſubitanea, ou arrebatada.*
[c1-l004 note] Subitanea and arrebatada both qualify morte as sudden or abrupt; they complete the preceding entry rather than introducing another sense.
[c1-l005] Tonji, zuru, ita. i, Tongiacu ſuru. *Eſtar*
[c1-l005 note] Tonji, tonzuru, tonjita is equated with tongiaku suru, being attached to or covetous of something. The Portuguese construction continues below.
[c1-l006 >] *com affeição entregue a algũa couſa. Vt,* Ta-
[c1-l006 note] The person is affectionately attached to some object. Vt introduces the example whose divided Ta- continues as tacara, riches, on the next row.
[c1-l007 >] carani tonzuru. *Estar com cubiça, & af-*
[c1-l007 note] Tacara ni tonzuru describes craving riches, matching cubiça and affeição. The physical Ta-/carani division is retained.
[c1-l008 >] *feição às riquezas. ¶* Vonnani tonzuru.
[c1-l008 note] The gloss completes attachment to riches, then vonna ni tonzuru introduces an analogous desire for women; the initial v is part of the historical spelling.
[c1-l009 >] *Darſe, ou entregarſe a molheres. ¶* Saqe,
[c1-l009 note] Dar-se or entregar-se a mulheres explains the previous example. Saqe begins the following example concerning drink and food.
[c1-l010 >] xocubutni tonzuru. *Ser todo dado a co-*
[c1-l010 note] Saqe, xocubut ni tonzuru means being wholly devoted to eating and drinking. Xocubut retains the historical checked final t before ni.
[c1-l011 >] *mer, & beber.*
[c1-l011 note] Mer completes comer across the physical break; beber completes the paired activities of eating and drinking.
[c1-l012] Tonjiqi. Xocuuo muſaboru. *Gula, ou ape-*
[c1-l012 note] Tonjiqi is gluttony or appetite, paraphrased by shoku wo musaboru, craving food. Reinspection after the supplied NINJAL lexical checkpoint confirms the damaged q and its descender, rather than OCR Tonjioi; muſaboru replaces maiſaboru. Lexical aid: NINJAL, Entry Words Data of Nippojisho, Hideyuki Ohshima and Taichi Aida, version 202510, CC BY 4.0 (https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/textdb_dataset/en/nipp/index.html); this scan decides the transcription.
[c1-l013 >] *tite de comer.*
[c1-l013 note] Tite completes apetite, specified as appetite for eating. The short row is a genuine continuation of Tonjiqi.
[c1-l014] Tonna. i, Niuacana. *Couſa repentina.*
[c1-l014 note] Tonna is equated with niwakana, sudden or unexpected. Niuacana is supported by the printed u and the Portuguese repentina, correcting OCR Ninacana.
[c1-l015] Tonni. *Adu. Repentinamente, ou de re-*
[c1-l015 note] Tonni is the adverb suddenly. Adu. is the italic grammatical label; the second Portuguese expression de repente is divided after re-.
[c1-l016 >] *pente.*
[c1-l016 note] Pente completes de repente, repeating the suddenness sense of Tonni rather than adding an independent entry.
[c1-l017] Tono. *Senhor de algũa terra, ou que tem cria*
[c1-l017 note] Tono is a lord of land or a person with retainers. The printed cria continues as criados without a supplied line-end hyphen.
[c1-l018 >] *dos, ou renda, &c. ¶ Item, Marido.*
[c1-l018 note] Dos completes criados, followed by income as another mark of lordship. The paragraph sign then introduces the distinct use meaning husband.
[c1-l019 >] *Palaura de que vſão algũas molheres honradas*
[c1-l019 note] The husband sense is identified as a word used by some respectable women. The worn initial h of honradas is read with the Portuguese context, not as OCR bonradas.
[c1-l020 >] *falando de ſeus maridos.*
[c1-l020 note] Falando de seus maridos completes the usage restriction: these women use tono when speaking of their husbands.
[c1-l021] Tonobara. *Homem eſcudeiro menos que fidal-*
[c1-l021 note] Tonobara is described as an esquire below a fidalgo or knight. The line ends with visibly divided fidal-, not the OCR period.
[c1-l022 >] *go, & caualeiro.*
[c1-l022 note] Go completes fidalgo, followed by caualeiro. The comparison concerns social rank in the preceding Tonobara definition.
[c1-l023] Tonodono. *Senhores de vaſſalos. Item,*
[c1-l023 note] Tonodono denotes lords with vassals; Item then introduces another sense whose explanation follows on the next row.
[c1-l024 >] *Caſas de ſenhores, ou fidalgos que estão juntas.*
[c1-l024 note] The second Tonodono sense is the adjacent houses of lords or noblemen. The plural casas governs the final juntas.
[c1-l025] Tǒno fa. *Certa laya de penas de hum paßaro*
[c1-l025 note] Tǒ no fa denotes feathers of a particular bird, valued for arrows. The final s of penas is severely abraded but a residual stroke remains after a; the plural boas below also supports the adopted reading. The exact bird is not identified beyond its printed name.
[c1-l026 >] *chamado, Tǒ, boas pera frechas.*
[c1-l026 note] The bird is called Tǒ; its feathers are suitable for arrows. Enlargement shows a downward caron rather than the OCR grave. The name retains the italic type visible within the Portuguese explanation and is marked separately for whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l027] Tonoi. *Vigias. ¶* Tonoibaramaqi. *Ar-*
[c1-l027 note] Tonoi means night watch or watchers. Tonoibaramaqi introduces the armour worn by these guards, with armas divided across the physical break.
[c1-l028 >] *mas que vestem eſtas vigias.*
[c1-l028 note] Mas completes armas; the line specifies armour worn by the watchers. The reference is protective equipment rather than weapons held in the hand.
[c1-l029] Tonoibito. i, Banxu. *Vigia.*
[c1-l029 note] Tonoibito is a watchman, equated with banxu and glossed Vigia. The damaged Portuguese word is clarified by the repeated vigias above and below.
[c1-l030] Tonoidoqi. *Grita dos ſoldados quando estão*
[c1-l030 note] Tonoidoqi denotes the soldiers’ shout while at war. Grita and soldados correct OCR Orita and saldados; the following lines explain the occasion.
[c1-l031 >] *na guerra com que acodem todos às tardes com*
[c1-l031 note] The soldiers respond together in the evening while campaigning. The sentence continues with their drawn swords and the preceding signal.
[c1-l032 >] *as Catanas nuas depois de ouuirem o ſinal da*
[c1-l032 note] The soldiers hold naked or drawn catanas after hearing a horn signal. Nuas and ſinal correct the garbled aias and ſinol without modernizing the Portuguese spelling.
[c1-l033 >] *buzina.*
[c1-l033 note] Buzina completes the horn signal that occasions the collective cry. It is a short continuation of the Tonoidoqi description.
[c1-l034] Tonoimono. *Vestido de que vſão eſtas vigias*
[c1-l034 note] Tonoimono is clothing used by the watchers; the following line explains its additional function during the night watch.
[c1-l035 >] *de noite em lugar de cama, ou cobertor, & q̃*
[c1-l035 note] The garment serves at night instead of a bed or cover. The abbreviation q̃ introduces the final clause and is not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l036 >] *fazem leuar ao lugar da vigia.*
[c1-l036 note] The watchers have this garment carried to their watch station. This completes the description of Tonoimono as portable bedding or covering.
[c1-l037] Tǒno tçuchi. *Aluayade.*
[c1-l037 note] Tǒ no tçuchi is glossed Aluayade, white lead. The caron and the separated tçuchi are retained rather than replaced with modern Japanese spelling.
[c1-l038] Tonozzucuri. *O edificar caſas principaes,*
[c1-l038 note] Tonozzucuri refers to building important residences. The scan supports principaes, correcting the OCR peinãpaes; the alternative paços follows below.
[c1-l039 >] *ou paços.*
[c1-l039 note] Ou paços identifies palaces or grand residences as the alternative expression for the houses being built.
[c1-l040] Tonpi. *Vt,* Tonpiuo vtçuſazu. *Depreßa,*
[c1-l040 note] Tonpi is illustrated by tonpi wo utsusazu, acting without delay. The Portuguese Depreßa supplies the practical meaning; the historical Japanese phrase is retained.
[c1-l041 >] *ſem tardança.*
[c1-l041 note] Sem tardança completes the explanation as without delay. It belongs to the preceding example, not the following entry on mental quickness.
[c1-l042] Tonqi. Fayaqi qi. *Viueza, ou animo eſper-*
[c1-l042 note] Tonqi is explained by hayaqi qi, quick disposition, and by vivacity or an alert mind. The last Portuguese adjective continues below.
[c1-l043 >] *to, & habil. ¶* Tonqina fito. *Homẽ*
[c1-l043 note] To completes esperto; habil adds ability. Tonqina fito then illustrates an alert or capable person, whose Portuguese gloss continues below.
[c1-l044 >] *viuo, eſperto, & habil.*
[c1-l044 note] Vivo, esperto and habil complete the characterization of the Tonqina fito as lively, quick and capable.
[c1-l045] Tonſacu. *Obra feita derepente, ou dito.*
[c1-l045 note] Tonſacu denotes an impromptu work or saying, made suddenly. The compact derepente is retained as printed.
[c1-l046 >] *¶* Tonſacuna fito. *Homem que diz, ou*
[c1-l046 note] Tonſacuna fito is someone who speaks or acts promptly; the Portuguese relative clause continues on the following row.
[c1-l047 >] *faz as couſas de preßa, & ſem muito cuidar.*
[c1-l047 note] The person does things quickly and without much prior thought. This is an indented continuation of the Tonſacuna fito gloss, not a fresh entry.

[c2-l001] Tonſocu. *Couſa apreſſada, oufeita com preſ*
[c2-l001 note] Tonſocu means something hurried or accomplished quickly. The scan supports n in the headword rather than OCR m; preſ continues as presteza without a supplied hyphen.
[c2-l002 >] *teza, & ligeiramente.*
[c2-l002 note] Teza completes presteza; ligeiramente adds the sense of speed or lightness. The row belongs to the Tonſocu definition.
[c2-l003] Tonſocuna. *Idem.*
[c2-l003 note] Tonſocuna is the corresponding adjectival form, referred back by Idem to the preceding hurried or quick sense.
[c2-l004] Tonſocuni. *Adu.*
[c2-l004 note] Tonſocuni is the adverbial form of the same expression. Adu. supplies the grammatical category without repeating the whole definition.
[c2-l005] Tonxa. Niuacani vtçuſu. *Tresladar de*
[c2-l005 note] Tonxa is rapid copying, explained by niwaka ni utsusu. The visible cedilla in vtçuſu and l in Tresladar correct OCR vtcuſu and Trestadar.
[c2-l006 >] *preſſa o Foquequiǒ repartindo as folhas por*
[c2-l006 note] The Lotus Sutra, named Foquequiǒ, is copied quickly by distributing its leaves. Enlargement shows two long-s types in preſſa and a caron, not a tilde, over the book title’s final o. The title retains the surrounding italic type; its Japanese identity is recorded separately for whole-word typeface review.
[c2-l007 >] *muitos eſcriuaẽs fazẽ iſto por algũ defuncto*
[c2-l007 note] The leaves are divided among many scribes, who perform the copying for a deceased person. The abbreviated fazẽ and algũ remain unexpanded.
[c2-l008 >] *nas exequias, ou enterramento, &c.*
[c2-l008 note] The copying takes place at funeral rites or burial. The printed double r in enterramento corrects the single-r OCR reading.
[c2-l009] Tonxei. l, tonjei. Youo nogaruru. *O rapar-*
[c2-l009 note] Tonxei, alternatively tonjei, is leaving worldly life, paraphrased by yo wo nogaruru. The Portuguese begins with shaving the head.
[c2-l010 >] *ſe. i, Deixar a caſa, ou o mundo. Vt,* Ten-
[c2-l010 note] Shaving is explained as leaving one’s house or the world. The example visibly begins Ten-, an anomalous vowel beside the headword Tonxei; it is retained rather than silently corrected.
[c2-l011 >] xeino mitonaru. *Fazerſe rapado desta*
[c2-l011 note] Ten-/xeino mi to naru means becoming a person who has renounced worldly life. The Portuguese describes becoming shaven in that manner; the full desta replaces OCR dest-.
[c2-l012 >] *maneira.*
[c2-l012 note] Maneira completes desta maneira. The visible e corrects OCR mancira and closes the renunciation example.
[c2-l013] Tonxi. l, tonji. Niuacani xiſuru. *Morte ſu-*
[c2-l013 note] Tonxi, alternatively tonji, repeats the sudden-death entry, paraphrased by niwaka ni shisuru. The Portuguese adjective is divided after su-.
[c2-l014 >] *bitanea. Vt,* Tonxi xerareta. l, xita. *Mor-*
[c2-l014 note] Bitanea completes subitanea. Tonxi xerareta, alternatively xita, gives the past-tense example, translated by morreo across the line break.
[c2-l015 >] *reo de morte ſubitanea.*
[c2-l015 note] Reo completes morreo: the person died suddenly. The scan supports ſubitanea rather than the OCR’s extra c in ſubitancea.
[c2-l016] Tonxin. Muſaboru cocoro. *Coração de aua-*
[c2-l016 note] Tonxin is a covetous or craving heart, explained by musaboru kokoro. The Portuguese begins the description of an avaricious person.
[c2-l017 >] *rento, cubiçoſo, & ſenſual, &c. S.*
[c2-l017 note] Rento completes avarento, joined by covetous and sensual. The final upright S. labels written usage rather than belonging to the italic gloss.
[c2-l018] Tonxu. *Palaura de corteſia, & humildade*
[c2-l018 note] Tonxu is a deferential formula expressing courtesy and humility. The scan supports Palaura, correcting OCR Paſaura.
[c2-l019 >] *que ſe eſcreue no cabo das cartas. Vt,* Qiô-
[c2-l019 note] The formula is written at the close of letters. Qiô- begins the example repeated across the line break.
[c2-l020 >] qiô tonxu.
[c2-l020 note] Qiô-qiô tonxu is the formal epistolary expression. Both qiô syllables have circumflexes; the second is not the OCR caron.
[c2-l021] Tonyocu. i, Yocuxin. *Auareza, ou cubiça.*
[c2-l021 note] Tonyocu is equated with yocuxin and glossed as avarice or covetousness. The headword’s desire sense accords with the surrounding ton- entries.
[c2-l022] Toppa. l, toppana. *Homem a tabalhoado,*
[c2-l022 note] Toppa, alternatively toppana, characterizes a heedless or disordered person. The visible spacing of a tabalhoado is retained as printed.
[c2-l023 >] *ou deſatentado. Vt,* Toppana mono. *Idẽ.*
[c2-l023 note] Desatentado supplies the alternative characterization, and toppana mono illustrates the same kind of person. Idẽ refers to the preceding definition.
[c2-l024] Toppo. i, Fitori ayomu. *O andar, ou ir*
[c2-l024 note] Toppo here means walking alone, explained by fitori ayomu. The printed ayomu is retained rather than modernized to ayumu.
[c2-l025 >] *hum ſoo.*
[c2-l025 note] Hum soo completes the idea of one person going or walking alone; it belongs to the first Toppo entry.
[c2-l026] Toppo. *O eſtar farto, ou ter comido tè mais*
[c2-l026 note] A second Toppo entry describes satiation or having eaten to one’s limit. The two p letters and final s of mais correct OCR Toupo and mair.
[c2-l027 >] *não poder, ou eſtar cheo, ou com ſobejo viço*
[c2-l027 note] The description continues with being unable to eat more, being full, or having excessive plenty and indulgence.
[c2-l028 >] *&c. ¶* Toppono amarini. *Com o de-*
[c2-l028 note] Toppono amarini introduces the consequence of excessive fullness; the Portuguese begins com o demasiado across the line break.
[c2-l029 >] *maſiado comer, & fartura, ou viço. Vt,*
[c2-l029 note] Masiado completes demasiado: excessive eating, satiety or indulgence. Vt introduces a full Japanese example on the following line.
[c2-l030 >] Toppono amarini monouo ſuru. *Fazer*
[c2-l030 note] Toppono amarini mono wo suru means doing something out of excessive satiation or indulgence. Fazer begins the explanatory Portuguese clause.
[c2-l031 >] *algũa couſa com que hum moſtra eſtar muito*
[c2-l031 note] The action demonstrates that someone is very full or satiated. The sentence is continued on the following physical row.
[c2-l032 >] *farto, ou cheo, ou com ſobigidão dalgũa couſa,*
[c2-l032 note] The person is full, supplied abundantly with something, or indulged. The historical ſobigidão is retained rather than replaced by a modern paraphrase.
[c2-l033 >] *& viço.*
[c2-l033 note] E viço completes the list of fullness and indulgence associated with the action described above.
[c2-l034] Tǒqe. *Eſta caſa, ou familia. ¶* Tǒqeno
[c2-l034 note] Tǒqe denotes this house or family. The example Tǒqeno begins a phrase completed by fito on the next row.
[c2-l035 >] fito. *Homem deſta caſa, ou familia.*
[c2-l035 note] Fito completes Tǒqeno fito, a person of this house or family, precisely matching the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l036] Tǒqe. *Nouidade, ou ſementeira deſte an-*
[c2-l036 note] A second Tǒqe denotes the current year’s crop or sowing. The capital T is clear in the scan, correcting OCR Fǒqe.
[c2-l037 >] *no de arroz que eſtà ainda em eſpiga no cam-*
[c2-l037 note] No completes anno; the entry concerns rice still standing in ears in the field. Campo is divided across this line and the next.
[c2-l038 >] *po. Vt,* Tǒqega yoi. l, varui. *Eſtar a*
[c2-l038 note] Po completes campo. Tǒqe ga yoi, alternatively varui, describes the crop as good or bad; the translation continues below.
[c2-l039 >] *ſementeira boa, ou ruim.*
[c2-l039 note] Sementeira boa ou ruim completes the contrast of a good and a poor crop; it is not a further headword.
[c2-l040] Toqe, ru, eta. *Desatarſe. ¶ Item, Der-*
[c2-l040 note] Toqe first means coming untied, then melting; the following example uses toquru. The second Portuguese infinitive is divided after Der-.
[c2-l041 >] *reterſe. Vt,* Rǒga toquru. *Derreterſe a ce-*
[c2-l041 note] Reterſe completes derreterse. Rǒ ga toquru gives melting wax; cera is divided after ce- at the end of the row.
[c2-l042 >] *ra. ¶* Cocoro toqete monouo yǔ. *Fa-*
[c2-l042 note] Ra completes cera. Kokoro tokete mono wo yuu illustrates speaking with one’s reserve dissolved, followed by the divided Portuguese falar.
[c2-l043 >] *lar ſẽ ter pejo; ou muito familiarmente.*
[c2-l043 note] Lar completes falar: speaking without embarrassment or very familiarly. The gloss explains the figurative opening of the heart in the preceding example.
[c2-l044] Toqeai, ǒ, ǒta. *Derreterenſe muitas couſas*
[c2-l044 note] Toqeai gives the reciprocal or collective melting of several things. The printed Derreterenſe is preserved without regularizing its inflection.
[c2-l045 >] *jũtas como caramelos, ou neues de muitas partes.*
[c2-l045 note] The things melt together, exemplified by ice formations and snow from several places. This is a continuation of Toqeai rather than a new entry.
[c2-l046] Toqei. Vſaguino michi. *Caminho eſtreito da*
[c2-l046 note] Toqei is the hare’s narrow path, equated with usagi no michi. The Portuguese begins its physical description among grass and scrub.
[c2-l047 >] *lebre por antre as eruas, ou mato. ¶* Daizǒ
[c2-l047 note] The hare’s path runs through grass or brush. Daizǒ begins the elephant example continued on the next page; the separate bottom toqeini is a catchword, not another body row.
