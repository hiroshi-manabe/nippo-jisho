[c1-l001] Vari. l, xiuono vari. *Marè vazia, ou baixa mar.*
[c1-l001 note] Wari, alternatively shio no wari, denotes ebb or low tide. The scan separates the alternative marker l from the headword; xiuo is the historical spelling of shio.
[c1-l002] Vari, u, atta. *Fender, ou rachar como canas,*
[c1-l002 note] Wari, waru, watta means splitting or cracking. The Portuguese begins with canes as objects that can be split; the principal parts retain their printed abbreviated form.
[c1-l003 >] *paos, &c. Item, Quebrar como louça, va*
[c1-l003 note] Wood continues the first list, and Item introduces breaking pottery or vessels. The physical row ends with va, continued by ſos below, without adding a conjectural hyphen.
[c1-l004 >] *ſos, &c. ¶* Pãouo varu. *Partir o pão*
[c1-l004 note] The Japanese example pan wo waru means breaking bread, matching Partir o pão. The scan has varu rather than OCR vatu, and the whole Japanese example, including Pãouo, is upright.
[c1-l005 >] *com as mãos. ¶* Xiuoga varu. *Vazar*
[c1-l005 note] Com as mãos specifies breaking bread by hand. Shio ga waru then introduces the tide's recession, whose Portuguese explanation continues on the next row.
[c1-l006 >] *amarè de todo. ¶* Givariuo ſuru. l, giuo
[c1-l006 note] The tide ebbs completely. The next example, jiwari wo suru, alternatively ji wo waru, turns to surveying or dividing land; its second variant continues below.
[c1-l007 >] varu. *Medir as terras, & repartilas.*
[c1-l007 note] Varu completes ji wo waru. Medir as terras, & repartilas explains both measuring and allotting land, not a continuation of the tidal sense.
[c1-l008 >] *¶* Xirouo varu. *Desfazer, ou derrubar*
[c1-l008 note] Shiro wo waru means dismantling a fortress. Desfazer and derrubar introduce the two Portuguese verbs governing a fortaleza on the following line.
[c1-l009 >] *a fortaleza. ¶ Item, junto cõ outros verbos*
[c1-l009 note] A fortaleza completes the preceding object. The entry then explains that waru has various meanings when compounded with other verbs, introducing the examples below.
[c1-l010 >] *tem varias ſignificaçoẽs. Vt,* Vchivaru. *Que-*
[c1-l010 note] Uchiwaru exemplifies breaking by striking. The Portuguese Que- continues into brar on the next row; the division is retained as printed.
[c1-l011 >] *brar cõ pancadas.* Qirivaru. *Cortando fen-*
[c1-l011 note] The first gloss ends with breaking by blows. Qirivaru, read kiriwaru, then means splitting by cutting; the upright i corrects OCR Qurivaru, and fen- continues below.
[c1-l012 >] *der. ¶* Voxivaru. *Quebrar por força. ¶* Na-
[c1-l012 note] Der completes fender. Oshiwaru, written Voxivaru, is glossed as breaking by force; the visible f in força replaces OCR long ſ. Na- starts nagewaru.
[c1-l013 >] guevaru. *Quebrar arremeſando. ¶* Fiqiva-
[c1-l013 note] Guevaru completes nagewaru, breaking by throwing. The following Fiqiva- starts hikiwaru, whose explanation shifts to crushing by grinding.
[c1-l014 >] ru. *Partir moendo. Vt,* Comeuo fiqivaru.
[c1-l014 note] Ru completes hikiwaru. The example kome wo hikiwaru applies it to rice, and the Portuguese explicitly says breaking up by grinding.
[c1-l015 >] *Moer arroz de maneira que não fique em fari-*
[c1-l015 note] The rice is ground in such a way that it does not become flour. The negative não is essential to the contrast with small pieces on the following row.
[c1-l016 >] *nha, mas empedacinhos.*
[c1-l016 note] Nha completes farinha; the rice is instead left in small pieces. The printed joined empedacinhos is preserved rather than normalized to modern word spacing.
[c1-l017] Variate, tçuru, eta. l, varitçuqe, quru.
[c1-l017 note] Wariate and waritsuke concern assigning something by division. The printed principal parts tçuru, eta and quru are preserved; the allocation sense is developed in the following Portuguese rows.
[c1-l018 >] *Diuidindo, ou fazendo partição encarregar a*
[c1-l018 note] The Portuguese explains allocating a share or task to someone. Encarregar a begins its personal complement on the following row.
[c1-l019 >] *alguem como parede, vallo, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] Alguem completes the recipient, and a wall or embankment illustrates the work apportioned. The clause belongs to the preceding allocation verbs.
[c1-l020] Varicudaqi, qu, ita. *Quebrando fazer em*
[c1-l020 note] Warikudaki, warikudaku, warikudaita means breaking into little pieces. The Portuguese predicate fazer em continues into migalhas below.
[c1-l021 >] *migalhas.*
[c1-l021 note] Migalhas completes the phrase for breaking into crumbs or fragments. The short continuation is a complete physical body row.
[c1-l022] Varifu. *Hũa parte da mutra que ſe faz em*
[c1-l022 note] Warifu denotes one part of a matching tally or token. Mutra is retained as the historical Portuguese term visible in the scan, rather than replaced by a modern equivalent.
[c1-l023 >] *dous papeis, ou em duas meas canas pera de-*
[c1-l023 note] The matching marks are made on two papers or two halves of a cane. The terminal de- belongs to depois on the next physical row.
[c1-l024 >] *pois ſe ſaber ſe reſponde hũa a outra. ¶* Va-
[c1-l024 note] The two parts can subsequently be checked for correspondence. Va- begins the example warifu ga oota, continued below.
[c1-l025 >] rifuga vǒta. *Quadrarhũa ametade da mu-*
[c1-l025 note] Warifu ga oota means that the tally parts matched. The Portuguese ametade, meaning one half, is damaged but supports t rather than OCR l; the printed joined Quadrarhũa remains.
[c1-l026 >] *tra com a outra.*
[c1-l026 note] Tra completes mutra: one half of the token agrees with the other. This closes the matching-token example before the travel-food entry.
[c1-l027] Varigo. *Comer leue, & facil que ſe leua pera*
[c1-l027 note] Warigo first denotes light, easily carried provisions. The definition continues with food for a journey and then distinguishes the container's stricter meaning.
[c1-l028 >] *o caminho como algũs Mochis, &c. propria-*
[c1-l028 note] Mochis illustrates travel food within the Portuguese explanation. Propria- begins propriamente, which introduces the more literal box sense below.
[c1-l029 >] *mente he hũa caixa que antigamente ſeruia de*
[c1-l029 note] Strictly speaking, warigo is a box formerly used for carrying food. The line connects to Bentǒ on the following row rather than introducing a new headword.
[c1-l030 >] *Bentǒ pera leuarem algum comer dentro.*
[c1-l030 note] Bentǒ names the food container in the Portuguese explanation. The scan supports the caron over o and the words algum and dentro, replacing OCR Bentõ, aigum, and demtro.
[c1-l031] Varinai. *Couſa impertinente, ou que alguem*
[c1-l031 note] Warinai describes an unreasonable or difficult matter. The Portuguese develops the sense of something a person will be reluctant to concede or relinquish.
[c1-l032 >] *concederà, ou apartarà de ſi difficultoſamen-*
[c1-l032 note] The person will concede it or part with it only with difficulty. The scan supports doubled f in difficultoſamen-, and the adverb continues on the next row without a word boundary before men-.
[c1-l033 >] *te. Vt,* Varinai coto naredomo. *Ain-*
[c1-l033 note] Te completes the adverb on the preceding row. Warinai koto naredomo introduces a concessive request: although the matter is unreasonable or difficult.
[c1-l034 >] *da que he couſa impertinente, & que com dif-*
[c1-l034 note] The Portuguese begins the concession: although it is an inconvenient or unreasonable matter. Dif- starts the noun dificuldade below.
[c1-l035 >] *ficuldade hajais de apartar de vos, ou cõceder.*
[c1-l035 note] Ficuldade completes dificuldade, correcting OCR ficuidade. The addressee will have difficulty parting with or granting what is requested.
[c1-l036] Varinocai. l, fiqivarigai. *Canja feita de ar-*
[c1-l036 note] Wari no kai, alternatively hikiwarigai, denotes gruel made from crushed rice. The terminal ar- begins arroz on the next row.
[c1-l037 >] *roz moido, & feito empedacinhos.*
[c1-l037 note] The rice is ground into small pieces before use in the gruel. This agrees with the earlier hikiwaru entry's explicit distinction between pieces and flour.
[c1-l038] Vari iri, ru, itta. *Entrar, ou romper por en-*
[c1-l038 note] Wari iri means forcing a way into a crowd. The initial is V, correcting OCR N, and the visible separation between Vari and iri is retained; ru and itta are its abbreviated principal parts.
[c1-l039 >] *tre gente. Vt,* Teqino nacaye vari iru, l,
[c1-l039 note] The example teki no naka ye wari iru means pushing into the enemy's midst. The alternative construction introduced by l is supplied on the next row.
[c1-l040 >] vatte iru. *Romper, ou entrar pollo meo*
[c1-l040 note] Watte iru gives the alternative phrase. The Portuguese says breaking or entering through the middle, with the enemies specified below.
[c1-l041 >] *dos inimigos.*
[c1-l041 note] Dos inimigos completes the object of the preceding phrase. The brief row remains part of the example of forcing entry among enemies.
[c1-l042] Varitçuqe, uru, eta. *Vide,* Variate, tçuru.
[c1-l042 note] Waritsuke refers back to wariate and its allocation sense. The scan shows tç in Varitçuqe, not OCR a ç; the cross-reference is retained rather than expanded.
[c1-l043] Varǒ. l, ſumitçubono cugui. *Preguinho a*
[c1-l043 note] Warǒ is equated with sumitsubo no kugi, the little pin used with a carpenter's ink line. The caron over o is visible and retained.
[c1-l044 >] *que ſe amarra a linha do carpinteiro.*
[c1-l044 note] The carpenter's line is tied to the small pin. Carpinteiro is one printed word; the OCR's car pinteiro introduced a false word boundary.
[c1-l045] Varobe. *Minino.*
[c1-l045 note] Varobe denotes a child or boy, matching Minino. NINJAL lists Varǒbe, but this impression does not establish a caron above the o; the unaccented scan reading is retained.
[c1-l046] Varubire, ruru, eta. *Mostrar alguem couar-*
[c1-l046 note] Warubire describes showing cowardice or timidity. The Portuguese couar- continues as dia below; the principal parts preserve the historical ruru form.
[c1-l047 >] *dia. Vt,* Iromo fenjezu varubireta tei-
[c1-l047 note] Iro mo henjezu describes not changing colour, while warubireta tei begins the phrase about a cowardly appearance. The example continues at the top of the second column; fenjezu is retained despite local abrasion.

[c2-l001 >] monǒ qiraruru. *Ser cortado ſem mudar*
[c2-l001 note] Monǒ completes the negative expression about showing a cowardly appearance; kiraruru is being cut down. Mudar, not OCR mudas, begins the Portuguese phrase about not changing colour.
[c2-l002 >] *as cores, nem moſtrar geito de couardia.*
[c2-l002 note] The Portuguese completes the cross-column example: without changing colour or showing any sign of cowardice. Its indentation marks a continuation of the preceding headword's example.
[c2-l003] Varugurui. *Ruins brincos, ou que não conuẽ.*
[c2-l003 note] Warugurui denotes improper or unruly play. Ruins brincos is qualified as play that is unsuitable or unbecoming.
[c2-l004 >] *¶* Varuguruiuo ſuru. *Brincar ſolta, &*
[c2-l004 note] Warugurui wo suru is the corresponding action, playing without restraint. The Portuguese manner phrase continues below.
[c2-l005 >] *deſordenadamente.*
[c2-l005 note] Deſordenadamente completes the description of playing in a disordered or unruly fashion. It is indented continuation text, not an independent entry.
[c2-l006] Varui. *Couſa ruim, ou que não conuem.*
[c2-l006 note] Warui means bad or unsuitable. The following short rows supply its related adverbial and nominal forms.
[c2-l007 >] Varǔ.
[c2-l007 note] Varǔ is the long-vowel adverbial form related to warui. The caron is clearly printed; this short form remains a separate physical row.
[c2-l008 >] Varuſa.
[c2-l008 note] Warusa gives the corresponding noun for badness. The long ſ is a letterform, not a vowel accent, and the row belongs with the adjective above.
[c2-l009] Vaſa. *Laço com que ſe arma aos paſſaros, &c.*
[c2-l009 note] Wasa denotes a snare used for catching birds. The Portuguese identifies the device by its trapping function.
[c2-l010] Vaſabi. *Hũa certa fruita do mato que ſerue pe*
[c2-l010 note] Wasabi is described as a wild fruit or plant product. The historical description is retained as given; pera is divided pe / ra across the physical rows.
[c2-l011 >] *ra Xiru frio, & outras iguarias.*
[c2-l011 note] Wasabi is used for cold shiru and other dishes. Xiru is embedded in the Portuguese explanation and appears in the same italic setting here, rather than the OCR's isolated upright X.
[c2-l012] Vaſabizu. *Salſa feita desta fruita,* Vaſabi.
[c2-l012 note] Wasabizu is a sauce made from wasabi. The headword's weak a is visible between ſ and b; fruita, not OCR frunta, identifies the ingredient in the explanation.
[c2-l013] Vaſada. *Varzeas que vem com o arroz cedo-*
[c2-l013 note] Wasada denotes fields whose rice matures early. The Portuguese describes the fields as coming with rice cedo, followed by the alternative wording below.
[c2-l014 >] *ou temporãas.*
[c2-l014 note] Temporãas completes the description of early-producing fields. Its printed nasal mark is retained rather than regularized from modern Portuguese spelling.
[c2-l015] Vaſamono. *Couſa temporaã, ou que vem ce-*
[c2-l015 note] Wasamono is something that comes or ripens early. The gloss's final ce- continues into do, and the printed temporaã retains its occurrence-specific tilde placement.
[c2-l016 >] *do. Principalmente ſe diz de arroz, trigo,ou*
[c2-l016 note] The term chiefly applies to rice, wheat, and the further crop category on the next line. Trigo,ou retains the close spacing visible in the gloss.
[c2-l017 >] *outro legume.*
[c2-l017 note] Outro legume completes the list of early crops. Legume is left in its broad historical usage rather than restricted to a modern botanical class.
[c2-l018] Vaſan. *Louuores que ſe dão aos Camis, & Fo*
[c2-l018 note] Wasan denotes praises offered to kami and buddhas. Camis and the divided Fotoques are Japanese terms embedded in the Portuguese explanation; Fo continues on the next row without an invented hyphen.
[c2-l019 >] *toques em eſtillo, ou palauras de Iapão.*
[c2-l019 note] Toques completes Fotoques. The praises use Japanese style or language; em, not OCR ent, is the preposition introducing eſtillo.
[c2-l020] Vaſauaſato. *Modo de eſtar, ou fazer algũa*
[c2-l020 note] Wasawasato describes doing or being in a lively, ready, cheerful manner. The explanation continues into its three manner terms on the next line.
[c2-l021 >] *couſa viua, prompta, & alegremente. ¶* Va-
[c2-l021 note] The lively, prompt, cheerful manner completes the gloss. Va- begins an example involving a lively gathering in a room.
[c2-l022 >] ſauaſato xitazaxiqi. *Ajuntamẽto de gente*
[c2-l022 note] Wasawasato shita zashiki describes a lively gathering or room. Ajuntamẽto de gente introduces the people whose manner is elaborated below.
[c2-l023 >] *q̃ moſtra eſtar contente, viua, & prazenteira*
[c2-l023 note] The gathering appears contented, animated, and cheerful. The abbreviated q̃ remains unexpanded, with its visible separation from moſtra restored.
[c2-l024 >] *no Zaxiqui.*
[c2-l024 note] No Zaxiqui places the gathering in the room. Zaxiqui is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese phrase, not a new standalone example.
[c2-l025] Vaſameqi, u, eita. *Modo de eſtar, ou falar*
[c2-l025 note] Wasameki, wasameku, wasameita means being or speaking animatedly. The final Portuguese r in falar is compressed by the right-edge curvature but visible, unlike OCR fala.
[c2-l026 >] *com viueza, alegria, &c. ou de fazer eſtrõ-*
[c2-l026 note] The action is performed with liveliness and joy, or makes noise. The visible terminal mark is retained in eſtrõ-, which continues as do below.
[c2-l027 >] *do, & alucroço.*
[c2-l027 note] Do completes the noise term; alucroço denotes commotion in context. The middle letters are worn and the OCR spelling is provisionally retained rather than silently modernized to alvoroço.
[c2-l028] Vasô. l, Vasôga. *Palaura com que chamão*
[c2-l028 note] Vasô, alternatively Vasôga, introduces a disparaging address to a Buddhist monk. The short s agrees with the adjacent variant and corrects OCR Vatô.
[c2-l029 >] *a algum Bonzo por tu, abatendoo, ou ameaçã*
[c2-l029 note] The monk is addressed as tu to belittle or threaten him. Algum corrects OCR aigum; the compressed final ameaçã retains its visible nasal mark and continues as doo below.
[c2-l030 >] *doo, &c.*
[c2-l030 note] Doo completes the preceding threatening expression, with the object suffix referring to the monk. The short row remains part of the address-term entry.
[c2-l031] Vaſure, uru, eta. *Eſquecerſe.*
[c2-l031 note] Wasure denotes forgetting, directly glossed Eſquecerſe. The historical principal parts uru and eta remain as printed rather than being expanded or modernized.
[c2-l032] Vaſure gatami. *Lembrança, ou couſa que ſe*
[c2-l032 note] Wasuregatami is a keepsake or object left as a remembrance. Lembrança is one word, and the compressed final Portuguese word is ſe rather than OCR ſi.
[c2-l033 >] *deixa pera memoria.*
[c2-l033 note] The object is left to preserve someone's memory. This completes the keepsake definition rather than introducing a second sense of forgetfulness.
[c2-l034] Vaſureguſa. *Erua aßi chamada. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l034 note] Wasuregusa first names a plant. The paragraph marker and Item introduce its further poetic use, explained below.
[c2-l035 >] *Eſquecimento apud poetas.*
[c2-l035 note] Among poets the word denotes forgetting or oblivion. Eſquecimento is a single word; apud poetas is the Latin usage qualification.
[c2-l036] Vaſſarito. *Adu. Modo de ſe aluoroçar, ou*
[c2-l036 note] Wassarito is labelled an adverb describing excitement or joyful celebration. The visible doubled long-s types are retained in the headword.
[c2-l037 >] *feſtejar algũa couſa.*
[c2-l037 note] Celebrating something completes the adverb's explanation. The abbreviated algũa remains unexpanded and distinct from other tilde placements on this page.
[c2-l038] Vaſſocu. *Modo de trazer o montante, ou ca-*
[c2-l038 note] Wassoku concerns carrying a great sword or katana slung across the body. The final ca- belongs to catana on the following row.
[c2-l039 >] *tana a tira collo. Vt,* Tachi vaſſocuni ca-
[c2-l039 note] A tira collo describes carrying it on a sling. Tachi wassoku ni kakete begins the Japanese example, with kakete divided at the printed ca- / qete boundary.
[c2-l040 >] qete. *Põdo o montãte, ou eſpada atira collo.*
[c2-l040 note] Qete completes kakete. The Portuguese describes putting the great sword or sword in that position; the nasal abbreviation in montãte replaces OCR montà te without expanding it.
[c2-l041] Vata. l, *minùs proprie.* Mauata. *Borra.*
[c2-l041 note] Wata denotes silk floss or wadding. Mauata is given as a less proper alternative, qualified by the italic Latin minùs proprie; the alternative marker l is followed by a visible comma.
[c2-l042 >] *¶* Qiuata. l, Momenuata. *Algodão.*
[c2-l042 note] Kiwata, alternatively momenwata, denotes cotton. The final Algodão has punctuation at the edge, not the OCR's extra e.
[c2-l043] Vata. *Tripas.*
[c2-l043 note] This separate wata entry means the entrails, directly glossed Tripas. It is distinct from the preceding wadding and cotton senses.
[c2-l044] Vataboxi. *Couſa feita de borra pera cubrira*
[c2-l044 note] Wataboshi is a head covering made of wadding. The Portuguese explanation continues with the covered body part and comparisons below.
[c2-l045 >] *cabeça: como pano de cabeça, ou como man-*
[c2-l045 note] The covering is compared with a headcloth or small mantle. The printed man- begins mantelhinha below, correcting OCR moi-.
[c2-l046 >] *telhinha.*
[c2-l046 note] Telhinha completes mantelhinha, the small mantle or veil comparison. The short row closes the head-covering entry.
[c2-l047] Vataca. *Certo peixe de rio aßi chamado.*
[c2-l047 note] Wataka is identified simply as a river fish of that name. The scan supports rio, correcting OCR tio; the separate item below is the following page's catchword.
