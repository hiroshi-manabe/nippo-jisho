[c1-l003 note] Susubana is the moisture or discharge running from the nose. The entry is distinct from the preceding soot compound despite the shared initial letters.

[c1-l006 note] Susudoi describes someone very judicious and shrewd. Muito prudente replaces the draft Muto pradente; the qualification of severity follows below.

[c1-l012] Suſugui, u, ſuida. *Enxaguar, ou lauar. Vt,*
[c1-l012 note] Susugi means rinsing or washing. The Portuguese second verb is lauar, with l rather than the OCR t; the printed principal parts are not expanded.
[c1-l013 >] Cuchiuo ſuſugu. *Lauar, ou enxaguar a*
[c1-l013 note] Kuchi wo susugu is rinsing the mouth. The Portuguese lauar and enxaguar provide near-synonymous explanations whose object follows below.
[c1-l014 >] *boca. ¶ Permet.* Fagiuo ſuſugu. *Vin-*
[c1-l014 note] Boca completes the mouth example. The figurative haji wo susugu means washing away dishonor; Vin- begins the Portuguese explanation of avenging oneself.

[c1-l015 note] Completes vingar-se and adds freeing oneself from an affront. Mimi wo susuida introduces ears that have been figuratively washed; the repeated m and the final i before the visible hyphen correct the draft.
[c1-l016 >] da. *Se diz quando hum folgou de ouuir al-*
[c1-l016 note] Da completes susuida, the past form. The expression is used when someone enjoys hearing a good discourse; the Portuguese al- continues algũa.

[c1-l017 note] Gũa completes algũa. A good discourse or sermon is what the listener enjoyed; prègação retains the accent of the supplied reading, and pa- continues parece.

[c1-l018 note] Rece completes parece: it seems the discourse has washed or purified the ears. This explains the metaphor in mimi wo susuida, not literal ear washing.
[c1-l019] Suſume. *Perſuaſão.*

[c1-l020] Suſume, uru, eta. *Perſuadir. Vt,* Ienuo

[c1-l021 >] ſuſumuru. *Exortar, ou mouer a alguem à*

[c1-l022 >] *virtude. ¶* Saqeuo ſuſumuru. *Perſua-*

[c1-l025 >] *& vontade de comer. ¶* Vmauo ſuſu-

[c1-l026 >] muru. l, ſuſume yaru. *Fazer andar, ou*

[c1-l028 note] Susumi is the corresponding self-directed or intransitive verb: impelling oneself, being persuaded, or advancing. The damaged Portuguese In iterſe is retained; Inciterſe is a likely reading, but the lost internal type is not securely visible.

[c1-l031 >] *te na virtude. ¶* Xocuga ſuſumu. *Ter*

[c1-l032 >] *vontade de comer.*

[c1-l033] Suſumichicazzuqu. *Chegarſe perto.*

[c1-l034] Suſumiide, zzuru, deta. *Sair a terreiro, ou*

[c1-l036] Suſumiyuqi, u, yuita. *Ir por diante com*

[c1-l038] Suſuqe, quru, eta. *Defumarſe a caſa, &c.*
[c1-l038 note] Susuqe describes becoming smoky or soot-darkened, illustrated by a house. The ending quru is retained as a historical principal form.
[c1-l039 >] *ou fazerſe preta, & velha. ¶* Goyeiga
[c1-l039 note] The alternative is becoming blackened and old. Fazerſe corrects fazorſe; goei ga begins an example with an image as its subject.
[c1-l040 >] ſuſuqeta. *A imagem està ja muito defuma-*

[c1-l041 >] *da, & velha.*

[c1-l042] Suſuqi. *Certa erua de que fazem vaſouras, eſ-*
[c1-l042 note] Susuki is described as an herb or grass used to make brooms. The gloss continues with mats; it does not itself specify a scientific species name.
[c1-l043 >] *teiras, &c.*

[c1-l044] Suſuqivara. *Lugar õde nace muita deſta erua.*
[c1-l044 note] Susukiwara is a place where much of the preceding grass grows. The headword ends vara, not vata; õde abbreviates onde, and the scan prints nace rather than the OCR nare.
[c1-l045] Suſuri, u, ſutta. *Soruer, ou chupar. Vt,*
[c1-l045 note] Susuri means sipping or sucking. The initial is a worn capital S, not lowercase b; sutta retains the visible doubled t.
[c1-l046 >] Cayuuo ſuſuru. *Soruer canja.*
[c1-l046 note] Kayu wo susuru means sipping rice gruel, rendered soruer canja. The Japanese object particle remains attached in Cayuuo.
[c1-l047] Suſuri. *Certa laya de peças de ſeda teſa, & crùa.*
[c1-l047 note] The scan prints Suſuri with r for a type of stiff raw-silk cloth; it is not silently changed to expected Suſuxi. The supplied NINJAL headword notes also identify this as a printed error. Second-pass enlargement confirms a grave on crùa. The row is an independent headword, not an indented continuation.

[c2-l001] Suta. *Muitos. Vt,* Sutano fito. *Muitos homẽs.*
[c2-l001 note] Suta means many, exemplified by suta no hito, many people. The abbreviated Portuguese homẽs retains its tilde.
[c2-l002] Sutabi. *Meliùs,* Sudo. *Muitas vezes.*
[c2-l002 note] Sutabi means many times, with Sudo recommended as the better form. Meliùs is wholly italic, with a grave over u; the draft's alternating roman fragments were spurious.
[c2-l003] Sutaca. *Falcão tomado do ninho.*
[c2-l003 note] Sutaka is a falcon taken from its nest. The gloss describes how the bird is obtained rather than a separate zoological species.
[c2-l004] Sutameqi, u, eita. *Resfolegar com trabalho,*
[c2-l004 note] Sutameki means breathing or panting with effort. The scan ends this clause with a comma, joining the fatigue description below.
[c2-l005 >] *& canſaço, ou fadiga.*
[c2-l005 note] The labored breathing is accompanied by weariness or fatigue. Both Portuguese nouns continue the preceding definition.
[c2-l006] Sutaqi, u, aita. *Idem.*
[c2-l006 note] Sutaqi has the same meaning, as indicated by Idem. Its principal parts are kept in their abbreviated printed form.
[c2-l007] Sutaqiayeqi, qu. *Resfolegar com trabalho,*
[c2-l007 note] Sutakiayeki again describes labored breathing, now elaborated as noisy panting. Trabalho has a visible initial t, omitted by the draft.
[c2-l008 >] *& fadiga, fazendo estrondo com a voz.*
[c2-l008 note] The panting involves fatigue and an audible noise of the voice. Fadiga corrects faciga; the provisional short-s reading of estrondo is retained rather than globally reclassifying the ligature.
[c2-l009] Sutare, ruru, eta. *Empeyorar, ou danificarſe.*
[c2-l009 note] Sutare means worsening or falling into disrepair. The scan has Sutare, not Suatare, and danificarſe retains the small i missing from the OCR.
[c2-l010] Sutarefate, tçuru, eta. *Acabarſe de danificar,*
[c2-l010 note] Sutarehate intensifies the preceding verb: becoming thoroughly damaged or ruined. The long s in Acabarſe and the missing i of danificar correct the draft; the final comma is visible.
[c2-l011 >] *ou arruinar. ¶* Miyacono tei qinnen yo-
[c2-l011 note] After the alternative arruinar, the example describes the capital's condition in recent years. Qinnen ends in n, not u; yo- begins yorozu on the next row.
[c2-l012 >] rozzu ſutare fateta. Mon. *As couſas*
[c2-l012 note] Rozzu completes yorozu: everything has fallen into ruin. Abrasion broke ſutare into misleading OCR fragments; the spacing before fateta is retained. Mon. is an upright citation label before the italic translation.
[c2-l013 >] *do Miyaco de poucos annos pera cà todas eſtão*
[c2-l013 note] The translation says that all the capital's affairs have deteriorated over the last few years. Annos and eſtão replace malformed OCR strings. Miyaco is a Japanese place designation within the Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l014 >] *desbaratadas, & deſtruidas.*
[c2-l014 note] The capital's affairs are disrupted and destroyed. The surviving b and the ending uidas support desbaratadas and deſtruidas, rather than the draft's corrupted alternatives.
[c2-l015] Sutate. *Hum liquor que ſe faz do trigo, & fei-*
[c2-l015 note] Sutate is a liquid seasoning made from wheat and beans. Fei- starts feijões; the description should not be silently replaced with a modern product name.
[c2-l016 >] *jões, que ſerue muito em Iapão pera temperar o*
[c2-l016 note] Jões completes feijões. The seasoning is much used in Japan to season food; pera is one word, and Iapão remains Portuguese, not a Japanese font-toggle term.
[c2-l017 >] *comer, & lhe dar ſabor.*
[c2-l017 note] Comer is the food being seasoned, and dar sabor explains the liquid's purpose. This completes the condiment entry.
[c2-l018] Sutçu. *Fardo, ou ſaco de arroz, &c.*
[c2-l018 note] Sutçu denotes a bale or sack, here of rice. Fardo and arroz replace Farlho and arrez; the Japanese u is unaccented, unlike the next headword.
[c2-l019] Sutçǔ. *Muitas cartas, ou eſcritos.*
[c2-l019 note] Sutçǔ means many letters or written documents. The caron over u distinguishes it graphically from the preceding sack entry.
[c2-l020] Sutebaye. *Vento Sul que ventade maneira que*
[c2-l020 note] Sutebaye is a south wind whose manner of blowing indicates that it will soon stop. The close spacing in ventade is retained from the aligned draft.
[c2-l021 >] *logo ha de acabar. Vt,* Cono caje ſutebaye
[c2-l021 note] The wind is about to end. Kono kaze sutebaye begins the illustrative assertion, completed by de gozaru below.
[c2-l022 >] de gozaru. *Eſte vento Sul logo ha de ceſ-*
[c2-l022 note] De gozaru completes the Japanese assertion. The Portuguese says this south wind will soon cease; the worn vowel supports ceſ- rather than the broken c ſ- of the draft.
[c2-l023 >] *ſar ſegundo o modo de ventar.*
[c2-l023 note] ſar completes ceſſar. The prediction is based on the wind's manner of blowing, closing the definition of sutebaye.
[c2-l024] Sute, tçuru, eta. *Deitar fora, ou lançar deſi.*
[c2-l024 note] Sute means throwing away or casting off. The row ends with deſi and a period, not deſ-: there is no lost continuation between this entry and Sutebuchi.
[c2-l025] Sutebuchi. *Vara com que dão no caualo por de*
[c2-l025 note] Sutebuchi refers to striking a horse with a rod from behind. The Portuguese prints caualo, not cauaſo; por de continues the directional phrase below.
[c2-l026 >] *trasem algũa preßa. Vt,* Sutebuchiuo vtte
[c2-l026 note] The blow is given from behind when in haste. The example begins sutebuchi wo utte; the initial S and doubled t are visible, replacing Du ebuchiuo vte. The printed close trasem is retained.
[c2-l027 >] nigueta. *Dando com a vara nas ancas do ca-*
[c2-l027 note] Nigueta says that the rider fled after the blow. The Portuguese locates the rod's strike on the horse's hindquarters and divides caualo after ca-.
[c2-l028 >] *ualo fugio.*
[c2-l028 note] Ualo completes caualo, and fugio gives the rider's flight. The horse is not feminine cauala, as the draft implied.
[c2-l029] Sutebune. *Embarcação que eſta ja varada, &*
[c2-l029 note] Sutebune is a discarded vessel already hauled ashore or stranded. The headword and Embarcação, eſta, and varada are recoverable from the scan, unlike the OCR fragments.
[c2-l030 >] *não presta pera nada.*
[c2-l030 note] The vessel is no longer useful for anything. This completes the abandoned-boat description; the provisional st typography in presta remains unchanged.
[c2-l031] Sutego. *Criança engeitada.*
[c2-l031 note] Sutego is an abandoned or foundling child, glossed criança engeitada. The historical Portuguese spelling is preserved.
[c2-l032] Sutemuchi. *Vide,* Sutebuchi.
[c2-l032 note] Sutemuchi refers back to Sutebuchi. Vide is italic; the Japanese cross-reference is roman and belongs to the explanatory apparatus.
[c2-l033] Sùua. l, ſuuaya. *Interjeição de eſpãto. Vt,*
[c2-l033 note] Suwa and suwaya are exclamations of surprise. Eſpãto contains p, not the draft's second long s. The faint grave-like mark over the first u is retained provisionally; it must not force a long-vowel interpretation of the exclamation.
[c2-l034 >] Suua core coſo vochǔto naretote. Taif.
[c2-l034 note] The quoted suwa exclamation identifies someone as a fugitive. The worn vochǔto is retained as printed rather than normalized to a modern ochiudo form; Taif. is the upright citation label, with its book number below.
[c2-l035 >] *Lib. 28. O que eſte certo he homẽ q̃ vẽ fugido.*
[c2-l035 note] Book 28 accompanies the citation. The Portuguese identifies the man as one who has come in flight; vẽ, not oẽ, is the abbreviated verb. The visible terminal period closes the quotation.
[c2-l036] Suuabuqi. l, *Potiùs,* Xiuabuqi. *Toſe.*
[c2-l036 note] Suwabuki denotes a cough, with shiwabuki explicitly preferred. Potiùs is italic, while l and the cited Japanese alternative are roman; the accented u is preserved.
[c2-l037 >] *¶* Suuabuqiuo ſuru. *Toſir, ou ter toſe.*
[c2-l037 note] Suwabuki wo suru means coughing or having a cough. The Portuguese single-s spellings Toſir and toſe are not modernized to double s.
[c2-l038] Suuai. *Raminhas, ou varinhas de aruore.*
[c2-l038 note] Suwai here means small branches or rods from a tree. This botanical noun is distinct from the homonymous brokerage entry below.
[c2-l039] Suuai. *Officio de corretor entre os que comprão,*
[c2-l039 note] The second Suwai denotes the work of a broker between purchasers and sellers. Corretor is the commercial intermediary, not a textual corrector.
[c2-l040 >] *& vendem. Vt,* Suuaiuo ſuru. *Fazer*
[c2-l040 note] Vendem completes the buyer-and-seller pair. Suwai wo suru means performing the broker's work; the scan does not show the draft's comma after Fazer.
[c2-l041 >] *este officio de corretor. ¶ Item, Corretagẽ*
[c2-l041 note] Completes the brokerage-work example. Item introduces the further sense of brokerage commission; Corretagẽ retains its abbreviation.
[c2-l042 >] *ou ganho que ſe tira deſte officio. Vt,* Suuai
[c2-l042 note] The further sense is the gain obtained from this occupation. Suwai begins the example of taking the fee, completed below.
[c2-l043 >] uo toru. *Tomar corretagem, ou ganho dã-*
[c2-l043 note] Suwai wo toru means taking brokerage or commission. The object particle is uo, not no; Tomar replaces Temar. The final dã- continues the phrase concerning the merchants.
[c2-l044 >] *tre os mercadores.*
[c2-l044 note] Tre completes the abbreviated dã-tre expression: commission obtained from between the merchants. The historical contraction is preserved rather than rewritten.
[c2-l045] Suuama. *Feição com certas aberturas, ou en-*
[c2-l045 note] Suwama describes a shape or pattern with openings, explained through river mouths and inlets. The Portuguese en- continues entradas.
[c2-l046 >] *tradas de rio, ou enſeadas a que ſe arremeda*
[c2-l046 note] Tradas completes entradas. River inlets or coves provide the model being imitated; enſeadas and arremeda correct the OCR, and the spurious l after que is removed.
[c2-l047 >] *nas Niuas, ou nas pinturas dos veſtidos.*
[c2-l047 note] The inlet-like form is imitated in gardens or garment designs. Niuas is a Japanese garden term embedded in Portuguese; pinturas and veſtidos correct the draft's malformed words. The example continues on f461, anticipated by the catchword.
