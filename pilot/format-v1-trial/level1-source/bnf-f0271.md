[c1-l003 >] *neſtas obras he mil taeis.*
[c1-l003 note] The dictionary translates the construction expense as one thousand taels. This is the source's own equivalence, not a modern currency conversion.
[c1-l004] Irimidare, uru. *Entrar de romania, ou de*
[c1-l004 note] Irimidare means entering in a mixed crowd or throng. Enlargement supports printed romania, with n, although romaria is the natural Portuguese comparison; the anomalous form is retained, and de tropel confirms the intended crowd sense.
[c1-l005 >] *tropel.*
[c1-l005 note] De tropel completes the description of entering in a crowd or rush, closing the irimidare entry.
[c1-l006] Irimochi. *Bolinhos torrados, ou aßados em pa*
[c1-l006 note] Irimochi denotes roasted or baked cakes. Panella is divided as pa / nella without a printed end hyphen.
[c1-l007 >] *nella, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes panella, the pot or pan in which the cakes are heated; doubled ll is preserved.
[c1-l008] Irimuco. *Ienro q̃ ẽtra pera erdar a caſa do ſogro.*
[c1-l008 note] Irimuko is a son-in-law who enters the father-in-law's household to inherit it. Portuguese Ienro uses consonantal I, while q̃ and ẽtra preserve printed abbreviations.
[c1-l009] Iriô, l, yeriô. *Custo, ou gasto dalgum veſti-*
[c1-l009 note] Irioo, alternatively yerioo, denotes the cost of having clothing made. Custo has a t rather than OCR l; the circumflexes on both Japanese forms remain as printed.
[c1-l010 >] *do que ſe talha, ou faz pera alguem. ¶* Co-
[c1-l010 note] Completes vestido and specifies cutting or making it for someone. Co- begins kono in the Japanese example below.
[c1-l011 >] no qirumonouo xitatçuruni iriô, l, yeriô
[c1-l011 note] The example asks about the expense of making this garment. Qirumono is the historical kirumono form; it is not replaced with modern kimono.
[c1-l012 >] nanbô fodo irǒzu. *Quanto ſera neceſſario*
[c1-l012 note] Nanboo hodo iroozu asks how much will be needed. The damaged final vowel of Quanto retains an oval trace compatible with o and the Portuguese question, rather than the OCR's e; the reading is usable but the impression is weak.
[c1-l013 >] *de gasto pera fazer eſte vestido?*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the question about the expense required to make the garment. The question mark and locally supplied st spellings remain visible source features.
[c1-l014] Iriqi. *Poder, & autoridade.*
[c1-l014 note] Iriki denotes power and authority. The Portuguese reads Poder, not OCR Pider.
[c1-l015] Iritçuqe, uru, eta. *Pegar algũa couſa no fo-*
[c1-l015 note] Iritsuke is the transitive action of making something stick while roasting it. The headword begins with capital I, not lowercase l; fogo continues below.
[c1-l016 >] *go torrandoa.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes fogo torrandoa, specifying the action of roasting over a fire. The following entry supplies the corresponding intransitive sticking sense.
[c1-l017] Iritçuqi, u, ita. *Pegarſe algũa couſa que ſe*
[c1-l017 note] Iritsuki is the intransitive counterpart: something sticks as it roasts. The principal part ita remains unexpanded.
[c1-l018 >] *està torrando.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes the roasting circumstance, with the source's grave accent in està and provisional st allograph retained.
[c1-l019] Iriumi. *Enſeada, ou braço de mar.*
[c1-l019 note] Iriumi is an inlet or arm of the sea. The separate entry is not merged with the closely related iriye below.
[c1-l020] Iriye. *Enſeada, ou braço de mar que entra pella*
[c1-l020 note] Iriye likewise denotes an inlet, here explained as an arm of the sea extending inland. The printed doubled l in pella is preserved.
[c1-l021 >] *terra dentro.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes the inland extension of the inlet, not a separate geographical term.
[c1-l022] Irizacaxino. *Hum certo eſcaueche como ſalſa.*
[c1-l022 note] Irizacaxino is described as a particular escabeche-like sauce or condiment. The unusual Japanese sequence is preserved from this impression rather than replaced with a modern culinary name.
[c1-l023] Iro. *Cor. ¶* Irouo vxinǒ. *Perder as*
[c1-l023 note] Iro means colour; iro wo ushinau is losing one's colour. The faint initial I and the r in Irouo are visible, correcting OCR ro and Itouo; vxinǒ keeps the source's long-vowel notation.
[c1-l024 >] *cores. ¶* Irouo cayete iſamuru. *Re-*
[c1-l024 note] Completes perder as cores, then gives iro wo kaete isamuru, admonishing with a changed expression. Irouo again has r, not OCR t.
[c1-l025 >] *prender a alguem, ou dar lhe conſelho por va*
[c1-l025 note] Completes reprender and adds giving someone counsel by various means. Varias is divided as va / rias without an end hyphen.
[c1-l026 >] r*ias vias. ¶* Irouo nauoſu. *Mudar a*
[c1-l026 note] Completes the counsel gloss, then introduces iro wo naosu, restoring or changing the complexion. The initial n of nauoſu is supported by the scan and the repeated nauoxi forms below, unlike OCR uauoſu.
[c1-l027 >] *cor do roſto, ou oſembrante que dantes estaua*
[c1-l027 note] The object is the face's colour or expression, previously altered. The close oſembrante spacing is retained without changing the meaning to a different word.
[c1-l028 >] *alterado, ou agaſtado, &c. ¶* Iro nauo-
[c1-l028 note] Completes the description of a disturbed or angry expression. Iro nauo- begins the related noun for changing colour, developed below as a clothing custom.
[c1-l029 >] xi. *Vt,* Iro nauoxiuo ſuru. *Mudar a*
[c1-l029 note] Completes iro naoshi and illustrates iro naoshi wo suru. The Portuguese introduces a change of clothing colour rather than another facial-expression example.
[c1-l030 >] *cor dos vestidos como fazẽ os meninos de vin*
[c1-l030 note] The dictionary begins its examples of changing clothing colour with infants at twenty or thirty days. Fazẽ is abbreviated, and vinte is physically divided without a terminal hyphen.
[c1-l031 >] *te, ou trinta dias por diante, os que caſão de*
[c1-l031 note] Completes twenty or thirty days onward, then turns to newly married people. De begins depois on the next row without a printed hyphen.
[c1-l032 >] *pois de dous, ou tres dias, & os que tirão o dò*
[c1-l032 note] Specifies two or three days after marriage and then those ending mourning. Dò is the source's accented word for mourning, not an omitted longer form.
[c1-l033 >] *por reſpeito dalgum defunto. Todos eſtes deſpẽ*
[c1-l033 note] Completes mourning for a deceased person, then says that all the groups just listed take off a garment. Deſpẽ preserves the abbreviation for despem.
[c1-l034 >] *vestido de cor branca, & ſe vestem de vari-*
[c1-l034 note] The garment removed is white, and the replacement clothing is of various colours. The source describes the custom; vari- continues below.
[c1-l035 >] *as cores.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes varias cores and closes the extended iro-naoshi clothing explanation.
[c1-l036] Iro. *Senſualidade. ¶* Ironi fuqeru. *En-*
[c1-l036 note] A separate iro entry denotes sensuality. Iro ni fukeru means giving oneself over to it, as the divided Portuguese entregar-se explains.
[c1-l037 >] *tregarſe à ſenſualiadade. ¶* Irouo vomon-
[c1-l037 note] Completes entregar-se à sensualidade, then begins iro wo omonzuru. The visibly extra vowel in printed ſenſualiadade is retained; the normal form appears again two rows below.
[c1-l038 >] zuru. *Idem. ¶* Irouo conomu. *Dar*
[c1-l038 note] Completes omonzuru with the same sensuality gloss, then introduces iro wo konomu. Dar begins dar-se on the next row without a visible end hyphen.
[c1-l039 >] *ſe à ſenſualidade. ¶* Irogonomiuo ſuru.
[c1-l039 note] Completes the Portuguese expression for indulging in sensuality and adds irogonomi wo suru as another equivalent construction.
[c1-l040 >] *Idem.*
[c1-l040 note] Idem applies the preceding sensuality gloss to irogonomi wo suru, closing the entry.
[c1-l041] Irocaxi, ſu, aita. *Secar ao fogo, ou enxugar.*
[c1-l041 note] Irokashi means drying by heat. The principal part aita reflects the historical inflection and is not modernized to ashita.
[c1-l042 >] *¶* Chauo irocaſu. i, Chauo faxiyagaſu.
[c1-l042 note] Cha wo irokasu is equated with cha wo hashiyagasu, drying tea. The scan includes the paragraph marker omitted by OCR and a comma after i; both are restored on the same physical row.
[c1-l043 >] *Secar, ou enxugar o Cha que esta humido. No*
[c1-l043 note] The Portuguese explains drying damp tea, then begins a regional usage statement. No accent is supplied to the weakly printed esta without secure graphic evidence.
[c1-l044 >] *Cami ſe diz,* Faxiyagaſu.
[c1-l044 note] Completes No Cami se diz, stating that hashiyagasu is used in the Cami region. Cami and the quoted verb belong to a Portuguese regional explanation, not a new headword.
[c1-l045] Iroco. *Eſcama de peixe. Vt,* Irocouo fuqu.
[c1-l045 note] Iroko means a fish scale; iroko wo fuku is illustrated by removing the scales. The source's i- initial is not regularized to modern uroko.
[c1-l046 >] *Eſcamar o peixe.*
[c1-l046 note] Directly translates the example as scaling a fish, completing the iroko entry.
[c1-l047] Irocogata. *Couſa de figura triangular, ou de*
[c1-l047 note] Irokogata describes a scale-like triangular shape. The damaged initial still supports Irocogata, not OCR rucogata; triangular has n, not r in the middle.
[c1-l048 >] *tres cantos.*
[c1-l048 note] Three corners completes and confirms the triangular-shape gloss. The scan reads tres cantos, not OCR tre centos; this is the final left-column body row.

[c2-l018] Iron. *Outra opinião differente no diſputar. Vt,*

[c2-l046 note] Completes the past-form alternative and describes teeth becoming blunted or set on edge, as when eating sour unripe things. The close Botaremſe spelling is retained; the weak final vowel of printed comi remains a graphic caveat rather than being silently modernized to come.
[c2-l047 >] *couſas verdes, & azedas.*
[c2-l047 note] Specifies green or unripe and sour foods, completing the teeth example. The final word reads azedas, not OCR azedao.
[c2-l048] Iruca. *Toninha.*
[c2-l048 note] Iruka is glossed by the Portuguese cetacean name toninha, broadly a porpoise or dolphin here; the brief entry does not support a narrower modern species identification.
