[c1-l001 >] *Deſtelhar o telhado. ¶* Vdeuo macuru.
[c1-l001 note] Continues Yaneuo macuru from f300: removing the roof covering. The new example Vdeuo macuru concerns rolling clothing back from the arms; the initial Deſtelhar is body text here, although it also appeared as the preceding page's catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *Arregaçar os braços. ¶* Vôtçu macuttçu
[c1-l002 note] Arregaçar os braços translates rolling up at the arms. The next example contrasts pursuit and being driven back in fighting, and continues with tatacǒ below.
[c1-l003 >] tatacǒ. *Pelejar ora leuando diante o inimi-*
[c1-l003 note] Tatacǒ completes the fighting example. The translation begins fighting while at one moment driving the enemy ahead; inimi- continues as inimigo.
[c1-l004 >] *go, ora tornãdo a tras. ¶* Faritçuqe, l,
[c1-l004 note] Go completes inimigo, and the alternative is falling back. Faritçuqe then introduces the pasted-paper object of another macuru example.
[c1-l005 >] xǒjiuo macuru. *Tirar o papel que eſta pe-*
[c1-l005 note] Xǒji is offered as an alternative to faritçuqe. Here macuru means stripping off the paper, with pe- beginning Portuguese pegado.
[c1-l006 >] *gado na parede, ou nas portas de papel. ¶* Nin
[c1-l006 note] The paper is attached to the wall or to paper doors. Nin begins the next Japanese example across the physical line break; no missing hyphen is supplied.
[c1-l007 >] juuo macuritatçuru. *Leuar diante deſi gẽ-*
[c1-l007 note] Ninjuuo macuritatçuru concerns driving people before oneself. The Portuguese begins the same explanation, dividing gente after gẽ-.
[c1-l008 >] *te em peleja, ou encõtro. ¶* Ninjuuo ma-
[c1-l008 note] Te completes gente: driving people ahead in fighting or an encounter. A further Ninjuuo example starts here and divides macuri after ma-.
[c1-l009 >] curi aguru. *Leuar diante deſi, ou fazer ſu-*
[c1-l009 note] Macuri aguru describes driving people ahead or making them go upward. The printed internal word gap is retained; the Portuguese verb subir continues below. The small worn terminal mark after ſu is retained as the aligned draft’s hyphen, but its exact shape remains uncertain.
[c1-l010 >] *bir por algum monte gente, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] Bir completes subir. The people are driven up a hill or mountain, completing the preceding directional example.
[c1-l011] Macuri idaxi, ſu, aita. *Botar fora com força.*
[c1-l011 note] Macuri idaxi is driving or casting something out forcefully. The principal part aita is the historical inflection of the s-stem, not a spelling to modernize.
[c1-l012 >] *Vt,* Ittaru teqiuo macuri idaita. *Botou*
[c1-l012 note] The example says that the enemies who had been inside were driven out. The visibly doubled t in Ittaru is retained, rather than silently regularized from the interpretation.
[c1-l013 >] *os inimigos fora que eſtauão dentro.*
[c1-l013 note] Os inimigos fora que eſtauão dentro completes the expulsion example: enemies formerly within are put outside.
[c1-l014] Macuriſague, uru. *Leuando diante botar em*
[c1-l014 note] Macuriſague is driving something downward. Only uru is printed as an additional principal form here; no missing past form is reconstructed.
[c1-l015 >] *baixo. Vt,* Ninjuuo macuri ſaguru, *vo-*
[c1-l015 note] Ninjuuo macuri ſaguru illustrates driving people down, followed by alternative verbs. Vo- begins voroſu below; this isolated final fragment is visibly italic, unlike the preceding upright Japanese example and the continuation on the next row.
[c1-l016 >] roſu, votoſu. *Leuando a gente diante de-*
[c1-l016 note] Voroſu and votoſu are further downward-motion alternatives. The Portuguese then begins explaining driving the people ahead and throwing them down; de- continues as deitala.
[c1-l017 >] *itala a baixo, ou derrubala.*
[c1-l017 note] Itala completes deitala: casting the people downward or overthrowing them. The printed a baixo remains separate words.
[c1-l018] Macuſa. *Grãos que comem os caualos. Vt,*
[c1-l018 note] Macuſa is horse fodder, described here as grain eaten by horses. The definition, rather than a modern plant classification, determines the interpretation.
[c1-l019 >] Macuſauo cǒ. *Dar grãos a comer aos caualos.*
[c1-l019 note] Macuſauo cǒ means feeding the horses. The Portuguese phrase is a comer aos caualos; the OCR incorrectly divided it as comera os.
[c1-l020] Macuzu. *Hũa erua que folgão os caualos de*
[c1-l020 note] Macuzu names a plant horses enjoy eating. The description continues with a comparison rather than an exact botanical identification.
[c1-l021 >] *comer, como Era.*
[c1-l021 note] Comer completes the feeding description. Era is the printed historical ivy-name used for the comparison, not an editorial heading or a Japanese synonym.
[c1-l022] Macuzuvara. *Lugar onde ha muita deſta erua.*
[c1-l022 note] Macuzuvara denotes a place where much of the preceding plant grows. Deſta erua refers directly back to Macuzu.
[c1-l023] Macuzucuxi. *Vide* Macuguxi.
[c1-l023 note] Macuzucuxi redirects to Macuguxi, the curtain-supporting poles on f300. Vide is italic apparatus; the cited Japanese headword remains upright within the cross-reference.
[c1-l024] Mada. *Ainda não, com verbos negatiuos.*
[c1-l024 note] Mada means not yet when used with negative verbs. The scan reads verbos, not OCR verbas; this distinction is grammatical, not a definition of negative words in general.
[c1-l025 >] *Vt,* Conoguiuo mada itaſazu. Mon. *A-*
[c1-l025 note] Conoguiuo mada itaſazu illustrates not yet having done this matter. Mon. is an upright citation label; A- begins the Portuguese Ainda across the break.
[c1-l026 >] *inda não fiz iſto. ¶ Item, Ainda cõ ver*
[c1-l026 note] The example is translated as not yet having done this. The visible gap in fiz iſto is restored. A second use begins: ainda with affirmative verbs, whose name continues without a printed hyphen after ver.
[c1-l027 >] *bos affirmatiuos. Vt,* Saqeua mada aruca?
[c1-l027 note] Bos completes verbos, and affirmatiuos corrects the OCR l to visible t. Saqeua mada aruca asks whether wine still remains, illustrating positive still rather than negative not yet.
[c1-l028 >] *Hà ainda vinho?*
[c1-l028 note] Hà ainda vinho? is the Portuguese question corresponding to the preceding Japanese example. The final mark is a question mark, not the OCR nasalized vowel ẽ.
[c1-l029] Madaqi. *P. Couſa apreßada.*
[c1-l029 note] Madaqi is labelled poetic and glossed as something hurried or early. P. introduces the explanation and retains the supplied italic setting.
[c1-l030] Madara. *Couſa malhada como tigre, gato, &c.*
[c1-l030 note] Madara means mottled or marked, compared here with a tiger or cat. The following examples develop the same colour-pattern sense.
[c1-l031 >] *Vt,* Madara inu. *Cão malhado de diuerſas*
[c1-l031 note] Madara inu is a dog with patches of different colours. Diuerſas leads into cores on the following row.
[c1-l032 >] *cores. ¶* Fara madarano gan. *Pato*
[c1-l032 note] Cores completes the dog example. Fara madarano gan introduces a wild waterbird with a marked underside; the Portuguese calls it pato, so no modern zoological identification is imposed.
[c1-l033 >] *brauo q̃ tẽ os peitos malhados de brãco e preto.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes the wild-bird description: the breast is mottled white and black. The scan supports malhados and brãco, correcting OCR malbados and brãto; the nasal abbreviation is retained.
[c1-l034] Madataqi. *O abrir, & fechar o olho, ou pes-*
[c1-l034 note] Madataqi is the opening and closing of the eye, or blinking. The alternative Portuguese verb divides after pes-; its short s is retained rather than changed to long ſ.
[c1-l035 >] *tenejar. Vt,* Madataqino aida, l, ma.
[c1-l035 note] Tenejar completes pestenejar. Madataqino aida, alternatively ma, expresses the interval of a blink; the Portuguese interpretation follows below.
[c1-l036 >] *Em hum breuißimo eſpaço. ¶* Madataqiuo
[c1-l036 note] The interval is translated as a very brief space of time. Breuißimo has no additional t after ß. Madataqiuo begins the next verbal example.
[c1-l037 >] ſuru. *Peſtenejar.*
[c1-l037 note] Suru completes Madataqiuo ſuru: to blink. The compact example and its translation remain on this separate physical row.
[c1-l038] Made. *Ate: vſaſe com algũa outra compoſi-*
[c1-l038 note] Made expresses a limit, up to or until. The colon after Ate introduces its use in combination with another expression; compoſi- continues on the next line.
[c1-l039 >] *ção. Vt,* Coremade ſanjita. *Vim atequi.*
[c1-l039 note] Ção completes compoſição. Coremade ſanjita illustrates having come as far as here; the printed Portuguese atequi is retained as one historical word.
[c1-l040] Mademo. *Ate. Tẽ eſtes modos, & outros ſeme*
[c1-l040 note] Mademo is introduced through the same up-to sense, with the following idioms showing its extended uses. Seme continues into ſemelhantes without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l041 >] *lhãtes no falar. Vt,* Soremademo gozaranu.
[c1-l041 note] Lhãtes completes the reference to similar ways of speaking. Soremademo gozaranu is the first idiomatic example, translated below in terms of lack of necessity.
[c1-l042 >] *Ißo não he neceßario. ¶* Qiqumademo go-
[c1-l042 note] Ißo não he neceßario gives the preceding idiom as that is unnecessary. The next example is Qiqumademo, with the narrow dotted i rather than the OCR u, and continues across go-/zanai.
[c1-l043 >] zanai. *Não he neceßario ouuir.* Mairuma-
[c1-l043 note] Zanai completes the negative predicate: it is unnecessary to hear. Mairuma- begins the parallel example about going, not a separate entry.
[c1-l044 >] demo nai. *Não he neceſſario ir, &c.*
[c1-l044 note] Demo nai completes Mairumademo nai: there is no need to go. The supplied double-long-ſ spelling in neceſſario is retained, not harmonized with the preceding ß forms.
[c1-l045] Mado. *Ianella.*
[c1-l045 note] Mado is a window, glossed by historical Portuguese Ianella. It is distinct from the following long-vowel Madǒ.
[c1-l046] Madǒ. Tenguno michi. *Caminho do diabo.*
[c1-l046 note] Madǒ is paraphrased as Tenguno michi. The Portuguese renders this as the devil’s path; this records the dictionary’s religious interpretation, not an identification adopted independently here.
[c1-l047] Madoi, ô, ôta. *Andar errado. ¶* Cocoromadô.
[c1-l047 note] Madoi denotes wandering or being mistaken. Cocoromadô applies the same idea to mental confusion; the printed circumflexes are preserved separately from the preceding headword’s caron.
[c1-l048 >] *Não eſtar ẽ ſi, ou eſtar muito perturbado.*
[c1-l048 note] The mental-state example means not being oneself, or being greatly disturbed. Perturbado is one printed word; the OCR’s internal space is removed.

[c2-l011] Madouo. Aida touoi. *Eſpaço ao longe, ou*

The chosen gloss Longe, ou em distancia repairs severe under-inking: the g descender, remnants of ou em, and the ending of distancia remain, while the immediately preceding definition supplies a same-typeface lexical comparator. Several intervening strokes remain uncertain.
[c2-l014] Mafitotçu. *Mais hum.*
[c2-l014 note] Mafitotçu means one more. Reinspection prompted by the attributed NINJAL checkpoint shows a damaged a followed directly by the fi types, not the draft’s extra i in Maifitotçu; the surviving spacing and letter count support the correction.

[c2-l016 note] Mafôxa denotes a practitioner associated in the gloss with superstitions, divination, and further rites continued below. The characterization belongs to the historical dictionary’s religious vocabulary; the printed Portuguese list is not rewritten to regularize its grammar.
