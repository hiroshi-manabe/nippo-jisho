[c1-l001] Ruichô. Tçucareta tori. *Paßaro canſado,*
[c1-l001 note] Ruichoo is explained by tsukareta tori, a tired bird. The initial is R, not the OCR's B; the circumflex on the final vowel is retained.
[c1-l002 >] *ou debilitado.*
[c1-l002 note] The alternative Portuguese description is a weakened or debilitated bird; this short row completes Ruichô rather than beginning another entry.
[c1-l003] Ruicon. Tçucare cutabiruru. *Grande canſa-*
[c1-l003 note] Ruikon is exhaustion, explained by tsukare and kutabiruru. The Portuguese noun for fatigue is divided after canſa- at the right edge.
[c1-l004 >] *ço, ou fraqueza.* S.
[c1-l004 note] Completes canſaço and adds weakness as an equivalent. The final upright S is the written-register label, not part of the italic gloss.
[c1-l005] Ruinen. Toxicaſanaru. *O acrecentarerenſe*
[c1-l005 note] Ruinen refers to years accumulating, as toshi kasanaru explains. Enlargement retains the unusual repeated re in acrecentarerenſe; this printed anomaly is not silently regularized.
[c1-l006 >] *os annos, ou muitos annos.*
[c1-l006 note] The accumulating things are years; the alternative gloss is simply many years. This completes the preceding infinitival construction.
[c1-l007] Ruiqua. i, Vonaji tagvino toga. *Peccado*
[c1-l007 note] Ruikwa is glossed through onaji tagui no toga, wrongdoing of the same kind. The Portuguese prints Peccado with two c letters.
[c1-l008 >] *da meſma eſpecie, ou peccado que da meſma*
[c1-l008 note] The gloss first defines sin of the same species, then begins an alternative explanation of guilt falling in the same manner upon someone.
[c1-l009 >] *maneira caye, ou carrega ſobre alguem como*
[c1-l009 note] This continues the inherited or shared-guilt explanation: wrongdoing falls upon, or burdens, someone. The following row supplies its example.
[c1-l010 >] *peccado dos pais ſobre os filhos, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] The example is the parents' sin falling upon their children. It completes this Ruiqua entry and must not be joined to the separate fire sense below.
[c1-l011] Ruiqua. *Fogo que ſe pega na caſa vizinha.*
[c1-l011 note] This homonymous ruikwa concerns a fire spreading from a neighboring house, not the wrongdoing defined in the preceding entry.
[c1-l012 >] *Vt,* Ruiquani vǒ. *Ser queimado com fogo*
[c1-l012 note] Ruikwa ni au is illustrated as being burned by a spreading fire. The historical vǒ spelling and its caron are preserved; Vt is italic apparatus.
[c1-l013 >] *que ſe pegou na caſa do vezinho.*
[c1-l013 note] The fire originated in the neighbor's house. The distinct printed forms vizinha above and vezinho here are retained rather than harmonized.
[c1-l014] Ru ire. *Vide,* Ru.
[c1-l014 note] Ru ire is referred back to Ru. The visible gap in the headword is kept; the cross-reference is not silently expanded into an unattested definition.
[c1-l015] Ruireqe. *Inchaços, ou carouços q̃ nacem no peſ-*
[c1-l015 note] Ruireqe denotes swellings or lumps around the neck. The scan has an initial R, and its final qe is preserved despite the following entry's similar spelling.
[c1-l016 >] *coço ao derredor da gargãta.*
[c1-l016 note] Coço completes peſcoço; the location is around the throat. The abbreviation tilde in gargãta is retained without supplying the omitted nasal letter.
[c1-l017] Ruireqi. *O chorar muito, ou por muito eſpa-*
[c1-l017 note] Ruireki is prolonged or abundant weeping. Unlike the neighboring neck-swelling entry, this headword ends in qi; the Portuguese eſpa- continues below.
[c1-l018 >] *ço.* S.
[c1-l018 note] Completes eſpaço, here duration: weeping for a long time. The upright S marks written usage and is separated from the italic definition.
[c1-l019] Ruitai. i, Daidai. *Idades acrecentadas, ou*
[c1-l019 note] Ruitai is equated with daidai, successive generations or ages. The gloss's alternative wording continues onto the following physical row.
[c1-l020 >] *muitas idades.*
[c1-l020 note] Many ages or generations completes the explanation of Ruitai. The short continuation is retained independently rather than merged into its headword row.
[c1-l021] Ruitocu. Tocuuo caſanuru. *O acrecentar*
[c1-l021 note] Ruitoku means accumulating merit, explained by toku wo kasanuru. The Portuguese infinitive acrecentar governs the noun on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *merecimentos, ou muitos merecimentos.*
[c1-l022 note] The accumulated things are merits; the alternative definition is much merit. This is the completion of Ruitocu, not a separate example.
[c1-l023] Rui v. Namidano ame. *Muitas lagrimas*
[c1-l023 note] Ruiu is interpreted as namida no ame, a rain of tears. The visibly separated Rui v is preserved rather than joined in the canonical text.
[c1-l024 >] *ou chorar muito. Vt,* Rui v faregataxi.
[c1-l024 note] The gloss adds abundant weeping. Rui v haregatashi supplies an example in which this rain of tears is difficult to clear or stop.
[c1-l025 >] *O não poder reprimir, ou interromper as mui-*
[c1-l025 note] The Portuguese explains inability to restrain or interrupt the tears. The negative belongs to the Japanese difficulty construction, and mui- continues below.
[c1-l026 >] *tas lagrimas.*
[c1-l026 note] Tas completes muitas: the example concerns many tears. This short row closes the Ruiu entry before the distinct accumulated-ages sense.
[c1-l027] Ruixe. Youo caſanuru. i, Daidai. *Muitas*
[c1-l027 note] Ruise denotes successive ages, explained by yo wo kasanuru and daidai. The Japanese particle remains attached in the printed Youo.
[c1-l028 >] *idades, ou ſeculos.*
[c1-l028 note] The Portuguese completes the definition as many ages or centuries. It reinforces the temporal sense of Ruixe rather than the tears of the previous entry.
[c1-l029] Ruixen. *Embarcaçoẽs, ou nauios que vão de*
[c1-l029 note] Ruisen denotes vessels traveling together. The headword begins with R, not OCR B; the tilde in Embarcaçoẽs is retained over its printed carrier.
[c1-l030 >] *conſerua.*
[c1-l030 note] De conſerua means sailing in company or convoy. This single-word row completes the fleet description above.
[c1-l031] Ruixet. *Cordas pera amarrar homens.*
[c1-l031 note] Ruisetsu is represented with the checked final t in Ruixet. The gloss specifies cords used to bind people, without expanding that final consonant.
[c1-l032] Ruixin. *Parentes.*
[c1-l032 note] Ruishin is kin or relatives, directly matched by Parentes. Its short definition is a complete independent entry.
[c1-l033] Ruiyon. *Deſcendentes, ou progenie.*
[c1-l033 note] Ruiyon means descendants or progeny. The Portuguese confirms a genealogical sense, distinct from the preceding general term for relatives.
[c1-l034] Ruiza. *Boyãoſinho como pucaro pera meter o Cha*
[c1-l034 note] Ruiza denotes a small pot, compared with a drinking vessel, for holding tea. The diminutive spelling Boyãoſinho is preserved as printed.
[c1-l035 >] *moido.*
[c1-l035 note] Moido specifies that the tea stored in this vessel is ground. This adjective completes the container's use, not a new entry.
[c1-l036] Runin. i, Nagaſarebito. *Deſterrado.*
[c1-l036 note] Runin is an exile, equated with nagasarebito. The small comma after i is visible; the Portuguese describes the banished person.
[c1-l037] Ruqei. i, Ruzai. *Deſterro por algum crime.*
[c1-l037 note] Rukei is penal exile, equated with ruzai. The scan supports Ruzai rather than OCR Rozai, and the gloss specifies punishment for a crime.
[c1-l038] Ruren. i, Nagare ariqu. *Andar de cà pera*
[c1-l038 note] Ruren is explained as wandering about, nagare ariku. The headword is abraded but readable; the Portuguese starts the expression for moving to and fro.
[c1-l039 >] *là deſenfadandoſe. ¶ Item, Eſtar fazen-*
[c1-l039 note] The visible grave in là completes de cà pera là, moving to and fro. A second sense begins: doing something while one's attention is elsewhere; fazen- continues below.
[c1-l040 >] *do algũa couſa com o ſentido noutras partes co-*
[c1-l040 note] Completes fazendo and describes an activity performed with the mind elsewhere. Com, not OCR dem, is supported by the remaining type and the syntax.
[c1-l041 >] *mo deſenfadandoſe.*
[c1-l041 note] Mo completes como, comparing the absent-minded action to amusing or distracting oneself. The faint reflexive ending is retained as ſe, not fe.
[c1-l042] Ruri. *Certa pedra precioſa azul.*
[c1-l042 note] Ruri names a blue precious stone. The general wording is retained without adding a more specific mineral identification to the transcription.
[c1-l043] Ruri. *Paſarinho de cor azul eſcuro.*
[c1-l043 note] A separate Ruri entry describes a dark-blue bird. The headword is upright throughout; eſcuro corrects OCR eſruro without changing the printed bird-name spelling.
[c1-l044] Ruri iro. *Cor da zul eſcuro.*
[c1-l044 note] Ruri iro is a dark-blue color. The conspicuous printed spacing da zul is retained rather than normalized to d'azul or de azul.
[c1-l045] Rurǒ. *Deſterro. ¶* Rurǒ ſuru. *An*
[c1-l045 note] Ruroo denotes exile, with an example meaning to wander in exile. The headword's final vowel is badly faded; Rurǒ is adopted from its residual outline and the clearer repeated form. No terminal hyphen is supplied after An.
[c1-l046 >] *dar deſterrado ſem caſa, nem lugar certo.*
[c1-l046 note] Dar completes Andar. The explanation is wandering in exile without a home or fixed place; the faded word deſterrado is kept intact rather than split as OCR suggested.
[c1-l047 >] *¶* Rurǒno mito naru. *Ser deſterrado.*
[c1-l047 note] Ruroo no mi to naru means becoming an exile. The abraded initial is R, not X; the phrase's no mi to construction supports the retained word division mito.

[c2-l001] Ruſu. Todomari mamoru. *O eſtar abſente*
[c2-l001 note] Rusu is paired with todomari mamoru, staying to guard, while the first Portuguese gloss describes absence. Both aspects are preserved; the scan restores the missing letters in Ruſu and abſente.
[c2-l002 >] *ou fora de caſa. ¶* Ruſuuo ſuru. *Ficar*
[c2-l002 note] The first sense is being away from home. Rusu wo suru introduces the complementary activity of remaining behind to look after the house.
[c2-l003 >] *em caſa vigiãdo em abſencia dalguẽ. ¶* Ru-
[c2-l003 note] The example means staying home on watch during someone's absence. The new Japanese example starts with Ru-, in upright type rather than part of the Portuguese.
[c2-l004 >] ſu naru. l, dearu. *Eſtar fora, ou abſente.*
[c2-l004 note] Ru- plus ſu forms rusu; naru and dearu are alternative predicates for being absent. The lowercase l introduces the alternative and is not a Japanese syllable.
[c2-l005] Ruſumori. i, Ruſuno ban. *O que fica guar*
[c2-l005 note] Rusumori is the person left to guard the house, explained as rusu no ban. The headword is wholly upright; guar continues below without an added hyphen.
[c2-l006 >] *dando, ou vigiando a caſa.*
[c2-l006 note] Dando completes guardando, followed by watching the house as an equivalent. The row belongs to the caretaker definition, not to the next memory-related entry.
[c2-l007] Rutçǔ. i, Cocoroni vcamuru. *Lembrarſe,*
[c2-l007 note] Rutsuu is explained as bringing something to mind, kokoro ni ukamuru. The printed vcamuru is preserved rather than changed to a modern expected verb form.
[c2-l008 >] *ou ſaber bem de memoria as couſas.*
[c2-l008 note] The second gloss is knowing things well by heart. It completes the memory sense of Rutçǔ and does not describe the following cycle of rebirth.
[c2-l009] Ruten. *Rodeo, ou reuolução. Vt,* Xǒji-
[c2-l009 note] Ruten denotes circulation or revolution. The example begins shooji, birth and death: the damaged vowel has a caron, supporting Xǒji rather than OCR Xiji.
[c2-l010 >] ni ruten ſuru. *Tornar a nacer, & morrer*
[c2-l010 note] Shooji ni ruten suru is cycling through birth and death. The Portuguese begins the interpretation as being born again and dying.
[c2-l011 >] *muitas vezes, como imaginão os gentios.*
[c2-l011 note] The gloss completes the repeated-birth interpretation and attributes it to non-Christian belief in the compilers' terminology. This describes the historical source's viewpoint.
[c2-l012] Ruzai. *O deſterrar, ou condenar ao deſterro*
[c2-l012 note] Ruzai means banishing or sentencing to exile. Its verbal Portuguese explanation continues with the person subjected to that sentence.
[c2-l013 >] *a alguem. Vt,* Ruzaini voconǒ. *Idem.*
[c2-l013 note] Ruzai ni okonau illustrates imposing exile upon someone. The historical voconǒ and caron are retained; Idem refers back to the preceding definition.

[c2-l014 initial=6] SA. *Adu. De exhortar. Vt,*
[c2-l014 note] Sa is an exhortative particle, illustrated below by an invitation to go. The decorated S belongs to this headword and spans six physical rows; OCR ornament fragments are not letters.
[c2-l015 >] Sa mairǒ. *Ea vamos.*
[c2-l015 note] Sa mairoo is an exhortation to go, matching Portuguese Ea vamos. The caron marks the Japanese long vowel; the row remains beside the enlarged initial.
[c2-l016 >] Sa. *Adu. Aßi, ou deßa*
[c2-l016 note] A separate sa means thus or in that way. Its explanation continues below; this ordinary-sized Sa is not part of the decorated initial above.
[c2-l017 >] *maneira. Vt,* Sa naquua.
[c2-l017 note] Sa nakuba is the conditional example, meaning unless it is so. The historical spelling naquua is retained instead of being modernized to the interpretive reading.
[c2-l018 >] *Se aßi não for. Item, He*
[c2-l018 note] The translation is 'unless it is so'. A grammatical description of the suffix sa begins with Item, He; there is no visible hyphen after He.
[c2-l019 >] *particula que no cabo dalgũs no-*
[c2-l019 note] The suffix is described as a particle added at the end of certain words. The decorated initial's lower ornament is excluded from the text; cabo replaces OCR cado.
[c2-l020 >] *mes, ou preſentes de verbos faz fazer nomes*
[c2-l020 note] Mes completes nomes; the explanation says the suffix forms nouns from certain names or present-tense verb forms. The source's grammatical categories are preserved without rewriting them.
[c2-l021 >] *ſuſtantiuos. Vt,* Migotoſa. *Couſa viſto-*
[c2-l021 note] The derived forms are substantives; migotosa illustrates the quality of being splendid or attractive. The printed ſuſtantiuos is not normalized by supplying b.
[c2-l022 >] *ſa, & apraziuel.* Cayeruſa. *Tornada,*
[c2-l022 note] Completes the pleasing-appearance gloss for migotosa, then gives kaerusa as another formation, denoting returning or the return occasion.
[c2-l023 >] *ou conjunção em que ſe hum torna.*
[c2-l023 note] The second paraphrase of kaerusa is the occasion on which one returns. This completes the suffix discussion before the independent Saba entry.
[c2-l024] Saba. *Salua que ſe toma do arroz cozido antes*
[c2-l024 note] Saba is a portion taken from cooked rice before eating. The purpose of setting this portion aside follows on the next physical row.
[c2-l025 >] *de o comer pera offerecer ao Fotoque. ¶* Sa-
[c2-l025 note] The rice portion is offered to the Buddha before eating. Fotoque is a Japanese religious term embedded in the Portuguese sentence; Sa- begins the following example.
[c2-l026 >] bauo toru. *Tomar eſta ſalua.*
[c2-l026 note] Sa- plus bauo gives saba wo toru, taking this offering portion. The Portuguese Salua sense is the reserved portion, not a modern ceremonial salute.
[c2-l027] Saba. *Hum certo peixe como ſauelha.*
[c2-l027 note] This separate saba entry names a fish and compares it to a shad-like fish. The historical Portuguese comparison is retained instead of substituting a modern species name.
[c2-l028] Sabacarino. *Couſa deßa laya, ou como eſ-*
[c2-l028 note] Sabakari no describes something of that sort or degree. The Japanese modifier is printed as one headword, and the Portuguese demonstrative continues across the break.
[c2-l029 >] *ſa, &c.*
[c2-l029 note] This completes eſſa, 'that one', in the comparison above. The short continuation is not a new Japanese word or an omitted headword.
[c2-l030] Sabaqi, qu, aita. *Deſempeçar fios, cabelos*
[c2-l030 note] Sabaki, sabaku, sabaita means disentangling threads or hair here. The principal forms are preserved; the Portuguese list continues below.
[c2-l031 >] *da cabeça, &c. Vt,* Camiuo ſabaqu. *De-*
[c2-l031 note] Kami wo sabaku illustrates disentangling the hair of the head. The final De- is the beginning of the Portuguese translation on the next row.
[c2-l032 >] *ſempeçar os cabelos da cabeça.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes Deſempeçar and translates the hair-disentangling example. This closes the physical handling sense before the administrative homonym.
[c2-l033] Sabaqi, qu, aita. *Ter cuidado, & negocear*
[c2-l033 note] A second sabaku entry means managing affairs. The final infinitive is negocear, not OCR negoceas; the Portuguese object follows below.
[c2-l034 >] *as couſas da caſa, &c. Vt,* Iye, chiguiǒ,
[c2-l034 note] The managed matters concern a household. The Japanese example begins with house and estate or revenue, iye and chigyoo, before continuing its object list.
[c2-l035 >] nandouo ſabaqu. *Ter cuidado, & admi-*
[c2-l035 note] Nando wo sabaku completes the example, managing such things. The Portuguese repeats taking care of and administering them, with admi- divided at the edge.
[c2-l036 >] *niſtrar a caſa, renda, &c.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes adminiſtrar and identifies the house and income as its objects. This is the translation of the preceding Japanese management example.
[c2-l037] Sabaqi. *Adminiſtração, ou meneo das cou-*
[c2-l037 note] The noun sabaki denotes administration or handling. Cou- begins the Portuguese noun for the things being managed on the next physical row.
[c2-l038 >] *ſas de caſa, &c.*
[c2-l038 note] Completes couſas and specifies household affairs. This ends the management noun before a separately defined cord or leash.
[c2-l039] Sabaqi. *Hum cordel, ou trela comque leuão*
[c2-l039 note] A further sabaki entry denotes a cord or leash. Its use is described by the relative clause continuing on the next line.
[c2-l040 >] *amarrados os caẽs à caça, &c.*
[c2-l040 note] The leash keeps dogs tied while they are taken hunting. The scan supports caẽs as one word; the following bottom-right fragment is a catchword, not another body row.
