[c1-l001] Tôgocu. Figaxino cuni. *Reinos da parte*
[c1-l001 note] Tōgoku is explained as higashi no kuni, countries to the east. The Portuguese plural Reinos retains the geographical scope rather than identifying one modern region.
[c1-l002 >] *do Oriente.*
[c1-l002 note] Do Oriente completes the preceding geographical definition: kingdoms on the eastern side. This short continuation belongs to Tôgocu.
[c1-l003] Tôgǔ. *Erdeiro, que ſocede no imperio ao Rey.*
[c1-l003 note] Tōgū denotes the heir who succeeds the king in the empire. The last headword vowel is a caroned u, not OCR o; the Portuguese Rey is wholly italic.
[c1-l004] Toguchi. *Porta, ou entrada da porta.*
[c1-l004 note] Toguchi is an entrance or doorway. The Portuguese Porta and entrada da porta give the architectural sense without changing the printed Japanese spelling.
[c1-l005] Toguchi. *Porta, ou taboa, que ſe poem na*
[c1-l005 note] The second Toguchi names a gate or board set at a river entrance. The following lines distinguish this hydraulic device from the preceding ordinary doorway.
[c1-l006 >] *entrada dalgũs rios dependurada, aqual com a*
[c1-l006 note] The board hangs at the entrance of certain rivers. Dependurada and aqual are preserved in their printed historical forms and spacing.
[c1-l007 >] *enchẽte da marè ſe fecha, & com a vazãte, ou*
[c1-l007 note] The gate closes with the incoming tide and changes position with the ebb. Both abbreviated nasal forms enchẽte and vazãte remain unexpanded.
[c1-l008 >] *corrente do rio ſe abre, & aleuanta.*
[c1-l008 note] The river current opens and lifts the board. This completes the mechanical explanation of the river-mouth gate.
[c1-l009] Tǒgun. *Eſta comarca, ou parte do Reino, on-*
[c1-l009 note] Tōgun is the district or part of a kingdom where the speaker is. The broken on- begins onde on the next physical line.
[c1-l010 >] *de hum eſtà.*
[c1-l010 note] De completes onde: the locality in which one is situated. The grave accent in eſtà is retained.
[c1-l011] Togura. *Caſa de paßaros.*
[c1-l011 note] Togura is a bird-house, matching casa de passaros. The printed ß-like sort in paßaros is retained rather than expanded or modernized.
[c1-l012] Tǒgura. *Enxada da China, que tem o cabo me-*
[c1-l012 note] Tōgura names a Chinese hoe with its handle inserted through the middle of the iron. The me- at the edge continues in metido below.
[c1-l013 >] *tido pollo meyo do ferro.*
[c1-l013 note] Tido completes metido; the handle passes through the middle of the iron blade. The visible rr in ferro corrects OCR fenro.
[c1-l014] Tôgua. *Abobora, ou combalenga.*
[c1-l014 note] Tōgua is glossed as abobora or combalenga, a gourd or melon-like vegetable. The historical Portuguese plant-name combalenga is retained without imposing a modern botanical identification.
[c1-l015] Tǒguat. Ataru tçuqi. *Eſte mes, ou eſta lũa.*
[c1-l015 note] Tōguat is explained by ataru tsuki, the present month or moon. The final t is the historical checked ending and is not expanded into an extra syllable.
[c1-l016] Tǒgi. i, Yuni iru. *Tomar os banhos, ou en*
[c1-l016 note] Tōgi is taking the waters or entering a hot spring, as yuni iru indicates. The scan reads Tomar rather than OCR Tomer; en continues as entrar on the next row without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l017 >] *trar nas caldas por algũa doença.*
[c1-l017 note] Trar completes entrar: entering the thermal baths for an illness. The historical caldas supplies the hot-spring context for yuni iru.
[c1-l018] Togi, zzuru, ita. *Tapar, ou fechar. Vt,*
[c1-l018 note] Toji, tozuru, tojita is the verb for shutting or closing. The printed headword is Togi, not OCR Tosri; the Japanese g here belongs to the historical ji/gi notation.
[c1-l019 >] Cado. l, monuo tozzuru. *Fechar apor-*
[c1-l019 note] Kado or mon wo tozuru illustrates closing a gate. The article remains attached in Portuguese apor-; the visible stop after Cado is a period.
[c1-l020 >] *ta. ¶* Cuchi. l, Meuo tozzuru. *Fe-*
[c1-l020 note] Ta completes aporta. The next example uses kuchi or me wo tozuru, closing the mouth or eyes; the Portuguese fechar is again split across rows.
[c1-l021 >] *char a boca, ou os olhos. ¶* Vaguemonouo
[c1-l021 note] Char completes fechar, then the next Japanese example begins wagemono wo, referring to bentwood containers. Its verb follows on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] tozzuru. *Liar, ou fechar caixas, bocetas,*
[c1-l022 note] Tozuru completes the container example: tying or closing boxes and small cases. Bocetas is the historical word for small boxes in this context.
[c1-l023 >] *&c. cõ algũa caſca de aruore, &c. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l023 note] The boxes are bound with tree bark or similar material. The abbreviated Itẽ introduces another application of the same verb and is not expanded.
[c1-l024 >] *Encadernar, ou coſer ajuntando. Vt,* Qiǒ-
[c1-l024 note] The next sense is binding a book or joining by sewing. Kyō wo tozuru begins as Qiǒ- here and continues on the next physical line.
[c1-l025 >] uo tozzuru. *¶* Côrini togiraruru. *Eſ-*
[c1-l025 note] Uo tozzuru completes the book-binding example. Kōri ni tojiraruru then describes being enclosed or immobilized by ice; the Portuguese estar begins at the edge.
[c1-l026 >] *tar enregelado, & como tolheito com frio.*
[c1-l026 note] Tar completes estar: being frozen and as if disabled by cold. The scan supports tolheito rather than the OCR fragment tolheuo.
[c1-l027] Togiai, ǒ, ǒta. *Fecharſe, ou pegarſe hũa cou-*
[c1-l027 note] Tojiai denotes soft surfaces closing or sticking together. The principal forms with caroned o are retained as printed historical inflections.
[c1-l028 >] *ſa molle com outra. Vt,* Mega togiyǒta.
[c1-l028 note] The definition completes one soft thing sticking to another. Me ga togiyōta introduces the example of the eyes becoming stuck shut.
[c1-l029 >] *Fecharamſe os olhos pegandoſe hũa peſtana, ou*
[c1-l029 note] The eyelids adhere to one another, explaining the eyes closing in the preceding example. Pestana is paired with capela do olho on the next row.
[c1-l030 >] *capela do olho na outra.*
[c1-l030 note] Capela do olho completes the anatomical paraphrase for an eyelid. The expression belongs to the sticking-eyelids example rather than a separate item.
[c1-l031] Togicomori, ru, otta. *Encerrarſe, ou eſtar*
[c1-l031 note] Tojikomori is shutting oneself in or remaining enclosed. The abbreviated ru and otta record the principal forms without expansion.
[c1-l032 >] *encerrado.*
[c1-l032 note] Encerrado completes estar encerrado, being shut in. This short continuation closes the preceding entry.
[c1-l033] Togifuſagui, u, ida. *Fechar, ou cerrar co-*
[c1-l033 note] Tojifusagi means shutting or blocking an opening. The gloss uses fechar and cerrar, then gives doors and entrances as examples on the following row.
[c1-l034 >] *mo porta, entrada, &c.*
[c1-l034 note] Mo completes como, introducing a door or entrance as the object that is closed. No independent headword begins in this short continuation.
[c1-l035] Togimame. *Certa iguaria, que ſe faz de fei-*
[c1-l035 note] Tojimame is a prepared food made from beans. The word feijões is divided after fei- at the physical line end.
[c1-l036 >] *jões torrados, & hũa raiz de erua moida.*
[c1-l036 note] Jões completes feijões; toasted beans are combined with a ground herb-root. The definition alone does not identify the exact root species.
[c1-l037] Togime, uru, eta. *Concluir, ou aueriguar al-*
[c1-l037 note] Tojime means concluding or establishing a matter. The scan supports Concluir, while OCR Conclar loses the weak medial letters.
[c1-l038 >] *gũa couſa. ¶* Cotobauo rogimuru. *A-*
[c1-l038 note] Gũa completes alguma. The example concerns verifying someone’s words, so tojimuru is expected from the headword, but the scan’s first type in rogimuru has the short r-shaped head recorded by OCR. That printed anomaly is retained; the Portuguese Averiguar begins with divided A-.
[c1-l039 >] *veriguar as palauras, que hum diße, ou por que*
[c1-l039 note] Veriguar completes Averiguar, checking what someone said or why it was said. The row begins with lowercase v, and the visible diße corrects OCR dißle.
[c1-l040 >] *as diße, &c. ¶* Iytogimete voqu. *Dei-*
[c1-l040 note] The first clause ends with why the words were spoken. Ii tojimete oku, printed continuously as Iytogimete voqu, introduces leaving a matter settled by speaking; Dei- begins deixar.
[c1-l041 >] *xar algũa couſa aueriguada, & bẽ arrecadada.*
[c1-l041 note] Xar completes deixar: leaving something settled and well secured. The weak but visible d in aueriguada corrects OCR aueriguaa.
[c1-l042] Togime. *Vt,* Togimeuo ſuru. *Idem.*
[c1-l042 note] The noun Togime with wo suru expresses the same act of settling or verifying. Idem explicitly refers back to the preceding verb entry.
[c1-l043] Tǒgin. Ataru gin. *Eſte arrayal, ou exercito.*
[c1-l043 note] Tōjin is the present encampment or army, explained by ataru jin. The final letter of gin is n rather than OCR u.
[c1-l044] Tôgiǒ. Tatacǒ niua. *Campo, ou terreiro da*
[c1-l044 note] Tōjō is a fighting-ground, explained by tatakau niwa in its historical printed form Tatacǒ niua. The Portuguese identifies a field or open space for battle.
[c1-l045 >] *batalha, ou briga.*
[c1-l045 note] The field is for a battle or fight. This short row completes the preceding Tôgiǒ entry.
[c1-l046] Tǒgiǒ. Catana, tçuye. *Eſpada, & pao, ou*
[c1-l046 note] A distinct Tōjō is explained as katana and tsue, sword and staff. The Portuguese second equivalent continues with bordão on the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *bordão. ¶* Qiǔxen tǒgioni tazzuſana-
[c1-l047 note] Bordão completes the staff gloss. The expression involving kyūsen and tōjō describes occupation with weapons and warfare. The scan has two z types in tazzuſana-, whose final ru continues across the column boundary; no missing second o accent is supplied in tǒgioni.

[c2-l007] Tǒgiǔ. i, Tǒgiǔgi. *Superior dagora, ou de*
[c2-l007 note] Tōjū, equated with tōjūji, is the current superior or incumbent. The paired forms are preserved with their visibly caroned o and u.
[c2-l008 >] *preſente.*
[c2-l008 note] De presente completes the definition of the superior who holds office now. It does not begin a new entry.
[c2-l009] Toi. *Homem em cuja caſa communmente ſe aga-*
[c2-l009 note] Toi describes a host or lodging intermediary whose house receives guests. The verb agasalhar is divided after aga- at the edge.
[c2-l010 >] *ſalhão os hoſpedes, & que os negocea do que tẽ*
[c2-l010 note] The host lodges visitors and negotiates or arranges what they need. Negocea is visibly one word, correcting the OCR separation nego cea.
[c2-l011 >] *neceßidade principalmẽte de embarcação.*
[c2-l011 note] Their needs concern transport by vessel in particular. The scan has contiguous neceßidade and an abbreviation tilde in principalmẽte, rather than the OCR space and grave accent.
[c2-l012] Tôi. Figaxino yebnù. *Gente ruſtica, ou bar-*
[c2-l012 note] Tōi names eastern peoples characterized by the gloss as rustic or barbarous. Figaxino means eastern; the following damaged word is evidently intended as yebisu, but its surviving printed sequence is retained as yebnù with uncertainty. The Portuguese reads ruſtica, not OCR rufaica.
[c2-l013 >] *bara da parte do Leſte.*
[c2-l013 note] Bara completes barbara, followed by the geographical qualification that these people are from the east. The historical characterization belongs to the source.
[c2-l014] Toi, ô, ôta. *Perguntar.*
[c2-l014 note] Toi, tō, tōta is the verb to ask or inquire. Its circumflexed principal forms are not replaced with modern Japanese conjugations.
[c2-l015] Toi. *Cano de pao poſtiço por onde corre a agoa*
[c2-l015 note] This Toi is a wooden gutter or conduit carrying roof water outside. The following example concerns setting the gutter in place.
[c2-l016 >] *dos telhados pera fora. ¶* Toiuo caquru.
[c2-l016 note] Water runs from the roofs outward; toi wo kakuru then names installing the gutter. The Japanese expression is roman amid the italic gloss.
[c2-l017 >] *Por eſte cano em ſeu lugar.*
[c2-l017 note] Por este cano em seu lugar translates the preceding example as putting the conduit in its place. Printed Por is retained without adding an unprinted accent.
[c2-l018] Toicaqe, ru, eta. *Perguntar a alguem.*
[c2-l018 note] Toikake is asking a person a question. The abbreviated ru and eta supply the remaining verb forms.
[c2-l019] Toicauaxi, ſu, ita. *Perguntar entreſi hum a*
[c2-l019 note] Toikawashi is asking one another, as in exchanging inquiries about health or circumstances. The headword reads Toicauaxi, without the spurious t in OCR Toicautaxi.
[c2-l020 >] *outro como eſtà, &c.*
[c2-l020 note] The two people ask each other how they are. This completes the reciprocal sense of the preceding verb.
[c2-l021] Toicotaye, uru, eta. *Pergũtar, & reſpõder.*
[c2-l021 note] Toikotaye combines asking and answering. The second Portuguese verb visibly has õ in reſpõder, not OCR ã, preserving its abbreviated nasal spelling.
[c2-l022] Tǒin. Taitǒno coye. *Voz, ou ſonjonete*
[c2-l022 note] Tōin is the voice or distinctive sound associated with China, explained as Taitō no koe. The Portuguese sonjonete denotes a characteristic manner or tone of speaking.
[c2-l023 >] *pro prio da Chinu.*
[c2-l023 note] The sound is particular to China. The narrow internal gap in pro prio is retained from the aligned OCR after enlargement; Chinu likewise retains the visible final u-like type rather than being silently regularized to China.
[c2-l024] Toiqi. *Suſpiros. ¶* Toiqiuo tçuqu. *Dar*
[c2-l024 note] Toiki is a sigh; toiki wo tsuku gives the expression for sighing. Portuguese Dar begins its translation on this row.
[c2-l025 >] *ſuſpiros.*
[c2-l025 note] Suspiros completes dar suspiros, to heave sighs. The scan’s piros corrects the malformed OCR pines.
[c2-l026] Toitomurai, ǒ, ǒta. *Viſitar a alguem, ou*
[c2-l026 note] Toitomurai is visiting someone or arranging a visit on their behalf. The final Portuguese alternative marker is ou, with a visible u that OCR omitted.
[c2-l027 >] *mãdalo viſitar por doẽte, ou por outro reſpeito.*
[c2-l027 note] The visit may be because the person is ill or for another reason. The tildes in mãdalo and doẽte remain abbreviated rather than expanded.
[c2-l028] Tojacu. i, Caqitçubata. *Lirio.*
[c2-l028 note] Tojacu is identified with kakitsubata and glossed lirio. The historical botanical equivalence is preserved without substituting a modern species label.
[c2-l029] Tôjacu. l, tôxacu. *Pardaes que eſtão pele-*
[c2-l029 note] Tōjacu or tōxacu denotes sparrows fighting. The Portuguese pelejando is divided after pele- at the edge.
[c2-l030 >] *jando, & picandoſe hum a outro. ¶* Tôja-
[c2-l030 note] Jando completes pelejando, and the sparrows peck one another. Picandoſe is printed contiguously; Tôja- begins the illustrative saying.
[c2-l031 >] cu fitoni vogizu. *Os pardaes que eſtão pele-*
[c2-l031 note] Cu completes Tôjacu. The saying states that fighting sparrows do not fear people; the Portuguese translation again divides pelejando after pele-.
[c2-l032 >] *jando não tem medo da gente.*
[c2-l032 note] The sparrows engaged in fighting are unafraid of people. This completes the preceding Japanese saying.
[c2-l033] Tǒjen. Xenni ataru. i, *Valer por mil ¶* Ichi-
[c2-l033 note] Tōzen means being worth a thousand, explicitly explained by sen ni ataru. Ichi- begins ichinin, one person, in the example on the next row.
[c2-l034 >] nin tǒjenno mononari. *Hum tem pormil.*
[c2-l034 note] Nin completes ichinin: one person is worth a thousand. The Portuguese Hum tem pormil gives the same valuation and has a visible final stop.
[c2-l035] Tojen. l, tojenna. *O eſtar ſò, & ſolitario.*

[c2-l038 >] *Ter fome.*

[c2-l039 >] Tojenni.
[c2-l039 note] Tojenni gives the adverbial form of the preceding word. It is printed on its own short indented row without a separate Portuguese definition.
[c2-l040 >] Tojenſa.
[c2-l040 note] Tojenſa gives the corresponding abstract noun. The isolated derivative remains its own physical line.
[c2-l041] Tôjen. Toboxibino maye. *Diante, ou jum*
[c2-l041 note] Tōjen is being before the lamp, explained by toboshibi no mae. The Portuguese begins Diante, ou jum, retaining the printed-looking m at the edge rather than normalizing the divided junto.
[c2-l042 >] *to da candea aceſa.*
[c2-l042 note] To completes the preceding divided word for being beside the lighted lamp. The definition ends with candea aceſa.
[c2-l043] Tôjen. *Vt,* Fuppô tôjen. *O dilatarſe a ley,*
[c2-l043 note] This Tōjen means spreading eastward. The badly abraded example is semantically Buppō tōzen, the Buddhist law spreading east, but its surviving first capital looks F-shaped; Fuppô is chosen provisionally rather than claiming a fully secure B.
[c2-l044 >] *ou doutrina de* Xaca *pera a parte de Oriente*
[c2-l044 note] The teaching of Xaca spreads toward the east. Xaca is a Japanese name embedded within the Portuguese explanation and is wholly roman; Oriente corrects the initial C and final a produced by OCR.
[c2-l045 >] *ſ. China, & Iapão.*
[c2-l045 note] The explanatory ſ. specifies China and Japan as the eastern lands. The historical Iapão spelling and its tilde are retained.
[c2-l046] Tôji. Fuyu itaru. *Coração do inuerno que he*
[c2-l046 note] Tōji is the winter solstice, explained as fuyu itaru. The Portuguese calls it the heart of winter and continues with its month on the next row.
[c2-l047 >] *na vndicima lua. ¶* Guexi. *He a for-*
[c2-l047 note] The winter solstice falls in the eleventh lunar month. The damaged vndicima lua is chosen from the surviving strokes, without supplying an unestablished tilde over lua. The following abraded Guexi identifies the summer solstice; its explanation begins He a for- and ends in the restored body continuation below. These readings remain nonblocking caveats.
[u2b-l001 >] *ça do verão.*
[u2b-l001 note] Ça completes força: the summer solstice is described as the strength or height of summer. This is the final body row, separated from the distinct right-hand catchword that the OCR had merged into it.
