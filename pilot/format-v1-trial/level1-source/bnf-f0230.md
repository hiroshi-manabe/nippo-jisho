[c1a-l001] Fǔzocu. *Cuſtume do reino, ou nação, cida-*
[c1a-l001 note] Fǔzocu is fūzoku, the customs of a country or community. The scan clearly has u with caron, not the OCR's second F, and nação rather than noção.
[c1a-l002 >] *de, &c.*
[c1a-l002 note] de completes cidade from the preceding physical row. Cities join kingdoms and nations as communities whose customs are being defined; no new entry begins here.
[c1a-l003] Fuzonbun. *Parecer inconſiderado, ou impru-*
[c1a-l003 note] Fuzonbun denotes an ill-considered opinion or imprudence. The negative fu and zonbun, a judgment or view, fit both the Japanese formation and Portuguese explanation.
[c1a-l004 >] *dencia. ¶* Fuzonbunna mono. *Peſ-*
[c1a-l004 note] The adjective fuzonbunna qualifies mono, a person. The Portuguese equivalent begins Peſ- here and continues with soa on the next physical line.
[c1a-l005 >] *soa imprudente.*
[c1a-l005 note] soa completes the word for person, followed by imprudente. This short continuation belongs to the same example and should not be mistaken for a separate headword.
[c1a-l006] Fuzuqi. *Paa comprida calçada de ferro de que*
[c1a-l006 note] Fuzuqi names a long iron-shod digging implement. Paa is the printed Portuguese spelling for a spade; it is not replaced by modern pá.
[c1a-l007 >] *vſão os lauradores, ou os que fazem vallos.*
[c1a-l007 note] The implement is used by farmers and people making ditches. vallos is an older Portuguese form; its doubled l is retained rather than modernized.
[c1a-l008 >] X. *No Cami ſe diz,* Suqi.
[c1a-l008 note] The regional label X. precedes the contrast with Cami, where Suqi is said. X. and the embedded Japanese name Suqi are upright here; the surrounding explanation is italic.
[c1a-l009] Fuzzucuri, u, utta. *Inuentar. ¶ Item,*
[c1a-l009 note] Fuzzucuri has the printed inflection u, utta and the sense of inventing or devising. Its historical consonant pattern is not regularized to a modern spelling.
[c1a-l010 >] *Cortar, & cercear papel. Vt,* Camiuo fuz
[c1a-l010 note] The second sense concerns cutting and trimming paper. Camiuo fuz continues across the physical break without supplying an unprinted hyphen.
[c1a-l011 >] zucuru, l, qiriſoroyuru. *¶ Itẽ, Tra-*
[c1a-l011 note] The continuation forms fuzzucuru, with qiriſoroyuru as an alternative, cutting evenly. Enlargement shows an irregular oblique ink trace above the second o, not a secure tilde or length mark; the unaccented OCR reading is retained with this uncertainty recorded.
[c1a-l012 >] *çar, & aparelhar algũa couſa caladamẽte.*
[c1a-l012 note] The third sense is planning or preparing something quietly. caladamẽte is the printed nasal abbreviation, not a reason to expand the diplomatic text.
[c1a-l013] Fuzzucuri. *Concerto, ou inuenção.*
[c1a-l013 note] The noun Fuzzucuri is an arrangement or invention. It closes the F material before the separate heading announcing words beginning with G.

[c1b-l001 initial=6] GA. *He particula de Nomi-*
[c1b-l001 note] The large decorated G combines with the small A to form GA, not the OCR's Bzmo A. The opening introduces the particle's nominative use; the whole initial needs an overlapping crop.

[c1b-l004 >] *hum homem. ¶* Soregaxi
[c1b-l004 note] The Portuguese completes 'a person came'; Soregaxi begins the next example. It is the first-person expression soregashi, not another Portuguese word.

[c1b-l005 note] ga mairǒzuru completes 'I shall go.' The historical future form in -ǒzuru is intelligible without replacing it by a modern conjugation.
[c1b-l006 >] *¶ Item, He particula de ge*
[c1b-l006 note] A new Item gives ga a genitive function. The split ge/nitiuo is physical line wrapping; the decorated initial beside this row is not additional text.
[c1b-l007 >] *nitiuo. Vt,* Soregaxiga mo

[c1b-l011 note] cacarǒtote maittaga means coming intending to meet someone, followed by an intervening obstacle. ſaxiuǒ is the historical obstacle expression; its unusual form is retained.
[c1b-l012 >] zatte, *&c. Vim para vos ver, porem ſo-*
[c1b-l012 note] go/zatte completes the polite existential verb. The Portuguese says 'I came to see you, but'; the &c. is explanatory typography, not Japanese reading material.
[c1b-l013 >] *breuindo hum negocio, &c. ¶ Item, Tem*
[c1b-l013 note] breuindo completes sobreuindo, an affair having intervened. The next Item opens another grammatical function rather than another lexical headword.
[c1b-l014 >] *força de infinitiuo. Vt,* Cǔtaga yoi. *He*
[c1b-l014 note] Cǔtaga yoi is glossed as it being good to have eaten. This is the contracted past of eating, not a word that should be changed to modern kutta.
[c1b-l015 >] *bom ter comido. ¶ Item, De accuſatiuo.*
[c1b-l015 note] The explanation closes the eating example and labels the next use accusative. This is the dictionary's grammatical classification, retained without imposing modern terminology.
[c1b-l016 >] *Vt,* Feiqeno yuraiga qiqitai. *Deſejo de*

[c1b-l018 >] *tros modos de falar menos principais os quaes*
[c1b-l018 note] tros completes outros from the previous row. The dictionary notes other, less central usages of ga; this is continuous Portuguese prose.
[c1b-l019 >] *ſe podem ver na arte.*
[c1b-l019 note] na arte refers the reader to a grammar for those additional uses. No title beyond the printed word arte is supplied in the transcription.
[c1b-l020] Ga. *Brio, coragem, ou parecer, & opinião.*
[c1b-l020 note] This Ga is now a lexical noun, pride, spirit, or one's own opinion. The scan supports opinião with its missing i restored, not OCR's opinão.

[c1b-l021 note] Gauo voru is ga wo oru, breaking or bending one's pride. The printed uo and vo conventions make the Japanese expression intelligible without modernizing it.
[c1b-l022 >] *ou brio. ¶ Item,* Gauo voru, *per met.*
[c1b-l022 note] The same expression is introduced again in a metaphorical use. per met. is explanatory Portuguese/Latin abbreviation and remains italic.
[c1b-l023 >] *eſpantarſe, ou ficar frio de ver algũa couſa*
[c1b-l023 note] The figurative sense is being startled or chilled by seeing something unusual. The adjective naming the unusual thing continues on the following line.
[c1b-l024 >] *inſolita. ¶* Gauo fucai, l, tçuyoi fito.
[c1b-l024 note] Gauo fucai and tçuyoi fito describe a deeply or strongly self-willed person. The conspicuous uo is retained even where modern idiom might suggest a different particle.
[c1b-l025 >] *Homem de grande brio, & eſpiritos. ¶* Ga
[c1b-l025 note] The gloss describes someone with strong spirit and pride. Ga at the end starts another example, not an isolated Portuguese word.
[c1b-l026 >] uo tatçuru. *Ter teſo em ſua opinião, ou*
[c1b-l026 note] uo tatçuru is ga wo tatsuru, maintaining one's position. The Portuguese has opinião; its i is not the OCR's j.

[c1b-l027 note] Gauo daſu is showing one's spirit or resolve. The scan reads plain u, and Mostrar has no extra h; the explanation continues at the top of column two.
