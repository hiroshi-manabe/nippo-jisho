[c2-l001] Moyai. *O liar, ou amarrar hũa embarcação à*
[c2-l001 note] Moyai here is the nominal act of tying or mooring one vessel to another. The final preposition leads directly into outra on the next physical row.

[c2-l003 note] Moya is a damp, rainy mist. The scan reads chuuoſa, retaining historical u for consonantal v; the draft inserted an extra n.
[c2-l004 >] Moyaga vorita. *Cahio, ou abaixouſe eſ-*

[c2-l005 >] *ta neuoa.*

[c2-l006] Moyaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer nacer, ou arrebentar*
[c2-l006 note] Moyashi here is the transitive act of making seed germinate. Fazer nacer and arrebentar are the two Portuguese descriptions of sprouting.
[c2-l007 >] *a ſemente de trigo, grãos, ou outros legumes*
[c2-l007 note] The seed is wheat, pulses, or other legumes. Semente is one printed word; the draft's se mente was an artificial split.
[c2-l008 >] *metendoa na agoa, & depois abafandoa.*
[c2-l008 note] The seeds are put in water and then covered or kept close. These preparation steps explain the preceding sprouting sense, not the burning sense below.
[c2-l009] Moyaxi. *Vt,* Mugui. l, mameno moya-
[c2-l009 note] The noun moyashi is illustrated by mugi or mame no moyashi, wheat or bean sprouts. The example's final word is divided after moya-.
[c2-l010 >] xi. *Olhos, ou gomos do trigo, ou grãos que*
[c2-l010 note] Xi completes moyashi. Olhos and gomos identify the shoots or buds produced by the grain or pulses.
[c2-l011 >] *arrebentam desta maneira.*
[c2-l011 note] Arrebentam desta maneira refers back to the germination method just described. The print's provisional st allograph is retained pending the specialist typography pass.
[c2-l012] Moyaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer arder. ¶* Fiuo
[c2-l012 note] A distinct moyashi entry means causing something to burn. Hi wo begins the fire-lighting example on the same row.
[c2-l013 >] moyaſu. *Acender o fogo.*

[c2-l014] Moye, uru, eta. *Arder o fogo.*
[c2-l014 note] Moye is the intransitive verb: the fire burns. The principal parts uru and eta are preserved in their historical form.
[c2-l015] Moyeagari, ru, atta. *Aleuantarſe grande*
[c2-l015 note] Moyeagari means flames rising high. The Portuguese reads grande, not gronde; the subject labareda follows on the next row.
[c2-l016 >] *labareda. Vt,* Yacatani fiuo caqetareba,
[c2-l016 note] The example begins yacata ni hi wo kaketareba, setting fire to a residence or building. The following rows describe the resulting flames and smoke.
[c2-l017 >] miǒyen ſacanni moyeagari, curoqemuri
[c2-l017 note] The flames rise vigorously and black smoke follows. The long-vowel spelling miǒyen is retained from the visible text, without substituting a normalized Japanese transcription.
[c2-l018 >] tenuo caſumetari. Taif. *Lib. 70.*
[c2-l018 note] Ten wo kasumetari completes the smoke darkening the sky. Taif. is the upright citation label; Lib. 70. is retained as visibly printed, rather than changing the number to fit an assumed source.
[c2-l019 >] *Pondo fogo nas caſas, & aleuantandoſe grãde*
[c2-l019 note] The Portuguese translation repeats setting fire to houses and the rising of a great flame. The abbreviation grãde preserves its nasal mark.
[c2-l020 >] *labareda a fumaça eſcurecia ao ceo.*
[c2-l020 note] The smoke darkened the sky, completing the quoted fire scene. The historical ceo is retained rather than modern céu.
[c2-l021] Moyecui. *Tição, ou pao que fica queimado a-*
[c2-l021 note] Moyekui is a charred stick or firebrand, even when it no longer burns. The final a- begins ainda on the next row.
[c2-l022 >] *inda que ſeja ſem fogo. ¶* Moyecuini figa
[c2-l022 note] The definition explicitly allows a firebrand without live fire. Moyekui ni hi ga begins an example about how easily it catches again.
[c2-l023 >] tçuqiyoi. *No pao queimado, ou tição fa-*
[c2-l023 note] Tsukiyoi completes the claim that fire readily catches on a previously burnt stick. The Portuguese facilmente is divided after fa-.
[c2-l024 >] *cilmente ſe pega o fogo. ¶ Item, per met.*
[c2-l024 note] Cilmente completes facilmente; the fire catches easily. Item, per met. introduces the figurative application that follows.
[c2-l025 >] *Eſtar facilitado pera cair ẽ peccado por via da*
[c2-l025 note] The figurative sense is susceptibility to falling into sin. This is the dictionary's moral comparison, not a literal second description of wood.
[c2-l026 >] *ocaſião primeira estar perto, &c.*
[c2-l026 note] The prior occasion of sin remains nearby, explaining the susceptibility. The printed estar is retained with the draft's provisional st treatment.
[c2-l027] Moyecuſa. *Eriſipula.*
[c2-l027 note] Moyekusa is glossed by the historical Portuguese disease-name Eriſipula. This entry is not the plant-germination sense suggested by the preceding moye- forms.
[c2-l028] Moyegui. *Verde eſcuro.*
[c2-l028 note] Moyegi is defined here as dark green. The dictionary's stated shade is retained without substituting a modern colour standard.
[c2-l029] Môyei. i, Fude. *Pena de eſcreuer.* S.
[c2-l029 note] Mooyei is equated with fude, a writing implement. The scan has a clear circumflex on o and an upright final S. usage label.
[c2-l030] Moyeide. zzuru, eta. *Começarem a nacer as*
[c2-l030 note] Moyeide in this entry means plants beginning to sprout. The printed zzuru principal part belongs to the historical idzuru form, not a doubled-letter OCR error.
[c2-l031 >] *eruas, trigo, &c.*
[c2-l031 note] Herbs, wheat, and similar plants are the subjects that begin to grow. This short continuation closes the germination sense.
[c2-l032] Moyeide, zzuru, eta. *Começar aleuantarſe*
[c2-l032 note] The next moyeide concerns flames beginning to rise. Its identical headword is intentionally repeated for the burning sense.
[c2-l033 >] *a labareda, ou acenderſe o fogo.*
[c2-l033 note] The flame rises or the fire catches. The scan reads acenderſe, with c rather than the draft r in arenderſe.
[c2-l034] Moyeſaxi. *Tição.*
[c2-l034 note] Moyesashi is a firebrand or partly burnt piece of wood. It is a separate synonym, not an inflected continuation of moyeide.
[c2-l035] Moyeſuſari. *Idem.* B.
[c2-l035 note] Moyesusari has the same meaning as the preceding firebrand term. The upright B. marks low or vulgar usage and remains outside the italic Idem.
[c2-l036] Moyetçuqi, u, uita. *Atearſe o fogo, ou*
[c2-l036 note] Moyetsuki means fire taking hold or catching. The scan has a continuous headword and the continuous Portuguese verb Atearſe, correcting both artificial spaces in the draft.
[c2-l037 >] *pegarſe.*

[c2-l038] Moyǒ. *Modo, ou gesto, ou meneos.*
[c2-l038 note] Moyoo denotes manner, gesture, or bodily movements here. The gloss is broader than a modern textile-pattern sense and is left as the source states it.
[c2-l039] Moyouoxi, ſu, oita. *Excitar, ou mouer.*
[c2-l039 note] Moyooshi means arousing, prompting, or moving someone. The examples that follow distinguish religious fervour from assembling people for war.
[c2-l040 >] *¶* Xinjinuo moyouoſu. *Excitarſe, &c.*
[c2-l040 note] Shinjin wo moyoosu means stirring up religious devotion. The Portuguese excitarſe is followed by a further expression for becoming inflamed with devotion.
[c2-l041 >] *acenderſe em deuação. ¶* Ninjuuo moyo-
[c2-l041 note] The scan reads acenderſe em deuação, lighting up with devotion, rather than the draft atenderſe. Ninju wo moyoosu then begins the example of mobilizing people.
[c2-l042 >] uoſu. *Excitar, & aparelhar a gente pera a*
[c2-l042 note] Uoſu completes moyoosu. The Portuguese describes rousing and preparing people for a purpose supplied below.
[c2-l043 >] *guerra, &c.*
[c2-l043 note] Guerra completes the mobilization example: preparing people for war. The continuation is not a separate military headword.
[c2-l044] Moyouoxi. *Fervor, ou aparelho dalgũa couſa.*
[c2-l044 note] The noun moyooshi is fervour or preparation for something. The worn middle letter of Fervor is read as v, not the draft t; the gloss matches the preceding examples.
[c2-l045] Mǒzǒ. *Eſpecies, ou fantaſmas que ſe repreſẽ-*
[c2-l045 note] Moozoo concerns images or phantasms of improper things. Both o marks point downward in this impression; the first is not the draft circumflex.
[c2-l046 >] *tão de couſas torpes, ou deſordenadas. ¶ Per*
[c2-l046 note] The images concern shameful or disordered things. Deſordenadas is the adopted reading of a heavily abraded span: surviving o/r/d traces and the Portuguese context support it, but several strokes remain weak.
[c2-l047 >] *met.* Mǒzǒuo miru. *Ver em ſonhos couſas*
[c2-l047 note] Per met. introduces moozoo wo miru, seeing such things in dreams. The Portuguese object continues in torpes on the bottom body row, which the draft had merged with the catchword.
[u2b-l001 >] *torpes.*
[u2b-l001 note] Torpes completes the improper things seen in dreams. This left-positioned italic word is running text, not the catchword; the separately placed Mozu. anticipates the next page’s bird entry.
