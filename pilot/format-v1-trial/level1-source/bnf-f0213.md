## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Foriume, ru, eta. *Entulhar cauando.*
[c1-l001 note] Horiume means filling by digging, as with a ditch. Its actual first row was clipped by the old geometry; the replacement crop now shows the whole definition and column boundary.
[c1-l002] Foriuocoxi, ſu, oita. *Tirar fora, ou arran-*
[c1-l002 note] Horiokoshi is digging something out or uprooting it. Arran- begins arrancar, and the historical oita ending is retained.
[c1-l003 >] *car como pedras, &c. cauando.*
[c1-l003 note] Stones are the objects extracted by digging. Car completes arrancar from the preceding row, not a new Portuguese noun.
[c1-l004] Foriuzzume, uru, eta. *Enterrar, ou cobrir*
[c1-l004 note] Horiuzume is burying or covering over. The compound combines excavation with filling; uru and eta are the printed inflectional endings.
[c1-l005 >] *com terra.*
[c1-l005 note] Com terra specifies covering with earth. The short line remains the completion of horiuzume's definition.
[c1-l006] Foriuzzumi, mu, unda. *Idem.*
[c1-l006 note] Horiuzumi is a related verbal form with mu and unda endings. Idem refers to the preceding burying sense rather than requiring its whole gloss to be repeated.
[c1-l007] Foro. *Certa diuiſa de que vſão na guerra como*
[c1-l007 note] Horo is a military identifying device. The Portuguese account describes its form in the next row, rather than treating foro as Portuguese jurisdiction.
[c1-l008 >] *pauelhanſinho que leuão os ſoldados nas coſtas.*
[c1-l008 note] It resembles a small pavilion carried on soldiers' backs. Pauelhanſinho is the historical diminutive; do not normalize it to a modern spelling.
[c1-l009 >] *¶* Forouo caquru. *Pòr nas costas eſta diuiſa.*
[c1-l009 note] Horo o kakuru is putting this device on one's back. Some strokes of Forouo are worn, but the repeated headword and verb fit the visible text.
[c1-l010] Forobi, uru, ita. *Deſtruirſe como caſas, rei-*
[c1-l010 note] Horobi, horoburu, is intransitive destruction or ruin. The examples begin houses and a kingdom; rei- continues reino below.
[c1-l011 >] *no, &c. ¶ Item, Permet.* Fitono xin-
[c1-l011 note] The kingdom example closes, and Permet. introduces a figurative sense. Hito no shin- begins the example concerning a person's estate.
[c1-l012 >] daiga foroburu. *Destruirſe o eſtado dalguẽ.*
[c1-l012 note] Shindai ga horoburu means one's estate or means falling into ruin. The cross-line shin/dai sequence is Japanese 身代 in context, not the modern sense of a bed frame.
[c1-l013] Forobiuxe, ſuru, eta. *Destruirſe algũa couſa*
[c1-l013 note] Horobiuse intensifies the destruction to disappearance. The older use/suru inflection is kept as printed rather than replaced by a modern paradigm.
[c1-l014 >] *ſem ficar nada, ou acabarſe.*
[c1-l014 note] Nothing remains, or it comes to an end. Acabarſe is visible and grammatical; OCR acaba ſe dropped the r and split the infinitive.
[c1-l015] Foroboxi, ſu, oita. *Deſtruir, ou desfazer.*
[c1-l015 note] Horoboshi is transitive, destroying something, contrasting with horobi. The Portuguese destruir/desfazer supports that opposition.
[c1-l016] Fôrocu. Tacara, tacara. *Riquezas.*
[c1-l016 note] Hōroku is glossed takara, takara, wealth. The repeated gloss is actually printed and should not be deduplicated as an apparent copying error.
[c1-l017] Fôrocu. *Panela de barro.*
[c1-l017 note] The homophonous hōroku is an earthenware pot. The circumflex spelling is retained independently of the unrelated wealth sense.
[c1-l018] Fôrocutçucuri. *Oleiro.*
[c1-l018 note] Hōroku-tsukuri is a potter, literally a maker of the pots just defined. The compound boundary is a reading aid, not punctuation added to the original.
[c1-l019] Foroforoto. *Adu. Modo deſe deſenrolar,*
[c1-l019 note] Horohoroto describes loosening or unrolling. Adu. is italic, and the reduplicated form belongs to a cluster of mimetic expressions rather than the verb horu, dig.
[c1-l020 >] *ou deſennouelar algum fio, ou deſe ſoltarem os*
[c1-l020 note] Thread unwinds and hair comes loose. Desennouelar is the historical Portuguese verb; its unfamiliar appearance alone is not evidence of an OCR error.
[c1-l021 >] *cabelos, ou deſe deſapegar a era daruore, &c.*
[c1-l021 note] Ivy detaching from a tree is another example. Era is the printed spelling for the plant, corresponding to modern hera, not the noun meaning an era.
[c1-l022 >] *¶ Item, Modo de ſe quebrar, ou esboroar al*
[c1-l022 note] The adverb also describes breaking or crumbling. The separately detected pilcrow belongs to this physical row and is retained in its widened crop.
[c1-l023 >] *gũa couſa como parede de barro, &c. ¶* Na-
[c1-l023 note] A clay wall is an example of something crumbling. Couſa, not OCR couſo, is visible; Na- begins namida in the following Japanese example.
[c1-l024 >] midaga foroforoto vochita. *Cairão lhe as*
[c1-l024 note] Namida ga horohoroto ochita describes tears falling freely. The initial Na- comes from the prior row; Cairão is retained as the historical printed past-form spelling in this context.
[c1-l025 >] *lagrimas em fio. ¶* Cazzura foroforoto
[c1-l025 note] The tears fall in a stream; then kazura introduces a creeper or vine example. Its zzu spelling should not be reduced to a modern romanization.
[c1-l026 >] toqi firogareba. *Deſamarrandoſe, &*
[c1-l026 note] Toki hirogareba describes the vine loosening and spreading. The worn e before ba is supported by enlargement and the conditional form; OCR firogaroba produced an anomalous ending.
[c1-l027 >] *eſtendendoſe a era.*
[c1-l027 note] The Portuguese completes the ivy's loosening and spreading. Eſtendendoſe expresses the latter action and belongs to the preceding Japanese clause.
[c1-l028] Foromeqi, u, eita. *Deſapegarſe o arroz,*
[c1-l028 note] Horomeki describes rice separating or crumbling. The scan ends arroz with a comma, not the OCR period; the description continues immediately below.
[c1-l029 >] *&c. que não he viſcoſo, ou eſboroarſe, ou eſ*
[c1-l029 note] The rice is not sticky; the sense also covers crumbling or breaking small. The enlarged right margin contains no separate dash after eſ, so the OCR's trailing hyphen is removed.
[c1-l030 >] *miuçarſe algũa couſa.*
[c1-l030 note] Miuçarſe completes eſmiuçarſe, becoming small fragments. The noun is couſa; OCR coula confused long s with l.
[c1-l031] Foromiſo. *Certa laya de Miſo. (leis.*
[c1-l031 note] Horomiso is a kind of miso, italic here in the Portuguese explanation. The far-right (leis. is a physically displaced completion of the next row's hōron definition; it is retained here and not duplicated as an invented row below.
[c1-l032] Fôron. Noriuo ronzuru. *Diſputa a cerca das*
[c1-l032 note] Hōron, nori o ronzuru, is debate about laws or doctrine. Its Portuguese completion leis. is printed up on c1-l031 after a displacement mark; obsolete duplicate c1-l033 was removed without renumbering later stable IDs.
[c1-l034] Foroqe, uru, eta. *Quebrarſe, ou esboroar ſe.*
[c1-l034 note] Horoke means breaking or crumbling apart. This is the next physical row after hōron, confirming that no separate leis row intervenes.
[c1-l035 >] *¶* Foroqeta nari. *Modo, ou feição de*
[c1-l035 note] Horoketa nari describes a disheveled appearance. Nari concerns outward form or dress, not a copular ending that needs to be silently separated.
[c1-l036 >] *hum andar mal vestido, ou roto.*
[c1-l036 note] The person is badly dressed or in torn clothes. Roto is the garment-related condition here, clarifying the figurative extension from crumbling.
[c1-l037] Fororifororito. *Adu. Modo deſe desfa-*
[c1-l037 note] Hororihororito is another mimetic adverb for falling apart. The scan has desfa-, not OCR desfu-, beginning desfazer.
[c1-l038 >] *zer, ou esboroar algũa couſa. ¶ Itẽ, Mo*
[c1-l038 note] Zer completes desfazer; esboroar describes crumbling. The scan supports b rather than OCR h in eshoroar; Mo begins modo below.
[c1-l039 >] *do de cairem as lagrimas dos olhos.*
[c1-l039 note] The adverb can also describe tears falling from the eyes. This repeats a related sense, but it is genuine dictionary text rather than duplicate OCR to remove.
[c1-l040] Fororito. *Idem. ¶* Fororito dǒxinga vo-
[c1-l040 note] Hororito has the same senses. Dōshin ga vo- begins an example of a religious inclination arising; the split belongs to okotta below.
[c1-l041 >] cotta. *Mouerſe tenramente à deuação.*
[c1-l041 note] Okotta completes the Japanese verb. The Portuguese says being tenderly moved to devotion; its à is visibly accented, and the historical deuação spelling is retained.
[c1-l042] Fororo. *Eſtrepito das aſas da galinha do ma-*
[c1-l042 note] Hororo is the noise of a wild bird's wings. Ma- begins mato; the description is narrowed to a pheasant by the following Japanese name.
[c1-l043 >] *to. ¶* Fororouo vtçu. *Fazer aquelle eſtre*
[c1-l043 note] Hororo o utsu is making that wing-beating sound. Eſtre continues eſtrepito without adding a hyphen not supported by the scan.
[c1-l044 >] *pito, ou ſom o* Quiji *quando bate as aſas.*
[c1-l044 note] Quiji, kiji, is the pheasant beating its wings. The Japanese bird name is visibly roman inside the italic Portuguese sentence; its text is retained but its run styling is corrected.
[c1-l045] Foſa. *Certa dignidade.*
[c1-l045 note] Hosa is a particular dignity or office. The concise gloss does not warrant choosing a modern administrative equivalent without further evidence.
[c1-l046] Foſano qenpei. i, Foſa.
[c1-l046 note] Hosa no kenpei refers back to hosa. Qenpei represents kenpei, and the final i introduces the equivalence rather than being part of the title.
[c1-l047] Fôſaqi. *Maçaãs do roſto.*
[c1-l047 note] Hōsaki denotes the prominent cheeks. Tight enlargement resolves the accent as a circumflex, also recorded in NINJAL Fôsaqi; the headword is not the unrelated plain hosaki, tip.
[c1-l048] Fǒſat. Cǒbaxij fuda. i, Fǒcan, l, fǒbocu.
[c1-l048 note] Hōsat is an honorific for an elegant letter, with kōbashii fuda as its gloss. Enlargement recovers xij and the damaged f/d in Cǒbaxij fuda, not OCR Cǒbai i, ſuca; the explanation continues in column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *Palaura pera louuar a carta eſcrita elegante-*
[c2-l001 note] The hōsat expression from the previous column praises an elegantly written letter. Elegante- continues elegantly on the next row; the noun being praised is carta.
[c2-l002 >] *mente honrando a peßoa que eſcreueo.*
[c2-l002 note] Mente completes elegantemente; the expression honors the writer. Peßoa retains its joined double-s glyph rather than being recast as two long s letters.
[c2-l003] Fôſo. *Dignidade de Rey.*
[c2-l003 note] Hōso denotes royal dignity. The circumflex and the unaccented final o are retained from the visible headword, independently of adjacent hōsō words.
[c2-l004] Fǒſǒ. Cǒbaxij cuſa. *Erua cheiroſa. S.*
[c2-l004 note] Hōsō is fragrant grass, kōbashii kusa. Both carons are visible, and S marks the written-register expression.
[c2-l005] Fǒſǒ. *Doença de bexigas.*
[c2-l005 note] The next hōsō denotes smallpox or the pox disease described by bexigas. The shared romanization does not make the botanical and disease entries duplicates.
[c2-l006] Fôſô. Tomano mado. *Freſta que ſe abre na*
[c2-l006 note] Hōsō, toma no mado, is a window through a mat covering. Both circumflexes are visible; the Japanese paraphrase differentiates it from the preceding caron forms.
[c2-l007 >] *Fune entre as tomas, ou eſteiras com que ſe*
[c2-l007 note] The opening is in the mats covering a boat. Fune and tomas are Japanese-derived terms inside the Portuguese explanation, visibly italic here; keep the scan's typefaces.
[c2-l008 >] *cobre.*
[c2-l008 note] Cobre completes the description of the covering. This short word is a verb here, not Portuguese cobre meaning copper.
[c2-l009] Foſo. *No Cami ſe diz feſo. Embigo.*
[c2-l009 note] Hoso means navel; in Cami the term is heso. The italic feſo is genuine Japanese embedded in the explanatory sentence, not an unrecognized Portuguese word.
[c2-l010] Foſoai. *Lugar eſtreito entre duas caſas como*
[c2-l010 note] Hosoai is a narrow space between two houses. The following azinhaga gives a lane as an example rather than a separate sense of ai.
[c2-l011 >] *azinhaga, &c.*
[c2-l011 note] Azinhaga is a narrow lane. The short continuation belongs to hosoai and remains distinct from the next cord entry.
[c2-l012] Foſobiqi. *Corda de linho.*
[c2-l012 note] Hosobiki is a linen cord. The q in biqi gives ki; the scan does not support normalizing it to a modern alphabetic spelling.
[c2-l013] Foſocubi. *Peſcoço.*
[c2-l013 note] Hosokubi means the neck. The Portuguese peſcoço confirms kubi, with no need to add an adjective that the original gloss does not contain.
[c2-l014] Foſofibo, l, foſofibono ito. *Seda ſem ati-*
[c2-l014 note] Hosohibo, or hosohibo no ito, is a silk term. The scan repeats fibo in the expanded name; OCR foſonbono lost that sequence. NINJAL independently corroborates the repeated compound.
[c2-l015 >] *lho, boa, & feita em fardo de Iapão.*
[c2-l015 note] Lho completes atilho, describing silk without binding, of good quality and packed as a Japanese bale. The historical wording is retained rather than recast as modern textile terminology.
[c2-l016] Foſogoxi. *Cinta, ou lugar por onde cingem*
[c2-l016 note] Hosogoshi is the waist, where a belt is tied. Voiced goxi corresponds to koshi in a compound and is not a mistaken g for q.
[c2-l017 >] *o cinto.*
[c2-l017 note] O cinto completes the belt description. It is the object of cingem on the preceding row, not a new dictionary definition.
[c2-l018] Foſoguchi. *Vaſo como gumil em que metem*
[c2-l018 note] Hosoguchi is a narrow-mouthed vessel resembling a gumil. The Portuguese establishes a container, while the Japanese compound points to its narrow opening.
[c2-l019 >] *eruas chiroſas, ou flores.*
[c2-l019 note] Fragrant herbs or flowers are placed in the vessel. Chiroſas is retained as printed; no e is inserted solely to match modern cheiroſas.
[c2-l020] Foſoi. *Couſa delgada, & comprida como li-*
[c2-l020 note] Hosoi means thin and long, with thread as an example. Li- continues linha, not the Japanese syllable ri.
[c2-l021 >] *nha, &c.*
[c2-l021 note] Nha completes linha. This row closes the adjective's gloss before its separately indented derived forms.
[c2-l022 >] Foſoſa.
[c2-l022 note] Hososa is the nominal form, thinness. It is printed as its own indented physical row; there is no missing Portuguese translation to invent.
[c2-l023 >] Foſô.
[c2-l023 note] Hosō is the adverbial contracted form of hosoi. Its accent is retained, and its separate physical row is preserved alongside hososa.
[c2-l024] Foſoiri. *Ladrãoſinho, ou ladrão eſcõdido.*
[c2-l024 note] Hosoiri denotes a sneaking or concealed thief. The scan supports eſcõdido, with õ rather than OCR ã; the Portuguese word and visible round vowel agree.
[c2-l025] Foſomari, u, atta. *Adelgaçarſe.*
[c2-l025 note] Hosomari is becoming thinner. It is intransitive, contrasting with the following hosome, and its printed atta ending is retained.
[c2-l026] Foſome, uru, eta. *Adelgaçar, ou fazer*
[c2-l026 note] Hosome means making something thin or small. The transitive Portuguese adelgaçar/fazer distinguishes it from hosomari.
[c2-l027 >] *pequeno. ¶* Cuchiuo foſomuru. *Fazer*
[c2-l027 note] Kuchi o hosomuru is making the mouth small. The older verb form is supported by the example and should not be modernized to hosomeru in Level 1.
[c2-l028 >] *a boca pequena como quẽ quer aſuuiar. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l028 note] The lips are narrowed as for whistling. Aſuuiar is the historical Portuguese form, not an OCR spelling to replace automatically.
[c2-l029 >] *per met.* Cuchiuo foſomuru. *Aper-*
[c2-l029 note] The same Japanese expression is used metaphorically. Per met. is an explanatory label, while kuchi o hosomuru remains roman.
[c2-l030 >] *tar a mão não dando tanto como primeiro.*
[c2-l030 note] The metaphor is becoming less generous, giving less than before. Apertar a mão explains tightening expenditure rather than literal mouth shape.
[c2-l031] Foſomichi. *Caminho eſtreito.*
[c2-l031 note] Hosomichi is a narrow path. This transparent compound connects hosoi with michi, confirming the consonant reading.
[c2-l032] Foſomono. *Ouro.*
[c2-l032 note] Hosomono is glossed gold. That is a specialized lexical sense: do not replace the clear Ouro merely because the compound literally suggests fine or thin things.
[c2-l033] Foſonagai. *Couſa delgada, & comprida.*
[c2-l033 note] Hosonagai means slender and long. The two adjectives in Portuguese map directly to the compound's components.
[c2-l034] Foſonouo, l, feſonouo. *Tripa que traz*
[c2-l034 note] Hoso no o and heso no o denote the umbilical cord. Tripa is Portuguese, including its capital T; the OCR's isolated roman T is restyled with the italic definition.
[c2-l035 >] *o menino no embigo quando nace.*
[c2-l035 note] The cord is attached to the newborn's navel. Nace is the printed historical form, not silently expanded to nasce.
[c2-l036] Foſoſugi, l, foſoſugina. *Couſa comprida*
[c2-l036 note] Hososuji, also hososujina, describes something long and narrow. Gi here belongs to the historical ji spelling, not modern gi; the generated kana must be interpreted accordingly.
[c2-l037 >] *& eſtreita. Vt,* Foſoſugina catana. *Ca-*
[c2-l037 note] Hososujina katana is the example. The Japanese phrase is roman, while Ca- begins the Portuguese borrowed noun's translation below.
[c2-l038 >] *tana de folha eſtreita.*
[c2-l038 note] The sword has a narrow blade. The borrowed catana is italic across the Ca-/tana boundary, unlike its roman occurrence inside the Japanese example.
[c2-l039] Foſoutçubo. *Aljaua pequena.*
[c2-l039 note] Hosoutsubo is a small quiver. Tç represents ts; the plain initial hoso component should not acquire a long-vowel accent from surrounding Sino-Japanese words.
[c2-l040] Foſoya. *Seta pequena: communmente ſe vſa*
[c2-l040 note] Hosoya is a small arrow. The definition then adds a pragmatic explanation: the expression often minimizes one's own possession.
[c2-l041 >] *falando hum deſi humildemente apoucando*
[c2-l041 note] The speaker refers humbly to himself. The scan supports deſi, de si, not OCR deji; this is Portuguese reflexive wording, not a Japanese syllable.
[c2-l042 >] *a ſua ſeta, &c.*
[c2-l042 note] The thing minimized is the speaker's arrow. The diminutive interpretation belongs to the honorific context, not a claim about the arrow's actual dimensions.
[c2-l043] Fǒſun. *Quantidade, ou medida de meo dedo em*
[c2-l043 note] Hōsun is a square measure described as half a finger. The caron is retained; a modern numerical conversion would go beyond this transcription note.
[c2-l044 >] *quadro.*
[c2-l044 note] Em quadro is completed here, specifying a square measure. Quadro is not an independent picture or frame entry.
[c2-l045] Foſſǒ. *Hũa certa Seita.*
[c2-l045 note] Hossō is the Buddhist sect. Two long-s stems are visible where OCR read ſt; Foſſǒ is also corroborated by NINJAL's lexical form Fossǒ.
[c2-l046] Foſſocu. Nori, nori. *Leis, ou ordenaçoẽs.*
[c2-l046 note] Hossoku is laws or ordinances, glossed nori, nori. The repeated Japanese gloss is intentional print and remains; the roman double long s is not the italic ß convention.
[c2-l047] Foſſocu. l, *potiùs,* faſſocu. Axiuo voco-
[c2-l047 note] Another hossoku, preferably hassoku, means setting out. Potiùs is wholly italic, not alternating isolated letters; the scan shows two s stems in Foſſocu where OCR omitted one.
