[c1-l001] Nadaracani. *Aduer.*
[c1-l001 note] Nadaracani is the adverb corresponding to Nadaracana at the end of f345. The r is visible, correcting OCR t; the brief Aduer. label does not supply a new definition.
[c1-l002] Nadare, uru, eta. l, nadare vochi, ru. *Vir*
[c1-l002 note] Nadare and the alternative nadare vochi describe sliding or coming down from above. The printed l introduces the alternative rather than belonging to either Japanese verb.
[c1-l003 >] *correndo, ou eſcorregando de lugar alto pe-*
[c1-l003 note] The Portuguese describes running or slipping from a high place. Pe- belongs to pera on the next row and is retained as a visible physical division.
[c1-l004 >] *ra baixo. ¶* Rǒga nadaruru. *Correr*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the downward direction and illustrates wax running down. Rǒ is wax; its clearly printed caron is preserved instead of imposing the expected modern long-vowel spelling.
[c1-l005 >] *a cera de cima pera baixo derretida. ¶* Su-
[c1-l005 note] The melted wax flows from top to bottom. A second example begins with Su-, which joins miga below to refer to charcoal or embers.
[c1-l006 >] miga nadaruru. *Palauras que ſe vſão no*
[c1-l006 note] Completes Sumi ga nadaruru. The Portuguese identifies this as terminology used in tea practice, with the setting and explanation supplied on the following rows.
[c1-l007 >] *Chano yǔ. Ideſt, abaixarẽ ſe as braſas, & des-*
[c1-l007 note] Chano yǔ names the tea setting; the embers settle down and disintegrate into ash. The visible mark over u is a caron, not the OCR tilde. The full embedded Japanese phrase is included for typeface review.
[c1-l008 >] *fazerem ſe em cinza.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes desfazerem se em cinza, crumbling into ash. This explains the behaviour of the embers in the preceding technical expression.
[c1-l009] Nade, zzuru, eta. *Por a mão brandamente*
[c1-l009 note] Nade, with the printed nazzuru principal form, means gently passing the hand over something. The double z and the historical inflection are not modernized to naderu.
[c1-l010 >] *por cima de algũa couſa. ¶* Per met. Ta-
[c1-l010 note] Completes the physical stroking sense and introduces a metaphorical use. The upright Per met. apparatus is distinguished from the surrounding italic gloss after the second comparison. Ta- is the beginning of tami, the people, continued with its object particle below.
[c1-l011 >] miuo nazzuru. *Ter compaixão dos laurado-*
[c1-l011 note] Tami wo nazzuru means treating the people compassionately. The translation first mentions cultivators, with lauradores divided at the physical line end.
[c1-l012 >] *res, ou pouo fazendo lhe fauor. ¶* Come
[c1-l012 note] Completes the benevolent treatment of cultivators or the people. The next example begins Come, rice, and continues with the object-marked verb on the following row.
[c1-l013 >] uo nazzuru. *Pilar arroz. ¶* Nadez-
[c1-l013 note] Come wo nazzuru means pounding rice. The narrow but visible word gap in Pilar arroz is restored; Nadez- begins the name of the pounding implement below.
[c1-l014 >] zuchi. *Malho de pilar arroz.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes Nadezzuchi, a mallet for pounding rice. The two z letters are distributed across the physical line division and must not be collapsed into one.
[c1-l015] Nadeague, uru, eta. *Pondo a mão por cima*
[c1-l015 note] Nadeague combines passing the hand over something with lifting it upward. The first explanation concerns raising the hair by stroking over the head.
[c1-l016 >] *da cabeça aleuãtar os cabelos, ou por cima dou*
[c1-l016 note] The hand lifts the hair on the head or passes over another object. The nasal abbreviation in aleuãtar and the unhyphenated division dou / tra are preserved.
[c1-l017 >] *tra couſa, &c. Vt,* Futarino vacadomo
[c1-l017 note] Tra completes doutra. The example introduces two young people; its continuation describes placing them on the speaker's two knees and smoothing their hair upward.
[c1-l018 >] uo ſayǔno fizani voite, vocureno camiuo
[c1-l018 note] The two young people are placed on the left and right knees, followed by the object phrase concerning their loose or trailing hair. The historical spelling vocureno is retained.
[c1-l019 >] nadeague, &c. Qir. *Tendo os dous mo-*
[c1-l019 note] Nadeague completes the hair-stroking action. Qir. is an upright citation label, retained without expanding its source name; the Portuguese translation begins the divided word moços.
[c1-l020 >] *ços ſobre os joelhos direito, & eſquerdo, &*
[c1-l020 note] The two young people sit on the right and left knees. This translation corroborates the Japanese sayǔ and the bodily arrangement in the preceding example.
[c1-l021 >] *pondo lhe a mão ſobre as cabeças lhes aleuanta-*
[c1-l021 note] The speaker places a hand over their heads and raises their hair. The imperfect verb is visibly divided after aleuanta- and finishes on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *ua brandamente os cabelos. ¶* Tçuchiuo
[c1-l022 note] Completes the gentle raising of the hair. A second example starts Tçuchi wo, introducing earth or clay as the object instead of hair.
[c1-l023 >] nadeaguru. *Aleuantar o barro com as mãos*
[c1-l023 note] Tçuchi wo nadeaguru means pushing clay upward with the hands. The printed -aguru form is retained rather than normalized to a modern conjugation.
[c1-l024 >] *pera cima quando vai caindo pera baixo, como*
[c1-l024 note] The clay is pushed upward as it tends to fall down. The following row provides wall plastering as the concrete example of this action.
[c1-l025 >] *quando barrão parede, &c.*
[c1-l025 note] Wall plastering illustrates pushing clay upward while it tends to slip down. The historical verb barrão is retained without modern spelling changes.
[c1-l026] Nadeguine. *Malho de pilar arroz.*
[c1-l026 note] Nadeguine is another name for a rice-pounding mallet. Its guine component and the identical gloss connect it with the Nadezzuchi implement already mentioned.
[c1-l027] Nadetçuqe, uru, eta. *Esfregar com a mão*
[c1-l027 note] Nadetçuqe means rubbing or smoothing something down with the hand. The partly worn initial of Esfregar is retained from the readable word and surrounding definition.
[c1-l028 >] *pegando, ou aßentando a couſa. Vt,* Cami
[c1-l028 note] The rubbing fixes or settles the object in place. The example begins Cami, which the following Portuguese translation identifies as paper rather than the homophonous hair.
[c1-l029 >] uo nadetçuquru. *Pegar, & aſsentar com*
[c1-l029 note] Cami wo nadetçuquru means pressing or smoothing paper into place. The mixed long-and-short s sequence in aſsentar is retained instead of regularized to another double-s form.
[c1-l030 >] *a mão papel. ¶* Fitouo nadetçuquru.
[c1-l030 note] The object is explicitly paper, smoothed by hand. A second example applies the verb to a person, preparing the figurative sense explained below.
[c1-l031 >] *Grangear, & trazer à ſua parte com mimos*
[c1-l031 note] Fito wo nadetçuquru is explained as winning someone over with favours or affectionate treatment. The historical Grangear is preserved rather than modernized.
[c1-l032 >] *a alguem.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes the figurative construction with someone as its object. This short line closes the explanation of winning a person over.
[c1-l033] Naguetçuqe. *Modo de amarrar os cabelos da*
[c1-l033 note] The entry describes tying the hair back. Although the following example uses nadetçuqe, this headword visibly has gue, including a descending g; the printed Naguetçuqe anomaly is retained rather than silently emended.
[c1-l034 >] *cabeça botados pera de tras. Vt,* Camiuo na-
[c1-l034 note] The hair is gathered toward the back of the head. Cami wo na- begins the example with hair as the object, contrasting with the paper sense above.
[c1-l035 >] detçuqeni yǔ. *Amarrar os cabelos desta*
[c1-l035 note] Completes nadetçuqeni yǔ, tying the hair in this arrangement. Its de spelling differs from the entry's printed gue; both local forms are preserved and the discrepancy is documented.
[c1-l036 >] *maneira. ¶ Item, O meſmo homem que*
[c1-l036 note] Completes tying the hair in this way, then extends the noun to the person who wears the hairstyle. The relative clause continues on the next row.
[c1-l037 >] *traz deſta maneira os cabelos amarrados.*
[c1-l037 note] The added sense is the man whose hair is tied in this fashion. This metonymic use is explicitly part of the same entry.
[c1-l038] Nadexico. *Erua que dà hũa flor como crauos.*
[c1-l038 note] Nadexico corresponds to nadeshiko, described as a plant with a flower resembling carnations. The visible i after x is restored; no more precise botanical identification is imposed.
[c1-l039] Nadezzuchi. *Malho de pilar arroz,*
[c1-l039 note] Nadezzuchi is separately entered as a rice-pounding mallet, repeating the earlier example. The visible terminal comma is preserved rather than repaired to a period.
[c1-l040] Nado. *E o de mais, ou outras couſas.*
[c1-l040 note] Nado means and the rest or other things, the enumerative use. The scan separates E o; OCR Eo incorrectly joined the conjunction and article.
[c1-l041] Nado. l, nadoca. i, Najoni. *Por que?* S.
[c1-l041 note] This separate Nado, alternatively nadoca and equated with Najoni, means why. The final S. marks written usage and is kept outside the italic translation.
[c1-l042] Nagaame. *Chuuas da primauera, que durão*
[c1-l042 note] Nagaame denotes prolonged spring rains. Two a letters distinguish the headword from Nagame later in the page; the scan supports Chuuas, correcting OCR Chauas.
[c1-l043 >] *muito tempo.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes the defining duration of the spring rains. This short row belongs to Nagaame and does not qualify the following clothing entry.
[c1-l044] Nagabacama. *Calções compridos.*
[c1-l044 note] Nagabacama refers to long hakama, rendered as long trousers. The voiced b in the compound is preserved rather than replaced by the independent form's h-row spelling.
[c1-l045] Nagabanaxi. *Pratica comprida.*
[c1-l045 note] Nagabanaxi is a lengthy conversation or talk. Portuguese pratica here denotes discourse, not merely repeated practice of an activity.
[c1-l046] Nagabitçu. *Caixão comprido.*
[c1-l046 note] Nagabitçu names a long chest. The headword is abraded, but the surviving descending g, bi, and tçu support this reading; OCR Na, abicgu creates false segmentation and an implausible ending.
[c1-l047] Nagaboye. *O ladrar muito o cão como vyuan-*
[c1-l047 note] Nagaboye denotes a dog's prolonged barking or howling. The Portuguese word vyuando is divided across the column boundary, not completed by the page's bottom catchword.

[c2-l001 >] *do. ¶* Inuga nagaboyeuo ſuru. *La-*
[c2-l001 note] Do completes vyuando from column 1. Inu ga nagaboye wo suru illustrates a dog barking or howling for a long time; La- starts its Portuguese translation.
[c2-l002 >] *drar o cão muito tempo como vyuando de noi-*
[c2-l002 note] Completes ladrar and describes prolonged, howl-like barking at night. The scan includes y in vyuando, omitted by the OCR; noi- continues as noite below.
[c2-l003 >] *te, &c.*
[c2-l003 note] Te completes noite, night, in the preceding barking example. The small row closes Nagaboye before the next headword begins.
[c2-l004] Nagacamoji. *Cabeleira comprida de molheres.*
[c2-l004 note] Nagacamoji is a long hairpiece or head of hair for women, as the Portuguese cabeleira indicates. The compound is preserved without replacing camoji with a modern explanatory term.
[c2-l005] Nagadôriǔ. *Detença comprida.*
[c2-l005 note] Nagadôriǔ denotes a long stay or prolonged sojourn. The circumflex over o and caron over u are separately visible and retained.
[c2-l006] Nagagatana. *Catana muito comprida.*
[c2-l006 note] Nagagatana is a very long sword. The voiced g in the compound and the Portuguese loanword Catana are reproduced as printed.
[c2-l007] Nagagoyui. *Hũa maneira de barretes que tem*
[c2-l007 note] Nagagoyui names a kind of cap. The next row identifies its distinguishing two long points rather than its material or the wearer's rank.
[c2-l008 >] *duas pontas compridas.*
[c2-l008 note] Specifies the cap's two long points. This is the physical feature supplied by the dictionary, without reconstructing an exact cap design.
[c2-l009] Nagaguſocu. *Armas compridas como lança,*
[c2-l009 note] Nagaguſocu denotes long weapons, illustrated first by a lance. The remaining example continues on the following row.
[c2-l010 >] *alabarda, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] Adds the halberd to the examples of long weapons. The short continuation belongs to Nagaguſocu, not to the adjective Nagai below.
[c2-l011] Nagai. *Couſa cõprida, ou que dura muito tẽpo.*
[c2-l011 note] Nagai covers both spatial length and long duration. The tildes abbreviate nasal material in cõprida and tẽpo and remain unexpanded.
[c2-l012 >] Nagaſa.
[c2-l012 note] Nagaſa is the corresponding noun, length or duration. It is printed as its own indented physical row under Nagai, not as an independently defined entry.
[c2-l013 >] Nagǒ.
[c2-l013 note] Nagǒ is the contracted adverbial form associated with Nagai. Its caron is visible; the form is not replaced by modern nagaku.
[c2-l014] Nagaiqi. *Vida comprida. ¶* Nagaiqiuo
[c2-l014 note] Nagaiqi denotes a long life. The repeated form with its object particle begins the example completed by suru below.
[c2-l015 >] ſuru. *Viuer muito.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes nagaiqi wo suru, living a long time. The Japanese example and Portuguese translation make clear that the length is temporal.
[c2-l016] Nagame, uru, eta. *Ver, ou eſtender os olhos*
[c2-l016 note] Nagame is gazing or extending one's view. The definition continues with a pleasing object and then supplies an example of looking at the surrounding scenery.
[c2-l017 >] *por algũa couſa apraziuel. ¶* Yomono qe-
[c2-l017 note] The gaze rests on something pleasant. Yomo no qe- begins the example concerning scenery in all directions, with qexiqi divided at the line break.
[c2-l018 >] xiqiuo nagamuru. *Ver per recreação por*
[c2-l018 note] Completes yomo no qexiqi wo nagamuru, viewing the surrounding scenery for enjoyment. The printed Portuguese per is retained, not changed to the por later in the same line.
[c2-l019 >] *todas as partes.*
[c2-l019 note] Completes looking in all directions. The translation corroborates yomo and closes the verbal entry before the noun Nagame follows.
[c2-l020] Nagame. *Viſta. Vt,* Nagame cotonaru
[c2-l020 note] The noun Nagame means a view or prospect. Its example begins with a view being different and continues with the evening on the next row.
[c2-l021 >] yǔbe. *A viſta deſtatarde he differente das*
[c2-l021 note] Yǔbe denotes the evening, not the OCR's yibe. The visible caron and the Portuguese tarde agree; das, not du, introduces comparison with other evenings, the tightly joined deſtatarde is retained rather than respaced from modern syntax.
[c2-l022 >] *outras.*
[c2-l022 note] Outras completes the comparison with other evenings or their views. It is the end of the example, not an omitted headword.
[c2-l023] Nagameyari, u, atta. *Ver ou botar os olhos*
[c2-l023 note] Nagameyari means casting one's gaze outward. The definition continues with distance, while the abbreviated principal parts remain exactly as printed.
[c2-l024 >] *ao longe. Vt,* Sono catano ſorauo faruba-
[c2-l024 note] The example looks far away toward the sky in that direction. Faruba- is the first part of farubaruto, retained at its physical division.
[c2-l025 >] ruto nagame yari tamayeba, &c. Taif.
[c2-l025 note] Completes the distant, honorific act of looking. The spaced nagame yari is kept; Taif. is the printed upright citation label, without expanding or respelling its title.
[c2-l026 >] *Lib. 4. Lançando os olhos pera os ceos*
[c2-l026 note] The citation specifies book 4, then translates casting one's eyes toward the skies. Lib. and the book number belong to the italic apparatus, unlike the preceding source label.
[c2-l027 >] *deßa parte ao longe.*
[c2-l027 note] Completes the direction and distance of the gaze, toward that region far away. The printed ß in deßa is retained as a graphic feature.
[c2-l028] Nagamochi. *Caixão comprido de Iapão.*
[c2-l028 note] Nagamochi names a long Japanese chest. Iapão is the Portuguese country name in the gloss, not a Japanese term to mark for whole-word typeface toggling.
[c2-l029] Nagamonogatari. *Pratica comprida.*
[c2-l029 note] Nagamonogatari denotes a lengthy tale or discourse, glossed like Nagabanaxi as long conversation. The entry is distinct even though the Portuguese wording repeats.
[c2-l030] Naganaga. *Adu. Muito compridamente,*
[c2-l030 note] Naganaga is an adverb meaning at great length. Its definition continues with a temporal paraphrase and an example of a prolonged stay.
[c2-l031 >] *ou por muito tempo. Vt,* Naganaga tôriǔ
[c2-l031 note] The temporal sense is for a long time. Naganaga tôriǔ begins an example of staying somewhere for an extended period, completed by suru below.
[c2-l032 >] ſuru. *Fazer detença por muito tempo.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes the example of making a long stay. Detença is a sojourn here, not necessarily imprisonment or involuntary detention.
[c2-l033] Naganagaxij. *Couſa muito comprida, ou de*
[c2-l033 note] Naganagaxij describes something very long, either in extent or duration. Its ii-like final ij is retained as the dictionary's adjectival notation.
[c2-l034 >] *muito tempo.*
[c2-l034 note] Completes the long-duration sense of Naganagaxij. The physical row remains separate despite containing only a short continuation.
[c2-l035] Nagaqiyo. *Noites cõpridas.* P.
[c2-l035 note] This Nagaqiyo means long nights and carries the upright P. label for poetic usage. The label is moved outside the italic gloss without changing its wording.
[c2-l036] Nagaqiyo. *Mundo comprido, ou vida eterna.*
[c2-l036 note] The identically spelled expression here concerns a lasting world or eternal life. The dictionary distinguishes it from the preceding long-night entry by meaning.
[c2-l037] Nagara. *Ainda q̃, poſto que. Vt,* Fabacari
[c2-l037 note] Nagara first receives a concessive interpretation, although or even though. Fabacari begins the example of a deferential reservation continued below.
[c2-l038 >] nagara. *Ainda q̃ ſeja deſcorteſia. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l038 note] Fabacari nagara is rendered as although it may be discourtesy. The scan has deſcorteſia, with i before a, not OCR deſcorteſſa; the visible word and the noun meaning agree.
[c2-l039 >] Tomoni. *Iuntamente. Vt,* Futari na-
[c2-l039 note] Adds a together sense, equated with the standalone Japanese synonym Tomoni. Futari na- begins the example meaning both people together; Tomoni is not embedded within a Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l040 >] gara. *Ambos juntamente. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l040 note] Completes Futari nagara and its translation, both together. Item introduces another construction on the next row rather than a new headword.
[c2-l041 >] *Aſi como eſtaua, &c. Vt,* Qirumonoto
[c2-l041 note] Introduces remaining just as one was. Qirumono to begins the coordinated clothing phrase in the example of entering water still dressed.
[c2-l042 >] dôbucuuo qinagara mizzuni fitatta. *Aßi*
[c2-l042 note] The person becomes immersed in water while still wearing clothing and an outer garment. The Portuguese begins Aßi; the OCR's extra t is absent from the visible type.
[c2-l043 >] *como eſtaua veſtido cõ Dôbucu ſe ẽſopou nagoa.*
[c2-l043 note] The translation confirms getting soaked in water while still dressed in Dôbucu. Its o has a circumflex, not the OCR grave; the embedded garment name is recorded for typeface review.
[c2-l044] Nagaraye, uru, eta. *Viuer. Vt,* Inochi
[c2-l044 note] Nagaraye means continuing to live. The example starts Inochi, life, and supplies its object particle and verb on the next row.
[c2-l045 >] uo nagarayuru. *Idem.*
[c2-l045 note] Completes inochi wo nagarayuru, prolonging or sustaining one's life. Idem refers to the preceding living sense; the historical -yuru form is not modernized.
[c2-l046] Nagare, uru, eta. *Correr agoa, ou couſa li-*
[c2-l046 note] Nagare is the flowing of water or another liquid. The Portuguese adjective liquida is divided after li- at the visible line-end mark.
[c2-l047 >] *quida. ¶* Namidaga nagaruru. *Cor-*
[c2-l047 note] Completes liquida and illustrates tears flowing. Cor- begins the translation continued in the body of f347; the lower rerem is an anticipatory catchword, not an extra body row.
