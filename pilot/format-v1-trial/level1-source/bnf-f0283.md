[c1-l001 >] *Por a meſa. ¶* Ienuo aguru. *Tirar a*
[c1-l001 note] Por a meſa completes the previous page’s Ienuo ſuyuru, setting the meal tray. Ienuo aguru introduces removing or raising it; its Portuguese translation continues below.
[c1-l002 >] *meſa, ou por a meſa. ¶* Ciojenga ſube-
[c1-l002 note] The gloss offers removing or setting the table. The next example is an honorific meal-tray expression ending in ſuberu. The initial of Ciojenga is C-like in this worn impression; it is retained, although the meal context suggests a gozen-related form. The visible space in por a is restored.
[c1-l003 >] ru, l, cudaru. *Aleuantaremſe, ou tirarẽ*
[c1-l003 note] Ru completes ſuberu, with cudaru as its alternative. Aleuantaremſe or tirarẽſe describes the trays being raised or taken away; the second verb continues below with its printed nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l004 >] *ſe as meſas de diante dalgũa peßoa honrada.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes the removal of the trays from before an honoured person. Dalgũa and honrada are supported by enlargement, correcting OCR dalgũs and bonrada; the source’s peßoa representation is retained.
[c1-l005] Ien acu. Yoxi axi. *Bem, & mal. ¶* Ien
[c1-l005 note] Ien acu is zen aku, good and evil, equated with yoxi axi. The scan restores initial I and the a in acu; the repeated Ien at the right begins the example continued on the next row.
[c1-l006 >] acuuo vaqimayuru. *Dicernir o bem, &*
[c1-l006 note] Ien acu wo vaqimayuru means discerning good and evil. The Portuguese is printed Dicernir, without the s of modern discernir; that historical form is not silently repaired.
[c1-l007 >] *o mal. ¶* Ien acuni catamuqu. *Incli-*
[c1-l007 note] After completing “evil”, the entry gives Ien acuni catamuqu, inclining toward good and evil. Capital I replaces OCR l, and the Portuguese divided word is Incli-narſe, not Inoli-narſe.
[c1-l008 >] *narſe ao bem, & mal. ¶ Item, Em todo*
[c1-l008 note] Narſe completes inclining toward good and evil. Item introduces a broader expression meaning “in any case”, which continues on the next row.
[c1-l009 >] *caſo. i, Porfas, & por nefas.*
[c1-l009 note] The additional gloss is “in any case”, clarified through the Latin contrast fas/nefas, right or wrong. Printed Porfas remains joined; the transcription does not modernize the multilingual expression.
[c1-l010] Ienbi. Mattacu ſonauaru. *Perfeiçoarſe, ou*
[c1-l010 note] Ienbi here means completeness or perfection, paraphrased mattacu ſonauaru. The Portuguese describes being perfected or finished, with the second verb continued below.
[c1-l011 >] *acabarſe. Vt,* Zoſacu jenbi tçucamatçut
[c1-l011 note] Acabarſe completes the gloss. Zoſacu jenbi tçucamatçutta says that the work or construction has been completed; the source divides the final verb after t without an added hyphen.
[c1-l012 >] ta. *A cabarãoſe as obras. ¶* Xiqixin
[c1-l012 note] Ta completes tçucamatçutta. The Portuguese states that the works were completed; enlargement supports the nasal ending in A cabarãoſe, retaining the conspicuous gap after A. The worn nasal vowel is a reading caveat. Xiqixin begins a new example concerning bodily form.
[c1-l013 >] jenbi ſuru. *Formarſe o corpo, ou tomar fi-*
[c1-l013 note] With Xiqixin above, jenbi ſuru describes the body becoming fully formed. The Portuguese explains formation of the body or taking human form, with fi-gura divided across rows.
[c1-l014 >] *gura de homem no ventre da mãe. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l014 note] The body takes human form in the mother’s womb. The damaged last word is read mãe, not OCR mão; the nasal mark and surviving letter tops support “mother”, but the worn final letter remains a caveat. Itẽ introduces a further sense.
[c1-l015 >] *Ter, ou incluir em ſi. Vt,* Xojenuo jenbi
[c1-l015 note] The next sense is possessing or including something within oneself. The printed verb is incluir, not ineluir; Xojenuo jenbi begins an example about possessing the various virtues.
[c1-l016 >] ſuru. *Ter todas as virtudes em ſi.*
[c1-l016 note] Suru completes the Japanese example: having all virtues within oneself. The translation closes this completeness/possession sense of Ienbi.
[c1-l017] Ienbi. Yoqu itçucuxixi. *Fermoſura. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l017 note] This Ienbi denotes beauty, explained by yoqu itçucuxixi. The historical Japanese adjective is preserved as printed; Portuguese Fermoſura, not Fermoſara, supplies the matching noun.
[c1-l018 >] *Fermoſura da virtude.* S.
[c1-l018 note] The additional sense is the beauty of virtue, not merely physical beauty. The scan again supports Fermoſura, and the final upright S. labels written usage.
[c1-l019] Ienbô. *Seita, ou ley dos Ienxùs.*
[c1-l019 note] Ienbô is defined as the sect or teaching of the Ienxùs, the Zen community. The embedded Japanese-derived name is kept in the spelling and current type visible in the gloss.
[c1-l020] Ienbǒ. *Caſa quieta.*
[c1-l020 note] Ienbǒ is a quiet house or residence. Its caron contrasts visibly with the preceding Ienbô’s circumflex; these adjacent headwords must not be homogenized.
[c1-l021] Ienbu. Mattacu ſuburu. *Hum corpo de li*
[c1-l021 note] Ienbu means a complete body or set, here a complete collection of books. The right edge shows li without a hyphen, despite the draft’s li-; the word continues as uros below.
[c1-l022 >] *uros inteiro.*
[c1-l022 note] Uros completes liuros, and inteiro stresses the completeness of the set. The short continuation retains the historical u/v spelling.
[c1-l023] Ienbut. Mayeno Fotoqe. *Fotoque antiguo q̃*
[c1-l023 note] Ienbut is a former Buddha, paraphrased maye no Fotoqe. The Portuguese uses Fotoque within its explanation and calls this figure ancient; q̃ is the printed abbreviation for the relative conjunction.
[c1-l024 >] *foi primeiro.*
[c1-l024 note] Foi primeiro explains that the Buddha came before. It completes the relative clause of the preceding entry rather than supplying a distinct chronological headword.
[c1-l025] Ienchô. i, Iendaino teivǒ. *Rey paßado.*
[c1-l025 note] Ienchô denotes a previous reign or sovereign, explained by Iendaino teivǒ. The scan gives one initial I and teivǒ, correcting the extra OCR l and the vowel in toivǒ; the Portuguese says a former king.
[c1-l026] Iendai. Mayeno yo. *Idade, ou mundo paſſa*
[c1-l026 note] Iendai is an earlier age or world, paraphrased maye no yo. Paſſa continues into paſſado without a visible hyphen, preserving the physical division.
[c1-l027 >] *do. ¶* Iendai mimon. *Couſa nunca vi-*
[c1-l027 note] Do completes paſſado. Iendai mimon means something unheard of in previous ages; the Portuguese broadens this to never seen or heard. The small terminal hyphen after vi is visible on enlargement.
[c1-l028 >] *ſta, nem ouuida. ¶* Iendai mimonno a-
[c1-l028 note] Sta completes vista, followed by “nor heard”. The next example starts Iendai mimonno a-, with a- continuing into the word for an evil deed on the following row.
[c1-l029 >] cuguiǒ. *Maldade nunca vista, nem ouui-*
[c1-l029 note] Cuguiǒ completes acuguiǒ, an evil act. The phrase describes unprecedented wickedness; the Portuguese again says never seen or heard, dividing ouuida across rows.
[c1-l030 >] *da. ¶* Iendai mimonno cǒmiǒ. *Grã*
[c1-l030 note] Da completes ouuida. Iendai mimonno cǒmiǒ introduces an unprecedented achievement or distinction, contrasted with the preceding unprecedented evil; Grã begins the intensified Portuguese description.
[c1-l031 >] *dißima façanha feita na guerra, ou nunca ou-*
[c1-l031 note] Dißima completes Grãdißima, an extremely great feat performed in war, or one whose like has never been heard. The source’s double-s representation and abbreviated nasal spelling are retained.
[c1-l032 >] *uida tal.*
[c1-l032 note] Uida completes ouuida, followed by tal: never having heard of such a feat. This closes the extended example under Iendai.
[c1-l033] Iendana. *Parteleiro onde ſe poem as meſas.*
[c1-l033 note] Iendana is a shelf for meal trays. The narrow, worn initial is I, not OCR F; Parteleiro is the printed historical Portuguese form and is not replaced by modern prateleiro.
[c1-l034] Iendǒ. Yoqi michi. *Caminho da virtude.*
[c1-l034 note] Iendǒ is the good path, yoqi michi, specifically the path of virtue. The moral meaning is established directly by the bilingual gloss.
[c1-l035 >] *¶* Iendǒni iru, l, itaru. *Entrar no ca-*
[c1-l035 note] Iendǒni iru or itaru means entering or reaching the path of virtue. The Latin l introduces the alternative Japanese verb, while ca- begins Portuguese caminho.
[c1-l036 >] *minho da virtude.*
[c1-l036 note] Minho completes caminho, closing the translation “enter the path of virtue”. The short continuation is distinct from the following homophonous Iendǒ for a quiet hermitage.
[c1-l037] Iendǒ. Xizucana dǒ. *Ermida quieta.*
[c1-l037 note] This Iendǒ is a quiet hall or hermitage, xizucana dǒ, not the preceding path of virtue. The initial is I rather than OCR F; both printed carons are retained.
[c1-l038] Iendocu. Ienno tocu. *Virtudes, ou mereci-*
[c1-l038 note] Iendocu denotes virtue or the merit of good action, paraphrased Ienno tocu. The worn headword begins with an upright I, not the OCR’s italic H; mereci- continues below.
[c1-l039 >] *mentos da virtude. Vt,* Iendocuuo tçumu.
[c1-l039 note] Mentos completes merecimentos. Iendocuuo tçumu is accumulating merits or virtues; the scan supports virtude, restoring the r omitted in OCR vitude.
[c1-l040 >] *Ajuntar muitos merecimentos, & virtudes.*
[c1-l040 note] Ajuntar muitos merecimentos, & virtudes translates the accumulation example. The row is entirely Portuguese and closes this entry.
[c1-l041] Iengacu. *Eſtado dos Ienxùs.*
[c1-l041 note] Iengacu is described as the state or condition of the Ienxùs. The embedded Japanese-derived community name is a typeface-review candidate; it is not an ordinary Japanese headword on this row.
[c1-l042] Ienguen. Yoi cotoba. *Boas palauras. ¶* Iẽ-
[c1-l042 note] Ienguen means good words, yoi cotoba. Iẽ- begins the example with an abbreviation tilde over e; the whole Japanese fragment is upright, correcting the spurious italic ẽ in the draft.
[c1-l043 >] guenuo faqu. *Dizer boas palauras.*
[c1-l043 note] Guenuo faqu completes Iẽguenuo faqu, uttering good words. The Portuguese directly repeats that meaning; the division and abbreviation above are not expanded in the canonical text.
[c1-l044] Ienguiô. Yocu tacumu. *Inuentar algũa cou-*
[c1-l044 note] Ienguiô concerns skilful devising, yocu tacumu, here inventing something good. The initial capital I is restored from the scan, replacing OCR ſ; the circumflex remains distinct from the next column’s caron-marked entry.
[c1-l045 >] *ſa boa. ¶* Ienguiô fôben. *O inuentar*
[c1-l045 note] Sa completes couſa. Ienguiô fôben illustrates skilful or resourceful means; the Portuguese begins an explanation of inventing something in that manner.
[c1-l046 >] *algũa couſa boa ſabia, & industrioſamente, &*
[c1-l046 note] The invention is described as good, wise, and skilful. Couſa replaces OCR conſa, and the visible ampersand after ſabia is restored; the final ampersand carries the explanation into the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *com boa ocaſião.*
[c1-l047 note] The final qualification is a suitable occasion or opportunity. The damaged but legible ocaſião replaces OCR ocaſũo; its nasal ending is retained rather than normalized to a different historical spelling.

[c2-l001] Ienguiǒ. Yoqi voconai. *Bõs exercicios, ou*

[c2-l004] Ienguiô. Yoqi xiuaza, l, jengô. *Boas o-*

[c2-l005 >] *bras. Vt,* Ienguiôuo faguemaſu. *Por to-*
[c2-l005 note] Bras completes obras. Ienguiôuo faguemaſu is applying oneself vigorously to good works; the visible capital I replaces OCR ſ. To- begins todas in the Portuguese translation.
[c2-l006 >] *das as forças em fazer boas obras.*

[c2-l007] Iengô. Ienno xiuaza. *Boas obras.*
[c2-l007 note] Iengô likewise means good works, literally Ienno xiuaza. The circumflex belongs to this separate headword and is copied independently of neighbouring accents.
[c2-l008] Iengo. Maye, vxiro. i, Ato ſaqi. *Diãte,*

[c2-l009 >] *& de tras, ou antes, & de pois. ¶* Ien-
[c2-l009 note] The pair also means before and after in time. Ien- begins a Japanese example across the line break; its e is upright, not the isolated italic character supplied by OCR.
[c2-l010 >] go ſayǔni banuo voqu. *Por vigias ẽ to-*
[c2-l010 note] Together with Ien- above, this means placing guards in front, behind, left, and right. Enlargement shows banuo, “guards + object particle”, not bando: the apparent ascender is damage from the hole above the u. The Portuguese begins “post watchmen everywhere”.
[c2-l011 >] *das as partes. ¶* Iengouo bǒzuru. i, A-
[c2-l011 note] Das completes todas as partes. A second example, Iengouo bǒzuru, is paraphrased with A-toſaqiuo vaſururu across the next boundary, forgetting what is before and behind.
[c2-l012 >] toſaqiuo vaſururu. *Não dar a cordo de*
[c2-l012 note] Toſaqiuo vaſururu completes the paraphrase, rendered as losing awareness of oneself. The Portuguese is visibly spaced a cordo and is not silently joined or modernized.
[c2-l013 >] *ſi. ¶* Iengouo voboyezu, l, xiranu. *Idẽ.*
[c2-l013 note] After completing “oneself”, the entry gives not remembering or not knowing front and back, with the same meaning. Voboyezu and the alternative marker l are separate: OCR voboyezuil incorrectly fused them. Idẽ retains its abbreviation.
[c2-l014] Iengon. Yoqi cotoba. *Meliùs,* Ienguen.
[c2-l014 note] This Iengon means good words; the apparatus recommends Ienguen. Cotoba corrects OCR coroba, and the editorial Meliùs retains the visible grave rather than the OCR tilde.
[c2-l015 >] *Boas palauras.*
[c2-l015 note] Boas palauras is the Portuguese equivalent of the preceding good-words entry. It is a short continuation, not an independent headword.
[c2-l016] Iengon. i, Ien no coto. *Virtudes, ou boas*
[c2-l016 note] A separate Iengon concerns virtue or good works, explained by Ien no coto. The scan restores the initial I omitted from the OCR, while preserving the printed spaces in the synonym.
[c2-l017 >] *obras. ¶* Ienji Iengonuo naſu. *Fazer*
[c2-l017 note] Obras completes the gloss. Ienji Iengonuo naſu means doing good deeds; the second term begins with a visible upright I, not OCR ſ. Fazer begins the matching Portuguese infinitive.
[c2-l018 >] *boas obras.*

[c2-l019 note] Iengo ſayǔ lists front, back, left, and right. Enlargement supports the worn a and a two-stroke caron over u, correcting OCR ſiyù; the Portuguese lists the first three directions here.
[c2-l020 >] *& direita, i, Por todas as partes.*

[c2-l021] Iengu. Saqibaxiri. *O que vai diante do Rey,*
[c2-l021 note] Iengu is a forerunner, paraphrased saqibaxiri. The scan does not contain the extra i in OCR Saiqibaxiri; the Portuguese describes someone going ahead of a king.

[c2-l025 >] *diante.*

[c2-l026] Iengiǒ. *O eſtar como abſorto em extaſi medi-*
[c2-l026 note] Iengiǒ denotes absorbed meditation, described in the Portuguese as an ecstatic state. The initial is upright I, not OCR ſ; medi- continues as meditando.

[c2-l028] Ien-i. Ienno curai. *Dignidade, ou grao de*

[c2-l031 note] Ieni is zeni, money in the form of copper cash coins. Caixas here refers to those coins, not storage boxes; q̃ retains the source’s abbreviated conjunction.

[c2-l035] Ieniguchi. *Buraco por onde ſe enfião as caixas.*

[c2-l037] Ieninaua. *Corda em que enfião as caixas. No*

[c2-l038 >] *vulgo ſe diz,* Iennaua.

[c2-l039] Ien-in. Xizzucana cague. *Camara, ou ſella*
[c2-l039 note] Ien-in is a quiet religious chamber, paraphrased xizzucana cague. The initial I and internal hyphen are visible; Portuguese ſella is retained as printed rather than modernized to cela.
[c2-l040 >] *dos Ienxùs.*
[c2-l040 note] Dos Ienxùs identifies the chamber or cell with the Zen community. The Japanese-derived group name is embedded in an otherwise Portuguese continuation.
[c2-l041] Ien-in. *Semente de virtude. i, Obras virtuo-*
[c2-l041 note] A different Ien-in means a seed or cause of virtue, explained by virtuous works. The source has a lexical hyphen, not the a in OCR Ienain; virtuo- continues below.
[c2-l042 >] *ſas de que hum hà de receber depois o pago.*
[c2-l042 note] These are virtuous works for which one will receive a later recompense. The scan reads the joined depois, not OCR de poir; the grave in hà is retained.
[c2-l043] Ienje. Saqino yo. *Idade, ou mundo paſſado.*
[c2-l043 note] Ienje is a previous age or world, saqi no yo. The entry preserves the dictionary’s broad wording without imposing a narrower interpretation such as a particular former lifetime.
[c2-l044] Ienjen. Maye maye. *Tempos paßados.*
[c2-l044 note] Ienjen means former times, maye maye. Capital I corrects OCR l, and the Portuguese reinforces the repeated temporal sense.
[c2-l045 >] *¶* Ienjenno gotocu. *Aßi como foi anti-*
[c2-l045 note] Ienjenno gotocu means as in former times. The Portuguese begins “as it was anciently”, with anti-guamente divided between physical rows.
[c2-l046 >] *guamente, ou no tempo paßado.*
[c2-l046 note] Guamente completes antiguamente, followed by “or in the past”. This closes the temporal Ienjen entry.
[c2-l047] Ienjenni. *Adu.* i, Xidai xidaini. *Pouco*
[c2-l047 note] Ienjenni is the adverb “gradually”, paraphrased xidai xidaini. The visible d restores Xidai from OCR Xicai, and Pouco replaces Pouro; the translation continues as “little by little”.
