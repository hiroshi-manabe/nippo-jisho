[c1-l001 >] *dura por hum mes. ¶* Nenguiǒji. *Of-*
[c1-l001 note] The opening continues the preceding definition of a month-long office. Nenguiǒji introduces the corresponding annual office; the worn n is read with the year-long gloss below. The visible Of- begins officio and is not a long-s form.
[c1-l002 >] *ficio que dura por hum anno. ¶* Sumǒno
[c1-l002 note] Ficio completes officio: an appointment lasting a year. Sumǒno begins the example concerning the official who supervises wrestling; the caron is retained.
[c1-l003 >] guiǒjini tatçu, l, ſumǒno guiǒjiuo ſuru.
[c1-l003 note] The alternatives mean serving as a sumo referee or performing that office. The printed l is the Latin alternative marker, not part of the Japanese phrase.
[c1-l004 >] *Ter officio de ajuntar, & fazer trauar os*
[c1-l004 note] The Portuguese explains the office as bringing the wrestlers together and having them engage. Trauar is the historical spelling and should not be modernized.
[c1-l005 >] *lutadores, como padrinhos da luta.*
[c1-l005 note] Lutadores completes the object of the preceding verbs. The comparison with padrinhos da luta describes the referee’s mediating or supervisory role.
[c1-l006] Guiǒji, zuru, ita. *Obrar, ou fazer obras*
[c1-l006 note] Guiǒji, zuru, ita gives the forms of the verb meaning to carry out religious practice. The Portuguese starts a definition in terms of performing works, continued below.
[c1-l007 >] *de penitencia, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] De penitencia specifies the works as penitential exercises. This short continuation belongs to the verb, not the fisherman entry that follows.
[c1-l008] Guiojin. Sunadoru fito. *Peſcador.*
[c1-l008 note] Guiojin is explained by sunadoru fito, a person who fishes, and directly glossed Peſcador. Abrasion weakens several strokes in the Japanese synonym without supporting a different word.
[c1-l009] Guioju. Vuo atçumaru. *Cardume de pei-*
[c1-l009 note] Guioju, literally fish gathering, denotes a shoal of fish. The initial is G, not OCR C; pei- is the printed beginning of peixes across the physical break.
[c1-l010 >] *xes. Vt,* Guioju nagareni ſacanoboru.
[c1-l010 note] The example says that a shoal of fish goes upstream against the current. The repeated Guioju clarifies the headword; the OCR Gutoju misread the short i.
[c1-l011 >] *O cardume de peixe ſobe contra a corrente.* S.
[c1-l011 note] This is the Portuguese translation of the upstream-swimming example. Peixe has a visible x, not the OCR c. Final S. is an upright written-register label, outside the italic translation.
[c1-l012] Guiǒjut. Voconǒ nori. *Deprecaçoẽs, cere-*
[c1-l012 note] Guiǒjut is a religious rite or practice, explained by voconǒ nori and by prayers and ceremonies. The final headword letter is t, not OCR r; the Sino-Japanese checked ending is retained rather than expanded.
[c1-l013 >] *monias, &c. Vt,* Guiǒjutuo tçucuſu.
[c1-l013 note] Monias completes ceremonias. Guiǒjutuo tçucuſu means carrying out these rites fully; the damaged opening is supported by the headword and surviving types, not the garbled OCR Ganǒjutuo.
[c1-l014 >] *Fazer eſtas deprecaçoẽs, & cerimonias com*
[c1-l014 note] The translation continues with performing these prayers and ceremonies. The printed deprecaçoẽs is preserved with its historical nasal notation rather than normalized.
[c1-l015 >] *todas as forças, & efficacia.*
[c1-l015 note] Todas as forças and efficacia complete the instruction to perform the rites with all one’s strength and effectiveness. The first vowel of todas corrects OCR tadas.
[c1-l016] Guiomocu. Vuono me. *Olhos de peixe. Vt,*
[c1-l016 note] Guiomocu is explicitly explained as the eyes of a fish. Vt, is italic and introduces the comparison on the following row; it is not part of the Japanese headword.
[c1-l017 >] Guiomocuuo totte xuguiocuni fiſu. *Cõpa-*
[c1-l017 note] Guiomocuuo totte xuguiocuni fiſu means taking fish eyes and comparing them with precious jewels. The f in fiſu belongs to the Japanese verb for comparison; Cõpa- begins the Portuguese infinitive on the next line.
[c1-l018 >] *rar os olhos do peixe a pedras precioſas.* S.
[c1-l018 note] Rar completes Cõpa-rar, and the translation explicitly compares fish eyes to precious stones. The final upright S. marks written usage rather than belonging to the italic sentence.
[c1-l019] Guiôna. *Vt,* Guiôna fito. *Homem en-*
[c1-l019 note] Guiôna is illustrated with a person who magnifies or exaggerates things. The clear G corrects OCR C, and the circumflex must not be assimilated to neighboring caroned headwords.
[c1-l020 >] *carecedor das couſas. ¶* Guiôna coto.
[c1-l020 note] Carecedor completes encarecedor. Guiôna coto supplies a second example, now applying the adjective to a thing rather than a person.
[c1-l021 >] *Couſa grande, & extraordinaria.*
[c1-l021 note] The second example is translated as something great and extraordinary. This short line closes Guiôna and is separate from the religious practitioner below.
[c1-l022] Guiǒnin. Voconǒ fito. i, Guiǒtaiuo ſuru
[c1-l022 note] Guiǒnin is a person who performs religious practice, further explained as someone who undertakes guiǒtai. Voconǒ fito and the longer paraphrase describe the same practitioner.
[c1-l023 >] fito. *Homem penitente, ou que faz obras,*
[c1-l023 note] Fito completes the Japanese paraphrase. The Portuguese calls this person penitential or one who performs works, whose description continues below.
[c1-l024 >] *& exercicios de virtude.*
[c1-l024 note] Exercises of virtue completes the description of Guiǒnin. The phrase is a continuation of the person’s practices, not a new headword.
[c1-l025] Guionicu. Vuo, xiximura. *Peixe, & carne.*
[c1-l025 note] Guionicu pairs fish with flesh or meat, matching Vuo, xiximura and the two Portuguese nouns. The visible separator after Vuo is a comma rather than the draft’s full stop.
[c1-l026] Guioqei. Von yorocobi. *Palaura com que*
[c1-l026 note] Guioqei is an honorific expression of joy or congratulation, paraphrased Von yorocobi. Several abraded headword strokes produced OCR Giioqei; the repeated example below supplies a local comparator.
[c1-l027 >] *ſe dão as boas feſtas, perabens, &c. Vt,*
[c1-l027 note] The expression is used for greetings on festive occasions and congratulations. Perabens retains the printed historical vowel, while italic Vt, introduces the example below.
[c1-l028 >] Guioqei medetai.
[c1-l028 note] Guioqei medetai gives the congratulatory formula. The scan shows the same headword as above, not OCR Curioqei; this short Japanese example remains a separate physical row.
[c1-l029] Guioqen. Votçurugui. *Eſpada del Rey, ou*
[c1-l029 note] Guioqen is an honorific sword, paraphrased Votçurugui and exemplified by the king’s sword. The initial sequence is Guio-, not the OCR Gaio-.
[c1-l030 >] *dalgum ſenhor grande.*
[c1-l030 note] The definition also includes the sword of a great lord. The damaged vowel in dalgum is compatible with u, completing the ordinary contracted Portuguese expression rather than OCR dalgam.
[c1-l031] Guiôqi. i, Yono ſuye. *Fim do mundo. Vt,*
[c1-l031 note] Guiôqi is explained as the end of the world, Yono ſuye. The headword’s visible circumflex is retained even though the following example has a differently directed mark.
[c1-l032 >] Youa guiǒqini voyobu. *O mundo vaiſe*
[c1-l032 note] Youa guiǒqini voyobu says that the world reaches its final period. The example’s caron is not regularized to the headword’s circumflex; the Portuguese begins describing a world nearing its end.
[c1-l033 >] *acabando, & enpejorando.*
[c1-l033 note] Acabando and enpejorando complete the explanation: the world is ending and worsening. The historical spelling with n before p remains unchanged.
[c1-l034] Guioqua. Sunadorinofi. *Fogo com que pes-*
[c1-l034 note] Guioqua is the fire used in fishing, literally sunadori no fi. Both the G of the headword and u of Sunadorinofi correct OCR confusions; the visible joined spelling of the paraphrase is retained.
[c1-l035 >] *cão de noite os peſcadores.*
[c1-l035 note] Cão completes pes-cão, explaining that fishermen use this fire at night. The broken word is preserved across its two physical rows.
[c1-l036] Guiôraxij. *Couſa grande. ¶* Guiôraxù
[c1-l036 note] Guiôraxij first describes something great; the example uses the adverbial Guiôraxù. The printed grave over the final u is distinct from the circumflex over o.
[c1-l037 >] monouo yǔ fito. *Homem que exagera*
[c1-l037 note] With the preceding adverb, monouo yǔ fito means a person who speaks exaggeratedly about things. The worn t in fito is recoverable from the remaining ink and the person-centered translation.
[c1-l038 >] *muito as couſas.*
[c1-l038 note] Muito as couſas completes the description of someone who exaggerates things greatly. It is the end of the example, not a detached lexical entry.
[c1-l039] Guioren. Von ſudare. i, Miſu. *Esteiras que*
[c1-l039 note] Guioren denotes an honorific hanging blind, paraphrased Von ſudare and Miſu. The Portuguese calls these mats or screens; the worn Guio- is not OCR Caio-.
[c1-l040 >] *dependurão nas portas, ou janellas de peſſoas*
[c1-l040 note] The blinds are hung in doors or windows belonging to people whose status is specified on the next row. Dependurão and janellas retain their printed spellings.
[c1-l041 >] *nobres.*
[c1-l041 note] Nobres completes the preceding definition: these blinds are associated with noble people. The word remains a distinct body row even though it is very short.
[c1-l042] Guioreo. Yamabuqi irono qinu. *Peça de*
[c1-l042 note] Guioreo denotes silk of yamabuki color. The unaccented final reo is retained and is distinct from the accented fishing term on the next entry; OCR Cutoreo is not supported.
[c1-l043 >] *ſeda de cor de hũas certas fulas amarelas.*
[c1-l043 note] The Portuguese explains the silk’s color by comparison with certain yellow flowers. The unusual printed fulas is retained as visible rather than replaced with modern flores.
[c1-l044] Guioreô. Sunadorino cari. *Peſcaria.*
[c1-l044 note] Guioreô, explained by Sunadorino cari, is fishing. This headword has the visible circumflex lacking in Guioreo above, and the Portuguese Peſcaria confirms a different entry.
[c1-l045] Guiorin. Vuo vrocuzzu. i, Vuo. *Peixe.*
[c1-l045 note] Guiorin gives fish and scales in its literal paraphrase, then explicitly equates the term with Vuo, fish. The double z in vrocuzzu is retained rather than normalized to a modern spelling.
[c1-l046] Guiǒriqi. Voconǒ chicara. *Força da peni-*
[c1-l046 note] Guiǒriqi is the power of religious practice, Voconǒ chicara. The worn caroned o remains part of a single headword; Força has a cedilla, and peni- continues below.
[c1-l047 >] *tencia, & outros exercicios virtuoſos.*
[c1-l047 note] Tencia completes penitencia; the rest adds other virtuous exercises. The scan supports outros exercicios virtuoſos rather than the draft’s garbled sequence.

[c2-l001] Guiorui. Vuono tagui. *Eſpecie, ou genero*

[c2-l003] Guiôſan. Fome auogu. *Louuar, & engrã-*
[c2-l003 note] Guiôſan is praising or extolling, literally Fome auogu. The Portuguese engrã- abbreviates the nasal consonant and continues onto the next row.
[c2-l004 >] *decer.* Guiôſanni yǔ. *Encarecer dema-*
[c2-l004 note] Decer completes engrã-decer. Guiôſanni yǔ introduces speaking in exaggerated praise, with the Portuguese dema- continued below.
[c2-l005 >] *ſiadamente as couſas.*
[c2-l005 note] Siadamente completes demasiadamente: praising or magnifying things excessively. The long-s opening follows the printed division rather than a new word.
[c2-l006] Guiôſanna. *Grande, & extraordinaria couſa.*
[c2-l006 note] Guiôſanna is the adjectival expression for something great and extraordinary. The doubled n is visible and distinguishes the form from a bare noun.
[c2-l007] Guiôſanni. *Grande, ou encarecadamente.*
[c2-l007 note] Guiôſanni is the corresponding adverbial form, glossed as greatly or with magnifying praise. It is a separate headword from the preceding -na form.
[c2-l008] Guioſat. i, Von fumi. *Carta falando com*
[c2-l008 note] Guioſat, paraphrased Von fumi, is an honorific term for a letter. The final t is the printed checked ending, not a defective modernized syllable.
[c2-l009 >] *reſpeito.*
[c2-l009 note] Reſpeito completes the explanation that this term for a letter is used respectfully. The short row belongs to Guioſat.
[c2-l010] Guiǒſǒ. Catachi catachi. *Figura, ou ſẽ-*
[c2-l010 note] Guiǒſǒ concerns appearance or facial expression. Both Catachi tokens are printed and retained; the Portuguese describes the figure or countenance of a living person.
[c2-l011 >] *brante de peſſoa viua. Vt,* Guiǒſǒmini
[c2-l011 note] The gloss specifies a living person, then begins the example Guiǒſǒmini-/cuxi. The adjective is divided without a visible terminal hyphen after mini, so none is supplied.
[c2-l012 >] cuxi. *Estar o roſto, ou home des figurado.*
[c2-l012 note] Cuxi completes the adjective describing an unpleasant or disfigured appearance. The Portuguese explains this as a disfigured face or person. The scan leaves home without a secure final m or tilde; that printed form and the locally spaced des figurado are retained rather than normalized.
[c2-l013] Guioſon. Sunadorino zaixo. *Lugar de pes-*
[c2-l013 note] Guioſon is a fishing settlement, explained by Sunadorino zaixo. The terminal pes- belongs to the Portuguese pescadores on the next physical row.
[c2-l014 >] *cadores.*
[c2-l014 note] Cadores completes pes-cadores, closing the definition of a fishermen’s settlement. It is not a separate headword.
[c2-l015] Guioſui. Vuo mizzu. *Agua de peixe, ou*
[c2-l015 note] Guioſui combines fish and water in its Japanese explanation. The Portuguese first says fish-water and then clarifies the relation in the continuation.
[c2-l016 >] *onde anda o peixe.*
[c2-l016 note] The clarification is water in which fish swim. This avoids interpreting the preceding phrase as water extracted from a fish.
[c2-l017] Guiǒtai. *Penitencia. Vt,* Guiǒtaiuo tçu-
[c2-l017 note] Guiǒtai is penitential practice. Guiǒtaiuo tçu- begins an example meaning to carry out such practices fully; the hyphen is visible at the column edge.
[c2-l018 >] cuſu. *Fazer grandes penitencias, & ou-*
[c2-l018 note] Cuſu completes tçu-cuſu, to carry out fully. The translation describes great penances and begins the reference to other good exercises, divided as ou-/tros.
[c2-l019 >] *tros exercicios bons.*
[c2-l019 note] Tros completes outros, adding other good practices to penitence. This short continuation closes the entry before the different sense of obstruction.
[c2-l020] Guiôtai. i, todocôru coto. *Impedirſe, ou*
[c2-l020 note] Guiôtai is explained by todocôru coto, being delayed or impeded. The initial G corrects OCR C. Only one arm of the headword’s vowel mark is strong; the draft’s circumflex is retained as the usable reading, with the graphic damage noted rather than silently turning it into a grave.
[c2-l021 >] *eſtoruarſe algũa couſa.* S.
[c2-l021 note] The Portuguese completes the sense of something being hindered or obstructed. The upright S. is a written-register label, while algũa retains the mark on u.
[c2-l022] Guiotan. Vuono qimo. *Fel de peixe. Vt,*
[c2-l022 note] Guiotan, literally a fish’s qimo, is here explicitly fish bile or gall. The short i in the headword corrects OCR Gutotan, and Vt, introduces the bitter-taste example.
[c2-l023 >] Guiotan nigaqi coto fanafadaxi. S. *O*
[c2-l023 note] The example says that fish gall is exceedingly bitter. S. labels the Japanese example as written usage; the following italic O begins its Portuguese translation.
[c2-l024 >] *fel do peixe he ſummamente amargoſo.*
[c2-l024 note] Completes the translation: fish gall is supremely bitter. The historical amargoſo is retained instead of modern amargo.
[c2-l025] Guiǒten. Tenni auogu. i, Vodoroqu. *Es-*
[c2-l025 note] Guiǒten combines looking up to heaven with Vodoroqu, being astonished. The worn Guio- and the caron are read with the repeated example below; Es- starts the Portuguese espanto.
[c2-l026 >] *panto.* Guiǒtenſuru. *Eſpantarſe.*
[c2-l026 note] Panto completes espanto, and Guiǒtenſuru means becoming astonished. The visible n before ſuru corrects OCR u without introducing an unprinted word boundary.
[c2-l027] Guioteqi. Sunadorino fuye. *Frauta de pes-*
[c2-l027 note] Guioteqi is the flute of a fisherman, Sunadorino fuye. Frauta is the printed Portuguese form; it is not modernized to flauta.
[c2-l028 >] *cador.*
[c2-l028 note] Cador completes pes-cador, identifying the flute’s owner or user. The short line remains in the body.
[c2-l029] Guiotǒ Vuono catana. *Cutelo, ou faca de*
[c2-l029 note] Guiotǒ is a fish-cutting blade, explained by Vuono catana. The Portuguese offers cutelo or faca. Only the rising arm of the caron is strong; no period is visible in the clear gap after the headword, so the draft’s period has been removed.
[c2-l030 >] *cortar peixe.*
[c2-l030 note] Cortar peixe specifies the blade’s function: cutting fish. It completes the preceding knife definition.
[c2-l031] Guiotô. Vuono atama. *Cabeça de peixe.*
[c2-l031 note] Guiotô means a fish’s head, exactly matched by Vuono atama and Cabeça de peixe. This is separate from the knife and fishing-light homophones around it.
[c2-l032] Guiotô. Sunadori, tomoxibi. *Fogo com*
[c2-l032 note] Guiotô here means a fisherman’s light or fire, paraphrased with Sunadori and tomoxibi. The initial sequence is Guio-, not OCR Guro-.
[c2-l033 >] *que peſcão de noite os peſcadores.*
[c2-l033 note] The fire is used by fishermen fishing at night. This repeats the functional explanation given earlier for Guioqua without merging the entries.
[c2-l034] Guiǒtocu. Voconǒ tocu. *Fruito, ou mere-*
[c2-l034 note] Guiǒtocu denotes the benefit or merit of religious practice, Voconǒ tocu. Mere- begins merecimento across the physical line break.
[c2-l035 >] *cimento de virtude, ou obras de penitencia.*
[c2-l035 note] Cimento completes merecimento, linking merit with virtue and penitential works. The Portuguese noun is virtude, not OCR virtide.
[c2-l036] Guiouô. Sunadorino voqina. i, Toxi yot-
[c2-l036 note] Guiouô is an elderly or experienced fisherman, first paraphrased Sunadorino voqina. Toxi yot- starts a second description whose past-tense form ends on the next line.

[c2-l037 note] Ta completes yot-ta, and guiojin is visibly separated as the fisherman noun. The phrase means an aged fisherman; the translation also allows long experience in the trade. OCR tagurojin concealed both the boundary and the i.
[c2-l038 >] *to tempo vſa o officio.*
[c2-l038 note] To completes mui-to: someone who has practiced the occupation for a long time. This is the experienced-fisherman alternative, not a further age definition.
[c2-l039] Guioxen. Sunadorino fune. *Embarcação*
[c2-l039 note] Guioxen, paraphrased Sunadorino fune, denotes a fishing vessel. The final adjective describing that vessel appears on the next row.
[c2-l040 >] *peſcareza.*

[c2-l041] Guioxin. *Dormir de peßoa honrada. ¶* Guio-
[c2-l041 note] Guioxin is sleep expressed respectfully of an honored person. The visible short o corrects OCR y; the repeated Guio- begins the example and is physically divided from xin below.
[c2-l042 >] xin aru, l, naſaruru. *Dormir peſſoa hõrada.*
[c2-l042 note] Xin completes Guio-xin. Aru and naſaruru are alternatives for saying respectfully that the person sleeps; l is the Latin alternative marker.
[c2-l043] Guiǒxiqi. Catachi, iro. *Figura, & cor.*
[c2-l043 note] Guiǒxiqi concerns form and color, Catachi, iro. The Portuguese capital is F in Figura. Enlargement shows the surviving rising arm on the right of the headword’s o; the caron reading also agrees with the subsequently consulted NINJAL headword, although the other arm is abraded.
[c2-l044] Guiôxǒ. Tacumi, tacumu. *Carpinteiro.* S.
[c2-l044 note] Guiôxǒ is glossed as a carpenter, with Tacumi and tacumu printed as its Japanese explanations. The upright final S. marks written usage, and the headword’s G corrects OCR Gai-.
[c2-l045] Guioxô. Sunadori, qicori. *Peſcador, &*
[c2-l045 note] Guioxô pairs a fisherman with a woodcutter, Sunadori, qicori. The Portuguese first supplies fisherman and continues the second occupation below.
[c2-l046 >] *homem que corta lenha.*
[c2-l046 note] Homem que corta lenha gives the woodcutter half of the paired expression. It is a continuation of Guioxô, not an independent Portuguese entry.
[c2-l047] Guioxǔ. Sunadorino fune. *Embarcação de*
[c2-l047 note] Guioxǔ is another term for a fishing boat, Sunadorino fune. The final e of Portuguese de is almost effaced, leaving only a small stroke at letter height; de is adopted with this caveat because the trace is compatible and peſcadores completes the phrase below.
[c2-l048 >] *peſcadores.* S.
[c2-l048 note] Peſcadores completes the fishing-vessel gloss and S. marks written usage. The signature and catchword printed farther right on this baseline have been separated into their own furniture zones rather than left inside the body text.
