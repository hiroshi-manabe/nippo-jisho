[c1-l001] Iraira. *Adu. Modo de tocar, ou picar algũa*
[c1-l001 note] Iraira describes a prickling or irritating sensation, here introduced as an adverbial manner of touching or pricking. The scan reads Iraira and tocar, not the OCR's Yeaira and toxar; enlargement also supports a narrow word space in picar algũa.
[c1-l002 >] *couſa na mão, ou outra parte do corpo, ſentir*
[c1-l002 note] Continues the definition with the hand or another part of the body as the affected location; sentir begins the alternative description of the sensation.
[c1-l003 >] *comechão, &c. Vt,* Teni irairato ſauaru. To-
[c1-l003 note] Teni irairato sawaru illustrates a prickling touch on the hand, matching the Portuguese itching gloss. To- begins Portuguese tocar, not a Japanese particle; its locally upright type is retained before italic car on the next row.
[c1-l004 >] *car na mão como picandoa. ¶* Irairato xi-
[c1-l004 note] Completes tocar na mão como picandoa, touching the hand as though pricking it. Irairato xi- begins a second example, completed by mu below.
[c1-l005 >] mu. *Sentir no eorpo como picaduras arden-*
[c1-l005 note] Xi- plus mu forms shimu, describing a penetrating or stinging sensation. The Portuguese compares it to burning pricks in the body and divides ardendo at the physical edge.
[c1-l006 >] *do, ou eſcozendo.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes ardendo and adds escozendo, burning or smarting. This is the end of the sensory definition, not part of the following character description.
[c1-l007] Irairaxij. S*er frenetico, & rabugento, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] Irairashii is characterized as fretful or irritable: frenetico and rabugento describe a person's agitated disposition rather than the physical prickling above.
[c1-l008 >] *¶* Irairaxij fito. *Homem comechoſo, &*
[c1-l008 note] Irairashii hito applies the adjective to a person. Comechoso extends the itching vocabulary figuratively to a restless or easily irritated man; the conjunction continues below.
[c1-l009 >] *rabugento.*
[c1-l009 note] Rabugento completes the personal description as peevish or ill-tempered, closing the irairashii example rather than introducing another entry.
[c1-l010] Irameqi, u, eita. *Sentir comechão, ou como*
[c1-l010 note] Irameki, irameku, irameita gives the verb for feeling an itch or prickling. The abbreviated u and eita principal parts are retained rather than expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l011 >] *picaduras na mão, ou outra parte do corpo.*
[c1-l011 note] The sensation is likened to pricks in the hand or another body part. Corpo is one printed word; OCR's internal cor po division is not supported by the crop.
[c1-l012] Iramuxi. *Hũs bichos de cabelos, ou ſedas com*
[c1-l012 note] Iramushi denotes hairy or bristly creatures that sting, as the next row confirms. The initial is capital I, and the Portuguese is bichos, not bicbos; the description alone does not require a modern species identification.
[c1-l013 >] *que picão.*
[c1-l013 note] Que picão says that the hairs or bristles are what these creatures sting with, completing the relative phrase com que rather than adding a separate verb entry.
[c1-l014] Iran. Tagai midaruru. *Diſcrepando, ou fal*
[c1-l014 note] Iran is explained by tagai midaruru and a Portuguese account of disagreement or failure to keep a promise. Capital I is visible. The unfinished fal continues as faltando without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l015 >] *tando nalgũa promeßa, &c. dizer varias cou*
[c1-l015 note] Completes faltando and specifies a promise, then describes saying various things. The source divides cousas as cou / ſas without a printed hyphen here.
[c1-l016 >] *ſas que embaração, & perturbão. ¶* Iran-
[c1-l016 note] The various statements cause difficulty and disturbance. Iran- begins the example iran wo yuu; unlike the preceding Portuguese division, this line has a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l017 >] uo yǔ. *Falar varias couſas desta maneira.*
[c1-l017 note] Iran wo yuu is speaking in the discrepant or obstructive manner just described. The Portuguese repeats that characterization; yǔ retains the visible caron rather than a modernized spelling.
[c1-l018 >] *¶* Cono gui iran aruna. *Não diſcrepeis,*
[c1-l018 note] Kono gi iran aru na forbids disagreement or objection in this matter. Portuguese não discrepeis begins the corresponding prohibition, which continues through the next two rows.
[c1-l019 >] *ou falteis mais, alem diſſo não digais couſas*
[c1-l019 note] Continues the prohibition against further default or disagreement, then adds not saying things that create difficulties. The two negative clauses belong to one example.
[c1-l020 >] *de embaraço, ou que não conuem, &c.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes the injunction against obstructive or inappropriate statements. Conuem is retained without imposing modern accentuation.
[c1-l021] Irara. *Erua aßi chamada.*
[c1-l021 note] Irara is identified simply as an herb so called. The gloss gives no further botanical description, so no specific modern plant is supplied.
[c1-l022] Ir*a*taca. l, irataca juzu. *Hũa feição de con*
[c1-l022 note] Irataka, alternatively irataka juzu, names a kind of Buddhist prayer beads. The scan has capital I in the headword, lowercase i in the alternative, and unhyphenated con / tas across the rows.
[c1-l023 >] *tas dos gentios. ¶* Irataca juzuuo voxi-
[c1-l023 note] Completes contas dos gentios, the missionaries' designation of the prayer beads. The example begins irataka juzu wo oshi-, completed by momu below; the printed voxi- is retained.
[c1-l024 >] momu. *Esfregar eſtas contas entre as mãos*
[c1-l024 note] Oshi-momu describes rubbing or kneading the beads between the hands. The Portuguese directly confirms that action and retains the source's mãos spelling.
[c1-l025 >] *como fazem os gentios quando rezão com fer-*
[c1-l025 note] Describes practitioners rubbing the beads while praying fervently. The Portuguese fervor is physically divided as fer- / uor, preserving historical u for consonantal v.
[c1-l026 >] *uor, &c.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes fervor and closes the account of the prayer-bead practice. This short continuation is not attached to the following answer-related entry.
[c1-l027] Iraye. i, Cotaye. *Reposta.*
[c1-l027 note] Iraye is the noun for a reply, equated with kotae. Printed Reposta is retained as the historical form rather than silently supplied as modern resposta.
[c1-l028] Iraye, uru, eta. *Reſponder.*
[c1-l028 note] The verbal iraye, irayuru, irayeta means to answer. The separate noun and verb entries are both present and should not be merged.
[c1-l029] Ire. *Crecenças que ſe metem de mais pera re-*
[c1-l029 note] Ire denotes extra material put in to make up a deficiency. Crecenças and the divided refazer introduce that supplementation sense, distinct from the general verb of putting in below.
[c1-l030 >] *fazer a falta dalgũa couſa como ſeda, ou outra*
[c1-l030 note] Completes refazer and mentions silk or another commodity needing supplementation. Enlarged inspection shows a dotted i in falia; the anomalous printed form is retained, although falta is the apparent intended meaning.
[c1-l031 >] *fazenda ruim. ¶ Item, Liga que ſe mete*
[c1-l031 note] Finishes the example of inferior goods and adds a metallurgical sense: an alloying material that is put into a melt. Liga begins a second gloss of the same noun.
[c1-l032 >] *na fundição da prata, ou de outro metal.*
[c1-l032 note] Specifies alloy added when melting silver or another metal. This completes the technical sense of ire rather than introducing a separate entry.
[c1-l033] Ire, uru, eta. *Meter dentro. ¶* Cocoro
[c1-l033 note] Ire, iruru, ireta means putting something inside. Kokoro begins the extended expression for putting one's heart or effort into something on the following row.
[c1-l034 >] uo iruru, l, nen, l, xeiuo iruru. *Por di-*
[c1-l034 note] Kokoro wo iruru, nen, and sei wo iruru express applying attention or effort. Portuguese por means to put or apply here; the divided diligencia follows. This is an indented continuation, not a new flush-left entry.
[c1-l035 >] *ligencia, & cuidado.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes diligencia and adds cuidado, diligence and care. The row continues the figurative examples of putting effort into something.
[c1-l036] Ire, uru. *Que vem de,* Iri, ru. *Por torrar.*
[c1-l036 note] This distinct ire is related by the dictionary to iri in the roasting sense. Torrar is visible, not OCR terrar; Iri, ru is a Japanese verb citation embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l037 >] *Torrarſe algũa couſa em panela, tacho, &c.*
[c1-l037 note] Explains the intransitive roasting sense: something roasts in a pot or pan. The indentation belongs to the entry above.
[c1-l038 >] *¶ Permet.* Qino ireta fito. *Homẽ fre-*
[c1-l038 note] The figurative ki no ireta hito describes an agitated or irritable person. Homẽ preserves the abbreviation tilde, and fre- begins frenetico on the next row.
[c1-l039 >] *netico, & comechoſo.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes frenetico and adds comechoso, characterizing the person's restless irritability rather than physical roasting.
[c1-l040] Irecaye, uru, eta. *Reuezar, ou trocar a gen*
[c1-l040 note] Irekaye means replacing or exchanging people or things. The stray initial ſ in the OCR is not a letter before Irecaye; the physical column rule is nearby. Gen continues as gente without a terminal hyphen.
[c1-l041 >] *te. Vt,* Ninjuuo irecayuru. *¶* Fitçu-
[c1-l041 note] Completes gente and illustrates ninjuu wo irekayuru, replacing personnel. Hitsu- begins the chest or box example on the next row; the introductory Vt is italic.
[c1-l042 >] ni monouo irecayuru. *Mudar algũa couſa*
[c1-l042 note] Hitsu ni mono wo irekayuru describes changing the contents of a chest. The Portuguese begins the same replacement operation, completed below.
[c1-l043 >] *do caixão metendo lhe outra.*
[c1-l043 note] Finishes the box example by specifying that another thing is put in its place. The row belongs to irekaye and is indented in the scan.
[c1-l044] Irechigaye, uru, eta. *Meter gente encontran*
[c1-l044 note] Irechigaye is first explained as putting troops into contact with the enemy. Encontrando is divided without a visible end hyphen.
[c1-l045 >] *doſe com os inimigos. Vt,* Ninjuuo irechi-
[c1-l045 note] Completes encontrando-se com os inimigos and begins the personnel example. Enlargement supports the narrow separation in doſe com; the next row completes the Japanese verb.
[c1-l046 >] gayuru. *¶* Qiuo irechigayete tçugu.
[c1-l046 note] Completes ninjuu wo irechigayuru, then gives a joining example whose Portuguese refers to two pieces of wood. Quo is retained at the damaged beginning: ki wo, normally Qiuo, is contextually expected, but enlargement does not securely isolate an additional i in the inked middle cluster.
[c1-l047 >] *Pegar, ou encaixar dous paos com as pontas de*
[c1-l047 note] The gloss describes joining or fitting two wooden pieces by their ends. Pegar has a visible P, not OCR F, and the narrow word division in com as is retained.
[c1-l048 >] *hum no outro.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes the joinery description: the end of one piece fits into the other. This is the final body row in the left column, above the bottom rule.

[c2-l001] Irecozzumi, u, unda. *Meter muito em hũa*
[c2-l001 note] Irekozzumi describes packing much into a container. Two z types are visible in the headword, correcting the OCR's single z; the container is specified on the next row.
[c2-l002 >] *vaſilha: mais ſe vſa,* Iricozzumu, *vide infrà.*
[c2-l002 note] Completes vasilha and says that Iricozzumu is more commonly used, with a reference below. The Japanese cross-reference is upright within the italic Portuguese apparatus; the scan supports vasilha and the Latin infrà, not OCR vaſilhu or inſrà.
[c2-l003] Irei. *Doença. ¶* Irei tçucamatçutta. *E-*
[c2-l003 note] Irei means illness; irei tsukamatsutta is translated as being ill. The Portuguese estou is split after E- at the physical line end.
[c2-l004 >] *ſtou do ente.*
[c2-l004 note] Completes estou doente, I am ill. The conspicuous printed gap within do ente is retained in the diplomatic text, not interpreted as two Portuguese words.
[c2-l005] Iremidare, uru, eta. *Meter ſem ordem co-*
[c2-l005 note] Iremidare describes putting in or mixing without order. Como begins an illustration involving troops on the following row.
[c2-l006 >] *mo gente que ſe encontra, ou miſtura com os i-*
[c2-l006 note] The illustration concerns troops meeting or mixing with the enemy. Both co- / mo and i- / nimigos are physical word divisions, not omitted words.
[c2-l007 >] *nimigos, &c.*
[c2-l007 note] Completes inimigos and the military illustration of disorderly mingling.
[c2-l008] Iremono. *Couſa em que ſe mete outra como cai*
[c2-l008 note] Iremono is a receptacle, literally something into which another thing is put. Caixa is divided as cai / xa without a visible end hyphen.
[c2-l009 >] *xa, &c.*
[c2-l009 note] Completes caixa, giving a box as the example of a container. This short row belongs to iremono.
[c2-l010] Iremugui. *Certa iguaria de trigo como letria*
[c2-l010 note] Iremugi names a wheat preparation compared with vermicelli. The gloss is a culinary comparison and does not justify substituting a different modern dish name.
[c2-l011 >] *cozida.*
[c2-l011 note] Cozida specifies cooked vermicelli, completing the food description.
[c2-l012] Iretate, tçuru, eta. *Meter refazendo como*
[c2-l012 note] Iretate gives the verb for putting something back to replenish it. The tsuru principal part is historical morphology and remains as printed.
[c2-l013 >] *dinheiro, ou fato alheo que hum vai gaſtan-*
[c2-l013 note] Money or someone else's goods being spent or used up illustrate what must be replenished. Fato here is property or goods, not a statement of fact.
[c2-l014 >] *do, &c.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes gastando and closes the replenishment example from the preceding row.
[c2-l015] Iretate. *O meter ſuprindo, ou refazendo que-*
[c2-l015 note] The noun iretate denotes the act of supplying or making good a deficiency. Que- begins quebras, losses or shortages, below.
[c2-l016 >] *bras de prata, gaſtos, &c. ¶* Iretateuo
[c2-l016 note] Completes quebras and mentions silver deficits and expenses. Iretate wo begins the construction with suru on the next row.
[c2-l017 >] ſuru. *Refazer deſta maneira. ¶* Ireta-
[c2-l017 note] Completes iretate wo suru, making good a shortage in the manner described. Ireta- starts a second example of the same noun across the line break.
[c2-l018 >] tega ita. i, Iretateuo xita. *Idem.*
[c2-l018 note] The first expression is explicitly equated with iretate wo shita, having made up the deficit. The printed tega ita is retained rather than repaired from the explanatory phrase; the gloss establishes its intended completed-action sense.
[c2-l019] Ireuoqi, u, oita. *Deixar a couſa metida dẽtro.*
[c2-l019 note] Ireoki means leaving something put inside. The abbreviated principal parts and dẽtro tilde are retained without expansion.
[c2-l020] Iri, u, itta. *Entrar. ¶* Iyeni iru. *En-*
[c2-l020 note] This iri is the verb of entering; ie ni iru illustrates entering a house. En- starts the Portuguese infinitive entrar on the next row.
[c2-l021 >] *trar em caſa.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes entrar em casa, the direct translation of the house-entry example.
[c2-l022] Iri, u, itta. T*orrar no fogo. ¶* Mameuo
[c2-l022 note] A separate homographic iri means roasting over a fire. Mame wo introduces beans or grains as the object on the next row.
[c2-l023 >] iru. *Torrar grãos.*
[c2-l023 note] Completes mame wo iru, roasting beans or grains. This example belongs to the roasting verb, not the preceding verb of entering.
[c2-l024] Iri, u, itta. *Ser neceßario. ¶* Core iru
[c2-l024 note] The third iri means being necessary. Kore iru begins an example asserting that something is needed; all three distinct entries remain separate.
[c2-l025 >] cotogia. *Iſto he neceßario.*
[c2-l025 note] Completes kore iru koto ja, explained as this is necessary. The source's cotogia spelling represents the historical copular expression without modernizing it.
[c2-l026] Iriai. *Ao por do ſol. ¶ Item, Sino que ſe*
[c2-l026 note] Iriai first denotes sunset and then the bell associated with that time. The generated contracted-syllable hint is not controlling; the compound is read iri-ai in this context.
[c2-l027 >] *tange a eſtas horas.*
[c2-l027 note] Says that the bell is rung at those hours, completing the second sense of iriai.
[c2-l028] Iriaidoqi. *Hora datarde, ou do ſol posto.*
[c2-l028 note] Iriaidoki is the evening or sunset hour. The printed close datarde spelling and the provisional short-s form posto are retained rather than normalized.
[c2-l029] Iricauari, u, atta. *Reuezarſe vindo gente*
[c2-l029 note] Irikawari means changing places or taking over, illustrated by fresh personnel arriving. The Japanese principal part atta and Portuguese reflexive construction agree on replacement.
[c2-l030 >] *de refreſco, &c. ¶* Tenma cocoroni i-
[c2-l030 note] Completes the arrival of relief personnel, then begins an example in which a demon enters the heart. The initial i- of irikawaru is physically divided from the rest of the verb.
[c2-l031 >] ricauaru. *Entrar o diabo em alguem tranſ-*
[c2-l031 note] Completes tenma kokoro ni irikawaru. The Portuguese explains a devil entering someone and begins transformando-se across the line break.
[c2-l032 >] *formandoſe nelle.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes the description of transformation or possession in the person, closing the figurative example of irikawaru.
[c2-l033] Irico. *Leſmas do mar ſecas. No Ximo ſe diz*
[c2-l033 note] Iriko is defined here as dried sea-slug or sea-cucumber material, not the later dried-small-fish sense. The sentence introduces the regional name used in Ximo; the scan has capital X.
[c2-l034 >] Cuxico.
[c2-l034 note] Kushiko completes the Portuguese statement No Ximo se diz, giving the regional alternative. Although alone on this physical row, it is a quoted Japanese name within that explanation.
[c2-l035] Iricozzumi, u, unda. *Entrar, ou recolherſe*
[c2-l035 note] Irikozzumi describes a crowd or many objects entering or being gathered into a space. This is the form referred to from Irecozzumi at the top of the column.
[c2-l036 >] *muita gente nalgũa caſa, ou muitas couſas jun-*
[c2-l036 note] The examples are many people in a house or many objects together. Jun- begins juntas on the following row.
[c2-l037 >] *tas em algũa caixa, vaſilha, &c.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes juntas and supplies a box or vessel as the receptacle for the assembled objects.
[c2-l038] Iricuchi. *Entrada, ou porta por onde ſe entra.*
[c2-l038 note] Irikuchi is an entrance or doorway through which one enters. The source's unvoiced c spelling is preserved rather than modernized to guchi.
[c2-l039] Iricumi. l, deiri. *Embaraço, ou negocio que*
[c2-l039 note] Irikumi, alternatively deiri, denotes an entanglement or unresolved matter. The Portuguese relative clause continues below.
[c2-l040 >] *naõ eſtà reſoluto. ¶* Chiguiǒno iricumi.
[c2-l040 note] Completes the description as not settled, then illustrates a dispute concerning chigyoo, a landholding or its income. The caron in Chiguiǒno and the printed naõ are retained.
[c2-l041 >] *Embaraço, ou demãda ſobre as terras, ou rẽda.*
[c2-l041 note] Translates the example as an impediment or lawsuit over land or revenue. The abbreviation tildes in demãda and rẽda remain unexpanded.
[c2-l042] Irifa. *Cabo, l, Remate do auto, l, obra que*
[c2-l042 note] Iriha denotes the ending of a dramatic piece or performance. Enlargement shows Cabo without a tilde; the apparent mark belongs to the descender on the preceding physical row, not to this word.
[c2-l043 >] *ſe repreſenta.*
[c2-l043 note] Completes obra que se representa, a work being performed. This indented continuation establishes the theatrical sense of iriha.
[c2-l044] Irifi. *Sol que ſe vai pondo.*
[c2-l044 note] Irihi is the setting sun, directly glossed as the sun going down. The f belongs to the historical h-row spelling.
[c2-l045] Irigome *Arroz torrado.*
[c2-l045 note] Irigome is roasted rice. No headword-final period is securely visible in the scan, so the source's unpunctuated headword is preserved.
[c2-l046] Irimame. *Graõs torrados.*
[c2-l046 note] Irimame denotes roasted beans or grains, complementing the roasted-rice entry. Enlargement confirms that the tilde in Graõs is over o, unlike grãos earlier in this column.
[c2-l047] Irime. *Quantidade do gaſto, ou cabedal q̃ ſe mete*
[c2-l047 note] Irime is the amount spent or capital put into something; the sentence continues on the next page. The scan supports Irime, the comma after gaſto, and abbreviated q̃, not OCR Inime or cabedalã. The isolated em below is the catchword, not an additional body row.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *em*
