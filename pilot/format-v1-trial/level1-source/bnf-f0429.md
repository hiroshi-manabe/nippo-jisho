[c1-l001] Sacuguiua. *Iunto, ou apar da tranqueira, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Sakugiwa is the area immediately beside a stockade. The Portuguese pairs junto with apar, both expressing proximity, and continues the description of the enclosure below.
[c1-l002 >] *cerca de paos q̃ ſe faz ao derredor da fortaleza.*
[c1-l002 note] The fence of stakes surrounds a fortress. The small q with its abbreviation mark is visible after paos; the draft retained only the detached mark.
[c1-l003] Sacu-i. *Obra, ou inuẽção, ou traça. Vt,* Rẽ-
[c1-l003 note] Saku-i denotes workmanship, invention, or design. Enlargement supports a faint abbreviation mark over the e of inuẽção. Upright Rẽ- begins the Japanese renga example after italic Vt.
[c1-l004 >] ga, dangui nadono ſacuiga yoi. *Acompo-*
[c1-l004 note] Renga, dangi nado no sakui ga yoi praises the construction of linked verse, preaching, and similar compositions. Acompo- begins the Portuguese rendering across the physical break.
[c1-l005 >] *ſição ou traça dos verſos, pregaçaõ, &c. he boa.*
[c1-l005 note] The composition or arrangement of the verses and sermon is good. The scan supplies the i in ſição; pregaçaõ retains the printed final-vowel tilde rather than modernizing its position.
[c1-l006] Sacui. *Couſa teſa, & facil de quebrar, como*
[c1-l006 note] Sakui first describes something stiff or brittle and easy to break. The Portuguese reads facil, with final l rather than the draft's long s.
[c1-l007 >] *pao, &c. ¶* Sacui fito. *Homem viuo,*
[c1-l007 note] Wood exemplifies the brittle-material sense; sakui hito then introduces a lively or quick person, a distinct figurative application of the same adjective.
[c1-l008 >] *& expedito.*
[c1-l008 note] Expedito completes the personal description as ready, capable, or quick in action; it is a short continuation, not another entry.
[c1-l009] Sacuji. *Obras de edificios.*
[c1-l009 note] Sakuji refers to building work. The concise Portuguese identifies construction rather than literary composition, distinguishing it from the surrounding saku vocabulary.
[c1-l010] Sacujit. Qinô. *Ontem.*
[c1-l010 note] Sakujitsu is equated with kinoo, yesterday. The terminal Japanese spelling is retained without expanding it to a modern romanization.
[c1-l011] Sacujit. i, Tçuitachi. *Primeiro dia da lũa.*
[c1-l011 note] This homophonous sakujitsu means the first day of the lunar month, explained by tsuitachi. The scan restores j in the headword and the missing letters in Tçuitachi.
[c1-l012] Sacumai. *Aparelho que hum faz com ardil,*
[c1-l012 note] Sakumai denotes an arrangement or preparation made with ingenuity. Ardil is read with the following inuenção rather than assumed to imply deception alone.
[c1-l013 >] *& inuenção. ¶* Sacumaiga yoi. l, varui.
[c1-l013 note] Sakumai ga yoi, alternatively warui, evaluates the preparation as good or bad. The paired Japanese adjectives govern the explanation on the following two rows.
[c1-l014 >] *Ser a inuenção, ou traça do apercebimento,*
[c1-l014 note] The Portuguese restates the invention or design involved in making preparations. Apercebimento belongs to this planning sense, not to a new Japanese headword.
[c1-l015 >] *que hum faz, boa, ou ruim.*
[c1-l015 note] This completes the judgment that someone's arrangements are good or poor, translating the yoi/warui alternatives above.
[c1-l016] Sacumai, ǒ, ǒta. *Fazer aparelho com inuen-*
[c1-l016 note] The verbal sakumai entry gives contracted principal forms and means preparing something ingeniously. The accented endings are retained rather than expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l017 >] *ção, & engenho como bãquete, feſta, &c. pera*
[c1-l017 note] The divided inuenção is completed, with a banquet or celebration as examples of an ingeniously arranged occasion. The abbreviated bãquete is not expanded.
[c1-l018 >] *muit agente.*
[c1-l018 note] The preparations are for many people. The uneven spacing is retained as muit agente from the supplied reading; it is understood as muita gente, not as a different Portuguese expression.
[c1-l019] Sacumô. Q eno vye. *Sementeira, ou noui-*
[c1-l019 note] Sakumoo concerns the growing crop or harvest, explained through ke no ue. The supplied spaced Q eno is retained; noui- continues the agricultural Portuguese noun novidade below.
[c1-l020 >] *dade de arroz, trigo, grãos, &c. ¶* Coto-
[c1-l020 note] The crop consists of rice, wheat, grain, and similar produce. The scan reads arroz rather than arraz; Coto- begins this year's assessment in the next line.
[c1-l021 >] xiua ſacumôga yoi. *Eſte anno ha boa no-*
[c1-l021 note] Kotoshi wa sakumoo ga yoi says that this year's harvest is good. The Portuguese translation again divides novidade at the physical line ending.
[c1-l022 >] *uidade de arroz, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] This completes the statement of a good rice harvest. Novidade here is agricultural yield, not news or novelty in the modern everyday sense.
[c1-l023] Sacumu. Qinôno yume. *Sonho de ontem,*
[c1-l023 note] Sakumu is explained as kinoo no yume, yesterday's dream. The Portuguese follows the Japanese temporal expression directly.
[c1-l024 >] *ou ſonho que paßou.*
[c1-l024 note] The alternative gloss calls it a dream that has passed. The printed ß-shaped sort is retained rather than silently expanded or modernized.
[c1-l025] Sacuna. *Obra, ou couſa feita com inuenção.*
[c1-l025 note] Sakuna describes work made with invention or artifice. The definition introduces the adjective illustrated by a sermon, a poem, and a person below.
[c1-l026 >] *Vt,* Sacuna dangui, ſacuna vta. *Prè-*
[c1-l026 note] Sakuna dangi and sakuna uta mean an artfully composed sermon and poem. The translation's Prè- is divided here; its printed accent is retained.
[c1-l027 >] *gação, ou verſo bem compoſto, ou de boa*
[c1-l027 note] This completes pregação and describes a well-composed sermon or verse. Boa modifies composição on the next row, so the line remains part of the same explanation.
[c1-l028 >] *compoſição, & artificio. ¶* Sacuna fito.
[c1-l028 note] The gloss concludes with composition and artifice, then introduces sakuna hito. The Japanese expression applies the same resourcefulness to a person.
[c1-l029 >] *Homem de boa traça, & inuenção. Item,*
[c1-l029 note] The person has good powers of planning and invention. Item introduces an additional description rather than a separate Japanese lemma.
[c1-l030 >] *Homem viuo, expedito, & reſoluto.*
[c1-l030 note] The additional description calls the person lively, capable, and resolute. These qualities complete sakuna hito and help distinguish it from the preceding material-work examples.
[c1-l031] Sacunin. Tçucuru fito. *Laurador.*
[c1-l031 note] Sakunin is explained through tsukuru hito and glossed as a cultivator. The Portuguese fixes the agricultural sense despite the broader literal idea of a maker.
[c1-l032] Sacuno mono. *Obra. ¶* Goſacuno mo-
[c1-l032 note] Saku no mono is a work or created thing. The honorific gosaku no mono begins a specifically Christian application and continues across the next physical row.
[c1-l033 >] no. *Obra, ou creatura de Deos. Palaura q̃*
[c1-l033 note] The divided Japanese mono is completed; the Portuguese identifies a work or creature of God. The abbreviated q̃ begins a usage statement continued below.
[c1-l034 >] *corre na Igreja.*
[c1-l034 note] This says the preceding expression is current in the Church. It qualifies the Christian use of gosaku no mono rather than asserting that every use of saku is religious.
[c1-l035] Sacuno qi. *Paos de tranqueira, ou cerca que*
[c1-l035 note] Saku no ki means the wooden stakes of a stockade. Here saku denotes a defensive fence, unlike the work or creation senses immediately above.
[c1-l036 >] *fazem ao derredor das fortalezas.*
[c1-l036 note] The stakes form an enclosure around fortresses. The line completes the defensive-fence definition and is not connected with the following cherry-tree vocabulary.
[c1-l037] Sacura. *Cereijeira.*
[c1-l037 note] Sakura is glossed as a cherry tree. The Portuguese historical spelling Cereijeira is retained without introducing a botanical species identification.
[c1-l038] Sacuradai. *Gorazes, ou pargos que tomão na*
[c1-l038 note] Sakuradai names fish compared with sea breams. The following timing clause, not a modern taxonomic identification, specifies which fish the dictionary has in view.
[c1-l039 >] *terceira lũa em Abril, ou Mayo.*
[c1-l039 note] The fish are caught in the third lunar month, described here as April or May. The lunar wording and the source's seasonal correspondence are preserved.
[c1-l040] Sacuragari. *P. O andar vendo as cereijei-*
[c1-l040 note] Sakuragari is going to view cherry trees in bloom. The locally italic P. is retained with the gloss; the supplied reference explains it as a poetic-usage marker.
[c1-l041 >] *ras floridas.*
[c1-l041 note] Floridas completes the description of flowering cherry trees. The visible l after f corrects the draft's foridas; the broken cereijei-ras remains divided between physical rows.
[c1-l042] Sacuragui. *Cereijeira, ou pao de cereijeira.*
[c1-l042 note] Sakuragi can mean the cherry tree or its wood. The gloss explicitly includes both the living tree and its material.
[c1-l043] Sacuran. *Perturbação, ou reuoltas de guerra.*
[c1-l043 note] Sakuran means disorder or disturbance, illustrated here by upheavals of war. It is unrelated in sense to the preceding sakura compounds.
[c1-l044] Sacurei. *Proua, ou teſtimunho dalgũa obra*
[c1-l044 note] Sakurei is precedent or attestation from an earlier work. The scan supports teſtimunho with h, not the draft's b, while the following lines specify the type of work.
[c1-l045 >] *feita por algum antiguo como compoſição de*
[c1-l045 note] The attested work was made by an earlier author, for example a poetic composition. The printed antiguo is retained rather than modernized to antigo.
[c1-l046 >] *verſos que chamão,* Renga, &c.
[c1-l046 note] Renga is the Japanese verse form named within the Portuguese explanation, so it is eligible for whole-word typeface review. The final abbreviation is retained as printed.
[c1-l047] Sacuri, v, utta. *Dar cõ a mão pera diante como*
[c1-l047 note] Sakuri, sakuru, sakutta describes a forward scooping motion with the hand. The printed v is preserved as a principal-form abbreviation; the explanation continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *quem bota terra, ou agoa. ¶* Mizzuuo
[c2-l001 note] The definition from column 1 continues with the motion of throwing earth or water. Mizu wo begins a Japanese example of scooping or sprinkling water by hand.
[c2-l002 >] ſacuri caquru. *Botar agoa com a mão com-*
[c2-l002 note] Sakuri kakuru completes the water-scooping example. The scan has initial long s, not capital I; the Portuguese ends in printed com-, whose expected comparative wording is not silently supplied.
[c2-l003 >] *quem agua a caſa. Itẽ,* Sacuri, u. *Ao*
[c2-l003 note] The example concerns sprinkling water in a house; a woodworking sense follows. Enlargement confirms the anomalous printed Ao before brir below; intended Abrir is not silently substituted.
[c2-l004 >] *brir pao, ou madeira com algum ferro dando*
[c2-l004 note] This describes opening or gouging wood with an iron tool. Com algum is visible in the scan: the draft misread com and omitted the entire word algum.
[c2-l005 >] *com elle pera diante.*
[c2-l005 note] The tool is thrust or worked forward. This completes the woodworking sense of sakuru, distinct from the preceding action of scooping water with the hand.
[c2-l006] Sacurigana. *Certo cepilho de laurar madeira.*
[c2-l006 note] Sakurigana is a particular woodworking plane. The Portuguese cepilho identifies the implement without specifying a modern tool subtype beyond the source.
[c2-l007] Sacuriô. *Iornal do carpinteiro. ¶* Sacu-
[c2-l007 note] Sakuryoo is a carpenter's wage. Iornal means pay for work here; the initial is I rather than the draft's F, and the example continues below.
[c2-l008 >] riôuo daſu. *Fazer paga aos carpinteiros.*
[c2-l008 note] Sakuryoo wo dasu means paying carpenters. The final form is daſu, supported by the surviving u and the payment gloss, rather than the draft's daſi.
[c2-l009] Sacurito. *Adu. Eſperta, viua, & expedi-*
[c2-l009 note] Sakurito describes acting alertly, briskly, and readily. The scan supports Eſperta rather than Eſperia; the final adverb is divided across the next line.
[c2-l010 >] *tamente. ¶* Sacurito xita fito. *Homem*
[c2-l010 note] This completes expeditamente and introduces sakurito shita hito, a brisk and capable person. The Portuguese noun Homem is completed by its adjectives below.
[c2-l011 >] *viuo, & expedito.*
[c2-l011 note] The man is lively and capable. This short row completes the example rather than beginning the next adverbial entry.
[c2-l012] Sacuſacuto. *Adu. Idem. Vt,* Sacuſa-
[c2-l012 note] Sakusakuto is given the same brisk, ready manner of action. The example repeats the adverb, divided after Sacuſa- at the physical margin.
[c2-l013 >] cuto monouo yǔ fitogia. *He homem que*
[c2-l013 note] Sakusakuto mono wo yuu hito ja describes a person who speaks readily and decisively. The Portuguese relative clause begins here and continues on the following row.
[c2-l014 >] *diz as couſas expedita, & reſolutamente.*
[c2-l014 note] The translation specifies speaking promptly and resolutely. It confirms the adverb's force in the preceding Japanese sentence rather than a description of the content spoken.
[c2-l015 >] *¶ Item, Modo de ſe partir, ou cortar com*
[c2-l015 note] A second sense introduces the manner of breaking or cutting something with the teeth. The instrument is completed on the next line.
[c2-l016 >] *os dentes algũa couſa teſa, & madura como*
[c2-l016 note] The object bitten is firm and ripe. This is the crisp-biting sense of sakusakuto, illustrated by fruit on the next physical row.
[c2-l017 >] *peras, ou figos de Iapão.*
[c2-l017 note] Pears and the fruit called Japanese figs illustrate the crisp texture. The historical descriptive name is preserved without forcing a modern species label into the transcription.
[c2-l018] Sacuxa. Tçucuru mono. *Official, ou homẽ*
[c2-l018 note] Sakusha is explained as tsukuru mono, a maker, and glossed as an artisan or a person who creates a work. Homẽ retains its abbreviation mark.
[c2-l019 >] *que faz algũa obra. ¶ Item, Poeta que faz*
[c2-l019 note] After the general maker sense, the entry introduces a poet skilled in composing a particular kind of verse. The verse names follow below.
[c2-l020 >] *bem hũs verſos que chamão,* Renga, l, ren-
[c2-l020 note] Renga and the divided rengu name the verses within the Portuguese explanation. The scan shows chamão with final o, omitted by the draft; the Japanese names are upright.
[c2-l021 >] gu. *Item, Homem que tem boa traça, ou*
[c2-l021 note] Gu completes rengu. The next sense is someone with good powers of design or invention, with its last noun continued on the following row.
[c2-l022 >] *inuenção no que faz. ¶* Goſacuxa. *Cria-*
[c2-l022 note] The general inventive-person gloss ends; gosakusha then introduces the honorific Christian term for the Creator. Cria- is physically divided from dor below.
[c2-l023 >] *dor: palaura que corre na Igreja.*
[c2-l023 note] This completes Criador and says that the term is used in the Church. The usage statement pertains to gosakusha, not every sense of sakusha.
[c2-l024] Sacuxei. *Obra que hum faz como verſos, &c.*
[c2-l024 note] Sakusei denotes a work one makes, such as verses. The initial of verſos is visibly v, correcting the draft's perſos.
[c2-l025] Sacuxeqi. Qinôno yǔbe. *Tarde de ontem.*
[c2-l025 note] Sakuseki is yesterday evening, explained as kinoo no yuube. The Portuguese tarde is read in conjunction with that explicit Japanese evening expression.
[c2-l026] Sacuxeqi. *Fruito, ou rendimento que fica ao*
[c2-l026 note] The second sakuseki entry concerns agricultural proceeds retained by the cultivator. Its meaning is distinct from the homophonous expression for yesterday evening.
[c2-l027 >] *laurador depois de pagar ao ſenhor o rendi-*
[c2-l027 note] The retained produce is what remains after the cultivator pays the lord. The divided rendimento continues on the next row.
[c2-l028 >] *mento de cada anno.*
[c2-l028 note] This completes the annual payment to the lord. The whole three-line definition concerns the cultivator's remaining yield after that obligation.
[c2-l029] Sacuya. *Ontem à noite.*
[c2-l029 note] Sakuya means last night. The concise gloss distinguishes it from the previous entry's earlier-evening expression without adding a modern temporal rule.
[c2-l030] Sadacana. i, Aqiracana. *Couſa clara, ou*
[c2-l030 note] Sadakana is explained by akirakana, clear or distinct. The scan reads clara, not the draft's elara, and the paired adjective follows below.
[c2-l031 >] *distincta.*
[c2-l031 note] Distincta completes the clarity definition. The supplied st spelling is retained; the joined type is not normalized through a general long-s rule.
[c2-l032] Sadacani. *Aduer. i*, Aqiracani. *Clara-*
[c2-l032 note] Sadakani is the adverb, equated with akirakani. The supplied italic apparatus Aduer. i is retained; the Japanese synonym is upright, and Clara- continues below.
[c2-l033 >] *mente. ¶* Sadacani miyezu. *Não apa-*
[c2-l033 note] Claramente is completed before the example sadakani miezu, not appearing or being seen clearly. The negative Portuguese example continues below.
[c2-l034 >] *recer clara, & diſtintamẽte.*
[c2-l034 note] The translation says not to appear clearly and distinctly. The marked e in diſtintamẽte abbreviates the final nasal material and is retained unexpanded.
[c2-l035] Sadamari, u, atta. *Determinarſe.*
[c2-l035 note] Sadamari gives the intransitive principal forms for becoming settled or determined. The reflexive Portuguese distinguishes it from the following transitive verb.
[c2-l036] Sadame, uru, eta. *Determinar, ou aßentar*
[c2-l036 note] Sadame gives the historical transitive forms for determining or settling something. The object of the Portuguese verbs is supplied on the next physical row.
[c2-l037 >] *algũa couſa.*
[c2-l037 note] Algũa couſa is the object of determining or settling. This short continuation completes the verb entry before the separate noun sadame.
[c2-l038] Sadame. *Determinação.*
[c2-l038 note] The noun sadame means a determination or decision. It is a separate entry even though its spelling matches the preceding verb's stem.
[c2-l039] Sadamete. *Aduer. Decerto, ou ſem duuida.*
[c2-l039 note] Sadamete is initially glossed as certainly or without doubt. The following qualification is essential to understanding the dictionary's account of ordinary usage.
[c2-l040 >] *poſto que o ſentido commum, & corrente he,*
[c2-l040 note] The concessive phrase says that the common, current sense differs from the preceding certainty gloss. It leads directly to the weaker interpretation below.
[c2-l041 >] *por ventura, ou prouauelmente.*
[c2-l041 note] The ordinary sense is perhaps or probably. The apparent tension with sem duuida is explicitly in the source and must not be harmonized away.
[c2-l042] Saden. *Hum dos cinco liuros que enſinão a-*
[c2-l042 note] Saden names a book the dictionary places among five books teaching government. The visible a- is retained at the line end without attempting to correct that historical classification.
[c2-l043 >] *gouernar.*
[c2-l043 note] Gouernar completes the statement about teaching how to govern. It is a short continuation of the book description, not a new headword.
[c2-l044] Sadǒ. i, Chajo. l, Chano yujo. *Lugar do*
[c2-l044 note] Sadoo is explained by chajo and cha no yujo, a place for tea. The alternative marker is lowercase l, not capital I; the Portuguese locates this place below.
[c2-l045 >] *Chanoyu na* Tera.
[c2-l045 note] The place is for chanoyu in a Buddhist temple. Both Chanoyu and Tera occur within the Portuguese explanation; the scan shows italic Chanoyu but upright Tera, eligible for whole-word typeface review.
[c2-l046] Safiô. *Vaſo em que metem o Cha moido.*
[c2-l046 note] Sahyoo is a container for powdered tea. Cha is a Japanese term within the Portuguese explanation, and the scan's typeface is retained rather than changed from language alone.
[c2-l047] Safô. i, Catagui, yǒdai. *Cuſtume, feição,*
[c2-l047 note] Sahoo is equated with katagi and yoodai and glossed as custom or manner. The scan restores the circumflex in Safô; the definition continues on f430, whose initial &c. is anticipated by the catchword.
