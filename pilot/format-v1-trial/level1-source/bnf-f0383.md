[c2-l001] Qentô. *Força, ou coração do Inuerno.*
[c2-l001 note] Kentou is the strength or heart of winter, its most intense part. The circumflex is retained as printed.

[c2-l003 note] Winter and snow complete the description of the cold. The final S. is the upright usage label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l004] Qenun. Me curumequ. i, Mega mǒ. *Doẽ-*
[c2-l004 note] Kenun is described through me kurumeku and me ga mou, dizziness or darkening vision. The Portuguese doença begins here with a nasal abbreviation and visible division.
[c2-l005 >] *ça de andar o miollo à roda, ou irſe o lume*
[c2-l005 note] The source describes the brain seeming to go round or the light departing from one's eyes. These are its descriptive glosses, not a modern diagnostic claim.
[c2-l006 >] *dos olhos.*
[c2-l006 note] 'Of the eyes' completes the phrase about losing the light of vision. The line finishes the dizziness entry.
[c2-l007] Qenvǒ. caxicoi vǒ. *Reyſabio.*
[c2-l007 note] Ken-ou is a wise king, kashikoi ou. The lowercase caxicoi and tightly set Reyſabio are retained, as are both printed carons.
[c2-l008] Qenxa. Caxicoi mono. *Homem prudente,*
[c2-l008 note] Kensha denotes a wise person, kashikoi mono. The Portuguese definition adds further qualities on the next row.
[c2-l009 >] *forte, ou constante, & abſoluto em ſuas acçoẽs.*
[c2-l009 note] The wise person is firm or constant and independent in actions. The nasal mark remains on the printed vowel in acçoẽs.
[c2-l010] Qenxa. *O que vai ver as rẽdas, ou terras pe-*
[c2-l010 note] A separate kensha is someone sent to inspect revenues or land. The initial Q is not a separate word, and pera is divided after pe-.
[c2-l011 >] *ra ſaber quanto tem.*

[c2-l012] Qenxei. *Fausto, & poder. S.*

[c2-l013] Qenxei. i, Xeijinno xei, qenjinno qen.
[c2-l013 note] This kensei is explained by the sei of seijin and the ken of kenjin. The source explicitly identifies the compound's elements; their stated order is not rearranged.
[c2-l014 >] *Prudencia de homem conſtante, & abſoluto,*
[c2-l014 note] The first kind of wisdom belongs to a constant, independent person. The following row contrasts a gentler, more accommodating kind.
[c2-l015 >] *& prudẽcia de homẽ brando q̃ cõtemporiza*
[c2-l015 note] The second kind belongs to a mild person who accommodates others. The scan reads cõtemporiza, with o after p rather than the OCR e; the nasal abbreviations are retained.
[c2-l016 >] *mais com os outros.*
[c2-l016 note] 'More with others' completes the description of accommodation. This finishes the contrast within the kensei definition.
[c2-l017] Qenxeqi. Xeme xemuru. i, Saiſocu. *Opri-*
[c2-l017 note] Kenseki is explained by seme semuru and saisoku, pressing or urging someone. The Portuguese oprimir begins with a visible line-end division.
[c2-l018 >] *mir, ou pòr em aperto, ou fazer força, como*
[c2-l018 note] Completes oprimir and gives alternatives of putting someone under pressure or applying force. The grave-like mark in pòr is preserved; the example follows below.
[c2-l019 >] *fazer pagar diuidas, &c.*
[c2-l019 note] Making someone pay debts illustrates the pressure or demand. The printed diuidas is preserved with historical u for consonantal v.
[c2-l020] Qenxi. Sono tocorono cotouo xiru tçucai.
[c2-l020 note] Kenshi is an emissary who ascertains a place's affairs. The scan has a complete roman Qenxi, correcting the OCR's italic O and separated enxi.

[c2-l021 note] The emissary is sent by a noble person to inspect a place. Algum is divided without a securely printed hyphen, so none is added.

[c2-l024] Qenxin. Caxicoi xinca. *Criado prudente. Vt,*

[c2-l031 >] *ou concerto feito dantes.*

[c2-l032] Qenyo. Fome cacaguru. *O louuar, & en-*

[c2-l033 >] *grandecer. Vt,* Qimino ichijenuo miru

[c2-l038] Qenyô. Inu, fitçuji. *Cão, & ouelha.*

[c2-l044] Qenzan. i, Temmocuno taguy. *Certa laya*
