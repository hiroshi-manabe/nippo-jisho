[c1-l001 >] *agoa não cabe em hum vaſo. ¶* Suiqua tǒ-
[c1-l001 note] The opening continues the preceding page's vessel comparison. The new example is explained below as torment by water, fire, and sword; its Japanese compound crosses the physical line break.
[c1-l002 >] xetno xeme. *Tormento de agoa, fogo, &*
[c1-l002 note] Xetno completes the compound begun with tǒ-; xeme denotes torment or punishment. The capital T in Tormento corrects OCR Formento.
[c1-l003 >] *eſpada.*
[c1-l003 note] Espada completes the list of means of torment, specifically the sword. This short continuation is not part of the watermelon entry below.
[c1-l004] Suiqua. *Pateca.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*real.*
[c1-l004 note] Suika is glossed as watermelon. The physically displaced real at the right completes the following entry's royal banner, not this fruit gloss; its opening parenthesis is retained separately.
[c1-l005] Suiqua. Tenvǒno fata. *Bandeira, ou guião*
[c1-l005 note] This distinct suikwa is identified by ten'oo no hata, a royal banner or standard. The gloss's real is printed at the preceding row's right edge; Tenvǒno retains its visible v.
[c1-l006] Suiquano chǒ. *Pauelhão de Rey.*
[c1-l006 note] Suikwa no choo denotes the king's pavilion or canopy. It continues the royal-regalia vocabulary rather than the earlier watermelon sense.
[c1-l007] Suiran. *Hũa erua como anil.*
[c1-l007 note] Suiran is described as a herb resembling indigo. The comparison alone does not warrant assigning a modern botanical species.
[c1-l008] Suiren. i, Cauadachi. *O nadar, ou mergu-*
[c1-l008 note] Suiren is equated with kawadachi and explained as swimming or diving well. The divided Portuguese mergu- continues on the next row.
[c1-l009 >] *lhar bem. ¶* Suiren uo yeta fito. *Ho-*
[c1-l009 note] Suiren wo eta hito describes a person proficient in swimming. The gloss first completes mergulhar bem, then begins homem across the next break.
[c1-l010 >] *mem destro em nadar, paßar o rio, &c.*
[c1-l010 note] The proficient person can swim and cross a river. Destro is the historical Portuguese word for skilled, not a Japanese form.
[c1-l011] Suiren. Midorino teguruma. *Carro, ou an-*
[c1-l011 note] A separate suiren is paraphrased as midori no teguruma, a green or ornamented carriage. The Portuguese offers carriage and litter as alternatives.
[c1-l012 >] *das bem ornadas.*
[c1-l012 note] Andas, completed from an-, means a litter or carrying chair; bem ornadas characterizes these conveyances as richly ornamented.
[c1-l013] Suiren. Midorino ſudare. *Eſteiras finas que*
[c1-l013 note] This suiren refers to fine hanging blinds, paraphrased as midori no sudare. The initial is S, not the draft's B.
[c1-l014 >] *ſe dependurão nas portas, ou varandas.*
[c1-l014 note] The blinds are hung in doorways or verandas. Dependurão retains the historical Portuguese spelling and completes the relative clause begun above.
[c1-l015] Suirin. Mizzuno va. *Roda, ou nora de ti-*
[c1-l015 note] Suirin, literally a water wheel in the supplied Japanese paraphrase, is a wheel or noria used to raise water.
[c1-l016 >] *rar agoa.*
[c1-l016 note] Rar completes tirar, so the definition specifies drawing or lifting water rather than merely a wheel located beside water.
[c1-l017] Suiriǒ. Voxifacaru. *Conjeituras. ¶* Sui-
[c1-l017 note] Suiryoo is inference or conjecture, explained by oshihakaru. The scan restores the damaged headword's initial S and ui sequence.
[c1-l018 >] riô ſuru. *Conjeiturar.*
[c1-l018 note] Suiryoo suru, divided after Sui-, means to conjecture. The example's faint vowel mark is retained as a circumflex, independently from the headword's caron; the exact worn mark remains a nonblocking uncertainty.
[c1-l019] Suirǒ. l, Rǒſui. Votoroye, voitari.
[c1-l019 note] Suiroo and roosui denote declining with age, paraphrased by otoroe and oitari. The intervening l is Latin vel, 'or', not Japanese.
[c1-l020 >] *Velhice, ou aparencia de velho ja gaſtado.*
[c1-l020 note] The Portuguese specifies old age or the appearance of an already worn-out old person, completing the preceding Japanese equivalents.
[c1-l021] Suirǒ. Mizzuno yagura. *Lugar alto, ou bai-*
[c1-l021 note] This separate suiroo is a tower or raised structure over water, as mizu no yagura indicates. Bai- begins the Portuguese architectural noun on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *leu feito ſobre agoa.*
[c1-l022 note] Baileu is completed across the line break and described as built over water. The scan reads agoa, not OCR atoa.
[c1-l023] Suiſan. Voxi, mairu. *Ir ſem ſer chamado,*
[c1-l023 note] Suisan means presenting oneself uninvited, paraphrased by oshi-mairu. The scan supports Suiſan rather than the draft's malformed Saiſani.
[c1-l024 >] *ou falar ſem ſer perguntado.*
[c1-l024 note] Speaking without having been asked is the second application of the same unsolicited or presumptuous action, not an unrelated definition.
[c1-l025] Suiſat. Voxi miru. *Conjeitura. Vt,* Sui-
[c1-l025 note] Suisat is inference or conjecture, explained by oshi-miru. The meaningful ſ/f confusion is resolved as ſ; the final t is a historical checked coda, not a missing syllable.
[c1-l026 >] ſat ſuru.
[c1-l026 note] This completes Suisat suru, to infer or conjecture. It is the continuation of the preceding example rather than a new headword.
[c1-l027] Suisǒ. Mizzu cuſa. *Erua dagoa*
[c1-l027 note] Suisoo means aquatic vegetation, literally mizu-kusa in the Japanese paraphrase. No final punctuation has been supplied to regularize the short gloss.
[c1-l028] Suiſon. Mizzuno ſon. *Dano, ou perda que*
[c1-l028 note] Suison is water damage or loss caused by water. The Japanese paraphrase uses son for damage, not a separate object associated with water.
[c1-l029 >] *faz a agoa.*
[c1-l029 note] The relative clause identifies water as the cause of the damage or loss. The subject follows the Portuguese verb.
[c1-l030] Suitai. Midorino mayuzumi. *Rosto, ou*
[c1-l030 note] Suitai is paraphrased through green-dark eyebrow pigment, while the Portuguese extends it to a beautiful face or countenance.
[c1-l031 >] *ſembrãte fermoſo. ¶* Suitai côgan fa-
[c1-l031 note] The gloss completes the beautiful-countenance sense. The example pairs suitai with koogan, a rosy face, before a comparison with flowers.
[c1-l032 >] na yorimo comayacanari. *Roſto mais bel-*
[c1-l032 note] Hana, divided after fa-, supplies the flowers in the comparison; komayaka nari characterizes the rich or delicate beauty. The Portuguese starts 'a face more beautiful'.
[c1-l033 >] *lo, & còrado que as roſas.*
[c1-l033 note] Bello is completed across the break, and the face is said to be rosier than roses. The printed Portuguese accent is retained.
[c1-l034] Suitai. Midorino coqe. *Muſgo verde.*
[c1-l034 note] This homonymous suitai means green moss, explicitly midori no koke, and is distinct from the preceding beauty vocabulary.
[c1-l035] Suitei. Mizzuno ſoco. *Fundo dagoa.*
[c1-l035 note] Suitei is the bottom of a body of water; mizu no soko and fundo dagoa give corresponding Japanese and Portuguese explanations.
[c1-l036] Suiteqi. Fuqu fuye. *O tanger a frauta.*
[c1-l036 note] Suiteki denotes playing the flute, explained through fuku fue. Frauta is preserved as the historical Portuguese spelling.
[c1-l037] Suitô. Mizzuno fotori. *Borda dagoa.*
[c1-l037 note] Suitoo denotes the water's edge, paraphrased as mizu no hotori. The circumflex is preserved rather than assimilated to neighboring caroned headwords.
[c1-l038] Suiton. *Certa iguaria.*
[c1-l038 note] Suiton is identified only as a particular dish. The brief historical gloss is not expanded into a modern recipe.
[c1-l039] Suiuon. Mizzuno voto. *Soido dagoa.*
[c1-l039 note] Suion means the sound of water, as mizu no oto confirms. The source's voto and Portuguese soido remain diplomatic spellings.
[c1-l040] Suixacu. i, Atouo taruru. *Estar o Cami, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Suishaku is explained by ato wo taruru, with the Portuguese describing a kami or Buddha acting in another's place. Cami occurs inside the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l041 >] *Fotoque em lugar de outro, ou com differente*
[c1-l041 note] Fotoque is the Japanese religious term hotoke embedded in the Portuguese sentence. The definition concerns substitution or manifestation under another identity.
[c1-l042 >] *nome fazendo officio do primeiro comoguardã-*
[c1-l042 note] The manifestation bears a different name while performing the original figure's office. Guardã- begins guardando, the act of protecting the place.
[c1-l043 >] *do aquelle lugar.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes guardando aquelle lugar, guarding that place. This belongs to the religious definition rather than the following water-purity entry.
[c1-l044] Suixei. l, *Potiùs,* Xeiſui. *Agoa pura, & limpa.*
[c1-l044 note] The entry prefers seisui to suisei for pure, clean water. Potiùs is Latin 'rather' and is wholly italic, unlike the surrounding Japanese forms.
[c1-l045] Suixen. l, *Potiùs,* Xenſui. Mizzuno izzumi.
[c1-l045 note] Sensui is preferred to suisen and paraphrased as mizu no izumi, a water spring. The OCR's fragmented Porùs is corrected to the printed Potiùs.
[c1-l046 >] *Fonte dagoa.*
[c1-l046 note] The Portuguese identifies a spring of water, completing the preceding entry and distinguishing it from the food name that follows.
[c1-l047] Suixẽ. *Certa iguaria feita da raiz de hũa erua.*
[c1-l047 note] This suisen is a dish made from a herb's root. The headword has an abbreviation mark over e; the scan restores S and iguaria rather than OCR Fuixe and iguria.

[c2-l001] Suixenqua. *Flor, ou roſa aßi chamada.*
[c2-l001 note] Suisenkwa is identified as a flower of that name. The initial of Flor is F, not OCR H; rosa here need not imply a modern rose species.
[c2-l002] Suixǒ. Mizzuno vye. *Sobre agoa.*
[c2-l002 note] Suishoo denotes being on or above water, explicitly mizu no ue. The printed vye is retained as the historical spelling.
[c2-l003] Suixǒ. *Cristal, ou vidro.*
[c2-l003 note] A distinct suishoo means crystal or glass. Its identical romanized shape does not make it a continuation of the preceding spatial expression.
[c2-l004] Suixocu. Mizzuno iro. *Cor dagoa.*
[c2-l004 note] Suishoku is the color of water, paraphrased as mizu no iro. The next entry supplies a different homonymous color expression.
[c2-l005] Suixocu. Midorino iro. *Cor verde.* S.
[c2-l005 note] This suishoku denotes green, as midori no iro specifies. The final upright S is the dictionary's written-language label, not part of the italic gloss.
[c2-l006] Suixu. *Hydropeſia.*
[c2-l006 note] Suishu is glossed with the historical Portuguese term for dropsy. The scan supports Hydropeſia, correcting the draft's Hodropeſia without modernizing the spelling.
[c2-l007] Suixu. *Marinheiros, ou homẽs do mar.*
[c2-l007 note] This suishu means sailors or seamen. It is a separate homonym, not a continuation of the preceding illness term.
[c2-l008] Suiyǒfi. *Certa flor, ou bonina do Outono.*
[c2-l008 note] Suiyoohi is described as a particular autumn flower. The gloss does not provide enough botanical detail to identify a species securely.
[c2-l009] Sujit. Cazu, fi. *Muitos dias.*
[c2-l009 note] Sujit means many days; kazu and hi explain its components. The final t is preserved as the historical checked ending.
[c2-l010] Sujǔnen. *Muitas dezenas de annos tè cento.*
[c2-l010 note] Sujuunen means several tens of years, described as extending up to a hundred. Annos has two n types, not the OCR's nx.
[c2-l011] Sujǔnichi. *Muitas dezenas de dias tè cento.*
[c2-l011 note] Sujuunichi gives the corresponding quantity in days. The extra i in the draft's Sujǔinichi is not present in the scan.
[c2-l012] Sujǔnin. *Muitas dezenas de homẽs tè cento.*
[c2-l012 note] Sujuunin counts several tens of people. Its damaged headword is supported by the visible ending and the parallel years and days entries immediately above.
[c2-l013] Sumai. *O morar. Vt,* Sumai ſuru. *¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l013 note] Sumai first means dwelling or residing, illustrated by sumai suru. The italic item marker introduces the second, architectural sense below.
[c2-l014 >] *Maneira de edificar, ou traçarcaſas, &c.*
[c2-l014 note] The second sense concerns the manner of building or laying out houses. It explains the following example of a well-designed residence.
[c2-l015 >] *¶* Sumaino yoi iye. *Caſa bem traçada.*
[c2-l015 note] Sumai no yoi ie means a house with a good arrangement or design, directly matched by casa bem traçada.
[c2-l016] Sumai, ǒ, ǒta. *Estar a caſa bem, ou mal lã-*
[c2-l016 note] This verb describes a house being well or badly laid out. The principal parts and their vowel marks are preserved as printed.
[c2-l017 >] *çada, ou traçada. ¶* Yǒ ſumǒta iye. *Ca-*
[c2-l017 note] Yo(o) sumoota ie illustrates a well-designed house. The example's marked o is visible despite abrasion; ca- begins the repeated Portuguese casa.
[c2-l018 >] *ſa bem traçada.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes casa bem traçada, translating the example rather than introducing another headword.
[c2-l019] Sumai, ǒ, ǒta. *Abolinar. ¶* Sumaini,
[c2-l019 note] This homonymous verb concerns sailing close to the wind. Abolinar is supported by the printed rounded vowel and the next line's bolina phrase.
[c2-l020 >] l, ſumǒte faxiru. *Nauegar polla bolina.*
[c2-l020 note] Sumaini and sumoote hashiru are alternatives for sailing on a bowline or close-hauled. The initial l is the alternative marker.
[c2-l021] Suman. *Muitos milhares. Vt,* Sumannin.
[c2-l021 note] Suman denotes many tens of thousands; sumannin applies that quantity to people. The Portuguese initially says many thousands and specifies the counting unit below.
[c2-l022 >] *Muitos milhares de homes contando de dez mil*
[c2-l022 note] The count is explicitly made in units of ten thousand people. The unexpanded homes is retained rather than supplied with an assumed nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l023 >] *por diante.*
[c2-l023 note] Por diante completes 'from ten thousand onward'. The initial d is visible, correcting the OCR's ciante.
[c2-l024] Sumaxi, ſu. *Aclarar. Vt,* Mizzuuo ſu-
[c2-l024 note] Sumashi, sumasu is transitive clearing or clarifying. The example begins mizu wo sumasu, making water clear; ſu corrects the meaningful f/ſ confusion.
[c2-l025 >] maſu. *Aclarar a agoa. ¶ Per met.* Riuo
[c2-l025 note] Masu completes the water-clearing example. The figurative application then begins ri wo, introducing the clarification of a reason or principle.
[c2-l026 >] ſumaſu. *Manifestar, & aclarar a razão.*
[c2-l026 note] Ri wo sumasu means making the reason clear or manifest. The scan supports ſumaſu and razão, not the OCR's ſumaru and vazão.
[c2-l027 >] *¶* Cocorouo ſumaſu. *Purificar, & pa-*
[c2-l027 note] Kokoro wo sumasu is purifying and calming the heart. The printed paragraph mark and initial Co restore material damaged in the draft.
[c2-l028 >] *cificar o coração.* Cujiuo ſumaſu. *A-*
[c2-l028 note] After completing pacificar, kuji wo sumasu introduces settling or adjudicating a lawsuit. A- begins the Portuguese verb on the next row.
[c2-l029 >] *ueriguar a demanda. ¶ Este verbo junto à*
[c2-l029 note] Aueriguar completes the lawsuit gloss; the e is heavily abraded but its position and the surviving word support the reading. The next explanation concerns attaching this verb to another verb's stem.
[c2-l030 >] *raiz de outros ſignifica acabarſe, ou fazerſe bẽ*
[c2-l030 note] As a second verb after another stem, sumasu conveys completion or doing something thoroughly and well. The tilde in bẽ is not expanded.
[c2-l031 >] *a couſa. Vt,* Q iqiſumaſu. *Ouuir bem,*
[c2-l031 note] Kikisumasu illustrates attentive or thorough listening. The visible gap after capital Q is retained in Q iqiſumaſu; bem corrects the draft's hem.
[c2-l032 >] *& inteiramente. ¶ Item, Pagar diuidas,*
[c2-l032 note] Inteiramente completes the listening gloss. The next sense is paying debts, with a visible plural ending in diuidas.
[c2-l033 >] *&c. Vt,* Voimonouo ſumaſu. *Item,*
[c2-l033 note] Oimono wo sumasu illustrates settling a debt. The vowel after V is abraded; Voimonouo is preferred to OCR Vuimonouo from its remaining rounded outline and the debt context.
[c2-l034 >] *Lauar, palaura de molheres. Vt,* Camiuo
[c2-l034 note] The dictionary next records washing as women's vocabulary. Kami wo begins the example concerning washing the head or hair; Vt is italic apparatus.
[c2-l035 >] ſumaſu. *Lauar a cabeça, ou os cabellos.*
[c2-l035 note] Kami wo sumasu completes the example and is translated as washing the head or hair. It is not the homonymous paper-making use found later.
[c2-l036] Sume, uru, eta. *Concluir, ou aueriguar. Vt,*
[c2-l036 note] Sume, sumuru, sumeta is given as concluding or settling a matter. The scan ends in the example marker Vt with punctuation, not a hyphen.
[c2-l037 >] Sanyôuo ſumuru. *Concluir as contas pagã-*
[c2-l037 note] Sanyoo wo sumuru means settling the accounts. The Portuguese specifies payment, with pagã- continued by do on the next row.
[c2-l038 >] *do tudo, &c. ¶* Riuo ſumuru. *Acla-*
[c2-l038 note] The accounts are settled by paying everything. Ri wo sumuru then supplies the figurative example of clarifying a reason or principle.
[c2-l039 >] *rar, ou manifestar a razão.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*xo dagoa.*
[c2-l039 note] Rar completes aclarar, to clarify. At the far right, xo dagoa belongs to the next entry's divided baixo dagoa, not to the reason gloss; its physical displacement is preserved.
[c2-l040] Sumi, u, unda. *Mergulhar, ou nadar de bai-*
[c2-l040 note] This sumu means diving or swimming underwater. Bai- continues in the displaced xo dagoa printed at the preceding row's right edge; OCR bal- confused i with l.
[c2-l041] Sumi, u, unda. *Aclàrarſe a agoa. Item,*
[c2-l041 note] This distinct sumu is intransitive: water becomes clear. The following continuation extends the verb to completion or settlement.
[c2-l042 >] *Concluirſe, ou acabarſe. ¶ Itẽ, Per met,* Ri-
[c2-l042 note] After 'be concluded or finished', the figurative example begins ri. The Japanese Ri- and its terminal hyphen are roman, separate from the italic apparatus.
[c2-l043 >] ga ſunda. *Eſta claro. ¶* Rino ſuma-
[c2-l043 note] Ri ga sunda is explained as 'it is clear'. Ri no sumanu then begins a contrasting negative example, divided after suma-.
[c2-l044 >] nu cotogia. *He couſa deſarrezoada.*
[c2-l044 note] Ri no sumanu koto ja describes something unreasonable or not justified. The negative nu completes the verb from the preceding row.
[c2-l045] Sumi, u, unda. *Morar, ou habitar.*
[c2-l045 note] Another sumu means dwelling or inhabiting. It is a separate homonym from clearing and underwater swimming.
[c2-l046] Sumi. *Tinta. ¶* Sumiuo ſuru. *Por tinta, ou*
[c2-l046 note] Sumi is ink. The example here concerns a carpenter applying an ink mark, as the next row specifies, rather than the later ink-grinding example.
[c2-l047 >] *ſinal o carpinteiro no pao, ou madeira. ¶* Su-
[c2-l047 note] The carpenter marks a piece of wood. The final Su- begins Sumiuo ſuri on the next page; the catchword miuo repeats the anticipated continuation and is not running text.
