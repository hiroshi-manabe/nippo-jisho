[c1-l002] Ino co. i, Inoxixinoco. *Bacorinho filho de*
[c1-l002 note] Ino ko is explained as inoshishi no ko, a young wild boar. The Portuguese begins 'a piglet, offspring of'; the damaged, closely set letters in filho replace the OCR's fuho.
[c1-l003 >] *porco montes.*
[c1-l003 note] Porco montes completes the identification as a wild-boar piglet, rather than a domestic pig. The short continuation is a complete physical row.
[c1-l004] Ino cogumo. i, Inocono gotocu madarana cu
[c1-l004 note] Ino kogumo is explained by clouds mottled like a young boar. The Japanese explanation continues with cu plus mouo on the next row; no line-end hyphen is supplied where none is visible.
[c1-l005 >] mouo yû. *Nuuens que aparecem car rega-*
[c1-l005 note] Completes kumo wo yuu, 'clouds so called'; the Portuguese describes clouds appearing laden. The narrow internal division in car rega- is retained provisionally rather than silently regularized to modern carregadas.
[c1-l006 >] *das & de varias cores, asquaes ſaõ ſinal de grã*
[c1-l006 note] Das completes the preceding Portuguese participle. The clouds have various colours and are said to indicate a great wind or storm; the printed abbreviation grã continues into de on the next row.
[c1-l007 >] *de vento, ou tempeſtade, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes grande vento, with tempestade offering the alternative 'storm'. This closes the weather explanation for the cloud name.
[c1-l008] Inococu. *Horas cõforme à conta de Iapaõ deſ-*
[c1-l008 note] Ino koku is the boar-hour in the Japanese reckoning described by the dictionary. The gloss's deſ- continues with das below; the actual numerical interval follows on the next line.
[c1-l009 >] *das 10. tè as 12. da noite.*
[c1-l009 note] The dictionary gives the interval as ten at night to twelve. The final number is visibly 12, not the OCR's Iz; the source's time convention is retained without modern adjustment.
[c1-l010] Inocoxi, ſu, ita. *Naõ atirar com todas as ſe*
[c1-l010 note] Inokoshi, inokosu, inokoshita describes leaving some arrows unshot. The Portuguese says not to shoot with all the arrows; atirar replaces OCR atirur, and ſe continues into tas without a printed hyphen.
[c1-l011 >] *tas.*
[c1-l011 note] Tas completes setas, 'arrows', and therefore the negative definition of leaving arrows unused. It is not a separate entry.
[c1-l012] Inocozzuchi. *Hũa erua aßi chamada.*
[c1-l012 note] Inokozuchi names a herb. The gloss only says 'a herb so called', so no botanical identification is added to the transcription.
[c1-l013] Inomoto. *Poço dagua.*
[c1-l013 note] Inomoto is glossed as a water well. The contracted historical spelling dagua is preserved, not expanded into modern d'água or de água.
[c1-l014] Inoqe, uru. *Afaſtar às frechadas.*
[c1-l014 note] Inoke means driving something away by shooting arrows. The Portuguese verb is Afaſtar, with a t in the joined st group, rather than the OCR's Afaſſar.
[c1-l015] Inoqi. *Preſa, dente de porco montes.*
[c1-l015 note] Inoki is the tusk or tooth of a wild boar. Preſa here means a tusk, as the following explanation makes clear, not prey or a captive.
[c1-l016] Inori. *Deprecações gentilicas.*
[c1-l016 note] Inori denotes prayers or supplications; gentilicas classifies them as non-Christian in the compilers' terminology. The note explains the historical gloss without adopting its religious evaluation.
[c1-l017] Inori, u, otta. *Fazer deprecações. ¶* Fo-
[c1-l017 note] The verb inoru is glossed as making supplications. Its principal parts include otta; the divided Fo- begins the following example addressed to a Buddha.
[c1-l018 >] toqeni. l, Fotoqeuo inoru.
[c1-l018 note] Completes Fotoqeni, with Fotoqeuo inoru as the alternative construction: praying to or invoking a Buddha. The Latin l introduces the alternative and is not part of the Japanese wording.
[c1-l019] Inoricoroxi, ſu, oita. *Matar com deprecaçõ-*
[c1-l019 note] Inorikoroshi means killing by imprecation or ritual prayer. The faint type between o and ta supports oita, the historical principal-part ending, rather than leaving the OCR gap o ta; the gloss continues below. The supplied NINJAL entry 132a11 independently supports the identification; the i remains weak in this impression.
[c1-l020 >] *es, ou feitiços.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes deprecações and adds 'or spells'. Together these describe the means of killing in the preceding headword, not an ordinary physical attack.
[c1-l021] Inorinoqe, uru, eta. *Vt,* Tenguuo ino-
[c1-l021 note] Inorinoke is illustrated by expelling a tengu through prayer. The headword and divided example mutually clarify the weak middle letters; Vt is the italic example marker.
[c1-l022 >] rinoquru. *Botar fora com deprecações ao de*
[c1-l022 note] Rinoquru completes inorinoquru, to drive away through prayer; the weak n is read in conjunction with the repeated headword. The Portuguese begins expelling the demon by supplications, with demonio divided after de. The damaged right shoulder of n remains a nonblocking reading uncertainty.
[c1-l023 >] *monio.*
[c1-l023 note] Monio completes demonio, the Portuguese designation used here for the tengu. This short line closes the exorcism example.
[c1-l024] Inoritçuqe, uru, eta. *Fazer com feitiços, &*
[c1-l024 note] Inoritsuke is the contrasting operation: causing a spirit to enter someone through spells and supplications. The Portuguese explanation continues on the next two rows.
[c1-l025 >] *deprecações entrar em alguem o demonio, ra-*
[c1-l025 note] The objects made to enter a person are the demon and a fox; ra- continues into raposa. This is the dictionary's account of spirit possession, not a new example of expulsion.
[c1-l026 >] *poſa, &c.*
[c1-l026 note] Poſa completes raposa, 'fox', and the list of spirits or agents of possession. The continuation belongs to inoritsuke.
[c1-l027] Inpon. i, Surifon. *Liuro impreßo.* S.
[c1-l027 note] Inpon is equated with surihon, a printed book. The initial I is not preceded by another l; the final upright S is the dictionary's written-usage label.
[c1-l028] Inqet. Vruuô chi. *Semente humana.*
[c1-l028 note] Inket is explained by a phrase involving moist blood and glossed as human seed or semen. The final t belongs to the historical Sino-Japanese spelling and is not expanded; the visible circumflex in Vruuô is retained.
[c1-l029] Inqi. *Abafamento. Vt,* Inqi xita toco-
[c1-l029 note] Inki first denotes stuffiness or oppression. Inqi xita, not the OCR's Iuqi xita, begins an example of a confined, airless place; the noun tocoro is divided at the line end.
[c1-l030 >] ro. *Lugar apertado, & abafado. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l030 note] Ro completes tocoro; the Portuguese says 'a cramped and stuffy place'. Itẽ introduces a second, emotional sense of the same word.
[c1-l031 >] Inqi xita. l, qiga inqi xita. *Tenho o co-*
[c1-l031 note] The paired examples describe feeling oppressed or heavy in spirit. The Portuguese begins 'I have my heart'; co- continues into coração below.
[c1-l032 >] *ração apertado, & abafado.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes coração apertado e abafado, a constricted or oppressed heart. This is the emotional counterpart to the airless-place example above.
[c1-l033] Inqiu. *Certa laya de peça com lauores douro.*
[c1-l033 note] This is a gold-ornamented fabric. The final printed type has a u-shaped bottom and is retained as Inqiu; the next entry clearly reads Inqin. The subsequently consulted NINJAL index (132a17) identifies this entry too as Inqin, but the possible damaged or inverted n is not silently substituted for the local u-shaped impression.
[c1-l034] Inqin. *Certa peça de ſeda dourada da China.*
[c1-l034 note] Inqin is specified as a gold-decorated silk fabric from China. The scan has initial capital I and capital C in China, unlike the draft's lowercase forms.
[c1-l035] Inqina. *Lugar abafado, ou coração abafado,*
[c1-l035 note] The adjectival form inqina applies either to an airless place or to an oppressed heart. It resumes the two senses of Inqi, not the intervening gold-fabric entries.
[c1-l036 >] *& apertado.*
[c1-l036 note] 'E apertado' completes the characterization as confined or constricted. Both the physical place and the figurative heart remain possible referents.
[c1-l037 >] Inqini. *Adu.*
[c1-l037 note] Inqini is the corresponding adverb, explicitly labelled Adu. Its indentation is visible and retained; the lack of a separate Portuguese paraphrase does not make this a missing line.
[c1-l038] Inqio. Cacure iru. *O entregar a herança ao*
[c1-l038 note] Inkyo is explained by withdrawing from public life. The Portuguese starts with handing the inheritance to a son; herança has h, not OCR b, and the recipient continues below.
[c1-l039 >] *filho, ou renunciar o eſtado, ou renda, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes the transfer to the son, or renunciation of one's position or income. The clearly comparable filho also helps resolve the closely printed letters in line c1-l002.
[c1-l040 >] *& recolherſe como fazem em Iapão. Vt,* In-
[c1-l040 note] The gloss continues with retiring or secluding oneself, described as a Japanese practice. In- starts the example inqio suru; the local tilde in Iapão remains on a.
[c1-l041 >] qio ſuru. *Idem.*
[c1-l041 note] Completes inqio suru, 'to retire', with Idem referring back to the preceding explanation. The example remains divided according to the printed lineation.
[c1-l042] Inracu. *Deleitação torpe, ou goſtos carnaes.*
[c1-l042 note] Inraku denotes sensual pleasure, evaluated negatively by the Portuguese gloss. The opening reads Inracu, not OCR ſuracu, and carnaes has the expected r-n sequence rather than cornaes.
[c1-l043] Inran. Tanoximi midaru. *Senſualidade.*
[c1-l043 note] Inran is paraphrased in Japanese as disorderly indulgence and glossed as sensuality. This separate headword supplies the basis for the prohibition in the next line.
[c1-l044 >] *Vt,* Inrãuo cototo ſurucoto nacare. *Não*
[c1-l044 note] The example prohibits making sensual indulgence one's occupation. The visible tilde in Inrãuo abbreviates the final nasal before uo; the Portuguese negative Não governs the following line.
[c1-l045 >] *vos deis aos goſtos da carne.*
[c1-l045 note] Completes 'do not give yourselves over to the pleasures of the flesh'. This translates the preceding negative injunction rather than introducing another sense.
[c1-l046] Inrô. *Bocetinhas onde metem mezinhas, ou ou-*
[c1-l046 note] Inro are small cases used to hold medicines or other things. The headword begins with a capital I, and the circumflex over o is visible; ou- starts outras on the next row.
[c1-l047 >] *tras couſas, que tem huns repartimentos encaxa-*
[c1-l047 note] Completes outras couſas and describes nested compartments in the cases. The ending of tras is s, not OCR r; encaxa- continues into dos below.
[c1-l048 >] *dos hum no outro.*
[c1-l048 note] Completes encaxados hum no outro, compartments fitted one into another. This is the last body row of the column, not a catchword.

[c2-l022] Inubaxiri. *Certo caminho muito estreito por on-*
[c2-l022 note] Inubashiri denotes a very narrow passage. The divided onde introduces the description of movement along a fortification on the next row.

[c2-l039] Inxei, l, inxǒ. Coye. *Vozes.*

[c2-l043 >] *municarſe com alguem por via de amizade cõ*

[c2-l044 >] *recados, &c.*

[c2-l045] Inxo. *Carta polla qual ſe viſita a alguem. Vt,*
[c2-l045 note] Insho denotes a letter used to pay one's respects or make a visit in writing. The italic Vt introduces the acknowledgement formula below.

[c2-l047 note] Completes the acknowledgement: the writer thanks the correspondent for the letter and is pleased with it. The scan restores viſitaſtes and agradeço from garbled OCR, and shows a real word space in com ella; the final S is upright.
