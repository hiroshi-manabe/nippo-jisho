[c1-l001] Nicqei. *Canela fina.*
[c1-l001 note] Nikkei is defined as fine cinnamon. This opening headword matches the preceding page's catchword Nicqei, which is not repeated as extra running text there.
[c1-l002] Nicqi. *Rol, ou papel de apontamentos que ſe*
[c1-l002 note] Nikki is a daily record or paper of memoranda. The scan's c before q corrects OCR Nitqi; the relative clause continues on the next row.
[c1-l003 >] *fazem cada dia.*
[c1-l003 note] The notes are made every day, completing the diary or daily-record definition. This short row belongs to Nicqi.
[c1-l004] Nicquǒ. Fino ficari. *Claridade do ſol.*
[c1-l004 note] Nikkoo is explained by hi no hikari, sunlight. The scan's quǒ sequence and downward o mark are retained without replacing them with a normalized modern form.
[c1-l005] Nicu. Xiximura. *Carne.*
[c1-l005 note] Niku is equated with shishimura and glossed as flesh or meat. Xiximura is a Japanese synonym, not a Japanese word embedded within a Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l006] Nicu. *Certo animal como lontra.*
[c1-l006 note] This separate niku names an animal compared with an otter. The comparison is retained without assigning an unsupported modern species.
[c1-l007] Nicuguen. *Olhos de carne, ou exteriores.*
[c1-l007 note] Nikugen denotes fleshly or external eyes. The Portuguese distinguishes ordinary bodily sight rather than merely repeating the meat sense of niku.
[c1-l008] Nicui. *Couſa odioſa, ou aborreciuel: junto eſte*
[c1-l008 note] Nikui first means hateful or detestable. The following explanation introduces its grammatical use after verb stems.
[c1-l009 >] *adiectiuo às raizes de muitos verbos faz ſer a*
[c1-l009 note] The adjective can attach to the roots of many verbs. The Portuguese proceeds to explain the resulting sense of difficulty rather than hatred.
[c1-l010 >] *tal acção difficultoſa. Vt,* Xinicui. *Couſa dif-*
[c1-l010 note] Such a combination makes the action difficult; shinikui is the example, something difficult to do. The divided Portuguese adjective continues below.
[c1-l011 >] *ficultoſa deſe fazer, &c.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes difficultoſa deſe fazer, difficult to do. The vowel after c is u, not the OCR's a, while the close-set deſe remains unregularized.
[c1-l012 >] Nicǔ. Nicuſa.
[c1-l012 note] Nikuu and nikusa are the adverbial and nominal forms associated with nikui. They are listed without separate translations.
[c1-l013] Nicujiqi. *O comer couſa de carne. ¶* Nicu-
[c1-l013 note] Nikujiki is eating meat. The example begins at the right edge and continues the same compound on the next physical row.
[c1-l014 >] jiqiuo yamuru. *Deixar de comer carne.*
[c1-l014 note] Nikujiki wo yamuru means ceasing to eat meat. The Portuguese directly confirms that this is giving up meat-eating.
[c1-l015] Nicumi, u, ûda. *Aborrecer, ou ter odio.*
[c1-l015 note] Nikumi, nikumu and its historical past form mean hating or detesting. The printed mark over the past ending's u points upward and is transcribed û, not the draft's grave; the principal part is not modernized to -nda.
[c1-l016] Nicumi. *Aborrecimento, ou odio.*
[c1-l016 note] Nikumi here is the noun hatred or aversion. It is a separate nominal entry despite sharing its spelling with the preceding verb stem.
[c1-l017] Nicuminji, zuru, ita. *Aborrecer, ou ter o-*
[c1-l017 note] Nikuminji and its principal forms give another verb for hating. The final Portuguese o- continues as odio below.
[c1-l018 >] *dio. S.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes odio, hatred. The upright S. marks written usage and is not part of the italic gloss. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l019] Nicunicuto. *Adu. Aborreciuelmente, ou*
[c1-l019 note] Nikunikuto is an adverb expressing hateful or hostile manner. The alternative Portuguese paraphrase follows on the next row.
[c1-l020 >] *com odio.*
[c1-l020 note] Com odio, with hatred, completes the adverbial definition. It is not a new headword or an omitted longer sentence.
[c1-l021] Nicuſabi. *Obras mortas da embarcação.*
[c1-l021 note] Nikusabi is explained by the nautical expression obras mortas, the vessel's upperworks. The gloss concerns ship structure, not literally dead objects.
[c1-l022] Nicuſaxi, ſu, aita. *Meliùs,* Nicutaſu. *Co-*
[c1-l022 note] Nikusashi is cross-related to the preferred form nikutasu by Meliùs. The gloss begins the sense of overcooking; the scan has a comma after Meliùs, whose grave accent is preserved.
[c1-l023 >] *zer a couſa atè ſe danar.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes cozer a cousa até se danar: cooking something until it is spoiled. The verb does not mean ordinary cooking without this excessive result.
[c1-l024] Nicutai. *Corpo.*
[c1-l024 note] Nikutai means the body. The initial is N, not OCR X, and the short Portuguese gloss is complete on this row.
[c1-l025] Nicutaxi, ſu, aita. *Vide,* Nicuſaxi, ſu.
[c1-l025 note] Nikutashi refers back to nikusashi for the overcooking sense. Vide is the italic editorial cross-reference, while the Japanese forms remain upright.
[c1-l026] Nicutei. *Couſa que cauſa aborrecimento.*
[c1-l026 note] Nikutei denotes something that causes annoyance or aversion. The example below applies this to offensive things said by a person.
[c1-l027 >] *¶* Nicuteiuo yǔ fito. *Homem que diz*
[c1-l027 note] Nikutei wo yuu hito is a person who says objectionable things. The relative clause of the Portuguese translation continues below.
[c1-l028 >] *couſas que cauſão aborrecimento, ou molestia.*
[c1-l028 note] The things said cause aversion or annoyance. The scan supports couſas rather than OCR coaſas; the printed historical molestia is not modernized or accented editorially.
[c1-l029] Nicuteina. *Couſa aborreciuel.*
[c1-l029 note] Nikuteina is the adjectival form, describing something objectionable or detestable. It follows the noun and its example as a separate entry.
[c1-l030] Nicuteini. *Adu.*
[c1-l030 note] Nikuteini is the associated adverbial form. Adu. classifies it without repeating the preceding definition.
[c1-l031] Nicuxin. *Odio, ou aborrecimento.*
[c1-l031 note] Nikushin here denotes hatred or aversion. The following entry has the same spelling but a different meaning.
[c1-l032] Nicuxin. *Corpo. S.*
[c1-l032 note] The second nikushin means the body and is marked for written usage. The scan reads Corpo, not OCR Corço, so the entry is not an animal name; S. is upright. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l033] Nicuxiqi. *Cor do corpo.*
[c1-l033 note] Nikushiki denotes the body's colour or complexion. The brief Portuguese phrase differentiates it from the preceding word for the body itself.
[c1-l034] Nicuzzucu. *Noz noſcada que ſerue de me-*
[c1-l034 note] Nikuzuku is nutmeg, here described for medicinal use. The doubled z is retained, and me- begins mezinha across the physical line break.
[c1-l035 >] *zinha.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes mezinha, a medicine or remedy. The short row closes the nutmeg entry.
[c1-l036] Nidaxi, ſu, ita. *Cozer algũa couſa como comba-*
[c1-l036 note] Nidashi means boiling something to extract its properties. The example substance begins comba- and continues below; the printed principal part ita is not expanded or regularized.
[c1-l037 >] *lamas, &c. pera lhe chupar a virtude, &*
[c1-l037 note] Completes the substance name combalamas and says that boiling extracts its efficacy and flavour. The final letter of lamas is read as s rather than OCR t; the botanical identity is not asserted.
[c1-l038 >] *ſabor.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*fundida.*
[c1-l038 note] Sabor closes the extraction-by-boiling gloss. At the far right, the printed parenthesis and fundida are displaced continuation material for Nidobuqi on the row below: silver melted twice. They remain on this physical row rather than being semantically relocated or lost in the OCR's fused ſahor.fundida.
[c1-l039] Nidobuqi. *Laya de prata que he duas vezes*
[c1-l039 note] Nidobuki is a kind of silver that has been melted twice. Its final Portuguese word fundida is printed at the far right of the preceding physical row and is represented there explicitly.
[c1-l040] Nifon. *Iapão.*
[c1-l040 note] Nihon is Japan. The dictionary's f spelling and the Portuguese Iapão are preserved rather than modernized.
[c1-l041] Nifongui. *Liuro em que estão eſcritas as cou-*
[c1-l041 note] Nihongi is described as a book recording the affairs of Japan. The description continues across the following rows and should not be replaced with a modern bibliographic definition.
[c1-l042 >] *ſas de Iapão deſde que ſe começou ate idade dos*
[c1-l042 note] Completes cousas and traces the account from Japan's beginnings to the age of its kings. The temporal clause continues below.
[c1-l043 >] *Reis, & deſdos Reis atè agora. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l043 note] The dictionary extends the description from the kings to the present and introduces another sense with Item. The contracted deſdos and the locally printed accents are retained.
[c1-l044 >] *Custume, ou leis que correm em Iapão.*
[c1-l044 note] The additional sense is the customs or laws current in Japan. It belongs to the Nihongi entry rather than being a new headword.
[c1-l045] Nigai. *Couſa amargoſa.*
[c1-l045 note] Nigai means bitter. The Portuguese amargoſa gives the taste quality, anticipating the nominal forms below.
[c1-l046 >] Nigaſa. Nigǒ.
[c1-l046 note] Nigasa is bitterness, and nigoo is the associated adverbial form. Their order and the downward o mark are preserved as printed.
[c1-l047] Nigami. *Amargòs.*
[c1-l047 note] Nigami denotes bitterness or bitter tastes. The unusual printed Portuguese Amargòs and its grave mark are retained rather than normalized to an expected modern noun.

[c2-l001] Niganigaxij. *Couſa queſe estranha, ou de que*
[c2-l001 note] Niganigashii describes something objectionable or distressing. The gloss's second alternative continues below; this is a figurative extension of bitterness.
[c2-l002 >] *ſe tem pena.*
[c2-l002 note] Completes the description as something that causes distress or regret. It belongs to the adjective above.
[c2-l003 >] Niganigaxiſa.
[c2-l003 note] Niganigashisa is the corresponding quality noun. The damaged vowel after the second g is read as a, in agreement with the surrounding adjective and adverb, rather than the OCR's e.
[c2-l004 >] Niganigaxǔ.
[c2-l004 note] Niganigashuu supplies the associated adverbial form. The downward u mark is preserved rather than rewritten as a modern long-vowel spelling.
[c2-l005] Nigari, u, atta. *Ficar ſentido de algũa cou-*
[c2-l005 note] Nigari means feeling hurt or resentful about something. The Portuguese object of the feeling continues as cousa below.
[c2-l006 >] *ſa que lhe dißerão, ou fizerão. ¶* Nigat-
[c2-l006 note] The hurt comes from something said or done to the person. Nigat- begins the past-form example nigatta on the next row.
[c2-l007 >] ta cotouo yǔ. *Dizer couſas que não conuẽ,*
[c2-l007 note] Nigatta koto wo yuu means saying objectionable things. The translation characterizes them as inappropriate and continues with their effect on others.
[c2-l008 >] *& que tocão, ou dão pena a outros.*
[c2-l008 note] The words touch a sore point or cause others distress. This completes the example, not a new sense involving physical contact alone.
[c2-l009] Nigauarai. *O rirſe ſecamente não ſendo de co-*
[c2-l009 note] Nigawarai is a dry or forced laugh, not coming from the heart. The Portuguese coração is divided across the next row.
[c2-l010 >] *ração. ¶* Nigauaraiuo ſuru. *Idem.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes coração and supplies nigawarai wo suru, giving such a forced laugh. Idem refers to the preceding explanation.
[c2-l011] Nigauari. *Homem que vai de ſobreſelente, ou*
[c2-l011 note] Nigawari is a relief porter, a person accompanying the carrier as a substitute. The Portuguese explains this by his unladen status and ability to take a turn below.
[c2-l012 >] *deſpejado pera ſe reuezar com o que leua o fato*
[c2-l012 note] The relief man travels without a load so that he can alternate with the person carrying the baggage. The location of that load follows on the next row.
[c2-l013 >] *às costas.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes carrying the baggage on one's back. The grave accent in às is visible and was missing from the draft.
[c2-l014] Nigaxi, ſu, aita. *Deixar fugir.*
[c2-l014 note] Nigashi means letting someone or something escape. Its printed historical past ending aita is retained rather than modernized to -shita.
[c2-l015] Nigǒ. i, Sayǔ. *Ambas as partes. i, Direi-*
[c2-l015 note] Nigoo is explained by sayuu, both sides, namely right and left. The right-side term in the Portuguese gloss is divided at the line end.
[c2-l016 >] *ta, & eſquerda. ¶* Nigǒni tçuranaru.
[c2-l016 note] Completes direita and gives esquerda, left. Nigoo ni tsuranaru means lining up on both sides; the scan supports r in eſquerda, not OCR n.
[c2-l017 >] *Porſe em fieira, ou por ordem dambas as par-*
[c2-l017 note] The example is translated as taking position in a line or in order on both sides. The final partes continues below.
[c2-l018 >] *tes. S.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes partes, sides. The separate upright S. marks the expression as written usage. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l019] Nigon. *Duas palauras. i, Tornar a dizer.*
[c2-l019 note] Nigon literally gives two words or utterances and is explained here as saying something again. The example below uses a negative commitment not to repeat oneself.
[c2-l020 >] *¶* Nigonto mǒſumai. *Não ei de tornar*
[c2-l020 note] Nigon to moosumai means that one will not say it a second time. Portuguese Não ei de tornar begins the corresponding future refusal and continues below.
[c2-l021 >] *a dizer.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes tornar a dizer, to say again. The scan has dizer with i, not OCR dezer.
[c2-l022] Nigoracaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer turuo.*
[c2-l022 note] Nigorakashi means making something turbid. The second letter is i, not the draft's u, and the historical past ending aita is retained.
[c2-l023] Ngoraxi, ſu, aita. *Turuar como agoa, vi-*
[c2-l023 note] This is the related nigorashi verb for clouding a liquid. The scan itself appears to omit i after N, so Ngoraxi is preserved as a printed anomaly; the past ending reads aita, not atta. Portuguese vinho begins at the right edge.
[c2-l024 >] *nho, &c.*
[c2-l024 note] Completes vinho, wine, as an example alongside water of something that can be made cloudy. The row is a short continuation of Ngoraxi.
[c2-l025] Nigori, u, otta. *Eſtar turua a agoa, ou vinho.*
[c2-l025 note] Nigori is the intransitive verb for water or wine being turbid. The final word is wholly italic vinho; the OCR's mixed-type vinba is not Japanese material.
[c2-l026] Nigori. *O eſtar a agoa turua, ou enlodada.*
[c2-l026 note] Nigori here is the noun for water's cloudiness or muddiness. It is separate from the preceding verb entry.
[c2-l027 >] *¶ Permet.* Togano nigorini ſômu. *Macu-*
[c2-l027 note] The figurative example toga no nigori ni somu concerns being stained by fault or sin. Permet. introduces the transferred sense. The mark over o is reduced to a small ink trace; the aligned draft's ô is retained rather than replacing it with an unmarked vowel on linguistic expectations alone.
[c2-l028 >] *larſe cõ peccado.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes macular-se com peccado, becoming stained with sin. This confirms the figurative interpretation of the preceding Japanese example.
[c2-l029] Nigorizaqe. *Vinho brãco como turuo de Iapão.*
[c2-l029 note] Nigorizake is Japanese liquor described as white and cloudy. The historical Portuguese vinho is the dictionary's category, not a claim that this is grape wine.
[c2-l030] Nigoxi, ſu, oita. *Turuar, ou fazer turuo.*
[c2-l030 note] Nigoshi is another transitive verb for making something turbid. The printed past ending oita is retained as historical morphology.
[c2-l031] Nigue, uru, eta. *Fugir. Vt,* Torumo-
[c2-l031 note] Nige and its principal forms mean fleeing. The following quotation starts toru mono mo across the row boundary.
[c2-l032 >] nomo toriayezu, mina cachi fadaxinite
[c2-l032 note] Toru mono mo toriayezu describes leaving without time to take anything; mina kachi hadashi nite says that everyone was on foot and barefoot. The comma after toriayezu is visible in the scan.
[c2-l033 >] yamaye nigueagaru. Chr. N. *Lib.*
[c2-l033 note] Yama ye nigeagaru completes the flight up into the mountains. The upright citation reads Chr. N., not the OCR's Chi. N.; Lib. begins the italic book reference without an inferred expansion of the abbreviated source title.
[c2-l034 >] *4. Sem podertomar nada com preßa descal-*
[c2-l034 note] Book 4 is followed by the translation: unable to take anything, they fled in haste and barefoot. The physical division descal-ços continues below; podertomar retains the close-set printed spacing.
[c2-l035 >] *ços, & a pee ſe acolherão aos montes.*
[c2-l035 note] Completes descalços and says that they took refuge in the mountains on foot. The scan supplies pee, with two e types, and separates the reflexive ſe; the draft's peſe obscured both.
[c2-l036] Niguecacure, uru, eta. *Fugindo eſconderſe.*
[c2-l036 note] Nigekakure means hiding while fleeing. The Portuguese combines flight and concealment, not merely departure from a place.
[c2-l037] Niguechiri, ru, itta. *Fugir eſpalhandoſe, ou*
[c2-l037 note] Nigechiri means fleeing in scattered directions. The Portuguese gives a second description of dispersal below.
[c2-l038 >] *treſmontandoſe per diuerſas partes.*
[c2-l038 note] Completes the scattering gloss with dispersal through different places. The final adjective is diuerſas, not OCR diuerſan; the historical treſmontandoſe is retained.
[c2-l039] Niguecubi. *Cabeça, que ſe toma naguerra dal-*
[c2-l039 note] Nigekubi is a head taken in war from a fleeing person. The description of that person continues below, with dal- beginning dalgum.
[c2-l040 >] *gum que vai fugindo. ¶* Niguecubiuo to-
[c2-l040 note] The victim is someone in flight. Nigekubi wo to- introduces taking such a head; fugindo is recovered from the OCR's fuginao.
[c2-l041 >] ru. *Tomar algũa cabeça deſtes, o que ſe tem*
[c2-l041 note] Completes toru and translates taking a head from one of these fugitives. The scan supports Tomar and deſtes, replacing the OCR's Poniar and deſies.
[c2-l042 >] *por pouca valentia.*
[c2-l042 note] The dictionary says that this is regarded as showing little valour. This assessment belongs to the historical gloss, not an added judgement by the reviewer.
[c2-l043] Niguecuzzuxi, ſu, uita. *Fugir com preßa*
[c2-l043 note] Nigekuzushi describes fleeing hastily while breaking formation. The printed past ending uita is a historical form and agrees with the example below.
[c2-l044 >] *deſordenandoſe, & desbaratandoſe. Vt,*
[c2-l044 note] The flight is disorderly and scattered. Vt introduces an illustration comparing the fugitives to leaves carried by the wind.
[c2-l045 >] Cajeni conofano chiru yǒni muramura
[c2-l045 note] Kaze ni konoha no chiru yoo ni compares the action to leaves scattering in the wind. Muramura names the settlements or their groups collectively, as the Portuguese translation confirms.
[c2-l046 >] batto niguecuzzuita. Tac. *Como folhas*
[c2-l046 note] Batto nigekuzuita describes their sudden disorderly flight. Tac. is the upright citation label; the Portuguese translation begins with the comparison to leaves.
[c2-l047 >] *que leua o vento todas as pouoações ſe derão à-*
[c2-l047 note] The wind carries the leaves, and all the settlements take to flight. Enlarged reinspection recovers the faint second o in pouoações and supports ſe derão à-; the next page continues preßa a fugir. The damaged word and ending remain nonblocking reading caveats, with the physical terminal hyphen retained.
