[c1-l004 note] The quotation prohibits renewing the disputation. Taif. is the source label, followed by Lib. for its book number. Its final letter has a broken upper hook; Taif. is retained rather than confidently changing the damaged type to t.

[c1-l005 note] Book 28 is followed by the Portuguese explanation: there is no need to reply or repeat the dispute. The printed a- is retained even though the article and following noun remain separate words in interpretation.

[c1-l007] Mǒnen. Midarina nen. *Maos penſamentos.*
[c1-l007 note] Moonen is explained as disorderly thoughts, midarina nen, and glossed as evil thoughts. The caron is visible and is not replaced from modern vowel expectations.

[c1-l008 note] The imperative setdan seyo tells the reader to cut off the bad thoughts. The second Japanese expression is retained as printed mǒriuo rather than silently replacing it with an expected doctrinal compound; Cortai begins its translation.
[c1-l009 >] *pellos maos penſamentos.*
[c1-l009 note] Completes Cortai pellos maos pensamentos, cutting through or putting an end to evil thoughts. This is the translation of the imperative above, not a separate sense.
[c1-l010] Mongatame. *Officio de guardar, & defen-*

[c1-l032] Monjen. Cadono maye. *Diante da porta.*

[c1-l043 >] *car deſmayado, ou fora de ſi.*

[c1-l045] Monjin. *Perguntar, ou por duuida. ¶ Itẽ,*

[c1-l047 note] Completes Este and states that this is the usual meaning. The damaged interior of oſentido is retained in the printed close spacing, while the final corre has two r types.
