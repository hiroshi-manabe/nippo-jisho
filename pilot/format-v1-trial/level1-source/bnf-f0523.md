## column-1 [column] Column 1

> Lexical coverage was checked after independent scan reading against Entry Words Data of Nippojisho, NINJAL, Hideyuki Ohshima and Taichi Aida, version 202510, CC BY 4.0; https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/textdb_dataset/en/nipp/index.html . The scan-derived transcription and English review notes are this review’s work; the index is not a diplomatic authority.

[c1-l001] Toyacacuya. *Aßi, ou não ſei como. Vt,*
[c1-l001 note] Toyakakuya expresses uncertainty about this or that way; the Portuguese says “thus, or I do not know how.” The initial is T, not OCR r, and the negative is não, not mão.
[c1-l002 >] Toyacacuyato yǔ vchini, &c. *Em quã-*
[c1-l002 note] Toyakakuya to yuu uchi ni begins an example about talking over this and that. The damaged speaking form is read yǔ: the scan shows a caron over its abraded u, rather than the OCR yui. The Portuguese em quanto continues across the line break.
[c1-l003 >] *to fallão decà, & delà, ou ſe ſera aßi, ou não,*
[c1-l003 note] Continues em quanto: while people discuss this or that, whether it will be so or otherwise. The scan supports fallão with f and the completed aßi; decà and delà retain the printed grave marks.
[c1-l004 >] *ou de que maneira, &c.*
[c1-l004 note] The final alternative, “or in what manner,” completes the preceding example of indecision. This short continuation is independent physical lineation, not a new entry.
[c1-l005] Tǒyacata. *Principe filho de Yacata.*
[c1-l005 note] Tooyakata is glossed as a prince who is the son of a yakata, a lord. Yacata is a Japanese title embedded in the Portuguese explanation; the scan supports Y rather than OCR V and retains italic type.
[c1-l006] Toyaxen cacuyaxen. *O não ſaber que ſe faça,*
[c1-l006 note] Toyasen kakuyasen describes not knowing what one should do, a deliberative this-way-or-that-way expression. The Portuguese translates that uncertainty directly.
[c1-l007 >] *ou que conſelho tome, &c.*
[c1-l007 note] The alternative “or what counsel to take” completes the preceding deliberative definition. No independent Japanese wording is supplied on this continuation.
[c1-l008] Toyefataye. *Gente, ou ſoldados que ſe acre-*
[c1-l008 note] Toehatae refers here to increased numbers of people or soldiers. The Portuguese acrescentão is divided after acre- and must remain on its two physical rows.
[c1-l009 >] *centão em numero. ¶* Toyefatayeni tori-
[c1-l009 note] Completes acrescentão em numero, then begins an example with toehatae ni tori-. The Japanese compound verb continues in the next row.
[c1-l010 >] mauaſu. *Cercar com muita gente, ou com*
[c1-l010 note] Mawasu completes torimawasu, surrounding or encircling. The gloss specifies doing so with many people, introducing its second formulation below.
[c1-l011 >] *muitas rodas degente.*
[c1-l011 note] “Many circles of people” completes the encirclement gloss. The close printed spacing in degente is retained rather than silently normalizing it.
[c1-l012] Tǒyô. *Neceßidade preſente. ¶* Tǒyôni
[c1-l012 note] Tooyoo denotes a present need; tooyoo ni introduces the example completed by tatsu. The first o has a caron and the second a circumflex in the printed headword and example.
[c1-l013 >] tatçu. *Preſtar, ou ſeruir em algũa nece-*
[c1-l013 note] Tatsu completes tooyoo ni tatsu, to be useful for a current need. The Portuguese necessity noun is split after nece-; its historical letterforms are retained.
[c1-l014 >] *ßidade preſente.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes necessidade presente, the present need for which something is useful. This is a genuine short continuation.
[c1-l015] Toyoxe. *Pao que ſe prèga ao comprido na om*
[c1-l015 note] Toyose is a wooden piece fixed lengthwise along a door jamb. The printed om ends the row without an inferred hyphen, despite continuing as ombreira below.
[c1-l016 >] *breira da porta pera q̃ entre a porta, & a ombrei*
[c1-l016 note] The description places the strip between the door and the jamb. The abbreviated q̃ is retained, and ombreira is again divided without adding an absent terminal hyphen.
[c1-l017 >] *ra não fique eſpaço aberto pera poder ver, ou me*
[c1-l017 note] Explains the purpose: leaving no open gap through which one can see or insert something. The final me continues into meter on the next row without a supplied hyphen.
[c1-l018 >] *ter os dedos.*
[c1-l018 note] “Put the fingers in” completes the protective function of the door strip. The short row belongs to Toyoxe above.
[c1-l019] Tôyu. *Certo azeite como verniz, ou charão.*
[c1-l019 note] Tooyuu is a particular oil compared with varnish or lacquer. The circumflex and the historical Portuguese charão are retained; the gloss does not itself establish a botanical species.
[c1-l020] Tǒza. Ataru za. *Este aßento, ou caſa onde*
[c1-l020 note] Tooza is explained by ataru za, the present seat or place, then as the house where people are gathered. The headword has a caron, unlike the preceding oil entry.
[c1-l021 >] *eſtà gente. ¶ Item, Adu. Agora de*
[c1-l021 note] Completes the gathering-place sense, then marks an adverbial use meaning “now.” Adu. is part of the italic explanatory apparatus.
[c1-l022 >] *preſente, ou neste comenos. ¶* Tǒzano co-
[c1-l022 note] The adverbial gloss means at present or at this juncture. Toozano co- begins the next example and joins to koto across the physical line break.
[c1-l023 >] to. *Couſa dagora, ou breue, & q̃ logo paßa.*
[c1-l023 note] Koto completes the phrase for a present or fleeting affair. The Portuguese adds that it is brief and passes immediately.
[c1-l024] Tôzai. Figaxi, nixi. *Leste, & Oeſte.*
[c1-l024 note] Toozai is identified with higashi and nishi, east and west. The Portuguese prints Oeste, not the OCR reading Deste; the spelling and provisional st allograph are otherwise kept.
[c1-l025 >] *¶* Tôzai nanbocu. *Leste, Oeſte, Nor-*
[c1-l025 note] Toozai nanboku enumerates east, west, south and north; the Portuguese orders the last two as north and south. Oeste is one word in the scan, correcting the OCR initial and internal break.
[c1-l026 >] *te, & Sul. ¶* Tôzaini chiſô ſuru. i, *Fa-*
[c1-l026 note] The Portuguese completes Norte and Sul. Toozai ni chisoo suru means bustling to and fro to provide hospitality; the scan has z in Tôzaini and italic Fa-, not the provisional mixed-type fragment.
[c1-l027 >] *zer muito agaſalhado mouẽdoſe de cà pera là.*
[c1-l027 note] Completes fazer and explains the effortful hospitality: moving from here to there. The historical agaſalhado and abbreviation tilde in mouẽdoſe are preserved.
[c1-l028] Tozama. *Fora, ou couſa de fora que não he de*
[c1-l028 note] Tozama is outside or an outside thing, leading into a contrast with what is internal or familiar. The Portuguese definition is not yet limited to a formal political status.
[c1-l029 >] *dentro, ou familiar. ¶* Tozamano fito,
[c1-l029 note] Completes the contrast with inside or familiar, then introduces tozama no hito, an outside person. The example is continuous with the alternative below.
[c1-l030 >] l, focaſamano fito. *Homem de fora, ou*
[c1-l030 note] Hokasama no hito is offered as an alternative to tozama no hito. The Portuguese means an outsider; the lower-case l is the Latin alternative marker.
[c1-l031 >] *que não he familiar.*
[c1-l031 note] “One who is not familiar” completes the outsider gloss. This row is explanatory continuation rather than a distinct noun.
[c1-l032] Tozamaxu. *Homẽs que ſeruem a algum ſenhor*
[c1-l032 note] Tozamashuu denotes men serving a lord from elsewhere. The scan supports plural Homẽs without the OCR comma; the following row supplies the condition of distance.
[c1-l033 >] *estando abſentes, ou longe.*
[c1-l033 note] The men are absent or far away while serving their lord. The historical Portuguese absent form is retained as printed.
[c1-l034] Tǒzan. Ataru yama. *Este monte, ou eſta Te-*
[c1-l034 note] Too-zan, paraphrased as ataru yama, is this mountain or this temple. The initial T corrects OCR r; the embedded Japanese temple word Tera begins as Te- and continues below.
[c1-l035 >] *ra que ordinariamente està em monte.*
[c1-l035 note] Ra completes Tera, and the gloss explains that such a temple is ordinarily on a mountain. The fragment is part of the Japanese term embedded in Portuguese and retains its italic setting.
[c1-l036] Tôzan. Yamani noboru. *Oirem os mininos*
[c1-l036 note] Toozan, yamani noboru, literally going up the mountain, introduces the practice of boys going to Buddhist temples. The printed Oirem is retained together; it functions as a Portuguese nominal infinitive construction.
[c1-l037 >] *às Teras dos Bonzos a aprender aler, & eſcre-*
[c1-l037 note] The boys go to the bonzes’ temples to learn to read and write. The print has Teras, a pluralized Japanese temple term embedded in the italic Portuguese explanation, rather than OCR Peras.
[c1-l038 >] *uer, & quando os pais os tirão da Tera que or*
[c1-l038 note] Completes eſcreuer, then describes parents taking the boys out of the temple. Tera is the Japanese term embedded in the italic gloss; or begins ordinariamente on the next physical row without an inferred hyphen.
[c1-l039 >] *dinariamente he depois de 3. annos, diz ſe,*
[c1-l039 note] The departure ordinarily occurs after three years; diz ſe introduces what that departure is called. This is the dictionary’s own description of the schooling practice.
[c1-l040 >] Gueſan ſuru. i, *Sair da Tera onde aprendião*
[c1-l040 note] Gueſan ſuru, corresponding to leaving or descending from the temple, supplies the contrasted term. The Portuguese says to leave the temple where the boys were learning; printed s is not modernized from the expected lexical identity.
[c1-l041 >] *aler, & eſcreuer. ¶ Item, Ir à Tera, ou*
[c1-l041 note] Completes learning to read and write, then adds the wider use “go to a temple.” Tera remains the Japanese noun embedded in the italic Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l042 >] *varela qual quer outra gente.*
[c1-l042 note] The broader definition includes any other people going to a temple or pagoda. Varela is the Portuguese glossing term here, not a new Japanese headword.
[c1-l043] Tôzan. Figaxi yama. *Monte do Oriente, ou*
[c1-l043 note] A separate Toozan is paraphrased by higashi yama, an eastern mountain. Both the initial T and Portuguese Oriente are supported by the scan, correcting OCR F and D.
[c1-l044 >] *pera Oriente.*
[c1-l044 note] “Toward the east” supplies the alternative orientation of the mountain. It completes the previous entry and must not be absorbed into the next verb.
[c1-l045] Tozaxi, ſu, aita. *Fechar a porta. Vt,* To-
[c1-l045 note] Tozashi, tozasu, tozaita means closing a door. The historical -aita principal part is preserved; the example begins with To-, the door object.
[c1-l046 >] uo tozaſu.
[c1-l046 note] Uo tozasu completes to wo tozasu, closing the door. The printed line division splits the object phrase across rows without changing its interpretation.
[c1-l047] Tǒzocu. Nuſubito. *Ladrões.*
[c1-l047 note] Toozoku is equated with nusubito and glossed as thieves. The faded initial T remains visible and is restored; the caron is kept rather than normalized to a modern spelling.

## column-2-part-1 [column] Column 2, part 1

[c2-l001 initial=6] V. i, Vſagui. *Lebre hum dos*
[c2-l001 note] U is equated with usagi, the hare. The decorated V is the headword’s initial and extends beside six rows; it is not the spurious numeral in the OCR. Its Portuguese description continues with the zodiac count.
[c2-l002 >] *12. animaes, ou ſignos por on-*
[c2-l002 note] The hare is one of the twelve animals or signs. The scan reads 12. animaes, replacing the broken OCR r2 and antmaes; onde is divided across the line ending.
[c2-l003 >] *de os Iapões contão os annos, &*
[c2-l003 note] Completes onde and explains that the Japanese count years by these signs. Os annos corrects the OCR’s compressed orannos; the sentence continues to the hours.
[c2-l004 >] *as horas. ¶* Vno cocu. *Das*
[c2-l004 note] The signs also designate hours. U no koku, the hour of the hare, introduces the printed clock-time explanation continued below.
[c2-l005 >] *cinco horas atè as 6. de polla-*
[c2-l005 note] The dictionary gives the period from five until six in the morning. The printed numeral is 6, not the OCR’s accented ô; the supplied historical hours are retained without converting them to a modern convention.
[c2-l006 >] *menhãa.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes polla-menhãa, “in the morning.” The visible tilde is over the first of the two a types in this impression; the supplied menhãa is retained rather than moved to match a general expectation.
[c2-l007] V. i, Vnotori. *Coruo marinho. ¶* Vno
[c2-l007 note] A second u is the cormorant, explained by u no tori. Vno begins the proverb below; the punctuation after the explanatory i is a comma in the scan.
[c2-l008 >] mane ſuru caraſuua mizzu curǒ. *O coruo*
[c2-l008 note] The proverb says that a crow imitating a cormorant will swallow water. The print retains mizzu curǒ; the Portuguese rendering explains the resulting drowning.
[c2-l009 >] *que quer mergulhar como o coruo marinho afo-*
[c2-l009 note] The crow tries to dive like the cormorant. The Portuguese afogar-se is divided after afo-, with its continuation on the next row.
[c2-l010 >] *garſe ha: diz ſe dos que intentão couſas ſobre ſu-*
[c2-l010 note] The crow will drown; the proverb applies to people attempting things beyond their capacity. The visible colon after ha separates the result from the explanation of the proverb.
[c2-l011 >] *as forças. ¶* Vno me, tacano me. *O-*
[c2-l011 note] As completes suas forças, their powers. U no me, taka no me introduces the sharp eyes of a cormorant and a hawk; the Portuguese olhos begins as O-.
[c2-l012 >] *lhos viuos, & ligeiros. ¶* Vnomiuo ſu-
[c2-l012 note] Completes olhos and describes lively, keen eyes. U nomi wo su- begins the further expression for swallowing something whole.
[c2-l013 >] ru. *Engulir algũa couſa ſem mastigar. No*
[c2-l013 note] Ru completes suru. The gloss specifies swallowing without chewing, then introduces a regional comparison with No on the next row.
[c2-l014 >] *Cami,* Vnnomini ſuru.
[c2-l014 note] Cami identifies the upper-region usage, where the expression is Vnnomini ſuru. The doubled n is retained from the print; Cami is Japanese embedded in Portuguese, not an ordinary entry headword.
[c2-l015] V. Aru. *Auer: mas não ſe vſa ſò por ſi.* Vto
[c2-l015 note] U is equated with aru, being or existing, and the Portuguese warns that it is not used alone. Vto starts the example combining affirmation and negation below.
[c2-l016 >] mo mutomo fenji nai. Xid. *Não reſpõ-*
[c2-l016 note] Vto mo mutomo fenji nai says there was no reply of either yes or no. Xid. is retained as the printed citation label; the Portuguese reſpõdeo is divided at the row boundary.
[c2-l017 >] *deo de ſi, ou de não. ¶* V muni fatasǒ-
[c2-l017 note] Completes the reply example. The next phrase, u-mu ni hatasoozu, promises completion regardless; the visible spacing V muni is retained, and the final verb continues below.
[c2-l018 >] zu. *Em todo caſo, ou por fas, & por ne-*
[c2-l018 note] Zu completes the resolve to finish the task. The Portuguese paraphrases this as in any case, whether by fair or foul means, dividing nefas across the row break.
[c2-l019 >] *faz o eide acabar.*
[c2-l019 note] Faz completes the printed ne-faz construction, and o eide acabar states “I shall finish it.” The historical written form eide is retained.

## column-2-part-2 [column] Column 2, part 2

[c2-l020 initial=2] VA. *Particula de que ſe vſa desta maneira.*
[c2-l020 note] Wa is introduced as a particle and the following rows illustrate its use. The V is a two-row enlarged initial in roman type, not italic OCR punctuation.
[c2-l021 >] *¶* Nuſumiua tçucamatçuranu. *Quã-*
[c2-l021 note] Nusumi wa tsukamatsuranu means “as for stealing, I do not do it.” The Portuguese topic construction quanto he begins with Quã- here.
[c2-l022 >] *to he furtar não o faço. ¶* Yomiua yome
[c2-l022 note] Completes the translation denying theft. Yomi wa yome begins the contrasting example that acknowledges reading while withholding understanding.
[c2-l023 >] domo, gatten xenu. *Quanto a ler leo, por*
[c2-l023 note] Domo completes the concessive yomedo(mo); gatten xenu says that the speaker does not understand. The Portuguese por em is divided between rows and is kept in its printed form.
[c2-l024 >] *em não entendo. Iunta tambẽ esta particula aos*
[c2-l024 note] Completes “but I do not understand,” then describes the particle joined to participles. Em is the continuation of por above; tambẽ retains the abbreviation tilde.
[c2-l025 >] *participios ſignifica,* Naraba, l, cotoua. *Vt,*
[c2-l025 note] The dictionary equates this attached use with naraba or koto wa. These forms occur within the grammatical explanation and are upright; italic Vt, introduces the example below.
[c2-l026 >] Fitobitouo tatazumaxeteua buſata gia. *Fa*
[c2-l026 note] Hitobito wo tatazumaxete wa busata ja means that leaving people standing is discourteous. The Portuguese fazer begins as Fa at the edge, with no inferred hyphen.
[c2-l027 >] *zer eſperar em pè os homẽs à porta he falta.*
[c2-l027 note] Completes fazer and specifies leaving the people waiting on their feet at the door. The gloss evaluates this as a fault or lack of courtesy.
[c2-l028] Va. Yau raguru. *Mas vſaſe ſempre com,* Xi,
[c2-l028 note] Wa is glossed by the damaged printed Yau raguru, pointing to yawaraguru, but is said always to be used with shi/suru. The internal a expected in the Japanese synonym is not securely visible, so its provisional spacing is retained. Enlarged comparison with the clear commas later on this row supports Mas: its faint final short s occupies the letter body above the baseline, rather than the below-baseline position of a comma.
[c2-l029 >] ſuru. *Declarar, ou interpretar noutra lingua.*
[c2-l029 note] Suru completes the conjugational pair after com above. The full construction means explaining or interpreting into another language; the upright verb is embedded in the Portuguese usage statement.
[c2-l030] Va. Ienxǔno coſocu. *Pontos de meditação*
[c2-l030 note] A separate wa denotes a Zen subject for contemplation, explained as Ienxǔno coſocu. The long-u mark and adjoining type are abraded; the supplied caron is retained provisionally rather than replaced from expectation.
[c2-l031 >] *que dão os Ienxùs.*
[c2-l031 note] The meditation topics are those assigned by Zen practitioners. Ienxùs is the Japanese sect name pluralized within the Portuguese explanation, with the printed grave retained.
[c2-l032] Va. *Roda, ou circulo.*
[c2-l032 note] Wa here means a wheel or circle. This brief independent entry is distinct from the preceding Zen term.
[c2-l033] Vaa. *Interjeição de eſpanto.*
[c2-l033 note] Waa is an exclamation of astonishment. Interjeição preserves the printed Portuguese spelling and must not be conflated with the preceding wa noun.
[c2-l034] Vabi, uru, ita. *Pedir miſericordia, ou ro-*
[c2-l034 note] Wabi, waburu, wabita covers asking mercy or pleading. The principal parts retain the historical -uru conjugation; the Portuguese rogar is divided after ro-.
[c2-l035 >] *gar. ¶ Item, Entristecerſe. ¶ Itẽ, Iũ*
[c2-l035 note] Completes rogar, adds becoming sad, then begins the explanation of a compound-verb use with Iũ-to. The printed capital I and tilde replace the OCR tũ fragment.
[c2-l036 >] *to à raiz de outros verbos, Fazer com difficul-*
[c2-l036 note] When joined to another verb’s stem, wabi conveys doing the action with difficulty. Verbos and difficul- are supported by the scan, correcting OCR verbot and diffical-.
[c2-l037 >] *dade o que ſignifica o verbo a que ſe ajunta. Vt,*
[c2-l037 note] Completes dificuldade and explains that the difficulty applies to the action of the attached verb. Italic Vt, introduces the illustrative compounds.
[c2-l038 >] Fitouo tazzune vaburu. *Buſcar alguem cõ*
[c2-l038 note] Hito wo tazune waburu means having difficulty finding someone. The damaged internal a of vaburu is compatible with the supplied reading and the surrounding principal forms; the Portuguese gloss starts “seek someone with”.
[c2-l039 >] *trabalho. ¶* Machivaburu. *Eſperar com*
[c2-l039 note] Trabalho completes the difficult search. Machiwaburu then illustrates waiting with difficulty or distress, continued in Portuguese below.
[c2-l040 >] *difficuldade, ou pena. ¶* Vabita teide goza-
[c2-l040 note] The waiting gloss concludes with difficulty or pain. Wabita tei de goza- begins a further example describing a distressed or impoverished appearance.
[c2-l041 >] ru. *Mostras, & maneira de hum estarpobre.*
[c2-l041 note] Ru completes gozaru; the Portuguese describes the signs and manner of a person being poor. Mostras, rather than OCR Mosiras, is the visible noun; the close printed estarpobre spacing is retained.
