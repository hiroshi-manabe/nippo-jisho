[c1-l001] Fodorai. *Quantidade como de preço, &c.*
[c1-l001 note] Fodorai is hodorai, an amount or degree, exemplified by a price. Quantidade como de preço supplies that quantitative sense; the widened crop now includes the punctuation and column rule.
[c1-l002 >] *¶ Item, Poſsibilidade, ou poſſe.*
[c1-l002 note] Item adds capacity or means, Poſibilidade, ou poſſe. The scan supports one s in the first word, not OCR's stray r; the two long s forms in poſſe are kept distinct from a beta-like ligature.
[c1-l003] Fǒfacuxi. *Hum certo liuro q̃ enſina aeſcreuer.*
[c1-l003 note] Fǒfacuxi names a book teaching writing. The short gloss does not securely identify its title beyond this printed name, so no speculative kanji title is imposed; the final escrever is fully visible after widening.
[c1-l004] Fǒfan. *Arroz cozido com outros comeres*
[c1-l004 note] Fǒfan is hōhan, a rice dish with other foods. The following lines describe finely cut toppings and broth; this culinary context distinguishes it from a generic cooked-rice entry.
[c1-l005 >] *miudamente cortados ſobre que botão Xiru, &*
[c1-l005 note] The other foods are finely cut, and xiru, shiru or broth, is poured over them. Xiru is an embedded Japanese food term but appears italic here; it is not made upright merely because of its language.
[c1-l006 >] *comem.*
[c1-l006 note] Comem finishes the preparation-and-eating description. This isolated Portuguese verb remains a continuation, not a new dictionary entry.
[c1-l007] Fôfei. *Papelinhos retalhados, & pegados*
[c1-l007 note] Fôfei is hōhei, an offering involving cut paper pieces. Papelinhos retalhados e pegados describes the paper attached to a stick, elaborated in the next rows.
[c1-l008 >] *na ponta de hum pao que com certas cerimoni-*
[c1-l008 note] The paper pieces are attached at a stick's tip and used with prescribed ceremonies. Cerimoni- visibly continues to as below; its right edge was missing in the old crop.
[c1-l009 >] *as ſe offerecem diante dos Camis.*
[c1-l009 note] The offerings are presented before the kami. Camis is the Portuguese pluralized Japanese term and is italic in this line, consistent with the surrounding explanatory sentence.
[c1-l010] Fôfen. Fome, ſoxiru. *O falar ſobre alguem*
[c1-l010 note] Fôfen is hōhen, praise and blame, glossed home, soshiru. The damaged initial letter is F, not OCR B; both the Japanese gloss and the nearby repeated fôfen support it.
[c1-l011 >] *louuando, ou murmurando. ¶* Cano fi-
[c1-l011 note] Louvando, ou murmurando explains praising or speaking ill. The r in murmurando is worn but supported by the remaining trace and the word; Cano fi- begins the Japanese example below.
[c1-l012 >] tono vyeni fôfen yoi, l, varui. *Sobre*
[c1-l012 note] Cano fitono vyeni fôfen yoi, or varui, concerns good or bad reports about that person. Vyeni is ue ni, and yoi/varui supplies the evaluative contrast mirrored in Portuguese.
[c1-l013 >] *aquelle homem tratão, ou falão bem, ou mal.*
[c1-l013 note] The Portuguese says that people discuss or speak well or badly of that man. It completes the hōhen example without requiring modernized Japanese syntax.
[c1-l014] Fofit. *O que em nome del Rei gouerna, ou*
[c1-l014 note] Fofit is hofit-, a checked-coda form of a word for assisting government. The definition identifies someone governing on the king's behalf; the final t is not expanded to tsu in the transcription.
[c1-l015 >] *deſpoem das couſas.*
[c1-l015 note] Depoem das cousas completes the administration-related gloss. Its printed spelling is retained even though modern Portuguese would suggest dispõe in this sense; the text is not silently modernized.
[c1-l016] Fǒfo. *Adu.* i, Yǒyǒ. *Eſcaſamente, &*
[c1-l016 note] Fǒfo is the damaged printed heading for hōhō, barely or with difficulty, glossed yōyō. The last o has no secure accent here; NINJAL itself notes the impression appears Fǒfo despite its normalized Fǒfǒ entry. The third letter is f, not ſ.
[c1-l017 >] *como de gatinhas a colherſe, &c. ¶* Fǒ-
[c1-l017 note] The Portuguese describes getting away as if crawling, with scarcely enough strength. Fǒ- begins the repeated Japanese adverb across the line break.
[c1-l018 >] fǒ nigueta, l, fǒfǒno teide nigueta. *Fu-*
[c1-l018 note] Fǒfǒ nigueta, alternatively fǒfǒno teide nigueta, means escaping in a desperate or barely successful manner. Nigeta is coherent with the Portuguese Fugir; the two accented syllables in the example need not be forced onto the damaged heading.
[c1-l019 >] *gir, ou acolherſe aßi deſta maneira.*
[c1-l019 note] Gir completes Fugir, followed by acolherse assi desta maneira. This closes the example of escaping or taking refuge in that fashion.
[c1-l020] Fofucu. Farabai farabǒ. *Por abarriga, ou*
[c1-l020 note] Fofucu is hofuku, prostrating or crawling on the belly; farabai farabǒ is the accompanying native verb gloss. OCR Forucu and Faraba lose meaningful letters; the scan supports f and final i respectively.
[c1-l021 >] *peito no chão por reuerencia.*
[c1-l021 note] The belly or chest is placed on the ground out of reverence. This establishes the prostration sense here, not simply crawling for locomotion.
[c1-l022] Fôga. *Offerecer algũa couſa ao Idolo, ou Tera.*
[c1-l022 note] Fôga is hōga, giving an offering to an idol or temple. Tera is an embedded Japanese term in Portuguese; its italic appearance is retained.
[c1-l023 >] *¶* Fôgani iru. *Entrar no numero dos que*
[c1-l023 note] Fôgani iru means joining the body of donors. The scan has a pilcrow before the example that OCR omitted; entering the number of those who give is completed in the next line.
[c1-l024 >] *fazem eſmola pera algũa Tera, ou obra pia.*
[c1-l024 note] The donors give alms toward a temple or pious work. Tera is again embedded and italic, while obra pia is a Portuguese category rather than another Japanese term.
[c1-l025] Fôgachǒ. *Rol, ou liuro deſta eſmola. ¶* Fô-
[c1-l025 note] Fôgachǒ is hōgachō, a donation register. Rol, ou livro identifies its function; Fô- starts the example of entering one's name in it.
[c1-l026 >] gachǒni tçuqu. *Eſcreuerſe no rol, ou liuro*
[c1-l026 note] Fôgachǒni tçuqu means being entered in the donation register. The Portuguese reflexive escreverse confirms registration, not physically sticking to a book.
[c1-l027 >] *dos que dão eſta eſmola.*
[c1-l027 note] Dos que dão esta esmola specifies the people entered in the register: those making this donation. This closes the previous example.
[c1-l028] Fǒgacu. *Parte, ou banda. ¶* Fitono yu
[c1-l028 note] Fǒgacu is hōgaku, direction or quarter. Fitono yu begins a sentence about where someone goes, rather than music, another modern word with a similar reading.
[c1-l029 >] qu fǒgacuuo xiranu. *Não ſaber a parte*
[c1-l029 note] Yuqu fǒgacuuo xiranu means not knowing the direction someone goes. The Japanese argument structure matches Não saber a parte; the long vowel in hōgaku remains as printed.
[c1-l030 >] *pera onde vai alguem.*
[c1-l030 note] Pera onde vai alguem completes the directional definition. Pera is the historical Portuguese form and is not replaced with para.
[c1-l031] Fogami. *Iunto da barriga, ou vazio antre*
[c1-l031 note] Fogami denotes the area by the lower belly or groin. The Portuguese vazio antre introduces a space between the thighs and abdomen, not an abstract emptiness.
[c1-l032 >] *as coxas, & barriga.*
[c1-l032 note] As coxas e barriga identifies those boundaries. The scan shows barriga, with rr, rather than OCR bartiga; the paired anatomical terms make the correction meaningful.
[c1-l033] Fogaracana. *Couſa clara, ou lucida como lũa,*
[c1-l033 note] Fogaracana is hogaraka na, clear or bright, exemplified by the moon. This physical clarity sense differs from the modern dominant cheerful-personality use.
[c1-l034 >] *&c. ¶* Fogaracana chiye. *Saber claro.*
[c1-l034 note] Fogaracana chiye is clear wisdom or understanding. OCR Fogatacana is inconsistent with both the visible r and the adjective above; Saber claro supplies the intellectual extension.
[c1-l035 >] Fogaracani. *Adu.*
[c1-l035 note] Fogaracani is hogaraka ni, the adverbial form. Adu. explicitly marks it as an adverb, with its sense inherited from the preceding clarity entry.
[c1-l036] Fogaſa. *Altura da vela que conforme ao vẽto*
[c1-l036 note] Fogaſa is hogasa, the height at which a sail is carried. The explanation refers to adjusting that height according to the wind, not to the sail's material or width.
[c1-l037 >] *ſe aleuanta, ou abaxa. ¶* Fogaſauo mo-
[c1-l037 note] The sail is raised or lowered. Fogaſauo mo- begins the corresponding example, with motçu continuing on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] tçu. *Por, ou leuar a vela em certa altura.*
[c1-l038 note] Motçu completes the Japanese expression for holding the sail at a certain height. The Portuguese gives setting or carrying the sail at that height; no extra noun is supplied.
[c1-l039] Fogaxi, u, aita. *Furar, ou abrir buraco. X.*
[c1-l039 note] Fogaxi is hogashi-, to pierce or make a hole. The Portuguese is Furar, not OCR Farar, and X. marks the regional use; its punctuation is kept with the upright label.
[c1-l040] Fôgiǒ. *Certa caſa, ou Zaxiqui que hà nas va-*
[c1-l040 note] Fôgiǒ is hōjō, a room or building within a temple establishment. The embedded room word is Zaxiqui, zashiki, not OCR Zaeiqui; its italic styling in this Portuguese line is retained.
[c1-l041 >] *relas como refeitorio onde comem. ¶* Fô-
[c1-l041 note] Varelas are the religious establishments in this account, and the room is compared with a refectory. Fô- begins the more specific hōjō no shitsu expression below.
[c1-l042 >] giǒno xit. *Caſa, ou cubiculo que ha den-*
[c1-l042 note] Fôgiǒno xit is hōjō no shit-, a checked ending for shitsu. Portuguese casa ou cubiculo explains the room; the final den- continues dentro below.
[c1-l043 >] *tro da varela.*
[c1-l043 note] Tro completes dentro, locating the cubicle inside the temple establishment. This short row remains part of the preceding definition.
[c1-l044] Fogue, uru, eta. *Eſburacarſe. X. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l044 note] Fogue is hoge-, to become holed or hollow. The regional X. is upright as printed; Itẽ introduces the hollow-tree example below, and the period is not assigned a separate italic run.
[c1-l045 >] *Fazer ſe oco como aruore velha, &c.*
[c1-l045 note] Fazer-se oco como arvore velha describes becoming hollow like an old tree. The historical aruore spelling and the source spacing are preserved.
[c1-l046] Fǒgue. Fanachi, cudaſu. *Abater alguem,*
[c1-l046 note] Fǒgue is hōge, dismissing or casting someone off, glossed hanachi, kudasu. The end mark after alguem is a comma in the scan, not a word-breaking hyphen.
[c1-l047 >] *& lançalo deſi. Vt,* Fitouo fǒgueſuru.
[c1-l047 note] Fitouo fǒgueſuru means dismissing a person. The scan supports Fitouo, not Ficouo, and retains an accent over the first o of fǒgue; the final mark is a stop, not an unfinished continuation into column 2.

[c2-l001] Fǒguen. Soxiru cotoba. *Murmuração.*

[c2-l004] Fogueta. *Verga do nauio.*
[c2-l004 note] Fogueta is hogeta, a ship's yard. The short first vowel distinguishes this nautical word from the preceding hōgeta for the jaw; Verga do navio confirms the sense.
[c2-l005] Fôguio. Von cuzzure. i, Teiuǒno xiqio.
[c2-l005 note] Fôguio is hōgyo, the sovereign's death, literally the honourable collapse in von cuzzure. Teiuǒno xiqio states the emperor's death explicitly; this is not an ordinary person's passing.
[c2-l006 >] *Morte del Rei.*
[c2-l006 note] Morte del Rei completes the hōgyo definition. The Portuguese specifies the ruler and matches the honorific Japanese expressions above.
[c2-l007] Fogomo. *Eſteira da vela, ou eſteira de pa-*

[c2-l010 >] *ueitão, ou materias enmẽdadas, & borradas.*
[c2-l010 note] The definition also includes corrected and blotted material. Enmẽdadas retains the old spelling and nasal abbreviation rather than being regularized to modern emendadas.
[c2-l011] Fô-i. Tacara, curai. *Riquezas, & hon-*
[c2-l011 note] Fô. i. is hō-i, wealth and rank, glossed takara, kurai. The i is part of this headword, not the explanatory Latin i.; the printed internal separator is retained even though it can confuse automatic kana hints.
[c2-l012 >] *ras. Vt,* Fô-i mini amaru. *Abun-*

[c2-l013 >] *dar em riquezas, & honras.*
[c2-l013 note] Dar completes Abundar: abounding in wealth and honours. This is the translation of the preceding Japanese example rather than a separate verb entry.
[c2-l014] Fǒ-i. Cǒbaxij cocoro. *Bom coração, ou*
[c2-l014 note] Fǒi is hōi, a kindly intention, literally kōbashii kokoro in the gloss. The dictionary uses fragrant figuratively for graciousness; the printed accent differs from the preceding wealth-and-rank entry.
[c2-l015 >] *boa, & amoroſa vontade, ou obra de amor. S.*
[c2-l015 note] The Portuguese gives a good and loving will or an act of love. Vontade, with a worn n, replaces OCR voitade; the final S. remains italic as a register label.

[c2-l016 note] Fô. i. here is hō-i, the force or efficacy of religious law, glossed nori no isei. Its i is part of the Japanese compound, not an explanation marker, and the Portuguese efficacy wording confirms that sense.

[c2-l017 note] Fô-i uo furù is hō-i o furū, displaying the teaching's authority. The grave-like accent on furù is left as the scan/OCR preserves it; it is not silently regularized to another long-vowel mark.

[c2-l019] Fô-i. i, Fôno curai. *Dignidade da lei, ou*
[c2-l019 note] A further Fô. i. is hōi, religious rank, explicitly fôno curai. The second i, followed by a comma, is the actual explanatory marker; the first belongs to the heading.
[c2-l020 >] *doutrina.*

[c2-l021] Foi. *Certa laya de veſtido de nono compri-*

[c2-l048 note] Mercè completes voßa mercè, the respectful address. The grave accent is visible; S. is retained as the register label, and Fojire below remains separate catchword furniture.
