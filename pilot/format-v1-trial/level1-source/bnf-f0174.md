[c1-l001 >] *do com papel algũa couſa, ou com peça fi-*
[c1-l001 note] The initial do completes grudando from f173: haritate means covering something completely by pasting paper or fine material onto it. The scan supports com, not dom; the latter disrupts the Portuguese construction.
[c1-l002 >] *na, &c.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes fina, qualifying peça in the preceding line. This is the end of the covering/pasting definition, not a separate entry.
[c1-l003] Faritçucai. *O ſangrar beſtas. Vt,* Fari-
[c1-l003 note] Hari-tsukai is the use of a needle or lancet, here specifically bloodletting in animals. The example repeats the headword across the line break.
[c1-l004 >] tçucaiuo itaſu.
[c1-l004 note] Together with Fari- this gives hari-tsukai wo itasu, to perform that bloodletting. Itasu supplies the action verb; this is not an independent tsukai entry.
[c1-l005] Faritçuqe. *Hũa maneira de portas de paſta de*
[c1-l005 note] Haritsuke here denotes pasted paper panels, not the unrelated crucifixion sense of the same modern reading. The long Portuguese architectural description governs the interpretation.
[c1-l006 >] *papel pegadas no parede com ſuas faſquias ao*
[c1-l006 note] The panels are pasted to the wall and have surrounding narrow wooden strips; fasquias is the key construction term. The scan appears to read no parede, despite the grammatical expectation na parede; the visible o is retained. Ao continues into redor below.
[c1-l007 >] *redor de pao vruxado de preto, pera que res-*
[c1-l007 note] The surrounding wood is black-lacquered. Vruxado is a Portuguese-shaped expression related to urushi; res- belongs to respondão on the next line, meaning that the panels match the other doors.
[c1-l008 >] *pondão às de mais portas de papel que eſtão n*
[c1-l008 note] Continues respondão: the fixed panels correspond to the other paper doors in the room. The final no is visually problematic: only an n-like form is clearly visible before the rule. Retained provisionally rather than treating the grammatical expectation as proof of a visible o.
[c1-l009 >] *Zaxiqi, & ſe correm, ou abrem, & fechão.*
[c1-l009 note] Zashiki names the room within the Portuguese explanation; the movable panels slide, open, and close. The scan has an uppercase Z and italic type, both preserved without imposing the more usual roman styling of Japanese insertions.
[c1-l010] Fariyaburi, uru, utta. *Raſgar, quebrar.*
[c1-l010 note] Hariyaburu means to tear or break, matching rasgar/quebrar. The inflectional abbreviations are kept as printed, including utta; they are not silently modernized from the kana guide.
[c1-l011] Farǒ. Nami. *Ondas.*
[c1-l011 note] Harō is the Sino-Japanese word for waves, explained by native nami and Portuguese ondas. This is unrelated to the following haru, spring.
[c1-l012] Faru. *Prima vera.*
[c1-l012 note] Haru means spring. The printed Portuguese separates prima vera; it is not joined to modern primavera in Level 1.
[c1-l013] Farubaru. *Adu. Ha muito.*
[c1-l013 note] Harubaru is treated here as an adverb of extended duration, glossed ha muito. Its familiar spatial sense should not overwrite this temporal Portuguese definition.
[c1-l014] Farubaruno. *Couſa comprida, ou longa. Vt,*
[c1-l014 note] Harubaru no modifies something long or extended. The following examples apply that length to a detour and a road.
[c1-l015 >] Farubaruno mauari. *Grande rodeo.*
[c1-l015 note] Harubaru no mawari is a long circuit or detour. Portuguese rodeo confirms mawari as going around, rather than an unrelated homophone.
[c1-l016 >] ¶ Farubaruno michi *Longo caminho*
[c1-l016 note] Harubaru no michi means a long road. Neither Japanese nor Portuguese is supplied with an editorial final period where the scan does not clearly show one.
[c1-l017] Farubi. *Cinta ou ſilha do cauallo.*
[c1-l017 note] Harubi is a horse's girth. Cinta and silha both identify a fastening strap, not the saddle itself.
[c1-l018] Farubi douoxi. *Buraco que eſta na ſella por*
[c1-l018 note] Harubi-dōshi names the saddle opening through which the girth passes. The printed douoxi represents the voiced compound form of tōshi; the following line completes its function.
[c1-l019 >] *onde paſſa a ſilha do cauallo.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes por onde: through which the horse's girth passes. This confirms that the headword denotes an opening rather than another strap.
[c1-l020] Faruca. *Longe, em muita distancia.*
[c1-l020 note] Haruka denotes great distance. The Portuguese is a direct spatial gloss, unlike the temporal use of harubaru earlier in the column.
[c1-l021] Farucaje. *Vento da prima vera.*
[c1-l021 note] Harukaze is spring wind: haru plus kaze, with je representing ze. It is not a derivative of haruka despite its neighboring position.
[c1-l022] Farucana, l, farucano. *Couſa diſtante, ou*
[c1-l022 note] Haruka na and haruka no are alternative modifying forms for something distant. The isolated l is the alternative marker, not part of Japanese.
[c1-l023 >] *longe. Vt,* Farucano michi. *Caminho*
[c1-l023 note] Haruka no michi exemplifies a distant or long road. Longe completes the adjective definition before the example begins.
[c1-l024 >] *comprido, ou distante.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the road example as long or distant. Comprido concerns extent, whereas distante can describe remoteness.
[c1-l025 >] Farucani. *Adu.*
[c1-l025 note] Haruka ni is the corresponding adverbial form. The short line supplies only the grammatical label, borrowing its meaning from the preceding entry.
[c1-l026 >] Faruqeſa. *P.*
[c1-l026 note] Harukesa nominalizes remoteness or distance. The P label is visibly upright here; its typeface is preserved separately from the italic Adu above.
[c1-l027] Faruco. *Bichos de ſeda da prima vera.*
[c1-l027 note] Haruko denotes spring silkworms. Bichos de seda establishes the insect sense; ko should not automatically be interpreted as a human child.
[c1-l028] Farugo. *Filho nacido de cão, ou animal ſeme-*
[c1-l028 note] Harugo, with voiced g, denotes a spring-born puppy or the young of a similar animal. The Portuguese distinguishes it from the silkworm entry above.
[c1-l029 >] *lhante na prima vera.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes semelhante and specifies spring as the time of birth. The seasonal phrase belongs to the offspring definition, not to an independent entry.
[c1-l030] Farugoma. *Cauallo que na prima vera brinca,*
[c1-l030 note] Harugoma is a horse frisking in spring. Goma is the voiced compound form related to koma; the next line adds its sexual excitement.
[c1-l031 >] *ou anda com o cio.*
[c1-l031 note] Cio gives an alternative description of the horse as being in heat. The definition concerns a springtime horse, not necessarily a wooden hobbyhorse.
[c1-l032] Faruſame. *Chuua da prima vera.*
[c1-l032 note] Harusame means spring rain. The Portuguese seasonal definition excludes the later food sense of the same Japanese word.
[c1-l033] Faſami. *Tiſouras, ou troquez, &c.*
[c1-l033 note] Hasami is the instrument, scissors or pincers. Troquez broadens the sense beyond cutting blades to implements that grip between two parts.
[c1-l034] Faſami, u, ǒda. *Apertar, ou tomar algũa*
[c1-l034 note] Hasami/hasamu is now the verb, to grip or hold something between two things. The abbreviated past ǒda preserves the historical contracted form rather than modern hasanda.
[c1-l035 >] *couſa entre duas como quem toma com tiſouras.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes the verb definition with the comparison of taking something in scissors. The shared concept is holding between opposing surfaces, not necessarily cutting.
[c1-l036 >] ¶ Sacanauo faſamu. *Tomar a Sacana*
[c1-l036 note] Sakana wo hasamu means taking food between chopsticks. The Portuguese reuses Sacana as an embedded Japanese noun; the broader food/accompaniment sense is possible, so it need not be narrowed to fish.
[c1-l037 >] *com as Faxis. ¶* Qirumono nadouo taqe
[c1-l037 note] Faxis is Portuguese-pluralized hashi, chopsticks, within the explanation. A separate Japanese example begins kirumono nado wo take: clothing or similar things held in bamboo.
[c1-l038 >] ni faſamu. *Meter o vestido atraueſado entre*
[c1-l038 note] Take ni hasamu completes the previous example: insert the garment across a split bamboo or between sticks. The locative ni identifies what holds it.
[c1-l039 >] *hũa cana aberta pello meo, ou ẽtre duas canas,*
[c1-l039 note] The Portuguese distinguishes a single cane split down the middle from two separate canes. Both arrangements explain the same Japanese gripping action.
[c1-l040 >] *ou paos. ¶ Permet.* Vma ippiqiuo fa-
[c1-l040 note] After completing the stick example, the dictionary marks a metaphorical use: uma ippiki wo hasamu, keeping a horse to ride. Ippiki is the animal counter, not a separate noun.
[c1-l041 >] ſamu. *Ter caualo em que hum poſſa caual-*
[c1-l041 note] Completes hasamu and explains having a horse on which one can ride. The physical image of gripping/straddling underlies the metaphorical use indicated above.
[c1-l042 >] *gar. Vt,* Vma ippiqi faſamu fodono fi-
[c1-l042 note] Cavalgar ends across the break. The Japanese example then describes a person of sufficient standing or means to keep one horse; fi- continues as fito.
[c1-l043 >] tode gozaru. *He homem baſtante pera ter*
[c1-l043 note] Completes fito de gozaru, the polite predicate describing that person. Bastante pera ter expresses sufficiency of means or status, not physical size.
[c1-l044 >] *hum cauallo, ou caualgar em cauallo. ¶* Fa-
[c1-l044 note] Ends the horse example and begins another Japanese phrase with Fa-. This fragment must be read with nauo below, not as an independent word.
[c1-l045 >] nauo fasǒde iru. *Eſtar diſſimulado ſem re-*
[c1-l045 note] Joined to Fa- above, the phrase reads hana wo hasōde iru. The Portuguese describes remaining reserved or dissembling rather than responding; the idiomatic connection with literal hana is uncertain. The scan supports the transmitted phrase, so no more familiar idiom is substituted.
[c1-l046 >] *ſponder, nem ter deuer com o que lhe perguntão.*
[c1-l046 note] Completes responder and describes not engaging with what one is asked. The historical wording ter deuer com is retained rather than recast as a modern idiom.
[c1-l047 >] ¶ Axiuo fasǒde fitouo itamuru. *Dar tor-*
[c1-l047 note] Ashi wo hasōde hito wo itamuru means hurting a person by clamping the feet. The next column supplies the torture apparatus; contracted hasōde belongs to hasamu, not a different verb.

[c2-l006] Faſamidaqe. *Cana em que leuão às costas al-*

[c2-l007 >] *gum veſtido, ou outra couſa.*

[c2-l008] Faſami ita. *Taboas entre as quaes dobrão, &*

[c2-l009 >] *apertão certas peças de Iapão.*
[c2-l009 note] Completes the paired actions dobrão/apertão, folding and pressing. The definition is of a two-board apparatus rather than a single tabletop.
[c2-l010] Faſamimono. *Couſa que ſe aferra cõ troquez,*
[c2-l010 note] Hasamimono is something grasped with pincers or between sticks. The following text narrows one common application to food taken with chopsticks.
[c2-l011 >] *ou dous paos, &c. & particularmente ſigni-*
[c2-l011 note] Two sticks provide an alternative to pincers. Signi- continues into fica; the ensuing special sense is an extension of the general gripping definition.
[c2-l012 >] *fica a* Sacana *que ſe toma com as Faxis.*
[c2-l012 note] Sacana and Faxis are embedded Japanese food and chopstick terms within Portuguese syntax. Faxis has a Portuguese plural ending; neither word is a fresh headword here.
[c2-l013 >] *¶ Item, Hum papel que poem por aluo me-*
[c2-l013 note] A second sense is a paper target, placed or held in a support. Aluo means target here, not simply something white.
[c2-l014 >] *tido entre hum bambu rachado.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes metido: the paper is inserted into split bamboo. The middle of rachado is worn, but the established reading fits the visible ascenders and the physical description; it is not replaced on shape alone.
[c2-l015] Faſamiqiri, u, itta. *Cortar com tiſouras,*
[c2-l015 note] Hasamikiri is cutting with scissors or pincers. The compact conjugation is retained as printed; its u abbreviation is not expanded in the diplomatic line.
[c2-l016 >] *troquez, &c.*
[c2-l016 note] Adds pincers or similar cutting implements to the scissors gloss. It concludes the preceding verb rather than introducing a new tool entry.
[c2-l017] Faſamitate, ru, eta. *Meter, ou por algũa*
[c2-l017 note] Hasamitateru is putting or fixing something between two supports. The tate element can also contribute an upright orientation, illustrated below.
[c2-l018 >] *couſa entre duas apertada. Item, Por al-*
[c2-l018 note] The first gloss emphasizes a tight fit between two things. Item begins a second, upright placement sense without creating a new headword.
[c2-l019 >] *gũa couſa aleuantada entre duas como adaga*
[c2-l019 note] Describes something standing between two objects, illustrated by a dagger. Aleuantada accounts for the tate element of the Japanese compound.
[c2-l020 >] *entredous Tatamis, &c.*
[c2-l020 note] The dagger stands between two tatami mats. Tatamis is an embedded Japanese noun with Portuguese plural s; the joined entredous is retained as printed.
[c2-l021] Faſamiuoroxi, ſu, oita. *Troſquiar. Vt,*
[c2-l021 note] Hasami-orosu means cutting or shearing off. The abbreviated past looks like oita in the scan; although morphology suggests oroshita, the diplomatic form is not expanded or repaired solely on that expectation.
[c2-l022 >] Amarino xencata naſani facufatuo faſami
[c2-l022 note] Amari no senkata nasa ni describes extreme helplessness. The object should semantically be hakuhatsu, white hair, confirmed by cabelos brancos below. However, enlargement shows a short t-like letter in facutatuo, not a clearly intact f: the visible reading is retained and the likely intended facufatuo is recorded here rather than silently substituted.
[c2-l023 >] voroſu. Feiq. L*ib. 4. Por mais não po-*
[c2-l023 note] Completes hasami-orosu; the source label Feiq. cites book 4 of the Heike. The scan shows the label and its period in roman type, followed by italic Lib.; the Portuguese describes cutting the hair in helplessness.
[c2-l024 >] *der troſquiou os cabelos brancos.*
[c2-l024 note] Completes poder and explicitly identifies the cut material as white hair. This is strong evidence for the intended Japanese hakuhatsu, but cannot repair an apparently defective printed letter by itself.
[c2-l025] Faſan. Sacazzuqiuo cubaru. *Mandar, ou*
[c2-l025 note] Hasan is explained by sakazuki wo kubaru, distributing or directing a drinking cup. This ceremonial sense is distinct from the accounting and brokerage homonyms below.
[c2-l026 >] *dirigir o* Sacazzuqi *a alguem. Vt,* Go-
[c2-l026 note] Sacazzuqi is the cup embedded in the Portuguese sentence. Go- begins the honorific form gohasan in the ensuing Japanese example.
[c2-l027 >] faſan are. *A quem quereis que ſe de o Sa-*
[c2-l027 note] Gohasan are is interpreted by the dictionary as asking to whom the cup should be given. The Portuguese cup name begins Sa- at the right edge and continues below.
[c2-l028 >] *cazzuqi.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes the Japanese loanword Sacazzuqi within the Portuguese explanation. Its scan type is italic despite the preceding occurrence being upright; the local distinction is preserved.
[c2-l029] Faſan. *O interceder, ou aueriguar opreço en-*
[c2-l029 note] This hasan is mediation or settling a price. Portuguese interceder and the following buyer/seller context distinguish it from both cup distribution and clearing an abacus.
[c2-l030 >] *tre os que comprão, & vẽdem. Vt,* Faſan
[c2-l030 note] Completes entre and identifies the parties as buyers and sellers. Hasan begins the example whose suru follows on the next line.
[c2-l031 >] ſuru. *Fazer eſte officio de corretor.*
[c2-l031 note] Hasan suru is acting as a broker. Corretor supplies the occupational sense and should not be confused with correcting written text.
[c2-l032] Faſan, i, faſuru. *O deſmanchar, ou desfazer as*
[c2-l032 note] Here hasan is clearing or undoing a completed reckoning, explained by hasuru. The following reference to counters establishes the abacus sense rather than modern financial bankruptcy alone.
[c2-l033 >] *cõtas depois de concluidas, ou baralhar os ten-*
[c2-l033 note] The reckoning has already been completed; its counters are then mixed or cleared. Ten- continues into tentos, the calculating counters, not attempts.
[c2-l034 >] *tos das contas. &c.*
[c2-l034 note] Completes tentos das contas. This physical resetting of counters explains hasan in its arithmetic sense.
[c2-l035] Faſǒ. Yabureta mado. *Ianela quebrada. P.*
[c2-l035 note] Hasō is a broken window, directly glossed yabureta mado. The P label and its point appear upright; the headword's open long vowel is not changed to the pronunciation of the following hason.
[c2-l036] Faſon. Yabure ſonzuru. *Quebrarſe o na-*
[c2-l036 note] Hason means damage or breaking; yabure sonzuru is the native explanatory phrase. The Portuguese applies it specifically to a vessel breaking or being wrecked.
[c2-l037 >] *uio ou fazer naufragio. Vt,* Funega faſon
[c2-l037 note] Completes nauio and gives fune ga hason shita as the example. Fune is the vessel and ga marks the subject.
[c2-l038 >] xita. *Quebrouſe, ou perdeoſe a embarcação.*
[c2-l038 note] Shita completes the Japanese past predicate. The vessel broke or was lost; perdeu-se here concerns shipwreck, not merely misplacing an object.
[c2-l039] Faſsacu. *Primeiro dia da oitaua lũa a que ſe*
[c2-l039 note] Hassaku is the first day of the eighth lunar month. Oitaua lũa identifies a calendrical month, not the eighth visible moon of a night.
[c2-l040 >] *faz festa em Iapão.*
[c2-l040 note] Completes the statement that this day is celebrated in Japan. The brief line belongs to hassaku and supplies its social significance.
[c2-l041] Faſſaina mono. *Molher que falla, ou palra*
[c2-l041 note] Hassai na mono describes a talkative woman in this definition. The uncommon adjective is supported by NINJAL's matching headword; it is not normalized to a more familiar modern insult.
[c2-l042 >] *muito.*
[c2-l042 note] Muito modifies talking or chattering on the previous line. It concludes the description of excessive speech, not the arithmetic entry below.
[c2-l043] Faſsan. *O diuidirnas contas. Vide* Giſan.
[c2-l043 note] Hassan denotes division in arithmetic, with a cross-reference to Gisan. The joined diuidirnas is retained; this is distinct from single-s hasan, clearing a calculation.
[c2-l044] Faſsǒ. *Oito eſtados que teue a vida de* Xaca.
[c2-l044 note] Hassō denotes eight stages of the Buddha's life. The scan shows an additional short s after the long one, matching NINJAL's Fassǒ; omitting it incorrectly merges this entry with hasō, a broken window. Xaca begins an embedded Japanese name in upright type.
[c2-l045 >] Bup.
[c2-l045 note] Completes Xaca-Bup, Shaka Buddha, from the previous line. This short fragment is body text in the definition of the eight stages, not a catchword or a new entry.
[c2-l046] Faſſocu. Axiuo vocoſu. *O partirſe pera a*
[c2-l046 note] Hassoku is setting out, explained as ashi wo okosu, lifting or setting the feet in motion. The scan supports uo, the object marker, not no; the Portuguese specifies a noble person's departure for war.
[c2-l047 >] *guerra algũa peſſoa nobre.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes the departure-for-war definition and identifies its subject as a noble person. The separate Fa at the lower right is the catchword, not part of this sentence.
