[c1-l001] Nuicucumi, u, unda. *Coſer por cima deixã-*
[c1-l001 note] Nuicucumi is nuikukumi, sewing over or enclosing something in a sewn covering. The Portuguese begins “sew over, leaving”; the printed headword has m, and deixã- continues on the next physical line.
[c1-l002 >] *do dentro algũa couſa, como bainha de fora que*
[c1-l002 note] The enclosed object remains inside, as with an outer sheath. This continues the definition rather than introducing another entry; the nasal abbreviation in algũa is retained.
[c1-l003 >] *ſe coſe ſobre a outra bainha mais interior da eſ-*
[c1-l003 note] The outer sheath is sewn over the other, inner sheath of the sword. The scan reads outra, not the OCR's ouira; eſ- continues as pada below.
[c1-l004 >] *pada, &c.*
[c1-l004 note] Pada completes eſpada, “sword,” and closes the example of a sewn outer covering. It is a genuine short continuation row, not furniture.
[c1-l005] Nuicucumizaya. *Sobrebainha que ſe vſa pa-*
[c1-l005 note] Nuicucumizaya names the sewn outer scabbard-cover just described. The Portuguese says an outer sheath used for a purpose continued below; the printed pa- is retained.
[c1-l006 >] *ra reſguardo da outra principal da Catana.*
[c1-l006 note] The covering protects the sword's main scabbard. Catana is a Japanese loanword embedded in the Portuguese explanation, not a new Japanese example.
[c1-l007] Nuifacu. *Bastidor com lauores dourados entre-*
[c1-l007 note] Nuifacu is nuihaku, textile decoration combining embroidery and gold work. Here bastidor denotes the embroidered work, with interspersed gold ornament; entre- joins ſachados below.
[c1-l008 >] *ſachados.*
[c1-l008 note] This completes entreſachados, describing the gold decoration interspersed in the embroidered work. The long initial ſ belongs to the Portuguese continuation, not a new headword.
[c1-l009] Nuimono. *Baſtidor, ou broslado.*
[c1-l009 note] Nuimono means needlework or embroidery. Both the missing i in the headword and broslado in the Portuguese are visible; broslado is the historical word for embroidered work, not the OCR's broclado.
[c1-l010] Nuimonoya. *Brosladores, ou caſa õde ſe faz*
[c1-l010 note] Nuimonoya denotes embroiderers or their workshop. Brosladores is retained in its printed historical spelling; the explanation continues with what is made there.
[c1-l011 >] *bastidor.*
[c1-l011 note] The workshop makes bastidor, embroidered work in this context. This short row finishes Nuimonoya and remains attached to that entry.
[c1-l012] Nuiſo. *Fio, ou linhas por torcer feitas de linho*
[c1-l012 note] Nuiſo denotes sewing fibre or thread; the Portuguese specifies thread not yet twisted, made from the material completed below. The long ſ in the headword is retained.
[c1-l013 >] *canemo.*
[c1-l013 note] Canemo completes linho canemo, hemp fibre. It identifies the material of the untwisted sewing thread rather than giving a separate Japanese term.
[c1-l014] Nuitate, tçuru, eta. *Acabar de coſer, ou de*
[c1-l014 note] Nuitate, nuitatsuru, nuitateta means to finish sewing or making up a garment. The historical tçuru ending is printed and should not be replaced with a modern conjugation.
[c1-l015 >] *fazer vestido, &c.*
[c1-l015 note] This finishes the alternative explanation “to make a garment, etc.” The existing short-s transcription of vestido is retained where the st ligature does not warrant an allograph change.
[c1-l016] Nuitçuqe, uru, eta. *Coſendo pegar. Vt,*
[c1-l016 note] Nuitçuqe, nuitsukeru, means to attach by sewing. Vt introduces the example on the next line and is part of the italic Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l017 >] Ixǒni amatano xuguiocuuo nuitçuqetari.
[c1-l017 note] The example says that many jewels were sewn onto a garment: isho ni amata no shugyoku o nuitsuketari. The final caron in Ixǒ and the historical x spelling are retained.
[c1-l018 >] *Part. 1.* Goſag. *Coſendo pegou muitas pedras*
[c1-l018 note] The citation reads Part. 1. Goſag.; the initial is G, not the OCR's C. The translation begins “by sewing, attached many stones.” The abbreviated source label is retained without a speculative expansion.
[c1-l019 >] *precioſas no veſtido.*
[c1-l019 note] These are precious stones attached to the garment, completing the preceding example's translation. The line is Portuguese throughout.
[c1-l020] Numa. *Atoleiro, ou lamaçal.*
[c1-l020 note] Numa is a marsh or bog; the Portuguese gives a mire or muddy place. This begins a new entry after the sewing vocabulary.
[c1-l021] Numameqi, u, eita. *Estar a couſa liſa,*
[c1-l021 note] Numameqi describes a thing being smooth or sleek. The Portuguese starts with liſa, “smooth,” and the printed historical form is retained rather than regularized to a different modern word.
[c1-l022 >] *& nedea.*
[c1-l022 note] Nedea adds “sleek” or “smooth” to liſa, completing the definition of Numameqi. No new headword is present.
[c1-l023] Numarito. *V,* Num arito xite, l, numeri-
[c1-l023 note] Numarito and the alternative numerito are adverbial expressions for a smooth or slippery manner. The visibly spaced Num arito in the example is retained as supplied, and numeri- continues below; V introduces the illustration.
[c1-l024 >] to xite. *Adu. Idem.*
[c1-l024 note] This completes numerito xite, an alternative to numarito xite. Adu. Idem identifies an adverb with the same sense as the preceding smoothness entry.
[c1-l025] Nume. *Peça, ou ſeda tecida de hũa ſoo cor ſem*
[c1-l025 note] Nume is a piece of plain, single-coloured silk fabric. The Portuguese explains that it is woven in one colour, continuing below with the absence of decoration; the printed joining ouſeda is retained.
[c1-l026 >] *lauores nem listras.*
[c1-l026 note] The cloth has neither decorative work nor stripes. This completes the definition of the fabric, not the preceding smoothness vocabulary.
[c1-l027] Numeracaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer eſcorregar.*
[c1-l027 note] Numeracaxi, numerakasu, means to make something slip. The causative sense agrees with fazer eſcorregar; aita is a historical inflection and is not an OCR error to modernize.
[c1-l028] Numeri, u, etta. *Meliùs,* Suberi, u. *Eſ-*
[c1-l028 note] Numeri means to slip, with Suberi recommended as the better expression by Meliùs. Suberi is thus a Japanese word within the dictionary's explanatory apparatus. Eſ- continues as corregar below.
[c1-l029 >] *corregar. ¶ Item, Ser algũa couſa liſa,*
[c1-l029 note] The first sense is “slip”; a second sense is for a thing to be smooth. The paragraph sign separates senses within Numeri, not separate entries.
[c1-l030 >] *& como eſcorregadia.*
[c1-l030 note] The second sense adds “and somewhat slippery.” Como qualifies the description rather than introducing a concrete example.
[c1-l031] Numeribiǔ. *Erua aßi chamada.*
[c1-l031 note] This is a plant name, explained only as “an herb so called.” Enlargement shows an abraded final u with a distinct caron, giving Numeribiǔ rather than the OCR's Numeribin; no botanical identification is supplied by the text.
[c1-l032] Numerito. *Vide,* Numarito.
[c1-l032 note] Numerito refers the reader back to Numarito, where the alternative adverbial forms were given. Numarito is a Japanese cross-reference inside the Latin-Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l033] Nuno. *Pano feito de linho canemo.*
[c1-l033 note] Nuno is cloth, here specified as fabric made of hemp. The entry's material-specific explanation is retained rather than broadened silently to all modern senses of nuno.
[c1-l034] Nunoco. *Vestido de linho canemo cõ borra.*
[c1-l034 note] Nunoco is a padded garment made of hemp cloth; cõ borra specifies the stuffing or wadding. The historical spelling with c is retained.
[c1-l035] Nunoya. *Caſa onde fazem, ou vẽdem nonos.*
[c1-l035 note] Nunoya is a place where cloth is made or sold. The embedded loanword is visibly nonos, a Portuguese plural, not an instruction to change the Japanese headword Nuno.
[c1-l036] Nuqe, uru, eta. *Paßar, ou ſair de parte a*
[c1-l036 note] Nuqe, nukeru, first means to pass or come out through something. The phrase de parte a parte continues onto the next row and means from one side to the other.
[c1-l037 >] *parte. ¶ Item, Aarrancarſe, ou tirarſe*
[c1-l037 note] A second sense is to come loose or be withdrawn. The scan retains the anomalous doubled initial Aa in Aarrancarſe; the ending is rſe, not the OCR's sfe. The spelling is not silently normalized.
[c1-l038 >] *como prègo, ou eſpada q̃ ſe deſem bainha porſi.*
[c1-l038 note] Examples are a nail that comes out or a sword that leaves its sheath by itself. The abbreviation q̃, the grave in prègo, and the supplied separation deſem bainha are retained.
[c1-l039 >] *&c. ¶ Item, per met. Eſcapulir, ou a-*
[c1-l039 note] A further, explicitly metaphorical sense is to slip away or escape. The terminal a- belongs to acolherſe on the next physical row.
[c1-l040 >] *colherſe alguẽ, que eſtaua preſo, &c. ¶* Yu-
[c1-l040 note] The metaphorical example concerns someone escaping from confinement. A new example then begins Yu-, which must be read with qinuquru below rather than as a separate word.
[c1-l041 >] qinuquru. *Paßar adiante dos outros an-*
[c1-l041 note] Yuqinuquru, begun above, means to go past others while walking. The Portuguese an- continues as dando, forming andando rather than an incomplete word.
[c1-l042 >] *dando. ¶* Qino nega nuqeta. l, fare-
[c1-l042 note] The damaged beginning completes andando; the lower remains of d support restoring it. The next examples are ki no ne ga nuketa and, across the line break, faremono no ne ga nuketa: a tree's root or the core of a swelling coming out.
[c1-l043 >] monono nega nuqeta. *Arrancarſe a raiz*
[c1-l043 note] This completes faremonono nega nuqeta. The Portuguese begins “the root comes out,” covering both the literal root and the core of a swelling; printed f represents the historical Japanese h-series pronunciation.
[c1-l044 >] *daruore, ou dalgum inchaço, &c.*
[c1-l044 note] The root belongs to a tree or, figuratively, to a swelling. Daruore and dalgum are retained contractions in the original Portuguese.
[c1-l045] Nuqegai. *O comprar algũa couſa às escondi-*
[c1-l045 note] Nuqegai is clandestine buying outside the normal trading arrangement. The scan reads couſa and escondi-, not the OCR's bouſa and es cendi-; the latter continues as das below.
[c1-l046 >] *das fora da armação, ou peço corrente.*
[c1-l046 note] The purchase is outside the trading arrangement or current price. The scan's weak word is retained as peço, as in the supplied transcription, rather than silently inserting an uncertain r to normalize it to preço.
[c1-l047 >] ¶ Nuqegai. l, nuqevriuo ſuru. *Coprar,*
[c1-l047 note] Nuqegai or nuqevri o suru means to buy or sell in this clandestine way; the Portuguese continues at the top of column 2. The OCR omitted nuqevriuo ſuru and the translation. The damaged printed Coprar is retained without supplying an unverified nasal mark.

[c2-l001 >] *ou vender algũa couſa deſta maneira.*
[c2-l001 note] This completes the bottom of column 1: “buy or sell something in this way,” namely clandestinely outside the usual trading arrangement. The column transition is continuous, with no missing intervening row.
[c2-l002] Nuqegara. *Pelle, ou caſca de cegarrega, ou*
[c2-l002 note] Nuqegara, nukegara, is a shed skin or shell. The first example is a cicada; the damaged Portuguese cegarrega retains a visible descending remnant of its second g, where the OCR substituted punctuation. The headword's weak second letter is u.
[c2-l003 >] *de outro qualquer bicho, que porſi tira, ou deſ-*
[c2-l003 note] Any other creature's shed covering is included: the creature removes or casts it off itself. The verb is tira, not the OCR's tura, and deſ- joins pede below.
[c2-l004 >] *pede do corpo.*
[c2-l004 note] This completes deſpede do corpo, “casts off from the body,” finishing the shed-skin definition. It is not the unrelated verb pede in isolation.
[c2-l005] Nuqenuqeni. *Adu. Eſcondidamente. Vt,*
[c2-l005 note] Nuqenuqeni means secretly or by slipping away unobtrusively. Adu. identifies an adverb and Vt introduces the following example.
[c2-l006 >] Racuchǔ fendono buximo nuqenuqeni
[c2-l006 note] The example refers to warriors in the capital and outlying districts coming secretly. The scan has Racuchǔ fendono buximo without the OCR's period after Racuchǔ; the caron belongs to the long final vowel of rakuchu.
[c2-l007 >] mairu. Tai. *Lib. 29. Os ſoldados de dẽ-*
[c2-l007 note] Mairu completes the Japanese sentence. The source citation is Tai., book 29, not the OCR's n9; the Portuguese translation begins “the soldiers from within,” with dẽ- continuing below.
[c2-l008 >] *tro do Miaco, & dos confins vinhão eſcondi-*
[c2-l008 note] The soldiers are from Miyako and the border districts and came secretly. Miaco is the embedded Japanese place designation; eſcondi- continues as damente below.
[c2-l009 >] *damente pouco a pouco.*
[c2-l009 note] The soldiers came secretly, little by little. This completes the adverbial example and its Portuguese translation.
[c2-l010] Nuqetouori, ru, otta. *Treſpaſſar de banda*
[c2-l010 note] Nuqetouori, nuketooru, means to pass through from one side to another. The printed touori spelling and its ru, otta inflections are retained.
[c2-l011 >] *a banda, ou ſer trespaßado.*
[c2-l011 note] The alternative Portuguese wording is “to be pierced through.” This completes the same entry rather than adding another headword or changing the Japanese inflection.
[c2-l012] Nuqevri. *Vide* Nuqegai.
[c2-l012 note] Nuqevri, nukeuri, cross-refers to Nuqegai, where clandestine buying and selling were treated together. Nuqegai here is an embedded Japanese reference after Vide.
[c2-l013] Nuqi. *Paos como ripas que atraueſão de co-*
[c2-l013 note] This noun Nuqi denotes horizontal timbers or ties resembling laths. The Portuguese starts to explain that they run from one column to another; co- belongs to colũna.
[c2-l014 >] *lũna a colũna de madeira.*
[c2-l014 note] The timbers run between wooden columns. The two colũna forms retain the printed nasal abbreviation and complete the constructional definition.
[c2-l015] Nuqi, qu, uita. *Arrancar, ou tirar. ¶* Ca-
[c2-l015 note] The verb Nuqi, nuku, means to pull or take out. A series of examples begins with Ca-, continued as catanauo nuqu on the next row; this is not the preceding architectural noun.
[c2-l016 >] tanauo nuqu. *Arrancar a Catana, &c.*
[c2-l016 note] Catanauo nuqu means to draw a sword. The second Catana is inside the Portuguese translation; the first belongs to the Japanese example split across rows.
[c2-l017 >] *¶* Manacouo nuqu. *Arrancar os olhos.*
[c2-l017 note] Manacouo nuqu illustrates pulling out the eyes. The example is Japanese, followed by its Portuguese translation; the historical manaco spelling is retained.
[c2-l018 >] *¶* Yauo nuqu. *Arrancar a ſeta que està*
[c2-l018 note] Yauo nuqu means to pull out an arrow. The printed mark over the final a in està is a grave, not a nasal tilde, and there is no full stop before the continuation describing the embedded arrow.
[c2-l019 >] *pegada, ou metida. ¶* Qeuo nuqu. *Ar-*
[c2-l019 note] The arrow is stuck or lodged in something. The next example, qeuo nuqu, concerns plucking hair, and its Portuguese verb Ar- continues below.
[c2-l020 >] *rancar os cabelos. ¶* Cuguiuo nuqu.
[c2-l020 note] The first phrase completes “pull out the hairs.” Cuguiuo nuqu then introduces pulling out nails; it belongs to the same series of examples for nuku.
[c2-l021 >] *Tirar, ou arrancar pregos. ¶* Axiuo nu-
[c2-l021 note] The translation explains removing nails. The next example, axiuo nuqu, begins the sense of extracting one's foot and is split after nu-.
[c2-l022 >] qu. *Tirar o pee como da neue, ou lama,*
[c2-l022 note] Axiuo nuqu means to pull the foot out, as from snow or mud. Pee and neue are retained historical Portuguese spellings.
[c2-l023 >] *&c. onde atolou. Vt,* Yuqini muneno a-
[c2-l023 note] The foot is being withdrawn from where it became stuck. A fuller example begins with snow reaching to the chest; a- starts atarimade across the line break.
[c2-l024 >] tarimade vochijtte axiuo nucanto ſure-
[c2-l024 note] The person has sunk into snow up to the chest and tries to pull the feet out. Nucanto is the intended-action form of nuku; ſure- continues into the concessive ſuredomo below.
[c2-l025 >] domo canauazu. Id. *Lib. 18. Acaruando-*
[c2-l025 note] The sentence ends “although he tried, he could not.” The citation is id., book 18, not iô; id. refers back to the previously named work. The Portuguese begins with the printed Acaruando-, describing becoming stuck.
[c2-l026 >] *ſe na neue tè os peitos poſto que fazia por ar-*
[c2-l026 note] Having sunk in snow as far as the chest, the person tried to pull free. Tè retains its printed grave accent, and ar- joins rancar below.
[c2-l027 >] *rancar os pees não podia.*
[c2-l027 note] Despite trying, the person could not pull out the feet. The scan reads podia, with i rather than the OCR's t in podta.
[c2-l028] Nuqi, qu, uita. *Enganar.*
[c2-l028 note] This separate Nuqi entry gives the figurative sense “deceive.” It has the same inflectional endings as the preceding physical extraction verb but is independently set as a headword.
[c2-l029] Nuqiauaxe, ſuru, eta. *Arrancar juntamẽ-*
[c2-l029 note] Nuqiauaxe means to draw together or in mutual opposition. The historical ſuru ending is retained; juntamẽ- continues the Portuguese adverb below.
[c2-l030 >] *te. Vt,* Tagaini catanauo nuqi auaſuru.
[c2-l030 note] The example has two people drawing swords against one another. The separated nuqi auaſuru is retained as printed rather than joined into a modern normalized form.
[c2-l031 >] *Arrancarem dous hũ contra outro as catanas.*
[c2-l031 note] The Portuguese explicitly says that two people draw their swords, one against the other. Catanas is a Japanese loanword embedded in this translation.
[c2-l032] Nuqiaxi. *O ir manſamente pee ante pee co-*
[c2-l032 note] Nuqiaxi means stepping quietly or stealthily, one foot after another. Both occurrences are printed pee; the second is not the OCR's shortened pe. Co- continues as como below.
[c2-l033 >] *mo com temor. Vt,* Tenni xe cugumari,
[c2-l033 note] The quiet stepping is described as fearful. The example begins with crouching or lowering oneself before heaven; xe is the dictionary's historical spelling, not a reason to alter the text to modern se.
[c2-l034 >] chini nuqi axiſu. *Abaxar a cabeça ao ceo,*
[c2-l034 note] The parallel phrase concerns treading cautiously on the earth. The Portuguese renders the preceding clause as lowering one's head before heaven; the spacing nuqi axiſu is retained.
[c2-l035 >] *& piſar a terra com medo, & reuerencia.*
[c2-l035 note] The second clause is translated as treading the earth with fear and reverence. This completes the paired example rather than defining a new religious term.
[c2-l036] Nuqicaqe, uru, eta. *Arrancar hum pouco,*
[c2-l036 note] Nuqicaqe, nukikakeru, means to begin drawing or draw a little. The following row specifies a partly drawn sword.
[c2-l037 >] *ou leuar mea eſpada.*
[c2-l037 note] The alternative wording describes drawing the sword halfway. Mea is retained as the printed historical form; it is not expanded to modern meia.
[c2-l038] Nuqi cutçurogue, uru, eta. *Arrancar hũ*
[c2-l038 note] Nuqi cutçurogue means to loosen or partly draw the sword. The final Portuguese word is visibly hũ, with a nasal abbreviation, not the OCR's hi; the separated headword elements are retained.
[c2-l039 >] *pouco a eſpada.*
[c2-l039 note] This completes “draw the sword a little,” specifying the limited action meant by Nuqi cutçurogue.
[c2-l040] Nuqifanaxi, ſu, aita. *Arrancar de todo, ou*
[c2-l040 note] Nuqifanaxi, nukihanasu, means to draw completely, with an alternative forceful sense continued below. The printed historical aita past form is retained.
[c2-l041 >] *com furia a Catana, &c.*
[c2-l041 note] The alternative sense is drawing the sword forcefully. Catana is embedded in the Portuguese explanation, not a standalone Japanese example.
[c2-l042] Nuqigaqi. *Cõpendio, ou recopilação. ¶* Nu-
[c2-l042 note] Nuqigaqi, nukigaki, is an extract or compilation. Nu- begins the corresponding verbal example on the next row; Cõpendio retains its nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l043 >] qigaqiuo ſuru. *Recopilar algũ liuro, &c.*
[c2-l043 note] Nuqigaqiuo ſuru means to make extracts or a compilation from a book. Algũ retains the printed nasal mark, and the example remains roman while its translation is italic.
[c2-l044] Nuqijiro. *Certa laya de peças.*
[c2-l044 note] Nuqijiro names a kind of textile or piece-goods. The Portuguese gives only this broad description, so a precise fabric identification is not inserted into the canonical text.
[c2-l045] Nuqiidaxi, ſu, aita. *Arrancar. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l045 note] Nuqiidaxi, nukiidasu, first means to pull out. The second scan pass and enlargement confirm two separate dotted i letters between q and d; the OCR's Nuqidaxi omitted one. Item introduces the further extract-or-compile sense below, and the historical aita inflection is preserved.
[c2-l046 >] *Recopilar. Mas não ſe vſa na pratica.*
[c2-l046 note] The second sense is to compile or excerpt, with a warning that this is not used in ordinary speech. The restriction belongs to this lexical sense rather than the whole page.
[c2-l047] Nuqijrede. *Mãos metidas por dentro das*
[c2-l047 note] Nuqijrede describes the hands placed inside the sleeves. The scan reads das at the row end, not the OCR's da-; the separate right-aligned italic mangas. below supplies the catchword for the continuation.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *mangas.*
[u2b-l001 note] This right-aligned word is printed below the last ordinary body row as the catchword; it continues the unfinished phrase about hands inside the sleeves. The italic word and its period are retained.
