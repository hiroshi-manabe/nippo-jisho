[c1-l001] Noriſonji, zuru, ita. *Caualgar mal ou contra*
[c1-l001 note] Norisonji introduces riding badly or contrary to proper practice. The zuru and ita principal parts belong to norisonzuru; the Portuguese qualification continues on the next row.
[c1-l002 >] *a regra, & ordem do caualgar. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the riding sense as violating its rules and order. Item introduces an additional, less exact use rather than a new headword.
[c1-l003 >] *minùs propriè, Caualgando danar, ou tratar*
[c1-l003 note] Latin minùs propriè qualifies the following sense as less proper: damaging or mistreating something while riding. The mark over u is a grave, like that over the final e, not a nasal-abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l004 >] *mal o caualo. ¶* Funeuo noriſonzuru. *Na-*
[c1-l004 note] The mistreated object is the horse. Fune wo norisonzuru then applies the same verb to navigating a boat badly; Na- begins the Portuguese verb continued below.
[c1-l005 >] *uegar mal, ou por a parte que não deuera.*
[c1-l005 note] Completes Na-uegar and explains going by an improper route. The boat example extends the riding verb to navigation, not to harming the horse again.
[c1-l006] Noriſute, tçuru, eta. *Deſcaualgando lar-*
[c1-l006 note] Norisute, norisutsuru, norisuteta denotes dismounting and leaving the horse behind. The lar- belongs to largar on the following physical line.
[c1-l007 >] *gar o caualo. ¶* Funeuo noriſutçuru. *Deſ-*
[c1-l007 note] After completing largar o caualo, fune wo norisutsuru describes abandoning a boat on disembarking. The second pass and enlargement retain the supplied hyphen after Deſ: a weak broken mark is visible to the right of ſ, so the first-pass omission was withdrawn.
[c1-l008 >] *embarcandoſe botar a embarcação por hi.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes Deſembarcandoſe: after getting out, leave the vessel lying about. Por hi is retained as printed, not modernized to a present-day expression.
[c1-l009] Noritçuqe, uru, eta. *Ser acustumado aſe ẽ-*
[c1-l009 note] Noritsuke first means being accustomed to riding or boarding. Acustumado is the visible historical Portuguese spelling; the nasal abbreviation in ẽ- begins embarcar across the break.
[c1-l010 >] *barcar, ou caualgar, &c. Vt,* Fune, l,
[c1-l010 note] Completes embarcar and introduces fune or uma as the vehicle ridden. The scan reads Fune, boat, rather than the OCR's Fone; l gives the alternative before the next line.
[c1-l011 >] vmani noritçuquru. *¶* Funeuo noritçu-
[c1-l011 note] Uma ni noritsukuru completes the habitual-riding example. A new construction, fune wo noritsukuru, then introduces bringing a vessel up to a destination or to others.
[c1-l012 >] quru. *Chegar com a Fune a algum porto, ou*
[c1-l012 note] Completes noritsukuru. The Portuguese says to arrive with the boat at a port; Japanese Fune is embedded in that explanation and is not an additional headword.
[c1-l013 >] *ſurgidouro, ou fazer ir a embarcação pegada,*
[c1-l013 note] Surgidouro supplies an anchorage as an alternative destination. The next sense has the vessel travel closely alongside or behind others, continued on the following row.
[c1-l014 >] *ou junto com as que vão diante. ¶* Vmauo
[c1-l014 note] The boats ahead complete the close-following sense. Vmauo starts the corresponding horse example, with the object particle attached as printed.
[c1-l015 >] noritçuquru. *Ir logo de tras, ou no alcã-*
[c1-l015 note] Uma wo noritsukuru is riding immediately behind or close upon others. The printed alcã- abbreviates the nasal in alcance; its terminal division is visible.
[c1-l016 >] *ce dos que vão diante a caualo. ¶* Noritçu-
[c1-l016 note] Completes alcance and specifies people riding in front. Noritçu- begins the imperative example inviting someone to draw nearer, continued below.
[c1-l017 >] qete gozare. *Ide chegando vos mais perto*
[c1-l017 note] Noritsukete gozare asks the listener to come closer while riding. The Portuguese is a directive to approach, with the reference point supplied on the next row.
[c1-l018 >] *dos caualos ou Norimonos, que vão diante.*
[c1-l018 note] The listener is to approach the horses or norimono conveyances ahead. Norimonos occurs within the Portuguese explanation, with its printed plural ending retained.
[c1-l019] Noritçure, uru, eta. *Caualgando, ou embar-*
[c1-l019 note] Noritsure means travelling in company while riding or embarked. The initial o has no secure vowel-length mark: the apparent OCR caron in Nǒritçure is not supported by the letter area.
[c1-l020 >] *candoſe ir em companhia.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes embarcandoſe and states the shared sense of going in company. This is a physical continuation of noritsure, not a separate entry.
[c1-l021] Noritodome, uru, eta. *Deter o caualo, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Noritodome denotes stopping or holding back the horse being ridden. The alternative object, the vessel, continues below.
[c1-l022 >] *a embarcação.*
[c1-l022 note] The vessel completes the pair of objects that may be stopped. This short row belongs entirely to noritodome.
[c1-l023] Noritoru, u, otta. *Tomar a fortaleza ſubin.*
[c1-l023 note] The verb denotes taking a fortress by mounting or climbing into it. The first printed form is retained as Noritoru despite the expected principal-part pattern; the faint line-end point after ſubin is also retained rather than regularized to a hyphen.
[c1-l024 >] *do a ella por manha, ou enganos eſcõdidamẽte.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes subindo: entering the fortress secretly by cunning or deception. The compressed nasal spellings in eſcõdidamẽte are preserved, not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l025] Norivatari, u, atta. *Nauegar.*
[c1-l025 note] Noriwatari is glossed simply as navigating or travelling by water. The printed u and atta are abbreviated principal parts, not independent Japanese words.
[c1-l026] Norivataxi, ſu, aita. *Paſſar nauegando, ou*
[c1-l026 note] Noriwatashi means crossing by boat or while riding. The visible v before ataxi restores the syllable wa omitted by OCR; aita is the historical s-verb past form and is not modernized to ashita.
[c1-l027 >] *a caualo. Vt,* Cauauo norivataſu. *Paſſar*
[c1-l027 note] Kawa wo noriwatasu illustrates crossing a river. The Portuguese reads caualo, with a damaged v, rather than canalo; the means of crossing is completed below.
[c1-l028 >] *a ribeira a caualo, ou na embarcação.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes the river-crossing translation: on horseback or in a vessel. Ribeira here supplies the watercourse crossed, not a separate destination on its bank.
[c1-l029] Norivcabe, uru, eta. *Fazer nadar a Fune,*
[c1-l029 note] Noriukabe denotes setting a boat afloat. Fune is a Japanese word embedded in the Portuguese causative explanation, while nadar describes floating rather than a person swimming.
[c1-l030 >] *ou polla em muita agoa de maneira que nade.*
[c1-l030 note] The explanation restates launching as putting the boat into sufficient water to float. Polla and agoa retain the historical Portuguese forms visible in the scan.
[c1-l031] Norivcabi, u, ǒda. *Nadar a embarcação*
[c1-l031 note] Noriukabi gives the corresponding intransitive sense: the vessel floats. The caron in ǒda is visible; the following clause specifies an occupied vessel.
[c1-l032 >] *em que està gente embarcada.*
[c1-l032 note] Specifies that people are aboard the floating vessel. The grave in està is preserved as printed, independently of modern Portuguese accentuation.
[c1-l033] Norivma. *Caualo de ſella. Mas mais propria-*
[c1-l033 note] Noriuma is a saddle horse, more precisely the horse someone rides. The first letter of ſella is long s, not f; the Portuguese saddle sense supports the visible type.
[c1-l034 >] *mente caualo em que hum caualga.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes propriamente and narrows the definition to a horse actually ridden by someone. This qualification belongs to noriuma rather than introducing another riding verb.
[c1-l035] Norivocure, uru, eta. *Ficar porſe embar-*
[c1-l035 note] Noriokure first means being left behind without embarking with the others. The printed vo sequence is retained; the interpretation does not require replacing it with modern spelling.
[c1-l036 >] *car com os outros. ¶ Item, Ficar detras*
[c1-l036 note] Completes embarcar with the others, then adds lagging behind as a second sense. The objects of detras appear on the following line.
[c1-l037 >] *das outras embarcaçoens ou dos que vão diante*
[c1-l037 note] The lagging traveller falls behind other vessels or people ahead. Embarcaçoens is the visible historical plural and is not standardized.
[c1-l038 >] *a caualo, &c.*
[c1-l038 note] Specifies that the people ahead are on horseback, completing the two travel settings for noriokure. The short continuation is ordinary body text.
[c1-l039] Norivtçuri, u, tçutta. *Paſſarſe de hũa em-*
[c1-l039 note] Noriutsuri denotes transferring from one vessel to another. The Portuguese reflexive is Paſſarſe, not OCR Paſſarte; em- begins embarcação across the line break.
[c1-l040 >] *barcação a outra. ¶ Item, Entrar o diabo*
[c1-l040 note] Finishes the vessel-transfer sense and introduces possession by a devil as another use of noriutsuru. The person entered is specified below.
[c1-l041 >] *em alguem. Vt,* Ano fitono cocoroniua
[c1-l041 note] Completes the possession gloss and begins the example 'in that person's heart'. The Japanese locative-topic sequence niua is attached to cocoro as printed.
[c1-l042 >] tenma fajunga norivtçutta. Xid. *Entra-*
[c1-l042 note] Tenma fajun is the demonic agent of possession; norivtçutta is the past of noriutsuru, with the visible v restored after nori. Xid. is the printed citation label, left unexpanded; Entra- begins the translation.
[c1-l043 >] *rão os diabos no corpo daquelle homem.*
[c1-l043 note] Completes Entrarão and translates the example as devils entering that man's body. The Portuguese renders the Japanese heart-expression more broadly as the person's body.
[c1-l044] Noriyaburi, u, utta. *Danar, ou tratar mal*
[c1-l044 note] Noriyaburi means damaging or maltreating the vessel one travels in. The past ending utta is retained as the printed principal part.
[c1-l045 >] *a embarcação os que vam nella.*
[c1-l045 note] The people aboard are the agents who mistreat the vessel. The printed vam is preserved rather than standardized to vão.
[c1-l046] Noroi, ô, ôta. *Rogar pragas. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l046 note] Noroi denotes uttering curses. The two circumflex-marked principal parts are kept; Item introduces another description of the same harmful invocation.
[c1-l047 >] *Fazer deprecaçoens pera que venha mal, ou*
[c1-l047 note] The invocation asks that evil should befall someone. Deprecaçoens is retained as the historical Portuguese noun rather than replaced with a modern paraphrase in the transcription.
[c1-l048 >] *a morte a alguem.*
[c1-l048 note] Death is the final alternative harm sought in the curse. This last line closes the entry and the column; it is not page furniture.

[c2-l001] Noroigoto. *Pragas. ¶* Noroigotouo yǔ.
[c2-l001 note] Noroigoto means words of cursing; noroigoto wo yuu illustrates uttering them. The final verb visibly has u with a caron, yǔ, not OCR yô.
[c2-l002 >] *Dizer pragas.*
[c2-l002 note] The Portuguese translates the preceding example as saying curses. This short indented row closes noroigoto.
[c2-l003] Noroxi. *Fogo queſe aleuanta por ſinal dalgũa cou*
[c2-l003 note] Noroshi is a signal fire. The visible cou continues as couſa on the next row without a terminal hyphen; the right rule is included in the crop.
[c2-l004 >] *ſa. Vt,* Noroxiuo aguru. *Aleuantar eſ-*
[c2-l004 note] Completes couſa and gives noroshi wo aguru, raising a signal fire. The Portuguese eſ- begins este across the physical break.
[c2-l005 >] *te fogo por ſinal.*
[c2-l005 note] Completes the translation as raising this fire for a signal. The row is the end of the noroshi entry, not part of the following word for ability.
[c2-l006] Nôſai. *Artes, ſciencia, ou ſaber. ¶* Nô-
[c2-l006 note] Noosai denotes arts, learning, or knowledge. The same word is divided as Nô-ſai in the example; both visible circumflexes are retained.
[c2-l007 >] ſai aru fito. *Homem que tem artes, ou ha-*
[c2-l007 note] Noosai aru hito is a person possessing skills or learning. The Portuguese ha- begins habilidades on the next row.
[c2-l008 >] *bilidades, & ſaber.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes habilidades and adds knowledge to the description. It supplies the abilities of the person in the Japanese example.
[c2-l009] Nôſô. *Bonzo letrado, ou ſabio.*
[c2-l009 note] Noosoo is a learned or wise Buddhist monk. Both vowels bear circumflexes here; the following similar-looking agricultural entry has a different final mark and short s.
[c2-l010] Nôsǒ. *O cultiuar, ou criar, & tratar bem a-*
[c2-l010 note] This noosoo entry concerns cultivation and careful tending, specifically of mulberries. The scan preserves short s and final caron in Nôsǒ, distinct from the preceding monk headword.
[c2-l011 >] *moreiras pera fazer ſeda, &c. S.*
[c2-l011 note] Completes amoreiras and explains their use in silk production. The final S. is an upright written-register label, outside the italic Portuguese gloss. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l012] Nôſui. Yoi mizzu. *Boa agoa. S.*
[c2-l012 note] Noosui is paraphrased by yoi mizzu, good water, and directly translated as Boa agoa. The Japanese paraphrase is not an embedded Portuguese term; final S. is the upright register label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l013] Notamai, ǒ, notamaixi. *Falar peßoa alta.*
[c2-l013 note] Notamai is speech by a high-ranking person. The supplied past form notamaixi and the caron in ǒ belong to the historical honorific verb, not a modernized notamau paradigm.
[c2-l014 >] *Vſaſe deste verbo nos liuros, & prègaçoẽs.*
[c2-l014 note] The usage note places the honorific verb in books and preaching. The historical spelling and printed vowel marks in prègaçoẽs are retained rather than regularized.
[c2-l015] Notame. *Hum certo inſtrumento cortado no*
[c2-l015 note] Notame names an implement for straightening arrows. This row begins its physical description, whose cut and purpose are completed below.
[c2-l016 >] *meo ao reues pera endereitar frechas, &c.*
[c2-l016 note] The instrument is cut across its middle and used to straighten arrows. Meo, reues, and frechas are transcribed as printed, not replaced with modern Portuguese forms.
[c2-l017] Notamegata. *Feição deste inſtrumento.*
[c2-l017 note] Notamegata denotes the shape of the arrow-straightening implement just described. The following example extends that physical shape into a figurative manner of acting or speaking.
[c2-l018 >] *¶* Notamegatani ſuru, l, yǔ. *Per met.*
[c2-l018 note] Notamegata ni suru or yuu is explicitly marked figurative by Per met. The two Japanese verbs mean doing and saying; the characterization follows below.
[c2-l019 >] *Fazer, ou dizer as couſas refolhadamente, &*
[c2-l019 note] The Portuguese describes doing or saying things with concealment or evasiveness. Refolhadamente is retained as the printed word, linked to the absence of simplicity on the next row.
[c2-l020 >] *ſem ſimplicidade.*
[c2-l020 note] Without straightforwardness completes the figurative gloss of notamegata. The phrase is not an independent definition of simplicity.
[c2-l021] Nôtçǔ. Nayami itamu. *O eſtar muito debilita-*
[c2-l021 note] Nootsuu is paraphrased by nayami itamu, suffering and hurting. The Portuguese reads debilita-, with a visible b rather than OCR l, and describes a greatly weakened condition.
[c2-l022 >] *do, & doente, ou mal dalgũa ferida, inchaço,*
[c2-l022 note] Completes debilitado and adds illness or distress from a wound or swelling. The phrase elaborates the bodily suffering of the preceding Japanese paraphrase.
[c2-l023 >] *&c. S.*
[c2-l023 note] Ends the illustrative causes of suffering and adds the upright S. written-register label. Even this brief continuation belongs to the nootsuu entry. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l024] Notorino coma. *Caualo tomado no mato que*
[c2-l024 note] Notori no koma is a horse caught in the wild. The relative clause continues below and distinguishes it from a fully tamed horse.
[c2-l025 >] *ainda não està bem domeſticado.*
[c2-l025 note] The wild-caught horse is not yet well tamed. The scan includes t in domeſticado, omitted by OCR; the negative and grave-marked està are retained.
[c2-l026] Nouaqi. *Vento rijo. Vt,* Nouaqi faxita-
[c2-l026 note] Nowaki is a strong wind. The example begins with hashitanoo, harshly or violently, divided in the print as faxita-nǒ; the Portuguese below makes the force of the wind explicit.
[c2-l027 >] nǒ fuite, côyô mina fuqi chiraſu. *O vẽ-*
[c2-l027 note] Completes faxitanǒ and says the wind blows all the autumn foliage about. The two circumflexes in côyô are visible; fuqi chiraſu retains its printed space.
[c2-l028 >] *to do Outono ventando rijo eſpalha, & derru-*
[c2-l028 note] Completes vento: the autumn wind blows hard, scatters, and knocks down the leaves. Derru- continues as derruba on the next row.
[c2-l029 >] *ba as folhas vermelhas, & velhas das aruores.*
[c2-l029 note] The objects scattered are the trees' red and old leaves. This completes the Portuguese interpretation of kooyoo in the example without altering the Japanese spelling.
[c2-l030] Noxe, ſuru, eta. *Fazer embarcar, l, caualgar.*
[c2-l030 note] Nose means making someone embark or ride. The abbreviated ſuru is the headword's historical principal-part ending, not a separate instruction to do something.
[c2-l031 >] *¶* Catani noſuru. *Por algũa couſa a os õ-*
[c2-l031 note] Kata ni nosuru means placing something on the shoulders. The damaged Japanese letters support Catani; the Portuguese õ- begins ombros, with its nasal abbreviation preserved.
[c2-l032 >] *bros. ¶* Niuo noſuru. *Embarcar o fato, ou*
[c2-l032 note] Completes ombros, then gives ni wo nosuru, loading baggage. The translation contrasts putting goods aboard with placing them on a pack animal below.
[c2-l033 >] *pollo na beſta, &c.*
[c2-l033 note] Completes the loading example by placing the baggage on a beast of burden. Pollo retains the printed historical object-pronoun construction.
[c2-l034] Noxi. *Hum instrumento de metal como eſquẽ-*
[c2-l034 note] Noshi names a metal pressing implement, compared with a warmer. The capital is N, not OCR X, and instrumento is a single printed word; the supplied st allograph is retained because the ligature does not securely decide s versus ſ.
[c2-l035 >] *tador, em que metem fogo peratirar as dobras*
[c2-l035 note] Completes esquentador: fire is put inside the implement to remove creases. The close peratirar spacing is retained rather than silently regularized.
[c2-l036 >] *do veſtido, & o dobrar bem, &c.*
[c2-l036 note] The implement smooths clothing and allows it to be folded neatly. This closes the noun entry before the corresponding verb begins.
[c2-l037] Noxi, ſu, oita. *Eſtender, ou tirar as dobras*
[c2-l037 note] Noshi, nosu, noita is the verb for extending or removing creases. The past ending oita is historical s-verb morphology and is not replaced by modern oshita.
[c2-l038 >] *com eſte instrumento. ¶* Xeuo noſu. *En-*
[c2-l038 note] The first sense uses the preceding implement. Se wo nosu then means straightening the back; Xe is ordinary Jesuit spelling for se, not an erroneous consonant.
[c2-l039 >] *dereitarſe a peßoa inclinada. ¶* Coxiuo
[c2-l039 note] Completes Endereitarſe: a bent person straightens up. The visible it restores dereitarſe rather than OCR dereuarſe; Coxiuo begins the parallel waist or lower-back example.
[c2-l040 >] noſu. *Idem. ¶* Xiuauo noſu. *Tirar as dobras*
[c2-l040 note] Koshi wo nosu has the same straightening sense. Shiwa wo nosu then concerns removing folds or wrinkles, with the affected material specified below.
[c2-l041 >] *do veſtido, ou estender as rugas da carne, &c.*
[c2-l041 note] Extends the smoothing sense from creases in clothing to wrinkles in flesh. The provisional st spelling of estender is retained, without a global allograph normalization.
[c2-l042 >] *¶ Itẽ, permet. Dilatar o coração, & eſparecer.*
[c2-l042 note] The figurative sense is to open or ease the heart and take recreation. Eſparecer is retained as printed rather than expanded to a modern-looking espairecer; Itẽ preserves the abbreviation mark.
[c2-l043] Noxi. *Lapas ſecas, & feitas em tiras compri-*
[c2-l043 note] This separate noshi entry concerns dried shellfish cut into long strips. The later noshiawabi synonym identifies the awabi connection; the Portuguese itself uses Lapas, which remains unchanged.
[c2-l044 >] *das que ſe guardão pera comer. No Ximo ſe*
[c2-l044 note] Completes compridas and says the strips are kept for eating. Ximo is embedded in the regional Portuguese usage note, which continues with the local name below.
[c2-l045 >] *diz,* Noxiauabi.
[c2-l045 note] Completes the regional statement: in Ximo the item is called Noxiauabi. This is a Japanese term within an ongoing Portuguese explanation, not an independent headword here.
[c2-l046] Noxiauabi. *Vide,* Noxi. *Idem.*
[c2-l046 note] The independent noshiawabi entry refers the reader back to Noxi and asserts the same meaning. Only the cross-reference Noxi, not the ordinary headword, qualifies as an embedded typeface term.
[c2-l047] Noxiba. *Vt,* Tacano noxiba. *Aſas hũ*
[c2-l047 note] Noshiba is illustrated by taka no noshiba, a hawk's extended wings. The Portuguese begins 'wings somewhat ...' and continues on f373; the isolated pouco below is its catchword, not another body row.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] *pouco*
[u2b-l001 note] The italic catchword anticipates the first word of f373. The OCR's final l is the nearby vertical rule, not part of the word.
