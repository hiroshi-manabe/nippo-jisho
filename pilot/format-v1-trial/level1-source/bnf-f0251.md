[c1-l001] Gǔyô. Faſuno fa. *Folhas dos golfãos de*
[c1-l001 note] The Japanese explanation is hasu no ha, lotus leaves, compared in Portuguese with leaves of aquatic lilies. The headword has a caron-bearing u, not OCR h; Folhas corrects Folhar. The plant name is read golfãos, with f rather than long ſ, from its word identity and the surviving cross-stroke.
[c1-l002 >] *lagoa.*
[c1-l002 note] Lagoa completes the description of the aquatic plants as growing in a pond or lagoon. This is a short continuation, not a separate plant name.
[c1-l003] Guzon. *Meu parecer baixo, &c.*
[c1-l003 note] Guzon is a self-deprecating expression for one's own opinion. The capital is G rather than OCR C; baixo conveys the speaker's humility.
[c1-l004] Guzzǔ. Firome touoru. i, Firomuru. *Di-*
[c1-l004 note] Guzzu concerns spreading or disseminating, explained by hirome and hiromuru. The Portuguese begins Di-uulgar; the scan has one ordinary D, not the draft's duplicated and separately styled letters.
[c1-l005 >] *uulgar.* Buppôuo guzzǔſuru. *Dilatar a ley*
[c1-l005 note] Uulgar completes divulgar. Buppo wo guzzu suru illustrates spreading Buddhist teaching; the repeated guzzu has a visible long-u mark, and ley restores the complete Portuguese word omitted by OCR ly. The following ſ is abraded, with a thin surviving stem and detached upper ink: ſuru is the chosen compatible reading, not a fully intact set of letters.
[c1-l006 >] *do Fotoque.*
[c1-l006 note] The Portuguese rendering is the law or teaching of the Buddha, named by embedded Fotoque. The explanation is retained without replacing its terminology with modern religious vocabulary.
[c1-l007] Guachiguiǒji. *Certo officio como de procu-*
[c1-l007 note] Gwachigyoji names a communal office comparable to that of a procurator. The gloss continues with the nature and duration of the appointment.
[c1-l008 >] *rador, ou mister dalgum pouo por eſpaço de*
[c1-l008 note] Rador completes procurador; the position is an office of a community held for a specified span. The scan supports por eſpaço, not the draft's par eſpeço.
[c1-l009 >] *hum mes.*
[c1-l009 note] The office is held for one month. This duration completes the Guachiguiǒji definition.
[c1-l010] Guachirin. Tçuqi. *Lũa circular, ou redõda.*
[c1-l010 note] Gwachirin refers to the round lunar disk, with tsuki as its Japanese equivalent. The Portuguese describes a circular or round moon.
[c1-l011] Guachô. Yeni caita tori. *Paſſaro pintado.*
[c1-l011 note] Gwacho is a painted bird, e ni kaita tori. The Portuguese directly confirms the depiction rather than a living species.
[c1-l012] Guacô. Yecaqi. *Pintor.*
[c1-l012 note] Gwako is a painter, also ekaki. The short definition gives an occupation, distinct from the painted object above.
[c1-l013] Guacqi. *Dia ẽ que morreo algũa peſſoa. ¶* Vo-
[c1-l013 note] Gwakki is the day of someone's death, used below as a commemorative occasion. The scan supports initial G, and Vo- begins oya no, referring to a parent.
[c1-l014 >] yano guacqi nareba, guacqi bǒzu nado-
[c1-l014 note] The example says that, because it is a parent's death observance, one calls the gakki bonze and others. The caron over bǒzu is preserved despite wear in the b.
[c1-l015 >] uo yobu. *Sendo dia em que morreo o pay,*
[c1-l015 note] Uo yobu completes the action of calling them. The Portuguese begins “being the day on which the father died.”
[c1-l016 >] *ou mãy chamar o Bõzo, & outros pera lhes*
[c1-l016 note] The parent may be the mother; a bonze and others are summoned. The tilde in Bõzo is the source's abbreviation, not a Japanese long-vowel caron.
[c1-l017 >] *comendarem a alma, & os conuidar.*
[c1-l017 note] The invited people commend the deceased parent's soul, and are entertained. Comendarem is retained as printed without adding an initial en-.
[c1-l018] Guacquǒ. Tçuqino ficari. *Claridade da lũa.*
[c1-l018 note] Gwakkwo is moonlight, tsuki no hikari. The initial letter is G rather than OCR C; the Portuguese directly confirms the lunar-light meaning.
[c1-l019] Guagu. Neru dǒgu. *Catre, cobertor, &*
[c1-l019 note] Gwagu denotes sleeping equipment, neru dogu, including a bedstead and a cover. The remaining category follows below.
[c1-l020 >] *outros aparelhos de dormir.*
[c1-l020 note] “Other equipment for sleeping” completes the bedding definition. The category is broader than either of the two examples already named.
[c1-l021] Guaibun. Focani qicoyuru. *Honra, ou*
[c1-l021 note] Gwaibun is reputation or honor, explained as what becomes known outside. The gloss continues with fame and illustrative expressions.
[c1-l022 >] *fama. ¶* Guaibunuo vxinǒ. *Perder*
[c1-l022 note] Gwaibun wo ushinau means losing one's reputation. The printed contracted form vxinǒ is retained rather than expanded to a modern spelling.
[c1-l023 >] *a honra. ¶* Guaibunuo tçucurô. *Ne-*
[c1-l023 note] The first example's translation ends “honor.” Gwaibun wo tsukuro then illustrates cultivating one's reputation, with the Portuguese verb divided after Ne-.
[c1-l024 >] *gocear, & grangear boa fama.*
[c1-l024 note] Gocear completes negocear; the expression is seeking and gaining a good reputation. The historical spelling grangear is preserved.
[c1-l025] Guaibun jitgui tomoni yoi. *Soceder bem*
[c1-l025 note] The expression says that both reputation and practical benefit are good. The scan reads continuous Guaibun, not the draft's Cua ibun.
[c1-l026 >] *aſsi na honra como no proueito.*
[c1-l026 note] The Portuguese explicitly pairs honor with advantage: “both in honor and in benefit.” The source supports aſsi na, correcting the draft's fused and malformed aſgina.
[c1-l027] Guaifǒ. i, guaijin. *Peßoa de fora como hos-*
[c1-l027 note] Gwaifo denotes an outsider, with gwaijin as its equivalent, such as a guest. The headword's weak caron is supported by its clearer repetition below; final n in guaijin corrects OCR u.
[c1-l028 >] *pede, &c. ¶* Guaifǒno fito. *Idẽ. X.*
[c1-l028 note] Pede completes hospede. Gwaifo no hito repeats the outsider sense; X. is an upright regional-usage label, not part of the Japanese phrase.
[c1-l029] Guaiji. Focano coto. *Couſa de fora.*
[c1-l029 note] Gwaiji is an outside matter, hoka no koto. The simple G initial replaces the OCR's spurious Ci sequence.
[c1-l030] Guaijin. Focano fito. *Homem foraſteiro, ou*
[c1-l030 note] Gwaijin is a person from outside, hoka no hito. The following qualification concerns belonging to the household, so the term is not restricted here to modern nationality.
[c1-l031 >] *que não he domeſtico.*
[c1-l031 note] The outsider is not of the household. This completes the definition's contrast between an outside person and a domestic member.
[c1-l032] Guaiqen. *Aparecer, ou moſtrar a outros.*
[c1-l032 note] Gwaiken is appearing or showing something to others. The next lines specify a negative construction and illustrate not showing a book.
[c1-l033 >] *Vſaſe ſempre no negatiuo. Vt,* Cono qiǒ-
[c1-l033 note] The dictionary says the expression is always used negatively. Kono kyo begins the example's reference to this book or scripture.
[c1-l034 >] uo guaiqen aruna. *Não moſtreis eſte liuro*
[c1-l034 note] The example prohibits showing the book: kono kyo wo gwaiken aru na. Its Portuguese translation likewise begins “do not show this book.”
[c1-l035 >] *a ninguem.*
[c1-l035 note] “To anyone” completes the prohibition. The very short row remains separate from the following headword.
[c1-l036] Guaiſǒ. Focano catachi. *Aparencia, ou*
[c1-l036 note] Gwaiso is outward form, hoka no katachi. The headword begins with G, not OCR C, and its caron contrasts with the next Guaiſô entry.
[c1-l037 >] *figura exterior.*
[c1-l037 note] “Exterior figure” completes the appearance definition. It does not concern the foreign monk described in the next entry.
[c1-l038] Guaiſô. Focano ſô. *Bonzo estrangeiro, ou*
[c1-l038 note] This Gwaiso is an outside or foreign monk, hoka no so. The circumflex in both Japanese forms is retained as distinct from the preceding caron.
[c1-l039 >] *foraſteiro.*
[c1-l039 note] “From outside” completes the foreign-monk definition. The Portuguese alternatives explain the same person, not two new categories.
[c1-l040] Guaiſobo. *. Auǒ da parte da mãy.*
[c1-l040 note] Gwaisobo is the maternal grandmother. The scan supports Guai-, not Guni-, and includes an extra separated point before the gloss. The accent over Auǒ is visibly caron-shaped and is not normalized to modern avó.
[c1-l041] Guaiſobu. *Auô da parte damãy.*
[c1-l041 note] Guaiſobu denotes the maternal grandfather, contrasted with Guaiſobo above. The middle ſ is badly worn and its surviving detached ink could be misread as a dotted letter; the chosen ſobu reading fits the surviving strokes, the paired entry and the supplied NINJAL index. The final consonant is b, not a silently modernized f. Auô has a circumflex, unlike the preceding Auǒ, and damãy remains joined.
[c1-l042] Guaixeqi, l, guexacu. *Parente por parte*
[c1-l042 note] Guaixeqi and the alternative guexacu denote a relative on the maternal side. The first capital is G, correcting OCR C; both alternative spellings remain roman, separated by the printed l, marker.
[c1-l043 >] *da mãy.*
[c1-l043 note] “Of the mother” completes the maternal-relative definition. The same relationship is shared by the preceding grandparent entries.
[c1-l044] Guan. Negǒ. *Deſejo. ¶ Item,* Voto.
[c1-l044 note] Gwan means a wish or vow, with negau as its Japanese equivalent. Negǒ is wholly roman. The Portuguese Voto is also locally roman, unlike italic Deſejo and Item and the italic Voto in c1-l047; this actual typeface change is preserved despite the shared language.
[c1-l045 >] *Vt,* Guanuo tatçuru, l, mǒſu. *Fazer*
[c1-l045 note] Gwan wo tatsuru and gwan wo mosu are alternative expressions for making a vow. The Japanese examples remain roman after italic Vt.
[c1-l046 >] *vato.*
[c1-l046 note] This completes Fazer vato, semantically making a vow. The worn second letter is retained as the aligned a reading: its dark body and separate right stroke do not securely establish the expected o. The apparent printed anomaly is recorded rather than silently changed to voto.
[c1-l047] Guandate, *l*, riǔguan. *Voto, ou promeßa*
[c1-l047 note] Gwandate and ryugan name a vow or promise. The scan supports riǔguan, not Aiuguan; the definition continues at the top of column two and is not artificially completed here.

[c2-l001 >] *feita aos Camis, ou Fotoques.*
[c2-l001 note] This completes Guandate / riǔguan from the preceding column: a vow or promise made to the kami or Buddhist sacred beings. Camis and Fotoques are embedded in the Portuguese sentence and are printed in its italic type.
[c2-l002] Guangu. Negai motomuru. *Deſejar, ou pro*

[c2-l003 >] *curar de alcançar. S.*
[c2-l003 note] This completes procurar de alcançar, seeking to attain the desired object. The terminal S. is a usage label, kept separate from the italic definition.
[c2-l004] Guanji, zuru, ita. *Fazer redondo entre as*
[c2-l004 note] The inflected verb is explained as making something round between one's hands. The following example identifies a medicinal preparation being rolled into a rounded form.
[c2-l005 >] *mãos. Vt,* Tôchincǒ nadouo guanzuru.
[c2-l005 note] The Japanese example applies guanzuru to Tôchincǒ and similar things; the gloss on the next row calls this a medicine. The two printed vowel marks are retained rather than homogenized.
[c2-l006 >] *Fazer redõda eſta mezinha, & outras couſas.*
[c2-l006 note] The translation is to make this medicine and other such things round. The nasal abbreviation in redõda sits over the rounded vowel, correcting OCR redãda; mezinha is the historical Portuguese word for a remedy.
[c2-l007] Guanjit. i, Xǒguachino tçuitachi. *Primei*
[c2-l007 note] Guanjit is explained by the first day of the first month: New Year's Day. Initial G replaces OCR C; the Portuguese word primeiro is divided across the physical line break with no added hyphen.
[c2-l008 >] *ro dia da primeira lũa do anno.*
[c2-l008 note] This completes primeiro dia da primeira lũa do anno, the first day of the first lunar month of the year. Lũa is retained with its visible nasal abbreviation.
[c2-l009] Guanjo. *Voto por eſcrito.*
[c2-l009 note] Guanjo denotes a vow committed to writing, not simply a spoken wish. The whole Portuguese gloss is italic.
[c2-l010] Guanju. *O que faz voto. ¶ Item, O que*
[c2-l010 note] Guanju names the person making a vow; the pilcrow introduces a related sense elaborated on the next two rows. The damaged but recognizable opening letter remains G.
[c2-l011 >] *por voto, ou deuação edifica algũa Tera, ou*
[c2-l011 note] The second sense is someone who, through a vow or devotion, builds a temple. Tera is an embedded Japanese noun within the Portuguese sentence, printed italic; the historical form deuação is retained.
[c2-l012 >] *faz outra obra pia.*
[c2-l012 note] The definition extends beyond temple construction to carrying out some other pious work. This is a continuation of Guanju, not a separate entry.
[c2-l013] Guanmǒ. Negǒ nozomi. *Deſejo.*
[c2-l013 note] Negǒ nozomi and Portuguese desejo identify a desire or wish. Enlargement resolves the headword sequence as n followed by m: the latter has three stems, unlike the OCR nin sequence. This scan reading also agrees with the supplied NINJAL lexical index; the final caron is faint but retained.
[c2-l014] Guanmon, l, guanjo. Negǒ fumi. *Voto*
[c2-l014 note] Guanmon and guanjo are alternative names for a written vow, explained literally by the Japanese phrase for a written wish. The italic definition continues below; l, separates alternatives and is not the numeral one.
[c2-l015 >] *por eſcrito em q̃ ſe pede ſaude, ou outro bem tẽ*
[c2-l015 note] The written vow petitions for health or some other temporal good. Both q̃ and tẽ preserve visible abbreviating marks; the latter continues as temporal on the next physical row.
[c2-l016 >] *poral pera alguem, & ſe promete de dar tal cou*
[c2-l016 note] The benefit is sought for someone, with a promise to give specified things if it is obtained. Pera is historical Portuguese; cou continues as couſa below, with no invented line-end hyphen.
[c2-l017 >] *ſa, & tal alcançandoſe a tal ſaude, &c.*
[c2-l017 note] This finishes the promised gift and makes its fulfilment conditional on obtaining the requested health. The initial ſa completes couſa from the preceding row, and the long-s spellings are retained.
[c2-l018] Guannen. Fajimaru toxi. *Anno nouo, ou*
[c2-l018 note] Fajimaru toxi explains the beginning year; the Portuguese supplies a new year or the beginning of a year. The wording is transcribed as printed rather than expanded to an inferred era-name definition.
[c2-l019 >] *principio do anno.*
[c2-l019 note] This completes the second Portuguese paraphrase for Guannen, the beginning of the year. It remains a short indented continuation.
[c2-l020] Guannin. *Os que fazem votos aos Camis, &*
[c2-l020 note] Guannin denotes people making vows to the kami and, in the continuation, Buddhist sacred beings. Camis is a Japanese-derived term embedded within the italic Portuguese definition.
[c2-l021 >] *Fotoques. ¶ Item, Peßoa que por algum*
[c2-l021 note] Fotoques completes the recipients of the vows; the pilcrow then introduces someone who undertakes a devotional construction project. The sharp-s form in Peßoa is preserved.
[c2-l022 >] *voto, ou deuação toma a ſeu cargo fazer algũa*
[c2-l022 note] This person takes responsibility for building something because of a vow or devotion. The tilde in algũa is visible, and the sentence continues directly to Tera below.
[c2-l023 >] *Tera, ou outra obra, & pera iſſo ajunta eſ-*
[c2-l023 note] The undertaking is a temple or another work, for which the person collects alms. Tera remains italic within the Portuguese sentence; the printed hyphen after eſ- carries eſmolas to the next row.
[c2-l024 >] *molas, perſuade aos outros, &c.*
[c2-l024 note] This completes eſmolas and adds persuading others to contribute. The final phrase remains part of the extended Guannin definition.
[c2-l025] Guanpon. Moto moto. *Principio, ou origẽ.*
[c2-l025 note] Moto moto explains the origin or beginning. The Portuguese origẽ retains the final nasal abbreviation rather than expanding it to origem.
[c2-l026] Guanrai. Motoyori conocata. *Deſdo prin-*
[c2-l026 note] The expression means from the beginning onward. The scan reads Guanrai, with r rather than OCR t and initial G rather than C. Motoyori is printed together: the o-y gap is ordinary interletter spacing, distinctly smaller than the following space before conocata; the OCR space after Moto is removed.
[c2-l027 >] *cipio ategora.*

[c2-l028] Guanriqi. Guanno chicara. *Efficacia do*

[c2-l029 >] *voto, ou deſejo.*
[c2-l029 note] This completes the two objects whose efficacy is meant: a vow or a desire. The row continues Guanriqi and does not begin a separate sense.
[c2-l030] Guanſo. *Cabeça, ou principio dalgũa gèração,*
[c2-l030 note] Guanſo is the head or originator of a lineage or family. The Portuguese reads geração rather than the OCR gdração; its nasal mark and the abbreviation in dalgũa are preserved.
[c2-l031 >] *ou familia.*
[c2-l031 note] The short continuation supplies family as the alternative to a lineage. It completes the ancestor or founder definition.
[c2-l032] Guanyacu. Marui cuſuri. *Mezinha feita em*
[c2-l032 note] Marui cuſuri explains a round medicine, with the Portuguese specifying the pill-like form below. The headword begins G, correcting the OCR C.

[c2-l033 note] The remedy is made into small balls, such as pills or the preparation called Saidaiji. The printed historical pirolas is not modernized; Saidaiji is a Japanese term embedded in the italic Portuguese comparison.
[c2-l034] Guanzan. *Tres principios, ou começos. ſ.*
[c2-l034 note] The entry gives three beginnings, enumerated on the next row. The damaged headword still supports Guanzan, not initial C; the final italic lower-case ſ introduces the enumeration rather than serving as a roman S. usage label.
[c2-l035 >] *do dia, do mes, do anno.*
[c2-l035 note] The three beginnings are those of the day, month and year. This explains the compound without changing the visible headword to a normalized reading.
[c2-l036] Guaraguarato. *Adu. Modo de cair monte*
[c2-l036 note] This adverb describes a rumbling or clattering manner, first illustrated by a mass of stones falling. Adu. is the printed grammatical abbreviation, not an extra headword.
[c2-l037 >] *de pedras, ou de ſoar trouão, &c. Vt,* Ca-
[c2-l037 note] The second application is the sound of thunder. The hyphenated Ca- begins the Japanese example's word for thunder on the next row; the example itself remains roman after italic Vt,.
[c2-l038 >] minariga guaraguarato naru.
[c2-l038 note] Together with Ca- above, the example reads Caminariga guaraguarato naru: thunder rumbles or crashes. The repetition of guara in the adverb is preserved.
[c2-l039] Guaramecaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer ſoar com rui*
[c2-l039 note] The verb denotes causing a noisy sound, contrasted with the intransitive entry below. Initial G corrects OCR C; rui continues as ruido without an added hyphen.
[c2-l040 >] *do, &c.*
[c2-l040 note] This finishes ruido and closes the causative-sounding definition. Although short, it is a distinct physical continuation row.
[c2-l041] Guarameqi, u, eita. *Fazerſe aquelle ruido,*
[c2-l041 note] This entry describes that noise occurring, rather than someone making it occur. The inflectional sequence u, eita is retained as printed, not regularized from a modern conjugation.
[c2-l042 >] *ou estrondo.*
[c2-l042 note] Estrondo gives the alternative sense of a crash or loud report. The st letterform is retained from the aligned transcription without imposing a global long-s normalization.
[c2-l043] Guarariguararito. *Modo de ſoarem as armas,*
[c2-l043 note] The reduplicative adverb describes the clattering of weapons, continued with spears below. The opening letter is G; the mark after armas is punctuation, not an additional s as in OCR armass.
[c2-l044 >] *lanças, &c. quando ſe peleja, ou quando ſe*
[c2-l044 note] The sound is associated with weapons and spears during fighting, or with the further action described on the next row. The repeated quando ſe belongs to the continuous Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l045 >] *arremeſa o caualo, &c. ¶ Item, Modo*
[c2-l045 note] The description includes urging a horse forward, then the pilcrow introduces another application of the same sound word. The printed single long s in arremeſa is not modernized to a doubled spelling.
[c2-l046 >] *de ſoarem as tripas quando rangem, ou de ſoar*
[c2-l046 note] The additional sense covers rumbling intestines and, in the continuation, a sounding cart. The terminal letter of rangem is m, not the OCR nt sequence; the definition's historical choice of verb is retained.
[c2-l047 >] *o carro, &c.*
[c2-l047 note] This finishes the cart-noise example; carro has two r letters, unlike OCR caro. The separate Gua- at the far right is the page catchword and is transcribed in its own furniture zone, not joined to this definition.
