[c1-l001] Tateyoco. *Ao comprido, & traues.*
[c1-l001 note] Tateyoco is lengthwise and crosswise; the Portuguese pairs ao comprido with traues. The two orientations belong to one headword.
[c1-l002] Tateyoxe, ſuru, eta. *Fechar hum pouco a por-*
[c1-l002 note] Tateyoxe is drawing a door partly shut. The listed suru form and the example below are historical forms, not a reason to replace the printed principal parts.
[c1-l003 >] *ta. Vt,* Touo tateyoſuru.
[c1-l003 note] Touo tateyoſuru illustrates partly closing the door; ta completes Portuguese por-ta. The upright Japanese follows the italic example marker Vt.
[c1-l004] Tatǒgami. *Papel dobrado, dourado, ou com-*
[c1-l004 note] Tatǒgami denotes folded paper, here gilded or decorated. Preserve the visible caron and the printed terminal hyphen after com, even though com is a complete preposition.
[c1-l005 >] *pinturas em que as molheres metem poſturas, ou*
[c1-l005 note] The decorated paper holds women's cosmetics or other belongings. Poſturas belongs to this material-use description, not a Japanese example.
[c1-l006 >] *varias couſas. ¶ Item, Papel dobrado que*
[c1-l006 note] The first description ends with assorted things; the paragraph mark introduces another use of folded paper. Its location is completed by the next row.
[c1-l007 >] *ſe traz no ſeyo.*
[c1-l007 note] The paper is carried in the bosom of one's clothing. This short continuation completes the second explanation of Tatǒgami.
[c1-l008] Tatoi. *Conjunção. Ainda que ſempre precede,*
[c1-l008 note] Tatoi is introduced as a concessive conjunction meaning even though. The statement that it precedes another expression continues below.
[c1-l009 >] *& depois ſe ſegue* Tomo. *Vt,* Tatoi toqi u-
[c1-l009 note] The dictionary says Tomo follows Tatoi. Tomo is consequently a Japanese grammatical term embedded in Portuguese, whereas Tatoi toqi u- begins a separate Japanese example.
[c1-l010 >] tçuri, coto ſarutomo. *Ainda que o tem-*
[c1-l010 note] The example continues utsuri, koto sarutomo: even though time and circumstances pass. Preserve its physical division after u- and the visible long s in ſarutomo.
[c1-l011 >] *po, & tudo paße, &c.*
[c1-l011 note] Po completes Portuguese tem-po; the rest says that everything passes. The translation completes the concessive example rather than adding a new sense.
[c1-l012] Tatoye. *Comparação. ¶* Tatoyeuo fiqu.
[c1-l012 note] Tatoye means comparison; tatoye wo hiku illustrates bringing forward a comparison or analogy. The following row supplies its Portuguese translation.
[c1-l013 >] *Trazer algũa comparação. ¶* Tatoyeuo
[c1-l013 note] Trazer algũa comparação translates adducing a comparison; a second expression, Tatoyeuo toru, begins after the paragraph mark.
[c1-l014 >] toru. *Idem.*
[c1-l014 note] Toru completes tatoye wo toru. Idem explicitly gives it the same comparison-making meaning as the preceding expression.
[c1-l015] Tatoye, uru, eta. *Comparar. ¶* Eccle-
[c1-l015 note] The verb means to compare. Eccle- begins Ecclesia inside a Japanese sentence; the scan shows upright letters throughout, not the OCR's alternating italic fragments.
[c1-l016 >] ſiauo tenno cunini tatoyuru. *Comparar*
[c1-l016 note] Ecclesia wo ten no kuni ni tatoyuru compares the Church to the kingdom of heaven. The Japanese object particle follows the divided loanword Ecclesia.
[c1-l017 >] *a Igreja ao reino do ceo.*
[c1-l017 note] The Portuguese completes the comparison between the Church and the heavenly kingdom. Preserve ceo rather than replacing it with modern céu.
[c1-l018] Tatoyeba. l, tatouaba. *Fazendo compara-*
[c1-l018 note] Tatoyeba and the alternative tatouaba introduce an example or comparison. The latter spelling is retained as printed rather than regularized to the preceding form.
[c1-l019 >] *ção, ou trazendo exemplo, ou aßi como, &c.*
[c1-l019 note] The gloss offers making a comparison, giving an example, or saying just as. It completes compara-ção across the physical line break.
[c1-l020] Tatta. i, Tada. *Tem varias ſignificaçòes.*
[c1-l020 note] Tatta is equated with tada and said to have several meanings. Both the headword and the examples support tt rather than OCR Taita. The small mark in ſignificaçòes is indistinct; the draft's grave-accent reading is retained as a nonblocking graphic caveat.
[c1-l021 >] *Vt,* Tatta fitocaiuo fucǒzu. *So hũa vez*
[c1-l021 note] Tatta hitokai wo fukoozu expresses an intention to blow only once. The scan supports fitocaiuo and fucǒzu, correcting OCR ſitocaiuo and focǒzu; the prospective ending is historical.
[c1-l022 >] *tangerei o bazio. ¶* Tattamononi xemu-
[c1-l022 note] The Portuguese completes the one-time sounding example; bazio is retained as the printed-looking spelling in the shell-horn context. Tattamononi xemuru starts an example of doing nothing but pressing or attacking.
[c1-l023 >] ru. *Não fazia ſe não apertar, ou combater.*
[c1-l023 note] Ru completes xemuru. The Portuguese says that he did nothing but press or fight; combater restores the damaged vowel and the coherent word in place of c mbater.
[c1-l024] Tattǒ. Michini taſſuru. *Perfeição em algũa*
[c1-l024 note] Tattǒ is explained through michi ni tassuru, attaining mastery in a discipline. The gloss continues the fields in which such perfection is attained.
[c1-l025 >] *arte, eſtudo, &c. Vt,* Tattǒno nin.
[c1-l025 note] The fields include arts and study. Tattǒno nin describes a master of such a discipline; the scan supports nin rather than OCR min and repeats the headword's tt.
[c1-l026 >] *Homem perfeito em algũa arte.*
[c1-l026 note] A person accomplished in an art completes the Japanese example. The damaged final letter in algũa is read as a, agreeing with the visible feminine arte, not OCR algũs.
[c1-l027] Tattoi. *Couſa ſancta, louuauel, bendita, &c.*
[c1-l027 note] Tattoi describes something holy, praiseworthy, or blessed. This religious and honorific range is kept distinct from the preceding mastery entry.
[c1-l028 >] Tattô.
[c1-l028 note] Tattô is the adverbial form associated with tattoi. Its upward circumflex is visible and is not assimilated to the caron in Tattǒ above.
[c1-l029 >] Tattoſa.
[c1-l029 note] Tattoſa is the corresponding abstract noun, the quality of being revered or venerable. The standalone form occupies its own indented physical row.
[c1-l030] Tattomi, u, ôda. *Venerar, honrar, &c.*
[c1-l030 note] Tattomi means venerating or honouring. Its listed contracted past ending ôda is preserved as historical morphology rather than replaced by a modern form.
[c1-l031 >] *Vt,* Qimiuo fucǒ tattomeba, xinuo vya-
[c1-l031 note] The example says that when one deeply honours the lord, one should also honour his retainer. The verb is tattomeba, not OCR tatromeba; vya- continues uyamau across the break.
[c1-l032 >] mǒ dǒri aruzo. Tac. *Honrando mui-*
[c1-l032 note] The Japanese finishes with a reason for honouring the retainer; Tac. is the upright source label. Honrando mui- begins the Portuguese rendering, whose next row completes muito.
[c1-l033 >] *to ao ſenhor, ha razão pera tambem eſtimar a*
[c1-l033 note] The translation explains that honouring the lord gives reason also to esteem his servant. Preserve the separate preposition a at this row's end.
[c1-l034 >] *o criado.*
[c1-l034 note] O criado completes the object of estimar a from the previous row. This short continuation is substantive body text, not furniture.
[c1-l035] Tattomiai, ǒ, ǒta. *Adorarem, ou reuerẽ-*
[c1-l035 note] Tattomiai describes multiple people joining in worship or reverence. The two caroned contracted endings remain distinct from the preceding verb's circumflex.
[c1-l036 >] *ciarem muitos algũa couſa.*
[c1-l036 note] Many people revering something completes the plural-action gloss. The line also finishes reuerẽ-ciar, preserving the printed nasal abbreviation.
[c1-l037] Taua. l, tauo. *Parte da ladeira, ou coſta q̃*
[c1-l037 note] Taua, alternatively tauo, denotes the less steep portion of a hillside or slope. The abbreviation q̃ introduces the relative clause continued below.
[c1-l038 >] *he menos ingreme. ¶* Tauouo coyuru. *Su-*
[c1-l038 note] Tauo wo koyuru is crossing the gentler slope. The entire Portuguese Su- is italic and begins subir; the OCR wrongly assigned its hyphen to roman type.
[c1-l039 >] *bir, ou paſſar algũa ladeira deſcanſada.*
[c1-l039 note] The example is translated as ascending or crossing an easy slope. The printed two long s letters in paſſar and the physical word division are retained.
[c1-l040] Tauabure. *Brinco, ou zõbaria, &c. ¶* Tẽ-
[c1-l040 note] Tauabure means play or jest. Tẽ- begins the Japanese tenshi, with an abbreviation tilde; its letters are upright, not partly italic as in the draft.
[c1-l041 >] xini tauabureno cotoba naxi. *O Rey não*
[c1-l041 note] Tenshi ni tawabure no kotoba nashi says that the sovereign has no frivolous words. The second-pass enlargement distinguishes a small word gap between Rey and não, restoring the separation in the Portuguese statement.
[c1-l042 >] *diz palauras leues, ou de zombaria.*
[c1-l042 note] The king does not utter light or jesting words. Diz and palauras correct the OCR's dez and pelauras; the historical u spelling is retained.
[c1-l043] Tauabure, uru, eta. *Zombar, ou brincar a-*
[c1-l043 note] The verb tauabure means to joke or play amicably. Its first Portuguese adverb is physically divided after a-.
[c1-l044 >] *migauelmente, &c. ¶* Cochô fanani ta-
[c1-l044 note] Migauelmente completes amigauelmente. The Japanese example says that a small butterfly plays among the flowers; ta- begins the repeated verb.
[c1-l045 >] uabururu. *A borboleta pequena anda brin-*
[c1-l045 note] The Japanese verb completes tauabururu; the Portuguese describes the little butterfly playing. Brin- is fully italic and ends in a hyphen, not the OCR's detached roman n and full stop.
[c1-l046 >] *cando, ou pouſando pollas fullas, ou flores.*
[c1-l046 note] The butterfly plays or alights among leaves or flowers. Preserve the printed-looking fullas rather than silently modernizing it to folhas.
[c1-l047] *¶ Item, per met. Fornicar.*
[c1-l047 note] The final paragraph adds a figurative sexual sense, fornication. It belongs to the same verb and remains a separate physical body row.

[c2-l001] Tauacoto. *Paruoices, deſuarios, &c. ¶* Ta-
[c2-l001 note] Tauacoto means foolish talk or nonsense. The divided Ta- begins the example containing the same noun rather than a separate entry.
[c2-l002 >] uacotouo yǔ. *Falar couſas fora de propoſito.*
[c2-l002 note] Tawagoto wo yuu is speaking things out of place or to no purpose. The Portuguese explains the pragmatic sense of the utterance.
[c2-l003] Tauame, uru, eta. i, Tauome, uru. *En-*
[c2-l003 note] Tauame is equated with tauome, a transitive bending verb. En- begins the Portuguese entortar and must remain on this physical row.
[c2-l004 >] *tortar, ou dobrar como bambu, pao, &c.*
[c2-l004 note] The gloss completes bending or making crooked, with bamboo and wood as examples. This is an action upon an object, not the later intransitive bending entry.
[c2-l005] Tauamure. *Vide,* Tauabure.
[c2-l005 note] The noun Tauamure explicitly refers to Tauabure, the earlier play or jest entry. The difference between m and b is preserved, not harmonized.
[c2-l006] Tauamure, uru, eta. *Vide,* Tauabure, uru.
[c2-l006 note] The corresponding verb likewise points to Tauabure, uru. This separate cross-reference preserves the distinction between the noun and verbal entries.
[c2-l007] Tauaqe, uru, eta. *Fazerſe paruo. ¶* Ta-
[c2-l007 note] Tauaqe means to act foolishly or become a fool. The example starts ta-waqeta mono, a person who has behaved foolishly.
[c2-l008 >] uaqeta mono. l, tauaqe. *Paruoeirão, ou*
[c2-l008 note] Tawaketa mono and tawake are alternative descriptions of a fool. The scan supports Paruoeirão, not OCR Parnoeirão; the Portuguese continues with a synonym below.
[c2-l009 >] *tolo, &c.*
[c2-l009 note] Tolo completes the pair of Portuguese words for a fool. It is a short continuation of the preceding example, not another headword.
[c2-l010] Tauaqemono. *Paruo, ou tolo.*
[c2-l010 note] Tauaqemono is independently glossed as a fool or simpleton. Its mono element is part of the headword rather than a Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l011] Tauara. *Fardo, ou ſaco darroz, trigo, &c.*
[c2-l011 note] Tauara is a bale or sack for rice, wheat, and similar goods. Darroz is retained with the printed contraction and spelling.
[c2-l012] Tauarago. l, Namaco. *Leſmas do mar que*
[c2-l012 note] Tauarago is equated with Namaco and described as an edible marine slug. The definition identifies the Japanese food name without requiring a modern species assignment.
[c2-l013 >] *ſe comem em Iapão.*
[c2-l013 note] The marine animals are eaten in Japan. This completes the definition of Tauarago/Namaco without extending it to unrelated sea slugs.
[c2-l014] Tauareme. i, Qeixei. *Molher publica.* P.
[c2-l014 note] Tauareme is equated with keisei and glossed as a prostitute or courtesan. The closing P. is an upright poetic-usage label, not part of the italic Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l015] Tauo. *Vide ſuprà.* Taua.
[c2-l015 note] Tauo sends the reader back to Taua above, the gentler part of a slope. The cross-reference does not supply an additional sense.
[c2-l016] Tauomacaxi, ſu, l, tauomaxi, ſu, aita. *Do-*
[c2-l016 note] Tauomacaxi and tauomaxi are alternative causative bending forms. Do- starts dobrar and is wholly italic; the OCR's isolated roman o was spurious styling.
[c2-l017 >] *brar, ou entortar como cana, ramo, &c.*
[c2-l017 note] The physical meaning is bending or twisting a cane, branch, or similar object. The following example extends this action figuratively to a person's heart.
[c2-l018 >] *¶* Fitono cocorouo tauomaſu. l, tauo-
[c2-l018 note] Hito no kokoro wo tawomasu, alternatively tawomakasu, means inclining someone's heart. The second alternative is divided after tauo-.
[c2-l019 >] macaſu. *Inclinar o coração dalguẽ a algũa*
[c2-l019 note] Macaſu completes the alternative verb. The Portuguese says to incline someone's heart toward something, with the destination noun continued below.
[c2-l020 >] *couſa.*
[c2-l020 note] Couſa completes the object of the inclination. This single-word continuation belongs to the preceding psychological example.
[c2-l021] Tauome, uru, eta. *Idem.*
[c2-l021 note] Tauome is assigned the same bending meaning by Idem. Its separate headword also supplies the target of the cross-reference near the top of the column.
[c2-l022] Tauomi, u, ôda. *Dobrarſe o pao, ou ramo.*
[c2-l022 note] Tauomi describes the wood or branch itself bending, contrasting with the preceding transitive forms. Preserve the circumflex in its contracted past ending ôda.
[c2-l023] Tauore, ruru, eta. *Cair no chão. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l023 note] Tauore means falling to the ground. Its historical ruru principal form is retained; the paragraph mark introduces a figurative application below.
[c2-l024 >] *permet. Cair de ſeu estado, renda, &c.*
[c2-l024 note] The figurative sense is falling from one's standing, estate, or income. The compressed permet. is retained as printed rather than expanded in the transcription.
[c2-l025 >] *¶* Xinxǒga tauoruru. *Cair o eſtado dalguẽ.*
[c2-l025 note] Shinshoo ga taoruru illustrates the ruin of someone's estate or standing. The Portuguese confirms this financial or social collapse, not a literal person falling.
[c2-l026] Tauorecacari, ru, atta. *Eſtar a couſa pera*
[c2-l026 note] Tauorecacari means being on the point of falling. The complement describing where the object may fall continues on the following row.
[c2-l027 >] *cair no chão, ou ſobre outra, &c.*
[c2-l027 note] The object may fall onto the ground or onto another object. This completes the impending-fall sense, distinct from the next entry's actual piling-up collapse.
[c2-l028] Tauorecaſanari, ru, atta. *Cair hũa couſa ſo-*
[c2-l028 note] Tauorecaſanari describes falling one upon another. The Portuguese begins the singular example and divides ſo-bre at the line end.
[c2-l029 >] *bre outra, ou hũs ſobre os outros.*
[c2-l029 note] The explanation expands from one thing falling on another to several falling upon each other. The worn conjunction is read as ou rather than OCR cu; the first vowel is weak.
[c2-l030] Tauorefuxi, ſu, ita. *Cair proſtrado.*
[c2-l030 note] Tauorefuxi means falling prostrate. The printed ita ending belongs to the listed historical forms and is not expanded to a modern past tense.
[c2-l031] Tauori, ru, otta. *Quebrar algũa couſa como*
[c2-l031 note] Tauori is breaking something off by hand. Its example objects, branches and flower sprays, follow on the next row.
[c2-l032 >] *ramos de aruore, ou raminhos de flores com*
[c2-l032 note] The objects are tree branches or small flowering twigs. Com continues into a mão below, specifying the means of breaking them.
[c2-l033 >] *a mão. Vt,* Fanauo tauoru.
[c2-l033 note] The action is performed by hand; hana wo taoru illustrates plucking or breaking off flowers. The Japanese example and the Portuguese description are mutually consistent.
[c2-l034] Tauoxi, ſu, oita. *Derrubar como aruores, &*
[c2-l034 note] Tauoxi is toppling something such as a tree. Preserve the historical principal part oita rather than substituting a modern -shita ending.
[c2-l035 >] *couſas que eſtão em pè. ¶* Taibocuuo qiri-
[c2-l035 note] The objects are things standing upright. Taiboku wo kiri-taosu begins an example of cutting down a large tree, divided after qiri-.
[c2-l036 >] tauoſu. *Derrubar algũ grande pao cortan-*
[c2-l036 note] Tauoſu completes the compound verb. The Portuguese likewise describes felling a large tree by cutting it; cortan- continues onto the next row.
[c2-l037 >] *doo. ¶ Item, permet. Dar com alguem*
[c2-l037 note] Doo completes cortandoo, cutting it down. The new figurative paragraph turns from felling trees to bringing about someone's downfall.
[c2-l038 >] *daueſo. i, Deſtruilo. Vt,* Neijinua fitouo
[c2-l038 note] The gloss explicitly explains the idiom as destroying someone. Neijinua fitouo begins a Japanese example concerning malicious flatterers and their victims.
[c2-l039 >] iy tauoſu. *Os liſonjeiros, & malicioſos deſ-*
[c2-l039 note] Ii taosu describes ruining someone through speech. The Portuguese identifies the agents as flatterers and malicious people; deſ- continues destruir across the break.
[c2-l040 >] *truem a os outros.*
[c2-l040 note] Truem completes deſtruem: they destroy others. The separate a os is retained as printed and belongs to this figurative example.
[c2-l041] Tauoyaca. *Couſa polida, & linda, branda, &*
[c2-l041 note] Tauoyaca describes something refined, lovely, soft, and graceful. The final coordinated adjective continues below.
[c2-l042 >] *gracioſa, &c.*
[c2-l042 note] Gracioſa completes the description of graceful softness. This is not the beginning of the following adjectival headword.
[c2-l043] Tauoyacana. *Idem.*
[c2-l043 note] Tauoyacana is given the same meaning as tauoyaca by Idem. The added na belongs to the Japanese adjectival form.
[c2-l044] Tauoyacani. *Adu. Polida, ou gracioſamẽte.*
[c2-l044 note] Tauoyacani is the adverb, glossed as acting gracefully or with refinement. Adu. is part of the italic grammatical apparatus.
[c2-l045 >] Tauoyacaſa.
[c2-l045 note] Tauoyacaſa names the corresponding quality of grace or softness. The form is printed alone on an indented row.
[c2-l046] Tavzzura. *Rũa que ſe come.*
[c2-l046 note] Tavzzura is explicitly said to be eaten. The unusual-looking Rũa is retained; it appears intended as the Portuguese frog word, but the vowel is poorly formed. NINJAL's supplied entry 243b23 corroborates the headword, not this gloss; an external lexical search did not resolve it further.
[c2-l047] Taxacu. Tano monouo caru. *Tomar empreſ-*
[c2-l047 note] Taxacu is borrowing another person's property, explained by tano mono wo karu. Tomar empreſ- is wholly italic and continues as emprestada on the next page; its catchword is separate furniture.
