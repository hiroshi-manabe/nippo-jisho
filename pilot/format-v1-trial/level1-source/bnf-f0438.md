[c1-l001] Saqiyaburi, ru, utta. *Raſgando romper, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Sakiyaburi describes tearing something apart. The scan gives initial S and the doubled t in utta, not OCR Baqiyaburi or uta; the explanation continues on the following row.
[c1-l002 >] *eſpedaçar.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the preceding definition with breaking or tearing into pieces. This is an indented body continuation, not a separate entry.
[c1-l003] Sara. *Bacio, ou ſalſeira.*
[c1-l003 note] Sara is a dish, here explained as a basin or a small sauce dish. The second Portuguese noun has two long-s forms, not f.
[c1-l004] Saraba. *Ficai em bora, ou ide embora. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l004 note] Saraba supplies a leave-taking expression addressed either to the person staying or to the person departing. The source varies em bora and embora on this same row; the abbreviated Itẽ introduces another use.
[c1-l005 >] *Eyaſus. ¶ Item, Iaque aßi he.*
[c1-l005 note] Continues saraba with an encouraging exclamation and the connective sense 'since that is so.' Eyaſus and the unseparated Iaque are Portuguese, not Japanese examples.
[c1-l006] Sadedani. *Seainda aßi, quãto mais, &c.* P.
[c1-l006 note] The gloss means 'if even so, how much more ...' and P. marks poetic usage. The tall third consonant supports printed Sadedani, also found in the supplied NINJAL index; its editorial suggestion Saradedani is not adopted into the transcription. Quãto, not OCR quãtao, is visible.
[c1-l007] Sarai. *Hum inſtrumento de bambu como pen-*
[c1-l007 note] Sarai names a bamboo rake, compared with a comb. The printed pen- is explicitly divided before tem on the next row.
[c1-l008 >] *tem comque ajuntam folhas das aruores caidas,*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the comb comparison and explains gathering fallen tree leaves. Comque remains joined as printed, and the row is indented beneath the headword.
[c1-l009 >] *ciſco, ou outra couſa.*
[c1-l009 note] Finishes the rake's uses with collecting litter or other material. Ciſco is Portuguese debris, not an additional Japanese word.
[c1-l010] Sarai. *Couſa de pouco fundo, como bacio, ou*
[c1-l010 note] This separate sarai entry means shallow, illustrated by a basin or tray. It must not be merged with the preceding tool-name homograph.
[c1-l011 >] *bandeja.* X.
[c1-l011 note] Bandeja completes the shallow-vessel comparison. The upright X. is the dictionary's regional usage label, outside the italic Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l012] Sarameqi, qu, eita. *Ser hũa couſaaſpera &*
[c1-l012 note] Sarameki denotes being rough or coarse. The principal forms qu and eita belong to the Japanese verb; the tight printed couſaaſpera is retained rather than editorially respaced.
[c1-l013 >] *toſca como pedra, &c.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the roughness definition with 'coarse like stone.' The indentation and Portuguese type connect it to sarameqi.
[c1-l014] Sarani. *Aduerb. De nenhũa maneira com verbos*
[c1-l014 note] Sarani is labelled an adverb; with negative verbs its sense is 'in no way.' The grammatical qualification continues on the following row.
[c1-l015 >] *negatiuos. ¶ Itẽ, Denouo, ou doutra maneira.*
[c1-l015 note] Closes the negative construction, then gives 'anew' or 'in another way' as further senses. The source's Denouo is not modernized or separated.
[c1-l016 >] *Vt,* Coreuo ſarani xôzu. *Farei iſto de ou-*
[c1-l016 note] The example says that this will be done afresh or differently. The scan supports capital Coreuo and Portuguese iſto, not OCR asto; xôzu retains its visible circumflex. The printed ou- continues below.
[c1-l017 >] *tro modo nouo.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes outro modo nouo: doing it in another, new way. This row belongs to the preceding example rather than beginning a new sense.
[c1-l018] Saranu tei. *Dißimulação.*
[c1-l018 note] Saranu tei is a pretence or an appearance of unconcern, matching dissimulation. The initial is S, correcting OCR Baranu.
[c1-l019] Sarariſararito. *Aduerb. Modo de fazer al*
[c1-l019 note] The repeated adverb describes doing something lightly and smoothly. Fazer has f, not OCR long s; no hyphen is visible after the divided al at the right rule.
[c1-l020 >] *gũa couſa ligeira, & expeditamente, ou ler algũa*
[c1-l020 note] Continues alguma and adds the application to reading fluently. The first and last tildes belong to u; the intervening Portuguese describes quick, unimpeded action.
[c1-l021 >] *couſa ſem empeçar, nem gaguejar, &c. Item,*
[c1-l021 note] Reading is done without stumbling or stammering. Enlargement supports gaguejar with a after j, replacing OCR gaguejer; Item begins another application.
[c1-l022 >] *Modo de correr de cima agoa, ſangue, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Gives flowing down or off a surface, exemplified by water and blood. De cima qualifies the flow and is not a Japanese phrase.
[c1-l023] Sararito. *Aduerb. Idem.*
[c1-l023 note] Sararito is the shorter adverbial form, referred back to the senses immediately above. Idem does not introduce missing text.
[c1-l024] Sara ſara. *Aduerb. Idem. ¶ Item, Com*
[c1-l024 note] The spaced reduplication sara sara shares those senses; a further construction starts with Com and continues below. The worn grammatical label is read as Aduerb., not the OCR fragment Adu rb.
[c1-l025 >] *negatiuo de nenhũa maneira.*
[c1-l025 note] With a negative, sara sara means 'not at all.' The first letter of nenhũa is n, not OCR m, and the whole line continues the preceding grammatical condition.
[c1-l026] Saraſarato. *Aduerb. Modo de eſfregar a*
[c1-l026 note] Sara sarato describes a rubbing or rustling motion. The initial is capital S, not OCR ç; the Portuguese construction continues with ir contas on the next row.
[c1-l027 >] *ir contas entre as mãos, l, de ir correndo, &c.*
[c1-l027 note] Explains passing or rubbing beads between the hands, then gives moving along at a run as an alternative. The printed wording eſfregar a / ir is retained; l is the alternative marker, not the numeral one.
[c1-l028 >] *Vt,* Ninin tomoni faxino vyeuo ſaraſarato
[c1-l028 note] Begins the example about two people crossing over a bridge briskly. Faxino vyeuo supplies 'over the bridge'; the Japanese sentence has not ended at this physical row.
[c1-l029 >] faxiriuatari. *Ambos de dous hião corrẽ-*
[c1-l029 note] Hashiriwatari completes the running-across example. No separate initial o is supported before f; that OCR addition is removed. The Portuguese says that both were running and prints a hyphen after corrẽ.
[c1-l030 >] *do por cima da ponte ſemparar nem tropeçar.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the example: over the bridge without stopping or stumbling. The scan supports final tropeçar, not tropeçor, while the source's joined ſemparar remains unchanged.
[c1-l031] Saraxi, ſu, aita. *Curar panos de linho, can-*
[c1-l031 note] Sarashi is the verb for bleaching or preparing cloth. The Portuguese specifies linen and begins the divided fabric name can-gas; the principal forms retain the historical aita ending.
[c1-l032 >] *gas, &c. ¶ Permet.* Fagiuo ſaraſu.
[c1-l032 note] Completes cangas and then introduces a transferred sense with Permet. Haji wo sarasu means exposing one's shame. The OCR's isolated Japanese-looking que was spurious: gas, &c. and Permet. are italic Portuguese apparatus.
[c1-l033 >] *Enuergonharſe. ¶* Cabaneuo ſaraſu. *Dei-*
[c1-l033 note] The first gloss means becoming ashamed. Kabane wo sarasu then describes exposing or leaving one's corpse; the worn e in Cabaneuo is preferred to OCR c on the surviving strokes and the corpse gloss, but remains a nonblocking damaged-glyph reading.
[c1-l034 >] *xar a vida no campo, ou o corpo ſem ſepultura.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes deixar: leaving one's life on the battlefield, or one's body unburied. This is the explanation of the corpse example, not a separate entry.
[c1-l035 >] *¶* Togauo ſaraſu. *Manifeſtar os peccados.*
[c1-l035 note] Toga wo sarasu is to disclose one's faults or sins. Manifeſtar requires the visible f after i; the OCR's Maniſestar confused letter identities rather than merely s-allographs.
[c1-l036 >] *¶* Gacumonni manacouo ſaraſu. *Dar-*
[c1-l036 note] Gakumon ni manako wo sarasu refers to applying one's eyes to study. The second n is worn but belongs to the particle ni after gacumon; OCR ui would misread that construction. The Portuguese Dar- continues below.
[c1-l037 >] *ſe muito ao estudo, ou estar ſempre ſobre o liuro.*
[c1-l037 note] Finishes dar-se: devoting oneself to study or always poring over a book. The provisional st expansions in estudo and estar are retained for the project's dedicated ligature audit.
[c1-l038] Saraxi. *Certos nonos curados.*
[c1-l038 note] This nominal sarashi entry refers contextually to prepared or bleached cloth. Enlargement nevertheless shows the anomalous printed nonos, not a securely legible panos; nonos is preserved rather than silently correcting the source.
[c1-l039] Saraye, uru, eta. *Alimpar como cano dagoa,*
[c1-l039 note] Saraye denotes cleaning out, illustrated by a water conduit. The Portuguese noun is cano, with a damaged final o rather than OCR c; dagoa retains the historical contraction.
[c1-l040 >] *fonte, &c. ¶* Inomotouo ſarayuru.
[c1-l040 note] Adds a spring to the cleaning examples, then gives inomoto wo sarayuru. Its meaning is established by the following explicit Portuguese gloss 'clean the well.'
[c1-l041 >] *Alimpar opoço. ¶* Foriuo ſarayuru.
[c1-l041 note] The first clause means cleaning the well. Hori wo sarayuru then concerns cleaning a ditch or moat; the scan's worn ri supports Foriuo rather than OCR Fonuo.
[c1-l042 >] *Alimpar a caua.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the ditch example with 'clean the ditch.' Alimpar is one word; the OCR's internal space has no basis in the printed type.
[c1-l043] Sareba. *Aduerb. Aßi que ſendo aßi.*
[c1-l043 note] Sareba is explained as 'thus, that being so.' Aduerb. is a single italic grammatical label, not OCR A duerb.
[c1-l044] Sarebacoſo. *Aduerb. Pois poriſſo.*
[c1-l044 note] Sareba koso strengthens the causal connection: 'precisely for that reason.' The headword is printed continuously despite the worn b; the OCR split Sareb acoſo is removed.
[c1-l045] Saredomo. *Aduerb. Porem, todauia, &c*
[c1-l045 note] Saredomo is the adversative 'however' or 'nevertheless.' The scan has no i between d and o, correcting Sarediomo; no terminal punctuation is supplied beyond the visible &c.
[c1-l046] Sarei. Chano rei. *Corteſias, ou comprimento*
[c1-l046 note] Sarei is the etiquette of tea, explained by cha no rei and Portuguese courtesies or formal greeting. The scan reads Sarei and Corteſias, not the OCR's Saiei and Corteſſas.
[c1-l047 >] *que ſe fazem antes. de beber o Cha.*
[c1-l047 note] Specifies courtesies performed before drinking tea. The small mark after antes is a visible full stop; the unusual punctuation is retained rather than regularizing the sentence. Cha remains in the printed italic gloss.
[c1-l048] Sari, ru, atta. *Apartarſe, ou irſe.* Toco-
[c1-l048 note] Sari is to depart or separate oneself. Toco- begins tokoro wo saru across the column boundary; it is body text, not a catchword, and the printed hyphen is preserved.
