## column-1-part-1 [column] Column 1, part 1

[c1-l001] Mozu. *Hum paßaro como picanço. ¶* Mo-
[c1-l001 note] Mozu is a bird compared with a shrike. Mo- begins mozu no kusaguki, the example naming the prey impaled by the bird; it also confirms the preceding page's catchword.
[c1-l002 >] zuno cuſaguqi. P. *Bichinhos, ou raãs*
[c1-l002 note] Zuno completes mozu no, followed by kusaguki. P. is a separate usage label; the Portuguese identifies small creatures or frogs, rather than another Japanese synonym.
[c1-l003 >] *que eſte paßaro deixa eſpetadas nos bambus, ou*
[c1-l003 note] The bird leaves these creatures impaled on bamboo or sticks. Preserve the printed feminine eſpetadas after the mixed list rather than grammatically regularizing the source.
[c1-l004 >] *paos pera quando não tem que comer que he no*
[c1-l004 note] The impaled prey is explained as food kept for a time when the bird has nothing to eat. The clause ends with the season on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] *inuerno.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*dia.*
[c1-l005 note] Inuerno completes the account of winter food. The separate far-right ( dia. is displaced text completing eſcorregadia in the following entry; it is retained at its physical position, not joined to inuerno.
[c1-l006] Mozzucu. *Certa erua do mar muito eſcorrega*
[c1-l006 note] Mozzucu is a very slippery sea plant. Eſcorrega continues in the displaced dia. above; no terminal hyphen is securely printed on this row.
[c1-l007] Mozzucubiyaxiru. *Hum* X*iru, ou caldo frio*
[c1-l007 note] Mozzucubiyaxiru names a cold broth made with the sea plant. Xiru is embedded Japanese inside the Portuguese explanation. The upright-looking capital X and italic lowercase iru retain the draft's mixed type treatment rather than forcing a whole-word font.
[c1-l008 >] *que ſe faz com esta erua.*
[c1-l008 note] The broth is made with this plant, completing the mozzuku entry. The following internal heading begins a new alphabetical subsection.

## column-1-part-2 [column] Column 1, part 2

[c1-l009 initial=2] MV. Naxi. *Não auer. Vt,* Mude go-
[c1-l009 note] Mu is explained by nashi, absence or nonexistence. The scan contains the enlarged two-line initial M before the small capital V; the draft lost M and treated the row as indented.
[c1-l010 >] zaru. *Ser nada, ou não auer.*
[c1-l010 note] Zaru completes mu de gozaru: being nothing or not existing. The row is indented beneath the enlarged initial and remains separate physical text.
[c1-l011] Muacu fuzǒ. *O fazer quantos peccados hà.* S.
[c1-l011 note] Muaku fuzoo is defined as committing every kind of sin. The negative elements in the Japanese phrase do not justify reversing the source's explicit Portuguese meaning; final S. is a written-language label.
[c1-l012] Mubai. Nacadachi naxi. *Sem terceiro, ou*
[c1-l012 note] Mubai, paraphrased nakadachi nashi, means having no intermediary. The Portuguese gives the same third-person or mediator sense.
[c1-l013 >] *não auer quem interceda.* S.
[c1-l013 note] No one is available to intercede, completing the mediator definition. The final S. label is upright rather than italic prose.
[c1-l014] Mube. P. Guenimo. *Com rezão, ou em*
[c1-l014 note] Mube is equated with genimo, truly or with good reason. The intervening label is P., not the draft F.; its print is upright.
[c1-l015 >] *verdade.*
[c1-l015 note] Verdade completes em verdade, the second paraphrase of mube. This short continuation belongs to that entry, not to the next health-related headword.
[c1-l016] Mubiǒ. Yamai naxi. *Boa diſpoſição.*
[c1-l016 note] Mubyoo is explained by yamai nashi, absence of illness. Boa diſpoſição accordingly refers to being in good health.
[c1-l017] Mubiǒna. *Peßoa bem deſposta, ou que não he*
[c1-l017 note] Mubyoona describes a healthy person. Preserve the historical deſposta in the Portuguese, without inserting the modern vowel of disposta.
[c1-l018 >] *doente.*
[c1-l018 note] Doente completes the negative phrase: someone who is not sick. It is a continuation of mubyoona, not a separate definition.
[c1-l019] Mubiǒxa. *Idem.*
[c1-l019 note] Mubyoosha is a person free of illness. Idem points back to the healthy person just described.
[c1-l020] Mubut xecaina mono. *Homem ſem primor*
[c1-l020 note] Mubut sekaina mono describes a person lacking refinement and religious concern. The Portuguese starts with absence of primor, not a literal account of an empty world.
[c1-l021 >] *nem corteſia, & que não tem deuer com a*
[c1-l021 note] The description adds lack of courtesy and lack of concern for what follows. The printed phrase ter deuer com is retained as part of the source's wording.
[c1-l022 >] *ſaluação, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Saluação completes the religious concern missing from the person described. This is the last part of a three-row definition.
[c1-l023] Mucaba. *Dentes dianteiros.*
[c1-l023 note] Mukaba means the front teeth. The clear Portuguese Dentes corrects the draft Denites, which inserted a nonexistent i.
[c1-l024] Mucabaqi. *Certo calçado de couro que ſerue*
[c1-l024 note] Mukabaki is described as a kind of leather footwear or leg covering. The next row specifies the occasions on which it is used.
[c1-l025 >] *na caça, ou em outros certos folguedos.*
[c1-l025 note] The leather covering is used in hunting and certain entertainments. These uses qualify mukabaki rather than introducing new entries.
[c1-l026] Mucade. *Centopeya.*
[c1-l026 note] Mukade is a centipede. Centopeya is the historical Portuguese spelling in the scan and is not modernized.
[c1-l027] Mucai. *Defronte.*
[c1-l027 note] Mukai is glossed as opposite or facing. This short positional entry is distinct from the conjugated verb below.
[c1-l028] Mucai, ǒ, ǒta. *Virarſe pera algũa parte, ou*
[c1-l028 note] Mukai, with its principal parts, means turning toward somewhere or placing oneself opposite. The printed long-o forms retain their downward marks.
[c1-l029 >] *porſe de fronte. ¶* Faruni mucǒ. *Chegar*
[c1-l029 note] The positional gloss ends in de fronte. Haru ni mukoo then illustrates approaching spring; Chegar continues with the reflexive particle below.
[c1-l030 >] *ſe a primauera. ¶* Teqini mucǒ. *Sair*
[c1-l030 note] Se a primauera completes the approach to spring. Teki ni mukoo is the contrasting example of going out to face an enemy.
[c1-l031 >] *ao encontro ao imigo.*
[c1-l031 note] Ao encontro ao imigo completes the enemy-facing example. Preserve imigo rather than expanding it to modern inimigo.
[c1-l032] Mucai. *Encontro. ¶* Mucaini ſanzuru,
[c1-l032 note] The noun mukai means going to meet someone. Mukai ni sanzuru begins a series of alternative verbs for going out to meet.
[c1-l033 >] mairu, izzuru. *Ir ao encontro. ¶* Mu-
[c1-l033 note] Mairu and izzuru continue the alternative verbs meaning to go out to meet. Mu- begins a new example of sending someone to meet another person.
[c1-l034 >] caini tçucauaſu, l, yaru. *Mandar ao en-*
[c1-l034 note] Kaini completes mukaini; tsukawasu and yaru are alternatives for sending. Mandar ao en- starts the matching Portuguese phrase.
[c1-l035 >] *contro.*
[c1-l035 note] Contro completes encontro, finishing the sending-to-meet example. The row is a short continuation, not a displaced catchword.
[c1-l036] Mucaibune. *Embarcação que vai ao encontro.*
[c1-l036 note] Mukaibune is a vessel sent or going out to meet someone. The Portuguese definition is complete on this row.
[c1-l037] Mucai tçubute. *Vt,* Mucai tçubuteuo ſuru.
[c1-l037 note] Mukai tsubute is illustrated with the verb suru, referring to throwing stones at one another. The next rows explain a preferred expression.
[c1-l038 >] *Iugar às pedradas: mas a propria palaura be*
[c1-l038 note] The gloss describes stone-throwing play and introduces the proper expression below. The final be-like form is retained: its lower bowl appears closed, unlike the clear h in c1-l004. Portuguese he is expected, so this remains a nonblocking glyph caveat.
[c1-l039 >] *Ingiuo ſuru.*
[c1-l039 note] Ingi wo suru is the alternative expression supplied inside the Portuguese sentence about the proper term. The scan prints this Japanese phrase in italic type; it is not an independent headword.
[c1-l040] Mucaitori, u, otta. *Meliùs,* Mucayetoru.
[c1-l040 note] Mukaitori is referred to the preferred mukayetoru form by Meliùs. The editorial Latin is italic, while the Japanese cross-reference is upright.
[c1-l041 >] *Indo ao encontro receber. Vt,* Coyeuo taz-
[c1-l041 note] The action is going out to receive someone. Koe wo tazunete begins a quotation about seeking the voices of those who call, with taz- divided before zunete.
[c1-l042 >] zunete mucayetoraxerareǒzurutono gui
[c1-l042 note] Zunete completes tazunete, followed by the honorific prospective form of receiving or welcoming. The quotation's gui continues as gui gia across the row boundary.
[c1-l043 >] gia fodoni, camayete nenbutuo vocota-
[c1-l043 note] The quotation urges care not to neglect nenbutsu. Vocota- is the start of the negative admonition completed below, not a new lexical item.
[c1-l044 >] raxerarena. Feiq. *Lib. 2. Porque o*
[c1-l044 note] Raxerarena completes the warning not to neglect the invocation. Feiq. is the upright source label, followed by italic Lib. 2. and the beginning of the Portuguese translation.
[c1-l045 >] *Fotoque vindo em buſca, & ao encontro dos*
[c1-l045 note] The Portuguese names Fotoque as the one who comes seeking and meeting those who call. Fotoque is an embedded Japanese term; the relative clause continues in the next column.

[c2-l001 >] *que o chamão os leua pera ſi, olhai que não*
[c2-l001 note] Continues the translation from column 1: those who call are taken to him, followed by a warning. The opening que is running text, not a new entry or a duplicated line.
[c2-l002 >] *falteis, ou vos deſcuideis em o inuocar.*
[c2-l002 note] The warning is not to fail or become careless in invoking him. This closes the quoted example under mukaitori.
[c2-l003] Mucaivma. *Caualo que vai ao encontro.*
[c2-l003 note] Mukaiuma is a horse going out to meet someone. The medial v in Mucaivma retains the source's positional spelling for the vowel of uma.
[c2-l004] Mucaizzura. *Frontaria, ou couſa que esta*
[c2-l004 note] Mukaizura is a frontage or something facing one. The definition continues with de fronte on the next row.
[c2-l005 >] *de fronte.* B.
[c2-l005 note] De fronte completes the facing-position gloss. The final upright B. is a usage label, not part of the italic explanation.
[c2-l006] Mucauaribi. *Dia que reſponde de pois do anno*
[c2-l006 note] Mukawaribi is the corresponding day after a year, an anniversary. Preserve the printed separation de pois rather than silently making depois.
[c2-l007 >] *ao dia em que hum morreo, ou fez algũa cou-*
[c2-l007 note] The anniversary refers to the day someone died or performed a notable deed. Cou- continues as couſa below.
[c2-l008 >] *ſa notauel, &c.*
[c2-l008 note] Sa notauel completes the reference to a notable act. This closes the anniversary-day entry.
[c2-l009] Mucauarizzuqi. *Mes que no anno ſeguinte*
[c2-l009 note] Mukawarizuki is the corresponding month in the following year. Its definition parallels the preceding anniversary-day term.
[c2-l010 >] *reſpõde a outro em q̃ hũ morreo, a doeceo, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] The month corresponds to a death, illness, or similar event. The printed a doeceo spacing is retained; semantically it is the verb for becoming ill.
[c2-l011] Mucaxi. *Antiguamente. ¶* Mucaxiuo xi-
[c2-l011 note] Mukashi means formerly or in olden times. Mukashi wo shinobu begins an example of longing for the past, divided after xi-.
[c2-l012 >] nobu. *Ter ſaudades das couſas paſſadas, &*
[c2-l012 note] Nobu completes shinobu. The Portuguese saudades describes nostalgic longing for things past.
[c2-l013 >] *antiguas.*
[c2-l013 note] Antiguas completes the paired description of past and old things. The historical spelling retains its u.
[c2-l014] Mucaxigatari. *Pratica de couſas paßadas,*
[c2-l014 note] Mukashigatari is talk or narration about past events. Pratica here refers to discourse, not to practical exercise.
[c2-l015 >] *& antiguas.*
[c2-l015 note] Antiguas completes the description of bygone matters. This continuation closes mukashigatari before the adjectival form below.
[c2-l016] Mucaxino. *Couſa antigua.*
[c2-l016 note] Mukashi no is an attributive expression for something old or former. The printed Mucaxino remains a single string.
[c2-l017] Mucaye, uru, eta. *Chamar a alguem a ſua*
[c2-l017 note] Mukaye means inviting or calling someone to one's house. The object and destination are completed in the next row.
[c2-l018 >] *caſa. Vt,* Qiacuuo mucayuru. *¶ Item,*
[c2-l018 note] Kyaku wo mukayuru illustrates inviting or receiving a guest. Item then introduces a second, marital application of the verb.
[c2-l019 >] *Caſar o homem com molher, ou chamàla a*
[c2-l019 note] The second application is a man marrying a woman or calling her to his house. Preserve molher and chamàla as the historical printed forms.
[c2-l020 >] *ſua caſa. Vt,* Tçumauo mucayuru.
[c2-l020 note] Tsuma wo mukayuru illustrates taking a wife into one's household. Sua caſa completes the Portuguese sentence begun on the preceding row.
[c2-l021] Mucayetori, u, otta. *Vide* Mucaitori.
[c2-l021 note] Mukayetori directs the reader to mukaitori, where the detailed gloss and quotation appear. Vide is italic Latin; the referenced headword is upright.
[c2-l022] Muchi. Chiye naxi. *Ignorancia.*
[c2-l022 note] Muchi is explained by chie nashi, lack of knowledge or wisdom. Ignorancia is the complete Portuguese equivalent here.
[c2-l023] Muchi. *Vara. ¶* Muchini abumiuo aua-
[c2-l023 note] A separate muchi means a riding switch or whip. Muchi ni abumi wo awa- begins an expression combining whip and stirrup.
[c2-l024 >] ſuru. l, ſoyuru. *Ir a todo correr, ou com*
[c2-l024 note] Suru completes awasuru; soyuru is the alternative verb. The whole expression means going at full speed or with great haste.
[c2-l025 >] *grande preſſa. ¶* Muchiuo ſuſumuru.
[c2-l025 note] Muchi wo susumuru supplies another expression for urging the horse on. Grande preſſa closes the preceding haste gloss.
[c2-l026 >] *Idem. Vt,* Iſogu michi nareba, fiqi vma-
[c2-l026 note] The example begins with an urgent journey and a horse being led. Fiqi vma- retains the source's separation and initial v rather than modernizing the compound.
[c2-l027 >] ni muchiuo ſuſumete xeqi yamauo vchi-
[c2-l027 note] The traveller urges the horse with the whip and crosses the mountain barrier. Vchi- begins the prefixed crossing verb continued below.
[c2-l028 >] coyenu. *Caminhando com grande preſſa,*
[c2-l028 note] Koyenu completes uchikoyenu, a completed crossing in this narrative, not a modern negative. The Portuguese begins by describing travel in great haste.
[c2-l029 >] *daua no caualo que leuaua pello cabreſto com*
[c2-l029 note] The traveller strikes the horse being led by its halter. The source's cabreſto and pello are retained in the literal explanation.
[c2-l030 >] *a vara, & paſſaua pellos montes, & ſerras.*
[c2-l030 note] The switch is used while passing through mountains and ranges. This completes the translation of the preceding Japanese journey example.
[c2-l031 >] *¶* Mixemuchi. *Vara com que dão no ca-*
[c2-l031 note] Misemuchi names the whip use explained as striking a horse on the right side of the neck. Ca- begins caualo below.
[c2-l032 >] *ualo na parte direita do peſcoço. ¶* Cacu-
[c2-l032 note] Ualo completes caualo and the right-side description. Cacu- introduces the contrasting kakushi-muchi term on the same row.
[c2-l033 >] xi muchi. *Vara com que ſe da no peſcoço*
[c2-l033 note] Xi muchi completes kakushi muchi. The Portuguese begins the corresponding description of striking the horse's neck on the other side.
[c2-l034 >] *do caualo da parte eſquerda.*
[c2-l034 note] The side is the left: da parte eſquerda. The scan reads da, correcting draft do before parte.
[c2-l035] Muchǔ. Yumeno vchi. *Em ſonhos. ¶* Mu-
[c2-l035 note] Muchuu is glossed by yume no uchi, in a dream. The scan prints vchi with positional v, not the draft uchi; Mu- begins the following example.
[c2-l036 >] chǔno teide gozaru. *Modo de estar hum*
[c2-l036 note] Muchuu no tei de gozaru describes the condition of being in a dream. The Portuguese begins an explanation of someone's state while sleeping.
[c2-l037 >] *dormindo, & ſonhando.*
[c2-l037 note] Sleeping and dreaming complete the explanation of muchuu no tei. This is not a new headword for sleep.
[c2-l038] Mucô. l, bucô. Cônaxi. *O não ter experien-*
[c2-l038 note] Mukoo, alternatively bukoo, is paraphrased koo nashi, lack of accomplishment or experience. The scan reads l, bucô, not a word Ibucǒ; the three visible long-o marks are circumflexes.
[c2-l039 >] *cia. ¶ Item, Não ter proueito nem pre-*
[c2-l039 note] Cia completes experiencia. A further sense is receiving neither benefit nor reward, with pre- beginning premio below.
[c2-l040 >] *mio do trabalho, ſeruiço, &c.* S.
[c2-l040 note] Mio completes premio: no reward from work or service. The final upright S. marks the written-language usage.
[c2-l041] Muco. *Genro. ¶* Mucouo toru. l, muco-
[c2-l041 note] Muko means son-in-law. Muko wo toru and muko-dori wo suru illustrate taking a son-in-law; the final mark after muco is a visible hyphen, not the draft period.
[c2-l042 >] doriuo ſuru. *Caſar a filha, ou tomar genro.*
[c2-l042 note] Dori wo suru completes the compound example. The Portuguese gives marrying off one's daughter or taking a son-in-law.
[c2-l043 >] *¶* Mucoiriuo ſuru. *Entrar o genro em caſa*
[c2-l043 note] Mukoiri wo suru describes the son-in-law entering the household. The following row specifies whose house and the purpose.
[c2-l044 >] *do ſogro pera caſar com a filha.*
[c2-l044 note] The house is the father-in-law's, entered to marry his daughter. This completes the example of mukoiri.
[c2-l045] Mucǒcaje. *Vento contrario, ou por proa.*
[c2-l045 note] Mukookaze is a contrary wind or wind from ahead. Por proa supplies the nautical description of a headwind.
[c2-l046] Mucodori. l, mucoiri. *Vide Muco.*
[c2-l046 note] Mukodori and mukoiri direct the reader back to Muco, the son-in-law entry above. The small c in the cross-reference is abraded but its faint trace and the explicit reference support Muco over draft Muo.
[c2-l047] Muconbon. Bup. *Sem principio.*
[c2-l047 note] Mukonbon is glossed as without beginning. Bup. marks Buddhist terminology and remains upright before the italic explanation.
