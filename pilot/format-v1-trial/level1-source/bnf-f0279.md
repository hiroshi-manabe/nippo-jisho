[c1-l001] Iya. *Pareas das molheres que parem: mas a pro*
[c1-l001 note] Iya denotes the afterbirth, as shown by the reference to women giving birth. The gloss contrasts it with the more proper term Yena below; pro continues into pria without a printed terminal hyphen.
[c1-l002 >] *pria palaura he,* Yena.
[c1-l002 note] Completes propria palaura: the dictionary prefers Yena, corresponding to ena, as the name for the afterbirth. Yena is a Japanese word embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l003] Iyagaqi. *Eſcreuer a couſa duas vezes.*
[c1-l003 note] Iyagaki is writing the same thing twice. The Portuguese infinitive escreuer establishes the repeated-writing sense rather than an interpretation based on the modern negative iya.
[c1-l004 >] *¶* Iyagaqi ſuru. *Idem.*
[c1-l004 note] Iyagaki suru adds the verb suru to the preceding noun; Idem explicitly repeats the same act of writing twice.
[c1-l005] Iyagari, u, atta. *Não querer, ou repugnar.*
[c1-l005 note] Iyagari, iyagaru, iyagatta expresses unwillingness or resistance. The principal parts agree with the Portuguese alternatives of not wanting and finding something repugnant.
[c1-l006] Iy ai, ǒ, ǒta. *Falar entre ſi. Vt,* Docoye
[c1-l006 note] Iy ai introduces reciprocal speaking, with historical long-vowel principal parts. Docoye begins the example asking where someone went; the first letter is capital I, not the OCR lowercase l.
[c1-l007 >] gocǒ naſaretacato iy auaruru. Feiq. *Lib.*
[c1-l007 note] Gocǒ naſaretacato iy auaruru continues the respectful question and the reciprocal saying. Feiq. is the citation label; the italic Lib. leads into its book number on the next row.
[c1-l008 >] *3. Falarão entre ſi perguntando hum ao outro*
[c1-l008 note] Book 3 concludes the citation. The translation describes people speaking among themselves and asking one another; the scan shows a stop after the numeral.
[c1-l009 >] *onde hia.*
[c1-l009 note] Onde hia completes the indirect question, asking where the person was going. Historical hia is retained rather than modernized to ia.
[c1-l010] Iyaitoco. *Primos ſegundos. Tambem ſe diz.*
[c1-l010 note] Iyaitoco denotes second cousins. The following clause introduces Futaitoco as another name; the scan supports Tambem rather than the OCR Tanbem.
[c1-l011 >] Futaitoco.
[c1-l011 note] Futaitoco is the alternative word for second cousins introduced by Tambem se diz above. Its indentation reflects continuation of that entry.
[c1-l012] Iya iya. *Adu. Não não.*
[c1-l012 note] This first Iya iya entry is the negative expression, translated literally as no, no. Adu. is the italic grammatical label.
[c1-l013] Iya iya. *Interieição do que louua, ouen care-*
[c1-l013 note] The repeated headword now introduces an admiring interjection used in praising or extolling something. The printed separation ouen care- is retained rather than silently respaced to modern ou encarece.
[c1-l014 >] *ce algũa couſa. ¶* Iya iya migotona co-
[c1-l014 note] Completes encarecer something and begins the admiring example, Iya iya migotona koto. The scan shows a word space before migotona, which was absent from the OCR.
[c1-l015 >] to. *O couſa apraziuel, ou pera ver.*
[c1-l015 note] To completes koto. The Portuguese exclamation describes something pleasing or worth seeing, confirming that this Iya iya expresses admiration rather than refusal.
[c1-l016] Iyamaxi, l, iyamaxini. *Adu.* Iyoiyo
[c1-l016 note] Iyamaxi and iyamaxini express increasing degree; the Japanese paraphrase begins Iyoiyo and continues below. The alternative marker l and the adverb label are apparatus, not part of the Japanese words.
[c1-l017 >] maſu. *Mais, & mais crecendo, &c.*
[c1-l017 note] Maſu completes iyoiyo masu, increasing more and more. The Portuguese crecendo is retained as printed instead of being regularized to crescendo.
[c1-l018] Iya mezzuraxij. i, Icanimo mezzuraxij. *Cou*
[c1-l018 note] Iya mezzuraxij is explained by Icanimo mezzuraxij, something exceptionally novel or remarkable. Cou begins the Portuguese noun continued below, without a visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l019 >] *ſa muito noua, & apraziuel.* S.
[c1-l019 note] Completes the gloss as very new and pleasing. S. is the upright written-usage label, not part of the italic Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l020] Iyana, l, iyano. *Couſa que não quero, ou*
[c1-l020 note] Iyana and iyano qualify something unwanted or displeasing. The relative clause continues below and leads to an example of an unwelcome thing.
[c1-l021 >] *me não contenta. Vt,* Iyana cotogia.
[c1-l021 note] Completes the sense of something that does not please the speaker. Iyana cotogia means that it is an unwelcome or disagreeable thing, with historical gia for the copula.
[c1-l022] Iyanaxǔ, l, iyana fito. *Peſſoa auorreciuel,*
[c1-l022 note] Iyanaxǔ, alternatively iyana fito, denotes an objectionable person. The Portuguese peſſoa confirms a person rather than an abstract quality; the printed vowel mark is retained.
[c1-l023 >] *& com que não folgo de tratar.*
[c1-l023 note] Completes the personal description: someone whose company or dealings the speaker does not enjoy. This belongs to Iyanaxǔ, not the following speaking verb.
[c1-l024] Iy arauaſu. *Declarar com palauras.*
[c1-l024 note] Iy arauaſu means expressing or declaring something in words. The printed space between Iy and arauaſu is preserved despite their compound interpretation.
[c1-l025] Iy ate, tçuru, eta. *Acertar, ou a deuinhar*
[c1-l025 note] Iy ate, with the historical tçuru form, means hitting on the correct answer or guessing correctly in speech. The printed Portuguese a deuinhar separation is retained.
[c1-l026 >] *de palaura.*
[c1-l026 note] De palaura specifies verbal guessing or stating the right answer and completes the gloss above. The short row is a genuine continuation.
[c1-l027] Iy auaxe, ſuru, eta. *Falar, ou tratar cõ ou-*
[c1-l027 note] Iy auaxe means consulting or discussing something with another person. The principal form ſuru belongs to the historical inflection of awase, not a separate modern suru construction.
[c1-l028 >] *tro ſobre algum negocio, ou aſſentar com elle*
[c1-l028 note] Completes outro and explains discussing a matter or reaching an arrangement with the other person. The object of aſſentar continues below.
[c1-l029 >] *algũa couſa.*
[c1-l029 note] Algũa couſa completes the agreement or settlement described in the preceding row. The tilde remains over the printed u.
[c1-l030] Iyaxi, ſu, aita. *Sarar. ¶* Qizu, l, ya-
[c1-l030 note] Iyaxi, iyaſu, with the printed past ending aita, denotes healing. The example begins wound or illness, with ya- continuing below. The visible initial I was omitted by OCR; the historical aita is not modernized.
[c1-l031 >] maiuo iyaſu. *Curar ſarando algũa ferida,*
[c1-l031 note] Maiuo completes yamaiuo, illness as an object of iyaſu; qizu supplies the alternative wound. The Portuguese describes curing a wound or disease, so iyaſu is transitive here.
[c1-l032 >] *ou doença.*
[c1-l032 note] Ou doença completes the second object of curing, parallel to ferida above. It remains a separate physical continuation line.
[c1-l033] Iyaxij. *Couſa vil, ou baixa.*
[c1-l033 note] Iyaxij means base, low, or contemptible. This is the adjective iyashii, distinct from the preceding healing verb despite the shared initial letters.
[c1-l034 >] Iyaxiſa.
[c1-l034 note] Iyaxiſa is the corresponding noun for baseness or lowliness. The scan supports a continuous word rather than the OCR space in Iya xiſa.
[c1-l035 >] Iyaxǔ.
[c1-l035 note] Iyaxǔ is the adverbial form of the preceding adjective. The initial is capital I, not long s; the historical marked-u ending is preserved.
[c1-l036] Iyaxime, uru, eta. *Deſprezar.*
[c1-l036 note] Iyaxime, iyaximuru, iyaximeta means despising or treating as contemptible. The Portuguese deſprezar distinguishes it from merely becoming low.
[c1-l037] Iybitçu. *Hũa caixa de figura ouada em que*
[c1-l037 note] Iybitçu names an oval box used for cooked rice. The Japanese initial I and the rice-container context correct OCR lybitçu; ouada describes its egg-like shape.
[c1-l038 >] *metem arroz cozido.* Iybitçu nari. *Fi-*
[c1-l038 note] The box holds cooked rice. Iybitçu nari introduces its oval form or shape; it is an independent Japanese illustrative phrase, not a word inserted syntactically into the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l039 >] *gura, ou feição desta caixa.*
[c1-l039 note] Gura completes Fi-gura, followed by a second Portuguese word for shape. The printed desta is retained without a global st/ſt normalization.
[c1-l040] Iycaqe, uru, eta. *Impor algũa couſa a outro.*
[c1-l040 note] Iycaqe means putting an accusation or matter to someone, with further senses developed below. The capital I replaces the OCR l; the Portuguese here introduces the imputing sense.
[c1-l041 >] *Vt,* Yoritomo qicoximexite nandaiuo iy
[c1-l041 note] The cited example begins with Yoritomo hearing something and being presented with a nandai, a difficult or unreasonable demand. The verb iy caqeraruru continues across the row boundary.
[c1-l042 >] caqeraruruya? Feiq. *Ouuindo Yoritomo,*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the interrogative verb and identifies the Feiq. citation. The Portuguese repeats the name Yoritomo; its first letter is Y, not the OCR V, and the name is embedded in the italic explanation.
[c1-l043 >] *diße impondes me couſas ſem razão, & de*
[c1-l043 note] The translation reports Yoritomo objecting that unreasonable and troublesome things are being imposed on him. Printed diße retains the supplied historical ligature representation.
[c1-l044 >] *embaraço? ¶ Item, Propor a alguem algũa*
[c1-l044 note] Embaraço completes the question about troublesome demands; the scan has a question mark, not a full stop. A new sense then introduces proposing a matter to someone.
[c1-l045 >] *couſa, começando a falar. Vt,* Fuxinuo iy
[c1-l045 note] Completes the sense of opening a conversation by putting a matter forward. Fuxinuo iy begins the example of presenting a doubt or question.
[c1-l046 >] caquru. *Propor duuida. ¶ Item, Ter*
[c1-l046 note] Caquru completes the example, explicitly translated as proposing a doubt. The next Item starts a third sense: having begun to speak without finishing.
[c1-l047 >] *começado de falar, & não acabado algũa cou*
[c1-l047 note] Continues the unfinished-speaking sense. The final cou continues as ſa at the top of column 2; the scan does not establish a terminal hyphen, so none is supplied.
