[c1-l001 note] Hissui no kotowari completes shōsha hissui from f198, the principle that flourishing is followed by decline. The prior page's isolated hissui is therefore a catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *& proſperão mas logo desfalecem, ou deſcaẽ.*
[c1-l002 note] Those who prosper soon weaken or decline. Desfalecem is one verb here, not des followed by a separate word.
[c1-l003] Fiſucaxina. *Peſſoa colerica, &riſpida.*
[c1-l003 note] Hisukashina describes an irascible, harsh person. Peſſoa is badly worn but its role and remaining types support the restoration.
[c1-l004] Fiſui. *Hum paſſarinho aſſi chamado de varias*
[c1-l004 note] Hisui is a small multicoloured bird, recognizably the kingfisher sense. The gloss itself supplies no further species distinction.
[c1-l005 >] *cores.*
[c1-l005 note] Cores completes varias cores, the bird's varied colours. The isolated word is a normal body continuation.
[c1-l006] Fiſuino canzaxi. *Certo toucado, & ornato da*
[c1-l006 note] Hisui no kanzashi is a head or hair ornament. The preceding bird entry offers context, but the gloss does not explicitly state its material.
[c1-l007 >] *cabeça, ou cabelos de molheres nobres.*
[c1-l007 note] The ornament is worn by noblewomen. Preserve molheres, the period spelling, rather than introduce a modern vowel.
[c1-l008] Fita. i Narucono tagvi. *Certa taramela pe*
[c1-l008 note] Hita is a clapper or other bird-scaring device, explained as a kind of naruko. The scan has tagvi, using v in the internal vowel position; the reading is tagui (a kind), not a different lexical item.
[c1-l009 >] *ra eſpantar os paſſaros, & outros animaes das*
[c1-l009 note] Its purpose is frightening birds and other animals away. Pe-ra connects the purpose clause across the physical line.
[c1-l010 >] *ſearas.*
[c1-l010 note] Searas specifies the crops or fields being protected. This closes the clapper's agricultural definition.
[c1-l011] Fitaca. *Francelho lagarteiro.*
[c1-l011 note] Hitaka is glossed as a kestrel-like falcon. The damaged F is resolved without assuming an exact modern species equivalence.
[c1-l012] Fitacabuto. *Soldados armados.*
[c1-l012 note] Hitakabuto denotes armed soldiers. Kabuto evokes armour, while the Portuguese explicitly refers to the people wearing it.
[c1-l013] Fitafitato. *Adu. Modo de chegarſe, ou de*
[c1-l013 note] Hitahita to means close contact or adherence here. The repeated fita pattern restores the garbled Firancato.
[c1-l014 >] *eſtar pegado, ou afferrado a algũa couſa.*
[c1-l014 note] Being pressed against or attached to something completes the adverb's explanation. Algũa repairs the damaged generic object phrase.
[c1-l015] Fitai. *Teſta.*
[c1-l015 note] Hitai is the forehead. The visible t and direct teſta gloss correct OCR's Ficai.
[c1-l016] Fitaigami. *Cabelos compridos dos meninos da*
[c1-l016 note] Hitaigami refers to children's long forehead hair. The compound uses the hitai just defined, not a separate hik- stem.
[c1-l017 >] *testa.*
[c1-l017 note] Testa completes the hair-location phrase. The short continuation should retain its own crop and ID.
[c1-l018] Fitaigane. *Hum ferro que poem os ſoldados na*
[c1-l018 note] Hitaigane is an iron forehead guard worn by soldiers. The next lines explain its mounting and purpose.
[c1-l019 >] *testa ſobre algum veo que amarrão na cabeça*
[c1-l019 note] It sits above a cloth tied around the head. ſobre repairs the OCR j while preserving veo as printed.
[c1-l020 >] *pera nelle tomarem as frechas.*
[c1-l020 note] The guard receives or stops arrows. Frechas is retained as the period Portuguese form.
[c1-l021] Fitajeme. *O atormentar, ou por em aperto ſem*
[c1-l021 note] Hitazeme means unremitting pressure or assault. The citation spelling Fitajeme is supported by the scan despite modern zeme expectations.
[c1-l022 >] *ceſar. Vt,* Xirouo fitajemeni xemuru.
[c1-l022 note] Shiro wo hitazeme ni semuru applies the expression to besieging a castle. The repeated fitajeme repairs the draft's ficajeme.
[c1-l023 >] *Ter de cerco a fortaleza combatendoa rijamen*
[c1-l023 note] The fortress is kept under siege and attacked vigorously. Rijamen continues into rijamente below.
[c1-l024 >] *te ſem ceſſar.*
[c1-l024 note] The attack proceeds without stopping. This completes the incessant-assault definition.
[c1-l025] Fitamono. *Adu. Muito, & continuada-*
[c1-l025 note] Hitamono is an adverb meaning greatly and continually. Continuada- is a single printed word before its ending on the next line.
[c1-l026 >] *mente. Vt,* Soreni yotte Chriſtão fita-
[c1-l026 note] Sore ni yotte introduces the result in a Christian example. Chriſtão is in the Japanese quotation and is not a new Portuguese gloss here.
[c1-l027 >] mononi fanjǒ tçucamatçuru. Mon*. Por*
[c1-l027 note] Hitamono ni hanjō tsukamatsuru describes continual flourishing. Mon is the citation label; the o mark in fanjǒ is visibly present.
[c1-l028 >] *iſſo a Chriſtãodade não fazia ſe não crecer, &*
[c1-l028 note] Por iſſo says that consequently Christianity kept growing. The badly damaged OCR phrase is restored from the scan, not paraphrased into modern Portuguese.
[c1-l029 >] *dilatarſe.*
[c1-l029 note] Dilatarſe completes growth and expansion. It remains part of the Christian example's translation.
[c1-l030] Fitan. Canaximi, naguequ. *Entristecerſe,*
[c1-l030 note] Hitan is sorrow or lament, explained as kanashimi and nageku. The scan resolves both the headword's final n and the missing n in Canaximi.
[c1-l031 >] *& afligirſe.*
[c1-l031 note] Afligirſe adds distress to becoming sorrowful. This closes the emotional rather than physical meaning.
[c1-l032] Fitari, ru, atta. *Enſoparſe, ou meterſe mui*
[c1-l032 note] Hitaru is soaking or immersing oneself deeply. The final mui is divided from muito, not a Japanese particle.
[c1-l033 >] *to na agoa, ou noutra couſa liquida.*
[c1-l033 note] Water or another liquid is the medium. The alternatives define immersion broadly rather than restrict it to water.
[c1-l034] Fitaſauaguini. *Adu. Modo de ſe perturbar,*
[c1-l034 note] Hitasawagi ni describes sustained agitation. Fitaſauaguini is recoverable from the scan and its repetition below.
[c1-l035 >] *ou aluoraçar muito, & continuadamente algũa*
[c1-l035 note] People can become greatly and continually agitated. Aluoraçar muito restores the lost vowel and word division in the OCR.
[c1-l036 >] *gente,ou animais. Vt,* Fitaſauaguini ſa-
[c1-l036 note] The agitation also applies to animals. Hitasawagi ni sa-wagu is the Japanese example; animais corrects the draft's aximais.
[c1-l037 >] uagu.
[c1-l037 note] Wagu completes sawagu, making a commotion. The split occurs inside the Japanese verb.
[c1-l038] Fitaſura,l, fitaſurani. *Adu. Modo de ro*
[c1-l038 note] Hitasura or hitasura ni describes persistent entreaty. The visible long-s forms restore the garbled headword and repeated alternative.
[c1-l039 >] *gar com humildade, & importunação.*
[c1-l039 note] Gar completes rogar, asking humbly and importunately. The initial is not a capital B or an independent word.
[c1-l040] Fitatare. *Hum veſtido que vſão os Cũgues, &*
[c1-l040 note] Hitatare is a garment worn by court nobles and soldiers. The clothing gloss and scan resolve the damaged headword and veſtido.
[c1-l041 >] *ſoldados por cima das armas.*
[c1-l041 note] Soldiers wear it over armour. ſoldados and armas repair letter errors without changing the claim about how the garment is worn.
[c1-l042] Fitato. *Adu. i,* Fitafitato. *Modo de*
[c1-l042 note] Hitato is equated with hitahita to. The repeated adverb confirms close contact, not the OCR's Cataxo.
[c1-l043 >] *eſtar pegado, ou de estar continuamente junto,*
[c1-l043 note] The sense is staying attached or continuously close. Eſtar pegado restores the garbled beginning of the Portuguese clause.
[c1-l044 >] *ou aferrado, &c.*
[c1-l044 note] Aferrado adds being fastened or firmly attached. This completes the cluster of contact expressions.
[c1-l045] Fitauchi. *Pancadas ſem interpolação. ¶* Fi-
[c1-l045 note] Hitauchi is repeated striking without intervals. Fi begins the identical example word on the next line.
[c1-l046 >] tauchini vtçu. *Eſpancar, ou dar pancadas*
[c1-l046 note] Hitauchi ni utsu is beating continuously. The scan's tauchini replaces the entirely misread quemini.
[c1-l047 >] *ſem deſcanſar.*
[c1-l047 note] Sem deſcanſar completes the uninterrupted action. It is body text and must not be mistaken for a footer.

[c2-l001] Fitaxi, ſu, aita. *Enſopar, ou molhar muito. Vt,*
[c2-l001 note] Hitasu means soaking or thoroughly wetting something. Vt introduces an example, not Vide pointing to another entry.
[c2-l002 >] Yamabato ſuxẽ tõbi qitatte tçubaſauo miz
[c2-l002 note] Thousands of mountain pigeons fly up and wet their wings. Su-xẽ and tõbi preserve nasal abbreviations; miz begins the word for water below.
[c2-l003 >] zuni fitaite, *&c.* Taif. *Lib. 29. Vin-*
[c2-l003 note] Zuni completes mizzu ni, in water, followed by hitaite. Taif is the source label and Lib. 29 its reference. Even in the enlargement no secure s can be restored in fitaite: retain the anomalous printed reading, although hitashite is expected from the verb and meaning.
[c2-l004 >] *do voando milhares de pombos trocazes banha*
[c2-l004 note] The Portuguese describes thousands of wood pigeons coming in flight. Banha continues the bathing verb on the next line.
[c2-l005 >] *uão as aſas na agoa. ¶* Vmi, cumouo fi-
[c2-l005 note] They bathe their wings in water. Umi, kumo wo hi-tasu then introduces a different example of sea waves reaching clouds.
[c2-l006 >] taſu. *Chegarem as ondas às nuuẽs como ba-*
[c2-l006 note] Taſu completes hitasu, not tatu. The waves seem to touch or wet the clouds, matching the Japanese image.
[c2-l007 >] *nhãdoas como parece ao longe.*
[c2-l007 note] The appearance is described as seen from afar. The dictionary qualifies the image rather than claiming literal clouds are submerged.
[c2-l008] Fitba, i, Vma. *Caualo.*
[c2-l008 note] Hitba, a Sino-Japanese horse term, is equated with uma. The consonant cluster includes b, not OCR's h.
[c2-l009] Fitbarasǒ. *Certa erua de Sião.*
[c2-l009 note] Hitbarasō names a Siamese herb. The gloss does not support a more precise botanical identification in Level 1.
[c2-l010] Fitbocu. Fude, ſumi. *Pena, & tinta.*
[c2-l010 note] Hitboku is fude and sumi, brush and ink. The Chinese-compound ending boku is directly supported by the explanatory Japanese.
[c2-l011] Fitçu. *Caixão.*
[c2-l011 note] Hitsu is a chest or box. Caixão is not restricted here to its modern coffin sense.
[c2-l012] Fitçuchigome, l, fitçuchino come. *Ar-*
[c2-l012 note] Hitsuchigome or hitsuchi no kome is a second rice growth. The damaged initial letters are resolved by the repeated hitsuchi and the NINJAL headword.
[c2-l013 >] *roz que vem, ou nace a ſegunda vez de pois*
[c2-l013 note] The rice grows a second time after the first harvest. ſegunda is the ordinal, not an f-initial word.
[c2-l014 >] *de ſegado o primeiro.*
[c2-l014 note] De ſegado o primeiro completes the condition: the first crop has been cut. This is regrowth, not simply a second sowing stated by the text.
[c2-l015] Fitçuji. *Ouelha.*
[c2-l015 note] Hitsuji denotes sheep. The following homonym uses the same zodiac animal for a time designation.
[c2-l016] Fitçuji. *Nome de hũa das horas de Iapão que*
[c2-l016 note] Hitsuji also names a Japanese hour after midday. Its time sense must be kept separate from the animal definition.
[c2-l017 >] *he depois de meo dia. ¶* Fitçujini naru,
[c2-l017 note] Hitsuji ni naru means reaching that hour. The damaged ni was misread as ri; the following alternative confirms the construction.
[c2-l018 >] l, fitçujino toqide gozaru. *He hũa hora tè*
[c2-l018 note] Hitsuji no toki de gozaru is the alternative time expression. The Portuguese gives one to two after midday, with tè functioning as until.
[c2-l019 >] *duas de pois de meo dia pouco mais, ou menos.*
[c2-l019 note] Pouco mais ou menos qualifies the approximate hour. It repairs two clear OCR errors without imposing a modern exact time conversion.
[c2-l020] Fitçuji ſaru. *Parte, ou partida que reſponde*
[c2-l020 note] Hitsuji-saru names a compass direction between the zodiac positions. The Portuguese correspondence follows below.
[c2-l021 >] *ao Sudueste.*
[c2-l021 note] Ao Sudueste identifies southwest. Ao is the required preposition and is visible, not the draft's do.
[c2-l022] Fitdai. *Vide,* Fiddai.
[c2-l022 note] Hitdai points to Fiddai, an alternative spelling. Preserve both forms instead of regularizing the gemination across references.
[c2-l023] Fitdǒ. Fudeno michi. *Arte de eſcreuer.*
[c2-l023 note] Hitdō is fude no michi, the art of writing. The literal brush-way paraphrase establishes the calligraphic meaning.
[c2-l024] Fitgiǒ. Canarazu ſadamaru. *Couſa certa,*
[c2-l024 note] Hitjō, written Fitgiǒ, is kanarazu sadamaru, certainly determined. Its gi spelling remains distinct from gui in the next entry.
[c2-l025 >] *& aueriguada.*
[c2-l025 note] Certa and aueriguada complete the assured or established quality. This is the certainty entry, not the brush-form entry below.
[c2-l026] Fitguiǒ. Fudeno catachi. *Feição da pena*
[c2-l026 note] Fitguiǒ is fude no katachi, the form of the writing brush. Gui and gi cannot be conflated merely because the neighboring headwords look similar.
[c2-l027 >] *de eſcreuer letra de Iapão. ( S.*
[c2-l027 note] The brush is used for Japanese letters. The far-right ( S. is physically preserved here; its marker warns that logical attachment may differ from this row's main gloss.
[c2-l028] Fitjen. Canarazu. *De certo, ou com certeza.*
[c2-l028 note] Hitzen means certainly, glossed kanarazu. Its checked-final-t spelling Fitjen is not expanded into modern syllables in Level 1.
[c2-l029] Fitjet. Fude, xita. i, Bunxǒ. *Eſcritura,*
[c2-l029 note] Hitzetsu combines brush and tongue, with bunshō as the explanatory term. The visible i is an explanation marker rather than a capital Japanese initial.
[c2-l030 >] *ou falar por eſcrito. ¶* Fitjetni tçucuxi
[c2-l030 note] Fitjet ni tsukushi-gatashi begins an expression of being impossible to exhaust in words or writing. Falar repairs the draft's fatar.
[c2-l031 >] gataxi. *Não ſe pode eſplicar por pena, ou*
[c2-l031 note] Gatashi completes the difficulty construction. The Portuguese says it cannot be explained by pen or writing.
[c2-l032 >] *eſcrito. S.*
[c2-l032 note] Eſcrito completes that gloss, followed by S. The extra apparent a is an ink blemish, not a transcribed word.
[c2-l033] Fitmet. Canarazu meſsuru. *Deſtruirſe, ou*
[c2-l033 note] Hitmetsu is inevitable destruction, kanarazu messuru. The damaged citation form is Fitmet, preserving its checked codas.
[c2-l034 >] *acabarſe. Vt,* Xǒja fitmet. *Os que nacẽ*
[c2-l034 note] Shōja hitmetsu says those born inevitably perish. This Buddhist maxim explains the general destruction sense.
[c2-l035 >] *ſem falta acabão.* Feiq*. Lib. 4.*
[c2-l035 note] The Portuguese states that those born unfailingly end. Feiq is the source label; the scan shows book 4, not the OCR's pilcrow.
[c2-l036] Fitǒ. Acai momo. *Flor de peſegueiro muito*
[c2-l036 note] Hitō, akai momo, is the red-peach blossom sense. Its marked o distinguishes it from hito, person, below.
[c2-l037 >] *vermelha.*
[c2-l037 note] Vermelha completes the blossom's intense red colour. It agrees with flor rather than implying the whole tree is red.
[c2-l038] Fito. *Homem, ou molher.*
[c2-l038 note] Hito includes either a man or woman. The broad human meaning is explicit rather than inferred from a masculine Portuguese noun alone.
[c2-l039] Fitoai. i, Nin ai. *Conuerſação, & trato huma*
[c2-l039 note] Hitoai is equated with nin-ai, human interaction or sociability. The i is explanatory, not an alternative-conjunction l.
[c2-l040 >] *no. ¶* Pedroua fitoaiga yoi. *Pedro tẽ*
[c2-l040 note] Pedro wa hitoai ga yoi means Pedro is pleasant in his dealings. Pedroua corrects the draft's Podroua while preserving the fused particle.
[c2-l041 >] *bom modo em tratar, & conuerſar, ou he a-*
[c2-l041 note] He has good manners in dealing and conversation. A begins apraziuel across the line break.
[c2-l042 >] *praziuel na conuerſação.*
[c2-l042 note] Praziuel completes pleasant or agreeable in conversation. This closes the sociability example.
[c2-l043] Fitoauaxe. *Hum encontro. ¶* Fitoauaxe
[c2-l043 note] Hitoawase is a single encounter. The repeated example begins before the continuation below.
[c2-l044 >] mo xeide nigueta fitogia. *He homem que*
[c2-l044 note] Hitoawase mo seide nigeta hito ja describes someone who fled without even one encounter. Xeide is the negative conjunctive expression in the printed spelling.
[c2-l045 >] *fugio ſem ter hum ſo encontro com alguem.*
[c2-l045 note] Fugio ſem ter hum confirms fleeing without having a single encounter. Several OCR substitutions had obscured this straightforward Portuguese clause.
[c2-l046 >] *¶* Fitoauaxemo auaxenu torigia. *Hega*
[c2-l046 note] Hitoawase mo awasenu tori ja concerns a cock that cannot be matched even once. The next line gives the bird explicitly, so this is not another human encounter.
[c2-l047 >] *lo que o não farão encontrar com outro: mas não*
[c2-l047 note] Galo completes the cock gloss, followed by a warning about an expression that should not be used. The prohibition continues onto f200.
