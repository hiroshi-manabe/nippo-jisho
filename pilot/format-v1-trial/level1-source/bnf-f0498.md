[c1-l001] Teifat. Cami ſoru. *O rapar a cabeça.*
[c1-l001 note] Teihatsu means shaving the head; kami soru gives the Japanese paraphrase, and rapar a cabeça supplies the Portuguese equivalent. The checked final t is retained without expanding it to a modern syllable.
[c1-l002] Teifei. *Soldado forte, & robuſto.*
[c1-l002 note] Teihei is glossed as a strong, robust soldier. The headword's period is upright; italic type begins with Soldado, correcting the draft's misplaced style boundary.
[c1-l003] Teiguet. i, Tçumogori. *O derradeiro dia*
[c1-l003 note] Teigetsu denotes the last day of the month. The synonym visibly reads Tçumogori here; although tsugomori is the expected Japanese identification, the transposed-looking printed syllables are retained rather than silently repaired.
[c1-l004 >] *do mes.*
[c1-l004 note] Do mes completes the preceding definition as the last day of the month. This short continuation remains a separate physical row.
[c1-l005] Teiguiô. *Couſas, que o Reyfaz, ou ordena.*
[c1-l005 note] Teigyoo covers the king's actions or ordinances. The circumflex and the closely set Reyfaz are retained as printed, rather than changing the vowel category or regularizing the spacing.
[c1-l006] Teigi. Fiſſague motçu. *Ter algũa couſa de-*
[c1-l006 note] Teiji is explained by hissage motsu, holding something hanging from the hand. The Portuguese de- begins dependurada on the following row.
[c1-l007 >] *pendurada na mão.* S.
[c1-l007 note] Completes dependurada na mão: suspended in the hand. The final upright S. labels the expression as written usage and is separated from the italic gloss.
[c1-l008] Teigio. Tadaxij vonna. *Boa, & caſta molher.*
[c1-l008 note] Teijo is explained as tadashii onna, a virtuous woman, and glossed as a good, chaste woman. The historical molher spelling and the initial v of vonna are preserved.
[c1-l009] Teigiǔ. *Reuerencia, & agaſalhado. Vt,*
[c1-l009 note] Teijuu denotes reverence and hospitable reception. The long-u mark has two converging strokes, matching the caron in the repeated example below rather than the draft's grave accent.
[c1-l010 >] Teigiǔuo itaſu. *Reuerenciar, ou agaſa-*
[c1-l010 note] Teijuu wo itasu means showing reverence or respectful hospitality. The Portuguese verb agaſalhar is divided after agaſa-, with the visible terminal hyphen retained.
[c1-l011 >] *lhar com reſpeito, &c.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes agaſalhar com reſpeito, receiving or treating someone with respect. It closes the example begun on the preceding row.
[c1-l012] Teijen. Niuano maye. *Diante da Niua. Vt,*
[c1-l012 note] Teizen is explained by niwa no mae, the space before the courtyard or garden. Niua is a Japanese term embedded in the Portuguese definition; its local italic presentation is retained.
[c1-l013 >] Teijenno fana. *Roſas, ou fulas da Niua.*
[c1-l013 note] Teizen no hana names the flowers before or in the courtyard. The gloss uses both rosas and the printed historical fulas; Niua is the Japanese term embedded in that Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l014] Teijit. *Verdade certa, & determinada.* S.
[c1-l014 note] Teijitsu is defined as certain, settled truth. The final S. is an upright written-register label, while the Portuguese definition remains italic.
[c1-l015] Teinei. *Grãde agaſalhado, & amor. ¶* Tei-
[c1-l015 note] Teinei denotes warmth of reception and affection in this entry. The divided Tei- starts the example teinei wo tsukusu on the next row.
[c1-l016 >] neiuo tçucuſu. *Fazer grande agaſalhado.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes teinei wo tsukusu, extending abundant courtesy or hospitality. The Portuguese makes the hospitable-action sense explicit rather than restricting the word to modern politeness.
[c1-l017] Teineini. *Adu. Amigauelmente, ou amo-*
[c1-l017 note] Teineini is the adverb: in a friendly or affectionate manner. The italic grammatical label Adu. and divided amo- belong to the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l018 >] *roſamente, com muito agaſalhado, &c.*
[c1-l018 note] Completes amorosamente and adds abundant hospitality or affectionate welcome. The row continues the adverbial gloss and introduces no new headword.
[c1-l019] Teineiſa. *Amor, & agaſalhado.*
[c1-l019 note] Teineisa is the noun for affection and hospitable treatment. The scan shows the descending g of agaſalhado, correcting the OCR's açaſalhado.
[c1-l020] Teiqet. *Paços do Dayri.*
[c1-l020 note] Teiketsu denotes the imperial palace, glossed as the paços of the Dairi. Dayri is a Japanese institutional title embedded in the Portuguese definition; the worn headword remains legible from its surviving types.
[c1-l021] Teiqiǔ. Naqi, u. *O chorar.* S. *¶* Fot-
[c1-l021 note] Teikyuu is weeping, explained by naki/naku and Portuguese chorar. The h and o of chorar correct OCR chirar; S. is upright. Fot- begins the compound hotsuro teikyuu, whose checked-coda t precedes ro on the next physical row.

[c1-l023 note] Teiro means displaying something held suspended, as hissage arawasu explains. The Portuguese cou-/ſa is divided across physical rows, and the visible terminal hyphen is retained.
[c1-l024 >] *ſa dependurada na mão.* S.
[c1-l024 note] Completes couſa dependurada na mão, something hanging in the hand. The upright S. marks written usage and is not part of the italic Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l025] Teitacu. l, teitǒ. *Adu. Com força, &*
[c1-l025 note] Teitaku, alternatively teitoo, is an adverb meaning forcefully. The Latin l gives an alternative form; the caron over the second form's o is visibly distinct from a circumflex.
[c1-l026 >] *impeto.*
[c1-l026 note] Impeto completes com força, & impeto: with force and impetus. The single-word continuation remains its own physical line.
[c1-l027] Teitai. Todomari, todocouoru. *Ser impe-*
[c1-l027 note] Teitai is impediment or delay, explained by Japanese verbs for stopping and becoming held up. This qualified correction replaces Todomiri; printed todocouoru and the divided Portuguese impedido are preserved.
[c1-l028 >] *dido, ou detido.* S.
[c1-l028 note] Completes impedido, followed by detido, being impeded or detained. S. is the upright label for written usage.
[c1-l029] Teitaracu. *Modo, feição, ou aparẽcia. ¶* Co-
[c1-l029 note] Teitaraku denotes manner, condition, or appearance here. The damaged initial is T, supported by its surviving top and repeated teitaracu below, rather than the OCR's F; Co- starts kono in the example.
[c1-l030 >] no xecaino teitaracu. *Modo, ou maneira*
[c1-l030 note] Completes kono sekai no teitaraku, the condition or manner of this world. Portuguese modo, ou maneira begins the corresponding paraphrase.
[c1-l031 >] *de como eſtà o mundo.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes the explanation as how the world stands or is constituted. The printed grave accent in eſtà is preserved.
[c1-l032] Teito. i, Miyaco. *Corte, ou cidade principal*
[c1-l032 note] Teito is equated with miyako, the imperial capital. The Portuguese explains it as the court or chief city, with the location of the sovereign supplied on the next row.
[c1-l033 >] *onde reſide o Rey.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes the capital-city definition as the place where the king resides. It belongs to Teito and is not a separate entry.
[c1-l034] Teitǒ. *Adu. Fortemente, & com impeto.*
[c1-l034 note] Teitoo here means strongly and impetuously. The caron marks this adverb and should not be assimilated to the circumflex of the different entry for order below.
[c1-l035 >] *¶* Teitǒ caxxen ſuru. *Pellejar forte, &*
[c1-l035 note] Teitoo kassen suru illustrates fighting with force. The Japanese suru corresponds to Portuguese pellejar, and the manner of fighting continues on the following row.
[c1-l036 >] *rijamente.*
[c1-l036 note] Rijamente completes the example's description of forceful, vigorous fighting. This short row closes the adverbial Teitǒ entry.
[c1-l037] Teitô. *Ordem. ¶* Xidai teitô. *Idem.*
[c1-l037 note] Teitoo in this separate entry means order or sequence, also in shidai teitoo. Both printed o marks point upward and are retained as circumflexes, distinct from the preceding carons.
[c1-l038] Teitocu. Teiuǒno tocu. *Virtudes, & boas*
[c1-l038 note] Teitoku means imperial virtue, explicitly paraphrased as teioo no toku. The visible t and c correct the garbled OCR Teiocu; the Portuguese list continues below.
[c1-l039 >] *partes do Rey, que gouerna bem, &c.*
[c1-l039 note] Completes the description of the virtues and good qualities of a king who governs well. Partes is used for personal qualities in this Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l040] Teiuǒ. *Rey, ou emperador.*
[c1-l040 note] Teioo is a king or emperor. The caron remains visible despite its close alignment with the o, and emperador retains the historical spelling.
[c1-l041] Teixei. i, Teiuǒno miyaco. *Corte del Rey, ou*
[c1-l041 note] Teisei is explained as the emperor's capital. The scan supports Corte, the royal court, rather than OCR Certe; its alternate city definition continues below.
[c1-l042 >] *cidade principal onde reſide o Rey.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the explanation as the chief city where the king resides. The repeated royal-capital wording agrees with teioo no miyako above.
[c1-l043] Teixei. Naqu coye. *Voz choroſa.* S.
[c1-l043 note] This Teisei entry concerns a crying voice, naku koe, rather than the capital. Choroſa is supported by the surviving letters and the bilingual sense, though the o after r is abraded; the written-register S. is upright.
[c1-l044] Teixin. Taxicana xinca. *Criado leal do Rey,*
[c1-l044 note] Teishin here denotes a dependable vassal or retainer, tashikana shinka. The Portuguese specifies a loyal servant of the king; the damaged middle of xinca is retained from the surviving word and its clear repetition below.
[c1-l045 >] *ou doutro ſenhor.*
[c1-l045 note] Extends the preceding loyal-retainer definition to another lord as well as the king. It is a short continuation of the same entry.
[c1-l046] Teixin. Micado, xinca. *Rey, & vaſallo.*
[c1-l046 note] This separate Teishin entry combines mikado and shinka, sovereign and vassal. The Portuguese pair rey and vaſallo confirms the two-party meaning.
[c1-l047] Teixin. Tadaxij cocoro. *Fidelidade. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l047 note] Teishin is the faithful or upright heart, tadashii kokoro, glossed as fidelity. The damaged headword ending, daxij, cocoro, and Fidelidade are restored from surviving strokes and bilingual agreement; Itẽ opens the chastity sense at the top of column 2.

[c2-l004 note] Teishoo is explained by niwa no ue, the courtyard or the space in it. Niua is repeated as a Japanese term inside the Portuguese definition, which continues with pateo below.

[c2-l006 >] *cocaras no pateo.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes estar em cocaras no pateo, being crouched in the courtyard. The printed cocaras is retained without supplying a modern accent or an expected extra letter.
[c2-l007] Teixǒ. Vtenano vye. *Em cima do trono, ou*
[c2-l007 note] This separate Teishoo is explained by utena no ue, on a raised seat or platform. The Portuguese specifies being above or on a throne and then broadens the definition below.
[c2-l008 >] *dalgũ lugar alto, & luſtroſo.*
[c2-l008 note] Extends the definition to some high and splendid place. Dalgũ abbreviates the nasal ending and is preserved exactly rather than expanded.
[c2-l009] Teixu. i, Iyeno nuxi. *Senhor da caſa.*
[c2-l009 note] Teishu is the master of the house, explicitly equated with ie no nushi. The gloss concerns household authority; it does not require importing the modern narrower sense of husband.
[c2-l010] Tejei. *Gente, ou ſoldados proprios.*
[c2-l010 note] Tezei denotes one's own men or soldiers. Proprios distinguishes a person's own following from soldiers in general.
[c2-l011] Tejina. *Arte, & meneo das mãos como em*
[c2-l011 note] Tejina here is skill and movement of the hands. The examples that follow are dancing and using a fan, so the definition should not be narrowed automatically to modern stage magic.
[c2-l012 >] *dançar, ou vſar do abano, &c.*
[c2-l012 note] Completes the examples of manual skill: dancing or handling a fan. Abano is the printed Portuguese word for the fan, and the clause remains part of Tejina.
[c2-l013] Tejita. *Couſa ſomenos, & inferior. Vt,* T-
[c2-l013 note] Tejita denotes something lesser or inferior. The example begins with a damaged or anomalously shortened T- before vye below; teue is contextually expected, but no e is supplied where this impression does not establish it.
[c2-l014 >] vye tejita. *Couſas altas, & baixas, ou in-*
[c2-l014 note] Continues the contrast of upper and lower things, teue and tejita. The physical T-/vye spelling is retained with the preceding caveat, while the Portuguese translates the contrast as high and low or inferior.
[c2-l015 >] *feriores.*
[c2-l015 note] Feriores completes inferiores from the preceding in-. It closes the definition of the contrasted lower things.
[c2-l016] Temachin. *Iornal, ou paga, que ſe dà a algum*
[c2-l016 note] Temachin denotes a daily wage or payment for labour. Portuguese Iornal is the historical wage term, and the following rows specify the worker who receives it.
[c2-l017 >] *official, ou a algum homem alugado pera tra-*
[c2-l017 note] Specifies payment to a craftsman or a man hired to work. The visible lowercase tra- corrects OCR Ira- and begins trabalhar on the next row.
[c2-l018 >] *balhar.*
[c2-l018 note] Balhar completes trabalhar, the work performed by the hired man. This short fragment belongs to the wage definition.
[c2-l019] Temareô. l, Temariô. *Idem.*
[c2-l019 note] Temaryoo is given in competing eô and iô spellings, both referring to the preceding payment for labour. The two circumflexes are visible and remain distinct from nearby carons.
[c2-l020] Temari. *Pela que ſe joga com a mão. ¶* Te-
[c2-l020 note] Temari is a ball played with the hand. The repeated name begins the example at the right edge, with a visible terminal hyphen after Te-.
[c2-l021 >] mariuo tçuqu. *Iugar à pela com a mão.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes temari wo tsuku, bouncing or playing with a handball. The Portuguese Iugar retains its printed historical initial and spelling.
[c2-l022] Temariqua. *Hũa roſa, ou fula branca.*
[c2-l022 note] Temarika is identified only as a white rose or flower. The definition does not by itself justify a more precise botanical identification, and the printed fula is retained.
[c2-l023] Temaſari. l, temaſarina. i, Temaxi. *Vide*
[c2-l023 note] Temasari and temasarina are equated with temashi. Vide begins the Portuguese cross-reference directing the reader to the fuller entry below.
[c2-l024 >] *infrà.*
[c2-l024 note] Infrà completes Vide infrà, see below. It belongs to the preceding cross-reference and remains a separate physical row with its printed grave accent.
[c2-l025] Tematai. *Homem ſimples, & recto.*
[c2-l025 note] Tematai describes a simple and upright person. The two indented forms below belong to this entry rather than being definitions accidentally omitted by the OCR.
[c2-l026 >] Temataſa.
[c2-l026 note] Tematasa is the related noun in -sa, the quality of being tematai. The scan gives the bare form without a Portuguese gloss; nothing is invented to fill the open space.
[c2-l027 >] Tematǒ.

[c2-l028] Temaxi. l, temaxina. l, temaſarina. *Peßoa*
[c2-l028 note] Temashi, temashina, and temasarina describe a person superior in manual work. The headword variants are linked by l, meaning or; Peßoa starts the Portuguese definition continued below.

[c2-l031] Temaye. *Couſa que toca, ou pertence a alguẽ,*
[c2-l031 note] Temae here denotes something concerning or belonging to a person. The range is broader than physical position in front of oneself, as the ensuing work and means examples show.

[c2-l034 >] *voſſa vez. ¶* Temaye iſogaxij. *Eſtar*

[c2-l035 >] *ocupado com algũa couſa, ou obra propria.*

[c2-l038 >] *ou empreitada que hum toma à ſua conta a qual*

[c2-l039 >] *pode acabar quando quiſer ſem ter deuer mais*
[c2-l039 note] The person can finish the undertaking when he wishes, with no further obligation or involvement. Mais is visible, correcting OCR mats; the closely set historical deuer is retained rather than silently respaced as modern de ver.
[c2-l040 >] *com nada. ¶* Temayeni canega nai fo-
[c2-l040 note] Completes the statement about having no further dealings with anything. Temae ni kane ga nai hodo ni begins the next example, explaining that the speaker currently has no money.
[c2-l041 >] doni caſaxerarei. *Porque não tenho agora*
[c2-l041 note] Completes hodo ni kashaserarei, a deferential request for a loan because the speaker has no money at hand. The abraded a in caſaxerarei is retained from the surviving form; the Portuguese explanation continues below.
[c2-l042 >] *prata empreſtaime.*
[c2-l042 note] Completes the example: since I currently have no silver or money, lend me some. Empreſtaime retains the printed joined pronoun construction.
[c2-l043] Temayedocu. *Vide,* Temaye.
[c2-l043 note] Temaedoku refers back to the preceding Temaye entry and its contract-work sense. Vide is italic apparatus; the complete referenced Temaye is upright, correcting the OCR's style break inside the word.
[c2-l044] Temochi. *Modo, ou geito de ter, ou tomar*
[c2-l044 note] Temochi here means the manner or ease of holding or taking something in the hand. The damaged initial is T, and geito, not OCR grito, is supported by the surviving vowel and the repeated wording below.
[c2-l045 >] *algũa couſa nas mãos como pena, abano, &c.*
[c2-l045 note] Gives a pen and a fan as examples of objects held in the hands. The fan is abano, with a visible b matching its earlier occurrence, rather than OCR ahano.
[c2-l046 >] *¶* Temochiga yoi, l, varui. *Bom, ou ru-*
[c2-l046 note] Temochi ga yoi or warui means that something handles well or badly. The alternative l is Latin or, and ru- begins Portuguese ruim on the next row.
[c2-l047 >] *im geito de tomar: ou ter na mão algũa couſa.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes ruim and the explanation of a good or bad manner of grasping or holding an object. Geito corrects OCR gento, and the colon after tomar remains visible. The entry continues with a new figurative sense on f499; the separate ¶ Per. below anticipates that continuation.
