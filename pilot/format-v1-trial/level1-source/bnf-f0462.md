[c1-l001 initial=6] TA. Bechi. *Outro, ou outra,*
[c1-l001 note] Ta is equated with bechi, “other”; the Portuguese gives masculine and feminine forms. The decorated T belongs to this headword and spans six physical rows, rather than representing the OCR ornament fragments.
[c1-l002 >] *ou outra couſa.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the first gloss with “or another thing.” This short continuation is printed beside the decorated initial.
[c1-l003 >] Tano. *Adject. Vt,* Ta-
[c1-l003 note] Tano is the adjectival form of ta. The example begins Ta- and continues as tano fito on the next physical line.
[c1-l004 >] no fito. *Outro homem.*
[c1-l004 note] No fito completes tano fito, “another person/man.” The Portuguese gives the same meaning; this is not a new headword.
[c1-l006 >] Ta. *Varzeas de arroz. ¶* Ta-
[c1-l006 note] This second Ta means a rice field, not “other.” The gloss identifies rice-growing lowlands; the final Ta- begins the first cultivation example. The overlapping OCR row c1-l005 was a duplicate of this physical row.
[c1-l007 >] uo ſuqu. l, ſuqicayeſu. *La*
[c1-l007 note] Ta- plus uo forms ta wo, the object of suku or sukikaesu: ploughing the rice fields. The Portuguese La continues as urar below, with no securely printed terminal hyphen.
[c1-l008 >] *urar as varzeas. ¶* Tauo tçucuru. *Cul-*
[c1-l008 note] Completes laurar, “to plough”; ta wo tsukuru introduces cultivating the rice fields. Cul- begins the Portuguese infinitive continued below.
[c1-l009 >] *tiuar as varzeas. ¶* Tauo vtçu. l, v-
[c1-l009 note] Tiuar completes cultiuar. The next examples are ta wo utsu or uchikaesu, here glossed as digging the fields; the alternative begins with a divided u.
[c1-l010 >] chicayeſu. *Cauar as varzeas.*
[c1-l010 note] Chicayeſu completes uchikaesu. Cauar as varzeas supplies the shared meaning “dig the rice fields,” preserving the historical Portuguese spelling.
[c1-l011] Tabacari, u, atta. *Enganar.*
[c1-l011 note] Tabacari, with its principal forms, means deceiving. The final u and atta are the printed abbreviated inflectional forms, not missing modern dictionary endings.
[c1-l012] Tabacari. *Engano.*
[c1-l012 note] The noun tabacari means deception or a stratagem, distinguished from the preceding verb by the single-form headword and nominal gloss.
[c1-l013] Tabacarigoto. *Idem.*
[c1-l013 note] Tabacarigoto is given the same meaning as the preceding noun: a deceptive scheme. Idem refers backward rather than supplying a new sense.
[c1-l014] Tabacarivotoxi, ſu, oita. *Enganar totalmẽ-*
[c1-l014 note] Tabacariotoshi means deceiving someone completely or bringing someone down through deceit. The Portuguese adverb totalmẽte is divided across this line and the next.
[c1-l015 >] *te, ou derrubar enganando.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes “completely,” then adds “or overthrow by deceiving.” The short continuation belongs to tabacariotoshi.
[c1-l016] Tabacariyoxe, ſuru, eta. *Trazer a algũe aſi,*
[c1-l016 note] Tabacariyose means drawing someone toward oneself through deception. The printed suru principal form and abbreviated algũe are retained rather than normalized.
[c1-l017 >] *ou fazelo chegar por enganos.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes the causative gloss: make the person approach by tricks. Fazelo remains the printed unaccented, unhyphenated form.
[c1-l018] Tabai, ǒ, ǒta. *Guardar bem, & conſeruar.*
[c1-l018 note] Tabai is keeping safe or preserving. Both long-vowel marks are downward carons, consistent with the repeated tabǒ examples below.
[c1-l019 >] *¶* Inochiuo tabǒ. *Guardar, ou poupar*
[c1-l019 note] Inochi wo tabǒ means preserving or sparing life. The Portuguese object a vida follows on the next physical row.
[c1-l020 >] *a vida. ¶* Conomi nadouo tabǒ. *Guar-*
[c1-l020 note] After completing the life example, konomi nado wo tabǒ illustrates keeping fruit and similar things. Guar- starts the Portuguese continuation.
[c1-l021 >] *dar fruita, & outras couſas.*
[c1-l021 note] Dar completes guardar: keeping fruit and other things. Fruita is the visible historical spelling and is not modernized to fruta.
[c1-l022] Tabane, uru, eta. *Fazer em feixe, ou ra-*
[c1-l022 note] Tabane, tabanuru, tabaneta means making a bundle or a bunch. The divided Portuguese ramalhetes is completed below.
[c1-l023 >] *malhetes. ¶* Ineuo tabanuru. *Ajuntar*
[c1-l023 note] Ine wo tabanuru illustrates gathering rice plants into sheaves; the Portuguese begins “gather.” The headword has historical tabanuru, not a modernized tabaneru.
[c1-l024 >] *o arroz das varzeas em feixes.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the example as gathering the rice from the fields into bundles. The short row is part of the same agricultural gloss.
[c1-l025] Tabane. *Feixe. Vt,* Fitotabane. *Hum*
[c1-l025 note] The noun tabane means a bundle. Hitotabane illustrates one bundle, with the Portuguese Hum completed by the following feixe.
[c1-l026 >] *feixe, ou molho. ¶ Item, per met. Se*
[c1-l026 note] Completes “one bundle or bunch,” then signals a figurative use to be explained by qimono tabane. The printed per met. is kept abbreviated.
[c1-l027 >] *diz,* Qimono tabane. *As entranhas jũ-*
[c1-l027 note] Qimono tabane denotes the gathered entrails, figuratively the heart. It is a Japanese expression introduced by Portuguese “se diz,” not an ordinary independent headword; jũ- continues as juntas below.
[c1-l028 >] *tas, ou o coração. Vt,* Vôyani qimono
[c1-l028 note] Completes “the entrails together, or the heart.” The example begins with a great arrow, Vôyani, and retains the visibly upward circumflex.
[c1-l029 >] tabaneuo touoſareta. *Traſpaßarão lhe as*
[c1-l029 note] Qimono tabane wo toosareta says that the entrails or heart were pierced through. The Portuguese translates the passive Japanese with an indefinite plural agent.
[c1-l030 >] *entranhas, ou coração com hũa grande ſeta.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the piercing example: the entrails or heart were transfixed with a large arrow. Hũa keeps its printed abbreviation mark.
[c1-l031] Tabaqi. *Couſa arreueſada de cão, ou gato. Vt,*
[c1-l031 note] Tabaqi names matter vomited by a dog or cat. Arreueſada is the historical Portuguese form for vomited material, not a description of something merely reversed.
[c1-l032 >] Neco, l, inu tabaqiuo xita. *O gato, ou*
[c1-l032 note] Neko, alternatively inu, tabaqi wo shita means that the cat or dog vomited. The printed Latin l supplies the alternative animal.
[c1-l033 >] *cão arreueſou.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes the translation with “the dog vomited.” This short continuation belongs to the example above.
[c1-l034] Tabaſami, u, ǒda. i, Teni faſamu. *Ter*
[c1-l034 note] Tabasami is explained by te ni hasamu, holding something between or across the hand. The printed principal form ǒda is retained, with its downward caron.
[c1-l035 >] *na mão algũa couſa como ſeta, &c. atraueſada.*
[c1-l035 note] The object is held crosswise in the hand, an arrow being the stated example. This specifies the manner of holding, not merely possession.
[c1-l036] Tabaſami. Yumiuo iru toqino reini aru coto
[c1-l036 note] The second tabasami entry concerns a courtesy in archery; its Japanese explanation says it is part of the etiquette when shooting a bow.
[c1-l037 >] nari. *Comprimento, ou corteſia que fazem*
[c1-l037 note] Nari completes the Japanese explanatory sentence. Portuguese Comprimento here means a ceremonial greeting or courtesy, not physical length.
[c1-l038 >] *entre ſi os que atirão com o arco, & frecha*
[c1-l038 note] The courtesy is exchanged by archers carrying bow and arrow. The Portuguese clause continues into the hand phrase below.
[c1-l039 >] *na mão.*
[c1-l039 note] Na mão completes “with bow and arrow in the hand.” It is a separate short physical row, not part of the following verb entry.
[c1-l040] Tabatta. *Preterito que vem de* Tabari, u.
[c1-l040 note] Tabatta is identified as the past form of tabari. The headword b is locally abraded but its surviving shape and the repeated tabatta in the following-column example support the reading.
[c1-l041 >] *Defect. Vſaſe particularmente neſte preterito.*
[c1-l041 note] Defect. labels the verb defective and says this past form is especially used. The gloss continues at the top of column 2; the grammatical label is italic.

[c2-l001 >] *i, Deu. Vt,* Gofucuuo tabatta. i, Ta-
[c2-l001 note] Continues tabatta with “gave.” Gofuku wo tabatta is restated as takara wo tamawatta across this and the next row, illustrating receiving wealth or good fortune.
[c2-l002 >] carauo tamauatta. *Deu me o Cami riquezas,*
[c2-l002 note] Ta- plus carauo gives takara wo, not the stale kana hint’s kara. The Portuguese says a kami gave the speaker riches; Cami is a Japanese noun embedded in that Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l003 >] *& proſperidade.*
[c2-l003 note] Adds prosperity to the riches of the preceding example. This completes tabatta before the new tabe entry.
[c2-l004] Tabe, uru, eta. *Comer, ou beber.*
[c2-l004 note] Tabe, taburu, tabeta means eating or drinking. The historically broader polite consumption verb is not restricted to modern “eat.”
[c2-l005] Tabevoqi, u, oita. *Dar fim ao beber. Vt,*
[c2-l005 note] Tabevoqi means bringing the drinking to an end. The example below tests whether the cup or drinking round should end with the speaker.
[c2-l006 >] Tabevoqimaraxôca? *Acabaràem mim o*
[c2-l006 note] The question asks whether the drinking should finish with the speaker. Enlargement resolves OCR Acabardem as the tightly set Acabaràem, with a grave-accented a; the historical spacing is retained.
[c2-l007 >] *beber, ou o Sacazzuqui?*
[c2-l007 note] Completes the question with “the drinking, or the sakazuki?” Sacazzuqui is the Japanese cup-name embedded in the Portuguese sentence, not a new headword.
[c2-l008] Tabi, u. *Dar peßoa alta a baixa.* S. *Al-*
[c2-l008 note] Tabi means a person of higher rank giving to a lower-ranking person. S. is the upright written-register label; the following Al- begins the qualification about women’s usage.
[c2-l009 >] *gũas vezes vſão as molheres deste verbo no*
[c2-l009 note] Completes Algũas vezes: women sometimes use this verb in the imperative. The qualification continues below and does not redefine its rank relationship.
[c2-l010 >] *imperatiuo. Vt,* Cochi tabai. *Dai cà.*
[c2-l010 note] Kochi tabai illustrates the imperative, “give [it] here.” The Portuguese prints Dai cà with a grave accent.
[c2-l011] Tabi. *Certo calçado de couro de que vſão os*
[c2-l011 note] This tabi is leather footwear used by Japanese people, distinct from both the verb above and the travel noun below.
[c2-l012 >] *Iapões.*
[c2-l012 note] Iapões completes the footwear gloss as “the Japanese.” The printed plural and tilde are preserved.
[c2-l013] Tabi. *Andar fora, ouo andar por terras eſtra-*
[c2-l013 note] Tabi means travelling away from home or through foreign lands. The enlarged edge shows eſtra-, including the t and terminal mark lost by OCR; ouo retains the printed close setting.
[c2-l014 >] *nhas, &c. ¶* Tabino vomoiuo naguſa-
[c2-l014 note] Nhas completes estranhas. Tabi no omoi wo nagusamuru means consoling the feelings of a traveller; the visible terminal hyphen divides naguſa-muru.
[c2-l015 >] muru. *Recrear, ou alegrar ao estrangei-*
[c2-l015 note] Muru completes nagusamuru. The Portuguese translates this as comforting or cheering a stranger, with the adjective triste following below.
[c2-l016 >] *ro triſte.*
[c2-l016 note] Completes “the sad stranger.” This specifies the emotional state of the traveller in the preceding example.
[c2-l017] Tabibito. i, Reojin. *Estrangeiro, ou pere-*
[c2-l017 note] Tabibito is equated with reojin, a traveller or stranger away from home. The reojin spelling is retained rather than modernized from expected ryojin.
[c2-l018 >] *grino que anda fora de ſua caſa.*
[c2-l018 note] Grino completes peregrino, followed by “who is away from his own house.” The definition emphasizes travel, not necessarily religious pilgrimage.
[c2-l019] Tabidachi, tçu, atta. *Ir pera fora.*
[c2-l019 note] Tabidachi, tabidatsu, tabidatta means setting out or going away. The printed tçu principal form supplies the historical tsu spelling.
[c2-l020] Tabigotoni. *Adu. Todas as vezes: ſem-*
[c2-l020 note] Tabigotoni means every time. Adu. labels the expression adverbial, while ſem- starts the usage rule continued below.
[c2-l021 >] *pre ſe poſtpoem aos verbos a que ſe ajunta.*
[c2-l021 note] Completes sempre and explains that tabigotoni follows the verbs to which it is attached. This is a word-order instruction, not another sense.
[c2-l022 >] *Vt,* Mǒſu tabigotoni. *Todas as vezes*
[c2-l022 note] Mǒsu tabigotoni illustrates “every time I speak.” The downward caron in Mǒſu is clearly visible.
[c2-l023 >] *que falo.*
[c2-l023 note] Que falo completes “all the times that I speak.” This short continuation closes the preceding usage example.
[c2-l024] Tabijin. *Estrangeiro, ou hoſpede.*
[c2-l024 note] Tabijin means a stranger or guest. The Portuguese noun is hoſpede, with an italic h rather than OCR b; the headword’s beginning is abraded but recoverable.
[c2-l025] Tabine. *O dormir em lugar estranho, ou fora*
[c2-l025 note] Tabine is sleeping in an unfamiliar place or away from home. The Portuguese phrase continues onto the next row.
[c2-l026 >] *de ſua caſa. Vt,* Tabineuo ſuru.
[c2-l026 note] Completes “outside one’s own house” and gives tabine wo suru, sleeping while travelling. It is a Japanese example rather than an embedded Portuguese loanword.
[c2-l027] Tabino ſora. *Ar, ou ceo eſtranho.*
[c2-l027 note] Tabi no sora means the air or sky of a foreign place. The gloss stays literal; it does not equate sora with travel itself.
[c2-l028] Tabitabi. l, dodo. *Muitas vezes.*
[c2-l028 note] Tabitabi and dodo are alternatives meaning many times or repeatedly. The unaccented printed dodo is retained.
[c2-l029] Tabiǔto. l, Tabibito. *Eſtrangeiro, & hoſpe-*
[c2-l029 note] Tabiuto is equated with tabibito. The first word is not a palatalized “tabyuuto” despite the supplied mechanical hint; hoſpe- begins the Portuguese guest-noun, correcting OCR boſpa-.
[c2-l030 >] *de, ou homem que anda fora de ſua caſa.*
[c2-l030 note] De completes hospede, followed by “or a man who is away from his own house.” This closes the traveller definition.
[c2-l031] Tabiǔtoya. *Morada, ou pouſada onde coſtu-*
[c2-l031 note] Tabiutoya is lodging for travellers. Enlargement resolves the narrow line ending as coſtu-, including the small terminal hyphen; together with mão below it gives costumão, not the draft’s costamão.
[c2-l032 >] *mão a pouſar homens de fora, ou eſtrangeiros.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes “where outsiders or strangers usually lodge.” The scan supports eſtrangeiros rather than OCR eſtrangeini.
[c2-l033] Tabixǒzocu. *Trajo, ou veſtido de caminho.*
[c2-l033 note] Tabishǒzoku denotes clothes or equipment for a journey. The Portuguese specifies travelling dress; the headword mark is a downward caron.
[c2-l034] Tabiyatçure. *Canſaço, ou magreza cauſada*
[c2-l034 note] Tabiyatsure is exhaustion or wasting caused by travel. The visible cauſada, not OCR cauſala, governs the following cause phrase.
[c2-l035 >] *do caminho, ou peregrinação.*
[c2-l035 note] The journey or pilgrimage is the cause of the fatigue and thinness. This completes tabiyatsure.
[c2-l036] Tabon. Tauo vocaſu. *Adulterio. ¶* Ta-
[c2-l036 note] Tabon is explained by ta wo okasu and glossed as adultery. The repeated Ta- begins the example tabon wo okasu.
[c2-l037 >] bonuo vocaſu. *Fazer adulterio.*
[c2-l037 note] Bonuo completes tabon wo; with okasu the phrase means committing adultery. The Portuguese repeats that act directly.
[c2-l038] Tabumeqi, u. *Fazer ſom, ou vaſcollejar*
[c2-l038 note] Tabumeki describes liquid making a sound or sloshing. The scan resolves the garbled OCR ending as vaſcollejar; the liquid subject is supplied on the next row.
[c2-l039 >] *agoa, ou vinho no vaſo. Vide* Tabutabu.
[c2-l039 note] Water or wine makes the sound in a vessel. Vide Tabutabu is a cross-reference; its Japanese target is visibly upright within the Portuguese apparatus.
[c2-l040] Tabu. l, Tabunoqi. *Hũa aruore como loureiro*
[c2-l040 note] Tabu, also tabunoki, names a tree compared with a laurel. The gloss alone does not warrant adding a modern species name or an unprinted terminal period.
[c2-l041] Tabu. *Hũa redezinha de mão feita em còuão*
[c2-l041 note] This separate tabu is a small hand-net made in a basket-like form. Enlargement shows a grave above the first o in còuão as well as the final nasal mark; neither is modernized.
[c2-l042 >] *que ſerue pera tomar o peixe.*
[c2-l042 note] Completes the net’s function: catching fish. It is a continuation of the implement definition, not the tree entry.
[c2-l043] Tabun. Vouocu qiqu. *Ouuir muitas couſas.*
[c2-l043 note] Tabun is here “hearing much,” explained by ōoku kiku. It is distinct from the following homophone meaning a large part.
[c2-l044] Tabun. Vouoi bun. *Grande parte, ou pella*
[c2-l044 note] The second tabun is explained by ōoi bun, a large portion, with the adverbial gloss “for the most part” continuing below.
[c2-l045 >] *mayor parte.*
[c2-l045 note] Mayor parte completes “for the greater part.” The historical mayor spelling is retained.
[c2-l046] Taburacaxi, ſu, aita. *Enganar, ou embair.*
[c2-l046 note] Taburakashi means deceiving or beguiling. The printed aita is its historical principal part and is not replaced by a modern -shita ending.
[c2-l047] Taburi. *O ſacudir das penas que fazem os fal*
[c2-l047 note] Taburi describes falcons shaking their feathers. The visible ending is fal, not OCR ſa, without a secure terminal hyphen; the separate cões. below functions as catchword rather than another full body row.
