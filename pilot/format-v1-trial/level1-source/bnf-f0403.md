[h1-l001] **Q ANTES DO I.**

## column-1 [column] Column 1

[c1-l001] Qiǔyǔ. Furuqi tomo. *Companheiro antiguo.*
[c1-l001 note] Kyuuyuu is an old friend or companion, furuqi tomo. This opening headword supplies the context for the preceding page's abraded Qiǔ catchword.
[c1-l002] Qixacu. *Certa doença.*
[c1-l002 note] Kishaku is identified only as a particular illness. The vague Portuguese definition does not justify supplying a modern diagnosis.
[c1-l003] Qixecaqe, uru, eta. *Por, ou carregar ſobre*
[c1-l003 note] Kisekake, kisekakuru, kisekaketa means putting or loading more upon someone. The Portuguese sobre continues into the already-burdened condition below.
[c1-l004 >] *carregado de trabalhos, &c. ¶* Iyaga v-
[c1-l004 note] Carregado completes the sense of imposing further troubles on someone already burdened. The example starts iya ga ueni, with initial v- divided from yeni on the next row.
[c1-l005 >] yeni xinrǒuo qixecaquru. *Sobrecarregar*
[c1-l005 note] Iya ga ue ni shinroo wo kisekakuru illustrates heaping further hardship on someone. Sobrecarregar begins the Portuguese rendering; the historic Japanese form and spelling are retained.
[c1-l006 >] *a alguem com trabalhos, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] The translation specifies overburdening someone with troubles. This is the continuation of Sobrecarregar, not a separate example.
[c1-l007] Qixei. Inori, chicǒ. *Deprecações. ¶* Qi-
[c1-l007 note] Kisei is prayer or supplication, explained by inori and chikoo. The repeated headword begins Qi- at the right edge; its apparent internal OCR space is not a word boundary.
[c1-l008 >] xei ſuru. l, mǒſu. *Fazer deprecações.*
[c1-l008 note] Xei completes kisei, followed by suru or moosu as alternatives for making supplications. Latin l is the alternative marker, not part of either Japanese verb.
[c1-l009] Qixen. Tattoqi iyaxij. *Nobres, & plebe-*
[c1-l009 note] Kisen contrasts the noble and the lowly, tattoqi iyashii. Nobres corrects the OCR Nebres; plebe- continues into the commoner designation below.
[c1-l010 >] *os: ou altos, & baixos.*
[c1-l010 note] Os completes plebeos, followed by the parallel contrast altos and baixos. The colon after os is retained as printed punctuation.
[c1-l011] Qixenaga. *Corpo de armas.*
[c1-l011 note] Kisenaga is glossed as a body of armour. The scan supports a single headword, not the OCR-separated Q ixenaga; the brief gloss does not specify every component of the armour.
[c1-l012] Qixi, ſuru, ita. *Encerrarſe, ou cõſiſtir. Vt,*
[c1-l012 note] Kishi, kisuru, kishita means being contained in or consisting in something. The Portuguese abbreviated cõſiſtir contains long s rather than the OCR f.
[c1-l013 >] Dǒriua coreni qixita. *A razão conſiſte*
[c1-l013 note] Doori wa kore ni kishita means that the reason or principle rests in this. The Portuguese present-tense paraphrase begins A razão conſiſte and is completed below.
[c1-l014 >] *niſto.*
[c1-l014 note] Niſto completes the explanation, consists in this. The short row remains attached to the verbal entry rather than the following riverbank noun.
[c1-l015] Qixi. *Terra alta cortada como parede, barroca,*
[c1-l015 note] Kishi is a bank or cliff described as high earth cut like a wall, a steep bank, or a rock face. The following row supplies its waterside location.
[c1-l016 >] *ou rocha que eſta ao longo do mar, ou rio.*
[c1-l016 note] The cliff or rock is along the sea or a river. The unaccented printed eſta is not regularized to modern está.
[c1-l017] Qiximacaxi, ſu, aita. *Ter difficuldade em*
[c1-l017 note] Kishimakashi, kishimakasu, kishimakaita means being reluctant or slow to give something. The principal parts are retained, including the historical -aita ending.
[c1-l018 >] *dar algũa couſa, ou ſer vagaroſo, & dar mui-*
[c1-l018 note] The reluctance manifests itself as slowness and the offering of many excuses. Mui- continues into muitas across the line break.
[c1-l019 >] *tas eſcuſas, ou buſcar muitos achaques primeiro*
[c1-l019 note] Tas completes muitas; the explanation adds seeking many pretexts before giving. Achaques here belongs to the excuses/pretexts sense within the gloss.
[c1-l020 >] *que a dè. Vide,* Qiximi, u.
[c1-l020 note] The phrase completes the pretexts sought before giving the thing, then refers to Qiximi, u. Enlargement shows a small grave over e in dè; it is retained as printed rather than modernized.
[c1-l021] Qiximen. *Certo comer feito de farinha de*
[c1-l021 note] Kishimen is described as a food made from flour. The particular grain is supplied by the following continuation; no recipe is added.
[c1-l022 >] *trigo.*
[c1-l022 note] Trigo completes farinha de trigo, wheat flour. The final vowel is o, not the OCR e.
[c1-l023] Qiximi, u, ǔda. *Eſtar apertado, ou não ſe*
[c1-l023 note] Here Qiximi describes being tight or jammed, unable to open or close, rather than an explicit sound of creaking. The Portuguese continuation supplies a door and another closure as examples. The printed principal parts u, ǔda are retained without regularizing their vowel.
[c1-l024 >] *poder abrir, & fechar, como porta, çapadou-*
[c1-l024 note] The examples are a door or a çapadoura that cannot open and close properly. The visibly printed cedilla and divided çapadou- are preserved rather than modernized.
[c1-l025 >] *ra, &c. ¶ Item, per met. Ter diffi-*
[c1-l025 note] Ra completes çapadoura. The entry then introduces a metaphorical sense of reluctance; the abbreviation reads per met., not the OCR permes.
[c1-l026 >] *culdade em dar algũa couſa, ou tardar mui-*
[c1-l026 note] Culdade completes difficuldade, reluctance to give something, with delay as the alternative. The next row completes muito.
[c1-l027 >] *to em a dar, &c. Mas não ſe vſa neſte ſen-*
[c1-l027 note] To completes muito, lingering before giving it. The Portuguese then restricts use of the metaphorical sense; a dar is visibly separated.
[c1-l028 >] *tido metaphorico, ſe não,* Qiximacaxi, u,
[c1-l028 note] Completes sentido metaphorico and directs the reader instead to Qiximacaxi. The scan prints only u as the abbreviated following form, unlike ſu in c1-l017; that printed inconsistency is retained.
[c1-l029 >] aita.
[c1-l029 note] Aita completes the principal-parts reference to Qiximacaxi. The small isolated form is body text, not furniture or an independent headword.
[c1-l030] Qixin. Yori ſuſumu. *Eſmola, ou renda que*
[c1-l030 note] Kishin is an offering or endowment, with yori susumu as the Japanese explanation. The Portuguese specifies alms or income, with the recipients on the next row.
[c1-l031 >] *ſe dà às varelas, ou moſteiros. ¶* Qixin
[c1-l031 note] The alms or revenue are given to temples or monasteries. Qixin then begins the example of making such an offering; the accents in dà às are retained.
[c1-l032 >] ſuru. *Fazer eſmola, ou dar algũa couſa à*
[c1-l032 note] Suru completes kishin suru, giving alms or making an offering. The final Portuguese word is the preposition à, not the OCR d.; its complement follows below.
[c1-l033 >] *Igreja.*
[c1-l033 note] Igreja completes the example of giving something to the church. The translation uses Christian Portuguese terminology while the preceding definition also mentions Buddhist establishments.
[c1-l034] Qixin. *Alma dalgum defunto que ſe fez Ca-*
[c1-l034 note] A different kishin is the soul of a deceased person believed to have become a kami. The Portuguese word Ca- is a Japanese religious designation divided across the row boundary.
[c1-l035 >] *mi, como cuidão os Chinas. ¶ Item,* Qi-
[c1-l035 note] Mi completes Cami; the author attributes this belief to the Chinese. A second formulation begins Qi- at the end of the row and continues into xin below.
[c1-l036 >] xin. Voni,cami. *Diabo, & Cami.*
[c1-l036 note] Xin completes kishin, here explained by oni and kami. Cami within the Portuguese gloss is a Japanese religious term; the Portuguese Diabo reflects the dictionary's own interpretive vocabulary.
[c1-l037] Qixiri, ru, itta. *O andar das rodas do carro*
[c1-l037 note] Kishiri, kishiru, kishitta first describes the motion of cart wheels. The full headword is continuous; the apparent space after Q came from OCR.
[c1-l038 >] *pello chão. ¶ Item, Tocar, ou roçarſe hũa*
[c1-l038 note] The wheels travel over the ground; a second sense is one thing touching or rubbing another. The printed hũa carries its nasal mark over u.
[c1-l039 >] *couſa com outra. Vt,* Tenjǒuo qixitte
[c1-l039 note] The touching sense is illustrated by tenjoo wo kishitte, reaching or pressing against the ceiling. The sentence continues with piled-up valuables below.
[c1-l040 >] tçumivocaruru qinguinua. *O ouro, &*
[c1-l040 note] Tsumivokaruru kingin wa supplies the piled-up gold and silver as the topic. The Portuguese begins O ouro, & and carries silver onto the next row.
[c1-l041 >] *prata que tinha junto, & chegaua até o forro*
[c1-l041 note] The collected gold and silver reached up to the ceiling lining. The scan supports chegaua rather than chegoua and shows an accent in até.
[c1-l042 >] *das caſas. ¶* Fune qixiuo qixiru. *Ir*
[c1-l042 note] Das caſas completes the ceiling reference. Fune kishi wo kishiru then gives a boat rubbing or passing close along the bank; the Portuguese translation begins Ir.
[c1-l043 >] *a embarcação roçandoſe ao longo da terra.*
[c1-l043 note] The vessel goes along the shore while rubbing against it. The scan joins embarcação, unlike the OCR embar cação.
[c1-l044] Qixiroi, ô, ôta. *Ficar encalhado, ou não*
[c1-l044 note] Kishiroi, kishiroo, kishiroota describes being stuck or stranded. The circumflexes in the principal parts are preserved.
[c1-l045 >] *poder ir por diante como carro, embarcação,*
[c1-l045 note] The cart or vessel cannot move forward. Poder ir is visibly two words rather than the OCR poderir.
[c1-l046 >] *&c.*
[c1-l046 note] The concluding &c. leaves the class of obstructed vehicles open. It remains its own physical continuation.
[c1-l047] Qixǒ. i, Xeimonuo caqu. *Iuramento fei-*
[c1-l047 note] Kishoo is a written oath, explained by seimon wo kaku. The scan restores initial Q instead of OCR B; the Portuguese feito continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 >] *to por eſcrito, em que hum chama pollos Camis,*
[c2-l001 note] To completes feito from the previous column: a written oath in which someone invokes the kami. Camis is the Japanese religious designation pluralized within the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l002 >] *& Fotoques que lhe dẽ tal, & tal caſtigo, ſe*
[c2-l002 note] The oath also invokes the hotoke, rendered as Fotoques, to inflict specified punishments. The nasal abbreviation dẽ is retained; the condition follows below.
[c2-l003 >] *não comprir o que jura. Vt,* Qixǒuo caqu.
[c2-l003 note] The punishment applies if the swearer fails to fulfil the promise. Kishoo wo kaku is then given as the Japanese example, writing such an oath.
[c2-l004 >] *Eſcreuer, ou fazer eſte juramento ſolenne.*
[c2-l004 note] The example means writing or making this solemn oath. This closes the entry begun at the bottom of column 1.
[c2-l005] Qixo. *Vos, ou v. m. honrando a peſſoa com*
[c2-l005 note] Kisho is an honorific form of addressing someone. The scan prints v. m., an abbreviated Portuguese address, not the OCR vom; the two tall s types in peſſoa are retained.
[c2-l006 >] *quem ſe fala.*
[c2-l006 note] The respect is directed to the person being spoken to. The short continuation completes com quem ſe fala.
[c2-l007] Qixocu. *Sembrante. ¶* Goqixocuga
[c2-l007 note] Kishoku is countenance or expression, glossed Sembrante. The honorific example begins gokishoku ga and supplies its evaluations below.
[c2-l008 >] yoi. l, varui. *Eſtar algũa peßoa honrada de*
[c2-l008 note] Yoi or warui gives good or bad as alternative evaluations. The Portuguese explicitly refers to an honoured person, matching the go- prefix in the example.
[c2-l009 >] *bom, ou ruim ſembrante. ¶* Qixocuuo
[c2-l009 note] The good-or-bad countenance gloss closes here. A new example begins kishoku wo, completed by tsukuroo on the next row.
[c2-l010 >] tçucurô. *Fazer por contentar a alguem & lhe*
[c2-l010 note] Kishoku wo tsukuroo is explained as endeavouring to please someone. The Portuguese continues with the goal of winning that person's goodwill.
[c2-l011 >] *ganhar a vontade.*
[c2-l011 note] Ganhar a vontade completes the explanation, winning the other person's favour. This is a continuation of the same example.
[c2-l012] Qixǔ. i, Modoru fune. *Embarcação que ſe*
[c2-l012 note] Kishuu is a returning vessel, modoru fune. The explanatory marker is i followed by a comma; the Portuguese reflexive phrase continues below.
[c2-l013 >] *torna. S.*
[c2-l013 note] Torna completes the returning-vessel definition. The initial letter is t, not OCR c; upright S marks written usage. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c2-l014] Qixucu. Tattoqi rǒjin. *Velho honrado, ou*
[c2-l014 note] Kishuku is an honoured elder, tattoqi roojin. The headword has x, not the OCR r, and the Japanese o bears a caron.
[c2-l015 >] *ancião.*
[c2-l015 note] Ancião supplies the alternative Portuguese designation, an elderly person. The nasal vowel is not regularized to any of the older spellings used elsewhere.
[c2-l016] Qixucu. *Vt,* Qixucu menqio. *Priuile-*
[c2-l016 note] A different kishuku is illustrated by kishuku menkyo, exemption from lodging obligations. The Portuguese Priuile- retains all its visible vowels and continues below.
[c2-l017 >] *gio pera não pouſar alguem nalgũa caſa. Vt,*
[c2-l017 note] Gio completes priuilegio: permission not to lodge anyone in a given house. Vt introduces the full example sentence on the following row.
[c2-l018 >] Cono iyeua qixucu menqiode gozaru.
[c2-l018 note] Kono ie wa kishuku menkyo de gozaru states that this house has the lodging exemption. This whole Japanese example remains roman and is not marked as an embedded Portuguese term.
[c2-l019 >] *Neſta caſa ha priuilegio pera não ſe agaſa-*
[c2-l019 note] The Portuguese begins the statement that the house has the privilege of not accommodating people. Agaſa- is divided across the physical line break.
[c2-l020 >] *lhar ninguem nella.*
[c2-l020 note] Lhar completes agaſalhar, to accommodate; ninguem has the second n lost by OCR. The phrase completes the house's exemption from lodging anyone.
[c2-l021] Qiyaxi, ſu, aita. *Apagar.* B. *Meliùs,* Qe-
[c2-l021 note] Kiyashi, kiyasu, kiyaita means extinguishing. Upright B labels the low-register form, and italic Meliùs recommends kesu, whose divided headword begins Qe- here.
[c2-l022 >] xi, ſu.
[c2-l022 note] Xi, ſu completes the reference to keshi, kesu. The whole Japanese fragment is roman, including the e on the preceding row.
[c2-l023] Qiye. i, Vyamai. l, chisô. *O venerar a*
[c2-l023 note] Kie is reverential attachment or respect, explained by uyamai or chisoo. The Latin alternative marker is l rather than a Japanese capital I; the Portuguese object of veneration follows below.
[c2-l024 >] *algum Bonzo como meſtre, capelão, &c.*
[c2-l024 note] The person venerates a Buddhist cleric as teacher or chaplain. These are the dictionary's Portuguese analogies for the relationship, not an added modern institutional identification.
[c2-l025 >] *fazendolhe agaſalhado, eſmolas, &c.*
[c2-l025 note] The reverence is expressed through hospitality, alms, and similar support. Fazendolhe retains the printed attachment of the pronoun.
[c2-l026 >] *¶* Qiyeno sô. *Bonzo, de quem hum he*
[c2-l026 note] Kie no soo denotes the monk toward whom someone maintains this relationship. The Portuguese begins describing the person as his follower or supporter.
[c2-l027 >] *frègues, ou venera deſta maneira.*
[c2-l027 note] Frègues supplies the analogy of a parishioner or adherent, followed by venerating the monk in this way. Enlargement confirms e and a grave-like accent rather than OCR frigues; the historical spelling is retained.
[c2-l028] Qiye, uru, yeta. *Apagarſe como fogo, can-*
[c2-l028 note] Kie, kiyeru, kiyeta means going out or being extinguished, as a fire or candle. Can- begins the divided Portuguese candea.
[c2-l029 >] *dea, &c. ¶* Iiga qiyeta. *Eſtar a letra*
[c2-l029 note] Dea completes candea without the spurious OCR c. Ji ga kiyeta is the example of a written character having faded or been erased.
[c2-l030 >] *apagada. ¶ Item, Nos liuros ſe vſa por*
[c2-l030 note] Apagada completes the faded-letter example. A further note explains that the verb is used in books with another sense, specified below.
[c2-l031 >] *morrer, &c. Vt,* Tçuyuno inochi qiye
[c2-l031 note] The literary sense is dying. Tsuyu no inochi kie begins an example comparing life to dew and continues with the incomplete-extinction expression below.
[c2-l032 >] yarazu. *Não acabando de ſe apagar a*
[c2-l032 note] Yarazu completes kie yarazu, not yet wholly vanishing. The Portuguese likewise says not finishing the extinguishing of the life described next.
[c2-l033 >] *vida que he como orualho.*
[c2-l033 note] The life is likened to dew. This closes the figurative example rather than adding a new lexical sense of orualho.
[c2-l034] Qiyeiri, ru, itta. *Eſmorecer, ou eſtar fora*
[c2-l034 note] Kieiri, kieiru, kieitta means fainting or losing consciousness. The Portuguese fora continues into fora de si below.
[c2-l035 >] *deſi com algum acidente, dor, &c.*
[c2-l035 note] The person is beside himself because of an attack, pain, or similar disturbance. Deſi retains its printed close setting.
[c2-l036] Qiyen. *Liãça, ou vinculo de amizade. Vt,* Ta-
[c2-l036 note] Kien is a bond or tie of friendship. Amizade corrects the OCR vowel; Ta- begins the example tashoo no kien below.
[c2-l037 >] xǒno qiyẽ. *Liãça de outro nacimẽto paſſado.*
[c2-l037 note] Tashoo no kien is explained as a bond originating in another, past birth. The Japanese final nasal is abbreviated in qiyẽ, and the Portuguese also retains nacimẽto.
[c2-l038] Qiyenocori, ru, otta. *Ficar ainda ſem ſe a-*
[c2-l038 note] Kienokori, kienokoru, kienokotta means remaining without disappearing. A damaged but contextually and graphically supported c restores Ficar; a- begins apagar on the next row.
[c2-l039 >] *pagar como fogo, ou ſem ſe derreter como ne-*
[c2-l039 note] The literal examples are a fire not going out or something not melting. Ne- begins neve, snow, across the row boundary.
[c2-l040 >] *ue, caramelo, &c. ¶ Per met.* Tçuyu
[c2-l040 note] Ue completes neve, snow; caramelo is then listed alongside it as something that can melt. The gloss itself does not require identifying it with the modern confection. Per met. introduces the figurative example beginning with Tçuyu, dew.
[c2-l041 >] no inochi qiye nocoru. *Avida breue, &*
[c2-l041 note] Tsuyu no inochi kienokoru describes a dew-like life surviving. The Portuguese begins Avida breue, retaining its close printed setting rather than inserting a space.
[c2-l042 >] *fraca como orualho eſcapou ſem ſe apagar como*
[c2-l042 note] The brief, frail life, like dew, escaped without being extinguished. The comparison with the lives of others continues below.
[c2-l043 >] *a dos outros.*
[c2-l043 note] A dos outros completes the contrast: unlike the lives of the others. This is the end of the survival example.
[c2-l044] Qiyet. Yorocobi. *Alegria. ¶* Qiyetno
[c2-l044 note] Kiyet is joy, equated with yorokobi. Its final t is retained; the following example begins kiyet no.
[c2-l045 >] mayuuo firaqu. *Alegrarſe muito.*
[c2-l045 note] Mayu wo hiraku completes the joyful-brow expression, glossed as being very glad. It is one Japanese example followed by its Portuguese rendering.
[c2-l046] Qiyevxe, ſuru, eta. *Morrer, & extinguirſe, ou*
[c2-l046 note] The compound Qiyevxe describes dying or becoming extinguished; the Portuguese continues with disappearing on the next row. The printed principal parts ſuru, eta and the joined headword are retained rather than reconstructed from modern forms.
[c2-l047 >] *deſaparecer como demonio, alma perdida, &c.*
[c2-l047 note] The disappearing beings are exemplified by a demon or a lost soul. Alma corrects OCR alima; the following signature and catchword are separate page furniture.
