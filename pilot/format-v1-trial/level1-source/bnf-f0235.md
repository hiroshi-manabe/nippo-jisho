[c1-l001] Gueinô. Yoqi xiuaza. *Artes liberaes de*
[c1-l001 note] Geinō, yoi shiwaza, is introduced as the liberal arts. The headword's long o has a circumflex; the geographical qualification follows.
[c1-l002 >] *Iapão, ouda China. ¶* Gùeiua Rei, gacu, xa,
[c1-l002 note] These are the arts of Japan or China; gei then introduces rei, gaku and sha. The unusual mark in Gùeiua and the close ouda spacing are retained provisionally from this impression.
[c1-l003 >] guio, xo, ſu. i, *Policia, muſica, tirar cõ*
[c1-l003 note] Gyo, sho and su complete the six arts. Their Portuguese equivalents begin with civilized conduct, music and shooting; Policia is wholly italic, not the OCR's mixed-font word.
[c1-l004 >] *arco, arte de caualgar, de eſcreuer, & cõtar.*
[c1-l004 note] Completes shooting with a bow, then riding, writing and counting. The six Portuguese glosses correspond in order to the Japanese list above.
[c1-l005 >] *¶* Nôua Qin, gui, xo, gua. *Arte de*
[c1-l005 note] Nō introduces kin, gi, sho and ga: instrumental music, the board game, writing and painting, elaborated below. This Japanese list is not embedded Portuguese prose.
[c1-l006 >] *tanger inſtrumento muſico, maximè crauo,*
[c1-l006 note] Explains playing a musical instrument, especially one likened to a cravo. The dictionary's analogy is retained without asserting an exact modern instrument identification.
[c1-l007 >] *arte de jugar o go, eſcreuer, e pintar.*
[c1-l007 note] The remaining accomplishments are playing go, writing and painting. Go is Japanese vocabulary occurring within an otherwise Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l008] Gueiſai. *Artes, & ſaber.*
[c1-l008 note] Geisai is glossed as arts and knowledge. The long s in the headword is retained as printed rather than modernized.
[c1-l009] Gueixa. Guei arumono. *Homem que ſabe*
[c1-l009 note] Geisha is here gei aru mono, a person possessing arts or skills. The gloss concerns an accomplished person, not a later occupational definition.
[c1-l010 >] *artes, ou tem habilidades.*
[c1-l010 note] Completes the description as someone who knows arts or has abilities. This is the continuation of Gueixa.
[c1-l011] Guejigueji. *Hum bicho que chupando arrãca*
[c1-l011 note] Gejigeji names a creature said to suck and pull out hair. This records the dictionary's account, not a verified zoological claim.
[c1-l012 >] *os cabelos ſem dor.*
[c1-l012 note] The hair is said to be removed without pain. This completes the description of the creature in the preceding row.
[c1-l013] Guejiqi. *Hum inſtrumento de bambu com que*
[c1-l013 note] Gejiki first denotes a bamboo implement. The relative clause below explains its use for removing hair.
[c1-l014 >] *arrancaõ os cabelos da cabeça os Iapoens.*
[c1-l014 note] Japanese people use the implement to pluck hair from the head. The scan places the tilde on the final o of arrancaõ, which is preserved.
[c1-l015 >] *¶ Item, Hum certo bicho que por ſi arranca*
[c1-l015 note] A second sense identifies a creature that itself removes hair. The visible paragraph mark before Item was omitted by OCR and has been restored.
[c1-l016 >] *eſtes cabelos de noite eſtando hum dormindo.*
[c1-l016 note] The creature supposedly removes hair at night while the person sleeps. The line continues the source's description rather than introducing an actual medical assertion.
[c1-l017 >] *No Cami ſe diz, Guejigueji.*
[c1-l017 note] The upper-region name is gejigeji. Both Cami and Guejigueji occur inside the Portuguese regional explanation and qualify for whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l018] Guejiqi, u, ijta. B. *Gaſtar mal, & eſperdi-*
[c1-l018 note] The verb gejiki is labelled low or vulgar usage and means squandering goods. The printed principal parts, including ijta, are preserved rather than expanded into modern inflections.
[c1-l019 >] *çar o fato, &c. ¶* Guejiqi vxinǒ. *Gas-*
[c1-l019 note] Completes wasting property, then gives gejiki ushinō as the example. The scan supports initial G, not C; Gas- begins its Portuguese translation.
[c1-l020 >] *tar, & eſperdiçar todo o fato.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes gastar and explains wasting all one's property or goods. The word is fato, with f, not OCR ſato.
[c1-l021] Guejǒ. Xiro yori voruru. *Decerſe da for-*
[c1-l021 note] Gejō, shiro yori oruru, means coming down from or surrendering a fortress. The headword begins G rather than OCR C.
[c1-l022 >] *taleza, ou entregala a os inimigos rendendoſe.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes fortaleza and specifies handing it to the enemy by surrendering. The printed separation a os is retained.
[c1-l023] Guejun. Tçuqino vouarino tôca. *Os der-*
[c1-l023 note] Gejun is the last ten days of the month, paraphrased tsuki no owari no tōka. Here the o in tôca has an upward-pointing circumflex, unlike the preceding headword's caron.
[c1-l024 >] *radeiros dez dias do mes.*
[c1-l024 note] Radeiros completes derradeiros: the final ten days of the month. This finishes the Guejun entry.
[c1-l025] Guen. Arauaruru. *Manifeſtarſe.*
[c1-l025 note] Gen is paraphrased arawaruru, to become manifest. The short Portuguese gloss gives the reflexive manifestar-se.
[c1-l026] Guen. *Sinal, ou effeito dalgũa couſa como de*
[c1-l026 note] A second gen means a sign or effect, illustrated by recovery from disease. The tilde in dalgũa is preserved on u.
[c1-l027 >] *doença que ſara, &c. Vt,* Qiyomori
[c1-l027 note] The example concerns illness being cured and introduces Kiyomori. The Japanese proper name starts the quotation continued below.
[c1-l028 >] xujuno qitǒ nadouo xeraretaredomo, ſo-
[c1-l028 note] The quotation says that various prayers were performed, but their effect did not follow. The scan supports xeraretaredomo, replacing OCR's c in the concessive ending; so- continues into sono.
[c1-l029 >] no guenga nacatta. Fei. *lib. i. Qiyomori*
[c1-l029 note] Completes sono gen ga nakatta, there was no effect. Fei. is the roman citation label; lib. and its following i-like book indicator are italic. Qiyomori begins the Portuguese translation and is also italic here.
[c1-l030 >] *fez varias deprecaçoens, &c. mas não ouu.*
[c1-l030 note] The translation says Kiyomori made various supplications, but there was no result. The damaged final verb is read provisionally as ouu.; ouue is expected linguistically, but a final e is not securely visible and has not been supplied.
[c1-l031 >] *ſinal nem effeito, ou melhoria da doença.*
[c1-l031 note] There was no sign, effect or improvement in the illness. This completes the negative result of the prayers in the quoted example.
[c1-l032] Guena. *Particula que ſe ajunta, & poſtpo-*
[c1-l032 note] Gena is a particle added after other words. The scan supports ajunta rather than ajuita; postpo- continues across the line break.
[c1-l033 >] *em a outras palauras. Vt,* Maitta guena.
[c1-l033 note] Completes postpoem, describing placement after other words, then gives maitta gena. The following gloss interprets it as an appearance or report of arrival.
[c1-l034 >] *Pareceque veo. ¶* Vacai guena. *Pare-*
[c1-l034 note] Maitta gena is translated as apparently he came; wakai gena begins a parallel example about being young. The joined Pareceque is retained as printed.
[c1-l035 >] *ce que he mancebo.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes parece que he mancebo, apparently he is a young man. This finishes the second example of Guena.
[c1-l036] Guenbucu. *Por nome de homem aos mininos,*
[c1-l036 note] Genbuku is explained as giving boys an adult male name. The next row supplies the accompanying first wearing of a sword.
[c1-l037 >] *& fazer lhes cingir Catana a primeira vez.*
[c1-l037 note] The boys are made to gird on a katana for the first time. Catana is a Japanese noun embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l038] Guenbutxa. *Fotoque que deſpacha depreßa,*
[c1-l038 note] Genbutxa describes a hotoke who responds promptly. Fotoque is embedded in Portuguese, and depreßa retains the source's double-s sort.
[c1-l039 >] *& ouue as petiçoens, ou dà logo premio, ou*
[c1-l039 note] The figure hears petitions or immediately gives a reward or the contrasting outcome below. This continues the description of responsiveness.
[c1-l040 >] *castigo.*
[c1-l040 note] Castigo supplies punishment as the alternative to reward. The source's st-type is retained without imposing a global long-s classification.
[c1-l041] Guencan. Qibixij ſamuxi. *Grande frio.* S.
[c1-l041 note] Genkan is severe cold, paraphrased kibishii samushi. The final S. is a roman written-register label, not part of the italic Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l042] Guengiû. *Iustiça, & ſeueridade. ¶* Guẽ-
[c1-l042 note] Genjū means justice and severity. The headword's u-mark points upward; the example uses abbreviated Guẽ- and continues on the next row.
[c1-l043 >] giùni xǒbatuo voconǒ. *Dar premio, ou*
[c1-l043 note] Completes genjū ni shōbatsu wo okonō, administering rewards or punishment strictly. Reinspection supports a grave-like stroke over u here, so giùni is retained rather than harmonized with the circumflex in the headword Guengiû above.
[c1-l044 >] *castigo recta, & ſeueramente.*
[c1-l044 note] Completes the giving of punishment or reward rightly and severely. The historical recta is left in the source's adverbial construction.
[c1-l045] Guengiǔ. Cayeri ſumu. *Tornando de longe,*
[c1-l045 note] This genjū is kaeri sumu, returning to live in one's home place. Its u-mark points downward, distinguishing the printed headword from the preceding Guengiû despite identical modern vowel length.
[c1-l046 >] *ou do desterro habitar ẽ ſua terra. Vt,* Guẽ-
[c1-l046 note] The return may be from exile to residence in one's native land. The scan reads habitar and abbreviated Guẽ-, rather than babitar and Cue-.
[c1-l047 >] giǔ itaſu.
[c1-l047 note] Completes genjū itasu, to make that return and resume residence. The caron and long s in itaſu are retained.

[c2-l001] Guengiùna. *Homem recto, juſto, & ſeuero.*
[c2-l001 note] Genjū na describes a righteous, just and severe person. The damaged initial retains traces consistent with G, not the OCR's iu; the u-mark here is kept as a grave.
[c2-l002] Guenguiǒ. Arauaruru catachi. *Figura, ou*
[c2-l002 note] Gengyō is paraphrased arawaruru katachi, an appearing or manifest form. The Portuguese begins with figure or thing.
[c2-l003 >] *couſa que aparece.* Teqi guenguiǒ itaita.
[c2-l003 note] Completes a thing that appears, then says the enemy has appeared. Printed itaita is retained as a historical inflection rather than modernized to itashita.
[c2-l004 >] *Ia osinimigos aparecerão, ou estão deſcuber-*
[c2-l004 note] The enemies have appeared or are now exposed. The close osinimigos spacing is retained; descubertos continues across the line break.
[c2-l005 >] *tos. ¶* Toga guenguiǒ xita. *Deſcu-*
[c2-l005 note] Completes discovered, then gives toga gengyō shita, a fault becoming manifest. The Portuguese translation starts with Descu-.
[c2-l006 >] *brioſe o peccado.*
[c2-l006 note] Completes descubriose o peccado: the sin or offence was revealed. This is the translation of the preceding Japanese example.
[c2-l007] Guenguio. Cotoba cataru. *Palaura.*
[c2-l007 note] Guenguio is explained through kotoba and kataru and glossed simply as a word or speech. Its unmarked final o is not assimilated to the preceding Guenguiǒ.
[c2-l008] Guenguio. *Hũ genero de traue que ſerue nos*
[c2-l008 note] A homonymous Guenguio is a kind of structural beam. The abbreviated Hũ is retained without expanding hum.
[c2-l009 >] *edificios, & caſas.*
[c2-l009 note] The beam is used in buildings and houses. This completes the architectural sense of Guenguio.
[c2-l010] Guenja. *O que faz milagres, ou obras de vir-*
[c2-l010 note] Genja is someone who performs miracles or works of power or virtue. Vir- continues into virtude below.
[c2-l011 >] *tude, & marauilhosas.*
[c2-l011 note] Completes virtude and adds wondrous works. The definition describes the figure through acts attributed to him.
[c2-l012] Guenje. *Este mundo.*
[c2-l012 note] Genze means this world, as opposed to another life or realm. The locally printed Este is retained without a global st/ſt change.
[c2-l013] Guenjen. Mayeni arauaruru. *Aparecer*
[c2-l013 note] Genzen is paraphrased as appearing before someone. The final Portuguese word Aparecer continues into its directional complement below.
[c2-l014 >] *diante.* S.
[c2-l014 note] Diante completes appearing in front. The final S. is the roman label for written usage, not part of the gloss.
[c2-l015] Guenji. Minamoto vgi. *Hũa gèraçao nome-*
[c2-l015 note] Genji is the Minamoto lineage, described as a renowned family. The scan supports initial G rather than C; the unusual Portuguese accent and spelling are preserved.
[c2-l016 >] *ada, & antiga em Iapão.*
[c2-l016 note] Completes nomeada and calls the lineage old in Japan. This finishes the family-name entry, distinct from the book-title entry below.
[c2-l017] Guenji. *Hũs certos liuros poeticos de his-*
[c2-l017 note] This Genji denotes a set of literary or poetic narrative books. The visible G corrects OCR C; the historical Hũs abbreviation is retained.
[c2-l018 >] *torias.*
[c2-l018 note] Torias completes historias, finishing the literary definition. The source does not spell out a longer title on this row.
[c2-l019] Guenji, zuru, ita. *Manifeſtar. ¶* Qi-
[c2-l019 note] Genji, genzuru, genjita is the verb to manifest or reveal. Qi- begins kidoku in the following example; the principal parts remain in their printed abbreviated form.
[c2-l020 >] docuuo guenzuru. *Fazer, ou manifes-*
[c2-l020 note] Kidoku wo genzuru means manifesting wonders or miraculous effects. The Portuguese gloss begins making or manifesting, divided at the row ending.
[c2-l021 >] *tar milagres.*
[c2-l021 note] Tar completes manifestar milagres, to manifest miracles. This completes the example under the first Guenji verb.
[c2-l022] Guenji, zuru, ita. *Minguar, ou quebrar*
[c2-l022 note] The second genji verb means diminishing or losing material. It is a distinct entry despite the identical printed principal parts.
[c2-l023 >] *como prata fundida, &c.*
[c2-l023 note] The loss is illustrated by silver when melted. This explains quebrar as reduction or wastage rather than only physically breaking an object.
[c2-l024] Guenjǒracu. *Hum certo deſcante de muitos*
[c2-l024 note] Genjōraku is a particular musical piece or ensemble performance. The following line specifies many instruments together; no more precise modern title is imposed.
[c2-l025 >] *instrumentos juntos.*
[c2-l025 note] Many instruments play together, completing the description of Genjōraku. The printed instrumentos and juntos remain separate Portuguese words.
[c2-l026] Gueni, l, guenigueni. *Adu. Com rezão,*
[c2-l026 note] Geni and genigeni are adverbs meaning reasonably, truly or indeed. The scan supports initial G rather than C; Latin l introduces the alternative.
[c2-l027 >] *ou de verdade.*
[c2-l027 note] Ou de verdade adds in truth as the alternative gloss. This continues the preceding adverb entry.
[c2-l028] Guenimo. *Adu. Com rezão.*
[c2-l028 note] Genimo is another adverb glossed com rezão, with reason or indeed. The damaged internal strokes do not justify changing the headword.
[c2-l029] Guenin. Xitano fito. *Seruo, ou peßoa de*
[c2-l029 note] Genin, shita no hito, denotes a servant or a person in service. The Portuguese peßoa retains its historical double-s sort.
[c2-l030 >] *ſeruiço.*
[c2-l030 note] Serviço completes the person-of-service definition. The opening is long s, and the entire short continuation is italic.
[c2-l031] Guenmit. l, Guengiǔ. *Couſa riguroſa, &*
[c2-l031 note] Genmit is equated with genjū and means rigorous or strict. The scan supports G in Guengiǔ; its locally printed caron is not replaced to match another occurrence.
[c2-l032 >] *recta. ¶* Guenmitni vôxetçuqerareta.
[c2-l032 note] Completes strict and correct, then gives genmit ni ōsetsukerareta, an order delivered rigorously. The circumflex in vôxetçuqerareta is retained.
[c2-l033 >] *Mandou com rigor, & rectamente.*
[c2-l033 note] The Portuguese says the order was given rigorously and correctly. This completes the example under Guenmit.
[c2-l034] Guenmu. Maboroxi yume. *Sonho vão, ou*
[c2-l034 note] Genmu combines maboroshi and yume, an illusion or dream. The gloss describes a vain or fleeting dream.
[c2-l035 >] *que dura pouco.* Bup.
[c2-l035 note] The dream lasts only a short time. Bup. is a roman Buddhist usage label and has been separated from the italic gloss.
[c2-l036] Guen, ni, bi, jet, xin, y. *Seis ſentidos que*
[c2-l036 note] Gen, ni, bi, zet, shin and i enumerate the six sensory faculties. The final t in jet and the single-letter y are retained as printed.
[c2-l037 >] *poem os Chinas, & Iapoẽs.* S. *¶* Ma-
[c2-l037 note] The dictionary attributes this sixfold classification to Chinese and Japanese usage. S. is roman; Ma- starts the Japanese explanatory list with manako.
[c2-l038 >] naco, mimi, fana, xita, mi, cocoro.
[c2-l038 note] Completes manako and lists eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind. These are standalone Japanese equivalents, not embedded Portuguese terms for font toggling.
[c2-l039 >] *Ver, ouuir, cheirar, gostar, palpar, ſenti-*
[c2-l039 note] The Portuguese maps the list to seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting and touching, then begins the final faculty. The original gostar spelling and line-end senti- are retained.
[c2-l040 >] *do comum.*
[c2-l040 note] Do completes sentido comum, the dictionary's term for the final mental faculty. It should not be reduced to the modern everyday sense of good judgment.
[c2-l041] Guen nicô. Manaco, mimi, cuchi. *Olhos,*
[c2-l041 note] Gen ni kō enumerates eyes, ears and mouth, also given as manako, mimi and kuchi. The printed spacing in Guen nicô is retained rather than resegmented.
[c2-l042 >] *orelhas, & boca.*
[c2-l042 note] Ears and mouth complete the Portuguese equivalent list. This short continuation belongs to Guen nicô.
[c2-l043] Guennin. Maboroxino fito. *Natureza ha*
[c2-l043 note] Gennin, maboroshi no hito, describes human existence as illusory. The line's final ha is anomalous before mana below, where humana is expected; the visible reading is retained rather than silently repaired.
[c2-l044 >] *mana miſerauel, & de pouca dura.* S.
[c2-l044 note] Completes the description of miserable, short-lived human nature. The ha/mana division above is preserved with its caveat; S. is a roman written-register label.
[c2-l045] Guenpu. Cayeri tçuqu. *O tornar a ſua*
[c2-l045 note] Genpu, kaeri tsuku, means returning or being restored. The Portuguese continuation specifies the former income or status.
[c2-l046 >] *renda, ou eſtado primeiro. Vt,* Guenpu
[c2-l046 note] The restoration is to one's previous revenue or position. Genpu starts the Japanese example completed on the next physical row.
[c2-l047 >] xerareta.
[c2-l047 note] Genpu serareta means having been restored to the former position. The dictionary's xerareta spelling is retained, and the separate catchword below is not body text.
