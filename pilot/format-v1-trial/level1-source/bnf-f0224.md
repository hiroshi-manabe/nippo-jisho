[c1-l001] Funagu. *Aparelhos, ou petrechos do nauio*
[c1-l001 note] Funagu (船具) denotes a vessel's equipment. The Portuguese aparelhos/petrechos agrees directly; neither term requires a more specialized modern nautical interpretation.
[c1-l002] Funaicuſa. *Guerra por mar.*
[c1-l002 note] Funaicuſa is funa-ikusa, naval warfare. Guerra por mar supplies a straightforward independent check of the Japanese compound.
[c1-l003] Funajômǒ. *O queimar a embarcação.*
[c1-l003 note] Funajômǒ denotes burning a ship (船焼亡). Both marked vowels are present; the first has a circumflex and the second a caron. The kana hint deliberately does not distinguish open and closed long o.
[c1-l004] Funamachi. *O eſperar polla embarcação.*
[c1-l004 note] Funamachi is waiting for a boat. Polla is the historical Portuguese contraction, not an OCR error to modernize.
[c1-l005] Funamoto. i, Funaba. *Surgidouro, ou porto*
[c1-l005 note] Funamoto is equated with Funaba, an anchorage or port. The connector i, belongs to the explanation, not to the Japanese headword.
[c1-l006 >] *onde ſurgem as embarcaçoẽs.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes the definition of Funamoto: the place where ships anchor. Surgem here is nautical 'come to anchor', not simply modern 'arise'.
[c1-l007] Funamoyoi. *A percibimento que ſe faz de Fu*
[c1-l007 note] Funamoyoi means preparations for a ship's departure. Enlarging the worn word supports A percibimento, with a damaged b; the repeated apercibimento below independently supports it. The separated initial A and italic Fu are retained.
[c1-l008 >] *ne que eſtà pera ſe partir. ¶* Funamoyoi
[c1-l008 note] Completes italic Fu/ne, the embedded Japanese word for ship, before the Japanese example. The printed eſtà concerns a ship about to leave; do not conflate it with the headword following ¶.
[c1-l009 >] uo ſuru. *Fazer este apercibimento, & a-*
[c1-l009 note] Funamoyoi uo ſuru means making those preparations. The Portuguese repeats apercibimento and adds aparelho; the printed word division at a- is kept.
[c1-l010 >] *parelho.*
[c1-l010 note] Completes aparelho, equipment or preparation, from the preceding physical row. It is not a new Japanese expression.
[c1-l011] Funamuxi. *Bicho que roe as embarcaçoẽs.*
[c1-l011 note] Funamuxi is a ship-associated insect, here specifically described as gnawing vessels. The Portuguese scope is narrower than simply every creature now called funamushi.
[c1-l012] Fǔnan. Cajeno nan. *Perigo do vento, ou*
[c1-l012 note] Fǔnan is wind calamity, explained as Cajeno nan (風の難). The initial caron is visible; j represents the voiced sibilant in kaze, not modern English j.
[c1-l013 >] *trabalho de tempeſtade.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the wind-calamity definition with hardship from a tempest. Trabalho has the older broader sense of trouble or suffering.
[c1-l014] Funaqeigo. *Melius,* Qeigobune. *Freta,*
[c1-l014 note] Funaqeigo is referred to the preferred Qeigobune, a guard vessel. Latin Melius introduces the preferred wording; the fleet/armada definition continues below.
[c1-l015 >] *ou armada por mar.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes the fleet or naval-force gloss. This explains the collective military use of the vessel terminology rather than requiring a one-to-one modern gloss.
[c1-l016] Funaſoco. *Quilha do nauio por dentro, ou fun*
[c1-l016 note] Funaſoco is the bottom of a vessel. The Portuguese contrasts its interior keel area with its bottom; fun continues as fundo, not the OCR fan.
[c1-l017 >] *do da embarcação.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes fundo da embarcação. The meaning confirms the preceding u without supplying an unprinted hyphen.
[c1-l018] Funatçu. *Porto, ou lugar maritimo onde an-*
[c1-l018 note] Funatçu denotes a harbor or maritime anchoring place. The Japanese tçu gives tsu; the Portuguese an- continues in ancorão.
[c1-l019 >] *corão as Funes.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes ancorão and names the ships as Funes. The embedded Japanese noun carries Portuguese plural s and appears italic here; retain that typeface.
[c1-l020] Funatçuqi. *Porto, ou ſurgidouro.*
[c1-l020 note] Funatçuqi is a landing/anchoring place. Porto and surgidouro agree with the preceding related terms without making them duplicate entries.
[c1-l021] Funatǒ. *Peſcador, marinheiro, ou homem*
[c1-l021 note] Funatǒ denotes a fisherman or mariner. Restored u in Funa and the damaged letters in Peſcador/marinheiro; the faint headword mark remains less visually secure than the consonants.
[c1-l022 >] *do mar.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes homem do mar, a seafaring man. It belongs to Funatǒ rather than introducing another sense of the following headword.
[c1-l023] Funauoſa. i, Xandô. *Piloto, ou mestre*
[c1-l023 note] Funauoſa is the ship's chief (船長/船の長), also Xandô, with pilot or master as its gloss. The worn round vowel is o, not OCR e; NINJAL independently lists Funauosa.
[c1-l024 >] *do nauio.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes mestre do nauio. The nautical meaning supports the captain/master interpretation of Funauoſa.
[c1-l025] Funaxu. *Marinheiro, ou peſcador.*
[c1-l025 note] Funaxu denotes a sailor or fisherman. Its xu is shu, not su; the two occupations are explicitly allowed by the Portuguese.
[c1-l026] Funayacata. *Camara do nauio, ou embarcação.*
[c1-l026 note] Funayacata is the vessel's cabin or chamber. Camara is retained without imposing a modern written accent.
[c1-l027] Funayacu. *Officios que ha na embarcação.*
[c1-l027 note] Funayacu refers to offices or duties aboard a vessel. The Portuguese plural officios explains yaku as functions rather than a boat's equipment.
[c1-l028] Funayagura. *Castello da popa, ou ſobrado*
[c1-l028 note] Funayagura is a raised ship structure, described as a sterncastle or upper deck. The definition continues with a specifically Japanese comparison.
[c1-l029 >] *alto que ha nas embarcações de Iapão.*
[c1-l029 note] Completes the description of a raised structure on Japanese vessels. The printed geographical name Iapão is retained.
[c1-l030] Funayobai. *O chamar bradando da embar-*
[c1-l030 note] Funayobai is calling aloud from a boat. Yobai belongs to calling here, as bradando makes clear, and should not be assigned an unrelated modern sense.
[c1-l031 >] *cação.*
[c1-l031 note] Completes embarcação from the preceding row. This short continuation has enough image context to establish its identity.
[c1-l032] Funayurugui. *O bulir o caualo com o corpo*
[c1-l032 note] Funayurugui surprisingly describes a horse's bodily movement. The continuation explicitly compares it to a boat, so the horse reference is not an OCR semantic error.
[c1-l033 >] *eſtando quedo à maneira de embarcação que eſ-*
[c1-l033 note] The horse stands still while its body moves like a vessel. Quedo means stationary; preserve the seemingly paradoxical contrast between stationary position and bodily motion.
[c1-l034 >] *ta dando balanços.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the comparison with a boat rocking. This resolves the apparent mismatch between the Japanese boat compound and the horse in its gloss.
[c1-l035] Funazoco. *Fundo da embarcação.*
[c1-l035 note] Funazoco is another form for a ship's bottom. Do not normalize its z to the ſ of Funaſoco above: both entries are present.
[c1-l036] Funazoroye. *O ajuntar frota de nauios, ou ẽ-*
[c1-l036 note] Funazoroye is assembling a fleet. The Portuguese deliberately introduces a qualification continued below; it need not be a military fleet.
[c1-l037 >] *barcações ainda que não ſeja pera guerra.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes that qualification: even if not for war. The negative clause is essential to the entry's scope.
[c1-l038] Funbari. *Vide,* Fumbari, u.
[c1-l038 note] Funbari refers back to Fumbari, u. The apparent extra i came from the worn n; the scan and NINJAL support Funbari. Preserve the printed n/m difference between the two forms.
[c1-l039] Funbet. *O entender, ou diſcernir do bem, ou*
[c1-l039 note] Funbet is discernment (分別), with final t representing the dictionary's syllable-final spelling. The definition begins by distinguishing good and bad.
[c1-l040 >] *mal, &c. ¶* Funbetuo curamaſu. *Eſ-*
[c1-l040 note] Funbetuo curamaſu is to obscure judgment. The repeated base and surviving n/b shapes support removing OCR's spurious i from Funibetuo.
[c1-l041 >] *curecer o entendimẽto. ¶* Funbetuo mi-
[c1-l041 note] Completes Eſcurecer and starts Funbetuo midaracaſu, to disturb judgment. The physical mi- split is retained rather than joining the rows.
[c1-l042 >] daracaſu. *Perturbar o entendimento. ¶* Fũ
[c1-l042 note] Completes midaracaſu and begins Fũbetuo on the next row. The nasalized Japanese abbreviation Fũ is roman throughout, not an italic final vowel.
[c1-l043 >] betuo meguraſu. *Deſcorrer com o entendi-*
[c1-l043 note] Fũbetuo meguraſu is to turn over a matter in one's judgment. Deſcorrer com o entendimento describes reasoning; its historical initial e is retained.
[c1-l044 >] *mento. ¶* Funbetni iqu, mairu, l, vo-
[c1-l044 note] Funbetni iqu, mairu, l, voyobu gives alternative ways of reaching understanding. The final vo- belongs to voyobu, not a detached object particle.
[c1-l045 >] yobu. *Entender, ou alcançar com o entendi*
[c1-l045 note] Completes voyobu, 'extend/reach', and the Portuguese 'understand or grasp'. No line-end hyphen is supplied merely because entendimento continues.
[c1-l046 >] *mento. ¶* Funbet ſuru. *Entender, ou*
[c1-l046 note] Funbet ſuru means to understand or discern. The enlarged final consonant supports t with its small lower terminal, not OCR r; the repeated entry supplies linguistic confirmation.
[c1-l047 >] *perceber.*
[c1-l047 note] Completes the Portuguese alternative perceber. It remains a continuation of the Funbet example.

[c2-l001] Funbetgamaxij. *O moſtrar que tem entendi-*

[c2-l004 note] Funbetgamaxij guinaredomo means roughly 'although it may seem a knowing matter'. The gi in gui is the historical spelling for gi, and the concessive naredomo agrees with ainda que.
[c2-l005 >] *trar me ſabedor não o ſendo.*
[c2-l005 note] Completes the concessive gloss: presenting oneself as knowledgeable when one is not. The first-person me should not be lost in the continuation.
[c2-l006] Funbetxa. *Homem de bom entendimento, &*
[c2-l006 note] Funbetxa is a discerning or knowledgeable person (分別者). The Portuguese explicitly describes good understanding.
[c2-l007 >] *ſaber.*
[c2-l007 note] Completes the addition '& knowledge'. This is a Portuguese continuation, not a separate headword.
[c2-l008] Funchǔ. Funno muxi. *Bicho que anda no*
[c2-l008 note] Funchǔ is an insect in dung, glossed Funno muxi. The u caron is visible; the definition confirms both the dung noun and insect noun.
[c2-l009 >] *eſterço.*
[c2-l009 note] Completes the insect's habitat, manure. The unusual cedilla spelling of eſterço is left as the current diplomatic reading rather than modernized.
[c2-l010] Funcot. Foneuo coni ſu. i, *Grande ſeruiço,*
[c2-l010 note] Funcot is strenuous labor (粉骨), literally Foneuo coni ſu, reducing bones to powder. This is an idiomatic intensity expression, not a literal instruction.
[c2-l011 >] *ou trabalho. ¶* Funcotuo tçucuſu. *Tra*
[c2-l011 note] Funcotuo tçucuſu means exerting oneself fully. The Portuguese Tra continues trabalhar, which explains the idiom as labor or service.
[c2-l012 >] *balhar, ou ſeruir com todas as forças. ¶* Fũ
[c2-l012 note] Completes working or serving with all one's strength and begins abbreviated Fũcot. The nasalized fragment is Japanese roman type, not mixed italic markup.
[c2-l013 >] cotno côuotçumu. *Ter trabalhado muito,*
[c2-l013 note] Fũcotno côuotçumu is accumulating the merit/result of hard work. The printed Portuguese interprets it as having worked a great deal; preserve its wording rather than translating the Japanese literally into the transcription.
[c2-l014 >] *ou paßados muitos trabalhos.*
[c2-l014 note] Completes the alternative 'having passed through many hardships'. The older participial construction is not corrected to modern grammar.
[c2-l015] Fundô. *Peſos da balança.*
[c2-l015 note] Fundô (分銅) denotes balance weights. Enlargement shows the faint circumflex missing from OCR, and the independent headword list confirms the long vowel.
[c2-l016] Fundoxi. *Encacho.*
[c2-l016 note] Fundoxi is the loincloth. Encacho is the printed Portuguese clothing term; do not replace it with a modern familiar synonym.
[c2-l017] Fune. *Embarcação. ¶* Funega moyǒ, l,
[c2-l017 note] Fune is a vessel. The example begins Funega moyǒ, with an alternate inflected form below; removed the artificial internal gap in Embarcação.
[c2-l018 >] moyǒta. *Eſtarem amarradas as embarcações*
[c2-l018 note] Moyǒta continues the mooring expression, interpreted as ships being tied together. Restored the final vowel sequence of embarcações from the scan instead of OCR embarcaçõos.
[c2-l019 >] *huas às outras. ¶* Funega nagaruru. *Ir*
[c2-l019 note] The ships are tied to one another; a new example then says Funega nagaruru, a ship drifting. The older -ruru form is retained.
[c2-l020 >] *a embarcação pella agoa a baixo. ¶* Fune-
[c2-l020 note] Completes the drifting-downstream gloss. The next Fune- starts another ship example and is not part of the Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l021 >] ga ſonzuru. *Deſmancharſe o nauio, ou dar*
[c2-l021 note] Funega ſonzuru means a ship being damaged. The Portuguese includes breaking up or running against something, continued below.
[c2-l022 >] *a costa, ou nalgum baixo. ¶* Funega ſuua
[c2-l022 note] Completes running ashore or onto a shoal, then begins Funega ſuuaru. Do not equate every use of Japanese 'sit' with a human posture.
[c2-l023 >] ru. *Eſtar em ſeco, ou encalhar o nauio.*
[c2-l023 note] Completes ſuuaru and explains a ship being dry or aground. The physical split lacks a supplied hyphen and is retained that way.
[c2-l024 >] *¶* Funega vorita, l, vyta. *Nadar a ẽ-*
[c2-l024 note] Funega vorita, l, vyta refers to the vessel being afloat or launched. The scan really supports vorita; the Portuguese does not justify silently replacing it with another verb. Vyta is the alternative 'floated'.
[c2-l025 >] *barcação, ou eſtar ja pera poder nauegar.*
[c2-l025 note] Completes being ready to navigate. This fuller gloss helps explain the previous launch/afloat wording rather than requiring both Japanese alternatives to be exact synonyms.
[c2-l026 >] *¶* Funeuo nauoſu. *Endereitar a embarca*
[c2-l026 note] Funeuo nauoſu is straightening or redirecting a ship. The direction sense is specified by the following Portuguese clause.
[c2-l027 >] *ção, ou virala pera outro rumo. Vt,* Fune-
[c2-l027 note] Virala pera outro rumo means turning it to another course. The scan supports virala, not OCR vtrala; Vt introduces a literary illustration.
[c2-l028 >] uo nauoxiqeruga, ſacamacu namini vchi
[c2-l028 note] Funeuo nauoxiqeruga describes having redirected the vessel, followed by waves surging against it. The connected Japanese requires reading on into vchi-cayeſarete.
[c2-l029 >] cayeſarete, &c. Taif. *Lib. 33. Go-*
[c2-l029 note] Restored passive cayeſarete, 'being overturned', instead of nonsensical cayeſatete. Roman Taif. is the Taiheiki citation label; Lib. 33 is italic and the numeral is retained.
[c2-l030 >] *uernarão a outro rumo, porem com as ondas ẽ-*
[c2-l030 note] The Portuguese describes steering to another course but encountering contrary waves. Its historical tense spelling is preserved.
[c2-l031 >] *contradas que vinhão ſendo virada, & ſoſo-*
[c2-l031 note] Completes the contrary-wave clause and describes the vessel being turned and overwhelmed. The printed ſoſo- is retained as a historical spelling, not changed to modern soço-.
[c2-l032 >] *brada a Fune. ¶* Funeuo cogu. *Remar*
[c2-l032 note] Completes the capsizing gloss, then Funeuo cogu means rowing the vessel. Corrected OCR Foneuo using the visible vowel and context; the embedded Fune before ¶ remains italic.
[c2-l033 >] *a embarcação. ¶* Funeuo daſu. *Fazer*
[c2-l033 note] Funeuo daſu means sending the vessel out. Fazer introduces a causative Portuguese equivalent continued below.
[c2-l034 >] *partir a embarcação. ¶* Funeni noru. *Em*
[c2-l034 note] Completes making the ship depart, then Funeni noru means boarding. The visible separation in partir a is restored; it is not the future verb partira.
[c2-l035 >] *barcarſe. ¶* Funeni meſaruru. *Embar-*
[c2-l035 note] Completes boarding and introduces the honorific Funeni meſaruru. The repeated maritime context does not remove the social distinction.
[c2-l036 >] *carſe peßoa nobre. ¶* Funeuo fuqimodo-
[c2-l036 note] Completes the noble-person boarding gloss and begins Funeuo fuqimodoſu, blowing a vessel back. The f is meaningful here and should not be changed merely to long s.
[c2-l037 >] ſu. *Fazer o vento arribar o nauio.*
[c2-l037 note] Completes the wind-driven return: the wind causes the ship to return or put back. Arribar is understood in its nautical context.
[c2-l038] Fûnet. *Febre cauſada de vento, ou ar.*
[c2-l038 note] Fûnet is wind-related fever (風熱). The enlarged scan clearly shows u with circumflex, a rare but attested printed form; do not normalize it to ǔ just because that is more common.
[c2-l039] Fungomi, u, gôda. *Meter o pè. Vt,* Ca
[c2-l039 note] Fungomi, u, gôda gives the verb for putting one's foot in. The example begins Cata axiuo, 'one foot'; the accent in the principal part is retained.
[c2-l040 >] ta axiuo anano nacaye fungôde iru teide
[c2-l040 note] Cata axiuo anano nacaye fungôde iru teide is having one foot in a hole. The visible mark over o points upward, agreeing with gôda above; corrected OCR caron without changing the inflection.
[c2-l041 >] gozaru. Mon. *Eſtaua com hum pè ja me*
[c2-l041 note] Gozaru completes the Japanese expression; roman Mon. labels the wording. The Portuguese already interprets it as having one foot in the grave.
[c2-l042 >] *tido na coua. i, Perto de morrer. ¶* Do-
[c2-l042 note] Completes that figurative interpretation explicitly: near death. Do- begins Doroni in the next example, not a Portuguese preposition.
[c2-l043 >] roni fungomu. *Estar atolado na lama,*
[c2-l043 note] Doroni fungomu is being stuck or sinking in mud. The older Japanese inflection is retained and matches atolado na lama.
[c2-l044] Funhoi. Cocorono gotoqu narazu. i, Fubẽ
[c2-l044 note] Funhoi is not being as one wishes, explained Cocorono gotoqu narazu and Fubẽ na coto. The nasalized e belongs to roman Japanese text; removed an accidental italic span.
[c2-l045 >] na coto. *Pobreza, ou falta do neceßario,*
[c2-l045 note] Fubẽ na coto continues into the gloss of poverty or lack of necessities. The entry is broader than a narrowly modern sense of inconvenience.
[c2-l046 >] *ou de poder, & habilidade pera fazer al-*
[c2-l046 note] The gloss also includes lack of ability or capacity. Restored poder from the worn round vowel rather than OCR peder; no modernization of habilidade is needed.
[c2-l047 >] *gũa couſa.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes 'to do something'. The final nasalized Portuguese form remains the scan/OCR reading, not a mechanically normalized alternate tilde placement.
