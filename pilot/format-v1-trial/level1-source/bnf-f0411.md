[c1-l001 >] *fauores del Rey.* S.
[c1-l001 note] Continues the preceding entry with favours from the king; the damaged word is Rey, not merely R. The final upright S. is the written-register label, outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l002] Quǒxen. Qina izumi. *Lugar de baixo da*
[c1-l002 note] Quǒxen is explained literally as yellow springs; the Portuguese begins the description of a place beneath the earth. Qina is one Japanese word, not the OCR fragments Q ina.
[c1-l003 >] *terra, onde vão ter as almas dos que morrem,*
[c1-l003 note] Completes beneath the earth and says that the souls of the dead go there. The clause belongs to the underworld entry, not to a geographical spring.
[c1-l004 >] *como cuidão os gentios.*
[c1-l004 note] The compilers qualify the preceding account as what the non-Christian population believes. This attribution is part of the historical gloss, not an assertion by the reviewer.
[c1-l005] Quǒxi. Qina cami. *Papel amarelo.* S.
[c1-l005 note] The Japanese explanatory phrase means yellow paper, agreeing directly with Papel amarelo. The headword is continuous and the final S. is an upright register label.
[c1-l006] Quǒ ya. Firoqi no. *Campo largo, & eſpa-*
[c1-l006 note] Quǒ ya is paraphrased as a broad field. The scan retains a real gap before ya, unlike the spurious OCR gap after initial Q; the Portuguese continues eſpa-/çoſo across the physical rows.
[c1-l007 >] *çoſo. ¶ Item, Campo inculto, & deixa-*
[c1-l007 note] Completes spacious and adds the sense of uncultivated, abandoned ground. The second physical division is deixa-/do, retained rather than joined.
[c1-l008 >] *do em mortorio.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes deixado em mortorio, describing ground left uncultivated or out of use. The short row is a genuine continuation of the preceding gloss.
[c1-l009] Quǒye. Qina coromo. *Veſtido amarelo, que*
[c1-l009 note] The explanatory phrase identifies a yellow garment. The following relative clause specifies its use by Japanese Buddhist clergy; the damaged long-o mark is retained as a caron.
[c1-l010 >] *veſtem os Bonzos de Iapão por cima doutro*
[c1-l010 note] Explains that Japanese Buddhist monks wear this yellow garment over another garment. Iapão is Portuguese within the italic gloss, not a Japanese typeface-toggle term.
[c1-l011 >] *veſtido.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes doutro vestido, the underlying garment. This single-word continuation is kept on its own physical row.
[c1-l012] Quǒyǒ. Ficari cacayaqu. *Reſplẽdor.* S.
[c1-l012 note] The Japanese phrase describes light shining and the Portuguese gives radiance. Enlargement establishes an abbreviation tilde over e in Reſplẽdor; both headword long-o marks are carons.
[c1-l013] Quǒyô. Qibamitaru fa. *Folhas amarelas*
[c1-l013 note] This separate entry concerns yellowed leaves. Its final circumflex contrasts with the preceding headword's caron; the damaged explanatory form reads Qibamitaru, not Q ibamitaru.
[c1-l014 >] *como as do outono que eſtão pera cair.*
[c1-l014 note] Specifies autumn leaves about to fall, completing the yellow-leaf sense. No extra botanical identification is required.

[c1-l015 initial=6] RA. *Hũa das 4 7. ſyllabas*
[c1-l015 note] Ra is one of the stated forty-seven Japanese syllables. The decorated R belongs to this headword and spans six physical rows; the OCR had omitted it and left only A. The spaced numerals are retained.
[c1-l016 >] *do Alfabeto Iaponico.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes the description of ra as a syllable of the Japanese alphabet. The adjacent ornament is the preceding line's enlarged R, not additional text on this row.
[c1-l017 >] Ra. *Virga genitalis.*
[c1-l017 note] A second ra entry gives the Latin anatomical gloss for the penis. The small a is abraded but the residual lower strokes support Ra; this is separate from the enlarged syllabary entry.
[c1-l018 >] Rabiǒ. *Doença das par-*
[c1-l018 note] Rabiǒ denotes a disease of the private parts, as the gloss continues below. The headword's initial R and damaged medial b correct the misleading OCR Babiǒ.
[c1-l019 >] *tes ſecretas.*
[c1-l019 note] Completes partes secretas, specifying the affected anatomical region. It continues Rabiǒ rather than the new animal entry below.
[c1-l020 >] Racco. *Certo animal domar*
[c1-l020 note] Racco is the marine animal whose fur is described next. The visible initial is R, not OCR F; domar retains the printed joined spelling rather than silently introducing a word boundary.
[c1-l021 >] *de cabelos q̃ pera qualquer parte q̃ os inclinão*
[c1-l021 note] Describes the animal's hairs as yielding in whichever direction they are inclined. Both abbreviation signs over q are retained without expansion.
[c1-l022 >] *cõ a mão ſe deitão. ¶* Raccono cauano yǒna
[c1-l022 note] Completes the observation that the fur lies down when stroked by hand. The Japanese example begins a comparison with racco skin, retaining the printed word spacing.
[c1-l023 >] fitogia. *He homem q̃ facilmente ſe inclina*
[c1-l023 note] Completes a phrase describing a person like that skin: someone who readily inclines to others' opinions. Fitogia contains the copular ending, not a separate headword.
[c1-l024 >] *ao parecer de quẽ quer, & pera qualquer parte.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the metaphor of a pliable person who follows anyone's opinion in any direction. The tilde in quẽ is abbreviation, not a Japanese vowel mark.
[c1-l025] Raccha. *Hũa mezinha como maßa miuda em-*
[c1-l025 note] Raccha is a medicine resembling fine dough or paste. The next line names tea as an ingredient; the printed em- is retained at the edge.
[c1-l026 >] *que entra* Cha.
[c1-l026 note] Completes the medicinal description by saying that tea enters into it. Cha is Japanese embedded in a Portuguese explanation and its upright type is restored.
[c1-l027] Rachi. *Sebe. ¶ Permet.* Rachiuo aquru.
[c1-l027 note] Rachi first denotes a hedge or enclosure, then introduces a figurative expression for making matters clear. The scan shows the object-particle form Rachiuo, not Rachino; the e in the compressed Permet. is faint.
[c1-l028 >] *Dar boa razão das couſas. ¶* Rachino ai-
[c1-l028 note] The Portuguese explains giving a clear account of things. A new example begins Rachino aita, continued below; unlike the preceding example, its particle is read no. The n is faint, so that distinction remains a chosen, nonblocking reading rather than a claim of an undamaged glyph.
[c1-l029 >] ta fito. *Homem ſingelo, & que logo obe-*
[c1-l029 note] Completes Rachino aita fito, characterized as straightforward and readily responsive to reason. The Portuguese verb is physically divided obe-/dece.
[c1-l030 >] *dece à razão. ¶* Rachiuo coyuru. l, ya-
[c1-l030 note] Completes obedience to reason, then introduces exceeding or breaking the enclosure figuratively. The headword repeated in the example begins R, not the OCR's N; l introduces the alternative yaburu.
[c1-l031 >] buru. *Quebrar a ley, & preceitos, ou ir*
[c1-l031 note] Buru completes yaburu. The Portuguese explains the figurative expressions as breaking law or precepts, with another paraphrase continuing below.
[c1-l032 >] *contra a rezão.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes going against reason. The printed historical form is rezão, with e, rather than the razão used a few rows above.
[c1-l033] Racocu. *Hũa caſcazinha delgada que tem o*
[c1-l033 note] This Racocu denotes a thin inner skin of bamboo, specified on the next line. The initial is R; the middle o is abraded, but the surviving sequence supports Racocu rather than OCR Facocu.
[c1-l034 >] *bambu por dentro.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the definition by locating the thin skin inside bamboo. It is not a general description of outer bark.
[c1-l035] Racocu. *Certa aguila.*
[c1-l035 note] A separate Racocu names a particular kind of aguila. Enlargement shows dotted i followed by l, not OCR agulla; the short gloss supplies no further description of the named material or kind.
[c1-l036] Racocu. *Hũa laya de vestido de ſeda fina que*
[c1-l036 note] Another Racocu denotes a kind of fine-silk garment. The following line identifies its royal wearers; the supplied short-s spelling in vestido is retained without a global st conversion.
[c1-l037 >] *os Reis da China, ou de Iapão veſtẽ por cima.*
[c1-l037 note] Explains that kings of China or Japan wear this garment over other clothing. The faint capital C in China is recoverable; veſtẽ retains the printed abbreviation tilde.
[c1-l038] Racqio. i, Vochitçuqu. *O aueriguarſe, ou*
[c1-l038 note] Racqio is glossed with the Japanese verb for settling down or reaching a conclusion. Vochitçuqu is a single upright Japanese form; the OCR's italic V was spurious.
[c1-l039 >] *concluirſe algũa couſa de embaraço como de-*
[c1-l039 note] Describes resolution of a troublesome matter, such as a lawsuit. The visible como replaces OCR cono; the abbreviation mark remains on u in algũa.
[c1-l040 >] *manda. ¶* Cujiga racqio xita. *Con-*
[c1-l040 note] Manda completes demanda; the Japanese example says the lawsuit was settled. The scan supports xita, not aita, and Con- begins the Portuguese conclusion at the top of column 2.

[c2-l001 note] The lawsuit was concluded; the next example concerns a fortress. The scan prints the full Concluiuſe despite Con- at the end of column 1, so the repeated beginning is preserved rather than silently deleted.
[c2-l002 >] xita. *Rendeoſe a fortaleza. ¶* Xeqen-

[c2-l003 >] ga racqio xenu. *Não estar ainda o reino*
[c2-l003 note] Completes the negative example: the realm has not yet become settled. Xenu is the negative of the verbal construction, not an independent headword.
[c2-l004 >] *apaziguado, & quieto de guerras, & reuoltas.*
[c2-l004 note] Explains the unsettled realm as not yet pacified or free of wars and revolts. The entire line continues the same negative example.
[c2-l005] Racqua. Fanano chiru coto. *O cairẽ as roſas,*
[c2-l005 note] Racqua is the falling of blossoms, explained by hana no chiru koto. The Portuguese mentions roses and continues with flowers below; cairẽ retains its tilde.
[c2-l006 >] *& flores. Vt,* Racqua yedani cayerazu.
[c2-l006 note] Completes the flower gloss and introduces the saying that a fallen blossom does not return to its branch. The apparent extra r after the ampersand was OCR noise.
[c2-l007 >] *A flor caida nõe torna ao ramo. Vſaſe per*
[c2-l007 note] Translates the fallen-flower saying and begins its figurative application. The abraded negative is retained provisionally as printed-looking nõe, rather than silently regularized to não; a tight but real space separates A flor.
[c2-l008 >] *met. Acerca da idade, ou annos que paſſados*
[c2-l008 note] Explains that the saying is applied figuratively to age or elapsed years. Per met. is divided between this row and the preceding one.
[c2-l009 >] *não tornão.*
[c2-l009 note] Completes the comparison: years once passed do not return. This short row finishes the application of the flower proverb.
[c2-l010] Racu. Tanoximi. *Goſtos, ou prazeres.*
[c2-l010 note] Racu is equated with enjoyment and glossed as delights or pleasures. The noun is followed by two distinct verbal examples.
[c2-l011 >] *¶* Racuuo ſuru, l, vquru. *Delitarſe.*
[c2-l011 note] The alternatives suru and ukuru express enjoying or receiving pleasure. The gloss visibly reads Delitarſe, without supplying the additional vowel of modern deleitar-se.
[c2-l012 >] *¶* Racuni fuqeru. *Darſe muito a delici-*
[c2-l012 note] Racuni fuqeru describes giving oneself over to pleasure. The Portuguese word for delights is divided delici-/as across the physical rows.
[c2-l013 >] *as, & prazeres.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes delicias and adds pleasures, finishing the explanation of indulgence. The opening as is a word ending, not an article beginning a new gloss.
[c2-l014] Racu aſobi. *Folguedo liure, & à vontade.*
[c2-l014 note] Racu asobi denotes recreation pursued freely and at one's ease. The initial R corrects OCR B, while the internal long s remains as printed.
[c2-l015] Racuba. Vma yori votçuru. *O cair do caua-*
[c2-l015 note] The Japanese phrase explicitly means falling from a horse. The right edge prints the continuation mark after caua; the Portuguese words cair do are distinct.
[c2-l016 >] *lo. ¶* Racuba ſuru. *Cair do caualo.*
[c2-l016 note] Lo completes caualo; the repeated Racuba suru gives the verbal expression for falling from a horse. The abraded repeated headword is supported by its surviving strokes and the line above, not OCR Raotba.
[c2-l017] Racubai. Votçuru vme. *O cair a flor desta*
[c2-l017 note] Racubai concerns falling ume blossoms. The Portuguese describes the blossom of this tree rather than a fallen fruit; the tree comparison continues below.
[c2-l018 >] *aruore que he como ameixieira.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes the tree description by comparing it to a plum tree. The abraded historical spelling ameixieira is retained, without importing a modern botanical name.
[c2-l019] Racuchǔ. Miyacono vchi. *Dentro do Mi-*
[c2-l019 note] Racuchǔ means within the capital, paraphrased by Miyacono vchi. Miyaco is divided across the row boundary. The u bears a caron; only a faint remnant survives where the supplied Mi- division mark stands, so that mark remains a nonblocking uncertainty.
[c2-l020 >] *yaco. ¶* Racuchǔ racuguaiuo femeguri
[c2-l020 note] Completes Miyaco in the gloss, then begins a Japanese example of travelling around inside and outside the capital. The repeated Racuchǔ has a visible long-u mark, omitted by the OCR.
[c2-l021 >] mauaru. *Rodear portodo Miyaco por den-*
[c2-l021 note] Mauaru completes the circuit-making verb. The Portuguese explains going throughout Miyaco; the printed joined portodo is preserved, and dentro is divided at the edge.
[c2-l022 >] *tro, & por fora.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes dentro and adds outside, matching the contrast between racuchǔ and racuguai in the example.
[c2-l023] Racuda. Votçuru votçuru. i, Bǒzu zocuni
[c2-l023 note] Racuda is paraphrased by repeated falling and then by a monk's return to secular life. The Japanese explanation continues with cayeru below; the repetition is printed, not an OCR duplicate.
[c2-l024 >] cayeru. *Fazerſe o religioſo ſecular. Vt,* Ra-
[c2-l024 note] Completes the monk's return to secular status. The damaged Portuguese verb reads Fazerſe, including r; Ra- starts the repeated verbal example.
[c2-l025 >] cuda ſuru.
[c2-l025 note] Completes Racuda suru, the expression for leaving religious status. This short Japanese continuation is not an independent entry.
[c2-l026] Racudai. *Certo modo de compor verſos, ou*
[c2-l026 note] Racudai here denotes a particular method of composing verse, not an automatically imposed modern failure or examination sense. The definition continues with songs below.
[c2-l027 >] *cantigas.*
[c2-l027 note] Adds songs to verses as the objects of the composing method. The single-word row completes the preceding Racudai entry.
[c2-l028] Racufat. i, Camiuo ſorivotoſu. *Raparſe*
[c2-l028 note] Racufat is explained as shaving off the hair. The scan contains the complete Raparſe at the right edge, not the OCR's truncated Raparſ-; the headword's final t is retained.
[c2-l029 >] *a primeira vez, ou fazerſe Bonzo. Vt,* Ra-
[c2-l029 note] Specifies the first shaving, or becoming a Buddhist monk. The next repeated example begins Ra- here and continues on the following row.
[c2-l030 >] cufatſuru.
[c2-l030 note] Completes Racufatſuru. The final-t stem and suru are joined in the print; no extra vowel or modern word division is supplied.
[c2-l031] Racugan. Votçuru cari. *Pato brauo que de-*
[c2-l031 note] The Japanese identifies a descending wild goose, while the Portuguese calls it a wild duck. This lexical mismatch is preserved rather than harmonized; de- continues into dece below.
[c2-l032 >] *ce, ou pouſa em baixo.* S.
[c2-l032 note] Completes descending or alighting below. The final upright S. is the register label, not part of the italic bird description.
[c2-l033] Racugi. Chini votçuru. *O cair em terra.* S.
[c2-l033 note] The Japanese phrase means falling to the ground, matching the Portuguese exactly. The damaged headword vowel is a, not OCR i, and the final S. is upright.
[c2-l034] Racugiacu. Vochi tçuqu. *O determinar, ou*
[c2-l034 note] Racugiacu concerns settling or determining a matter, expressed through ochitsuku. The printed space in Vochi tçuqu is retained rather than normalized to the joined form elsewhere.
[c2-l035 >] *ſentenciar. Vt,* Cujiga racugiacu xita. *A-*
[c2-l035 note] Completes determining or passing judgment and gives the settled-lawsuit example. The small line-end mark after the Portuguese article A is visible and retained despite the unusual division.
[c2-l036 >] *demanda està concluida, ou dada ſentença.*
[c2-l036 note] The lawsuit is concluded or judgment has been given. The cedilla in ſentença is visible; the historical grave in està remains unchanged.
[c2-l037 >] *¶* Mada xianga racugiacu xenu. *Não*
[c2-l037 note] The Japanese example says one's deliberation has not yet settled. The negative Portuguese translation begins here and continues on the next row.
[c2-l038 >] *eſtar ainda reſoluto no parecer.*
[c2-l038 note] Completes not yet being resolved in one's opinion. This is the mental-decision sense of the same entry, contrasting with the lawsuit example.
[c2-l039] Racuguai. Miyacono foca. *Fora do Miyaco.*
[c2-l039 note] Racuguai means outside the capital, matching Miyacono foca. The restored R and full Miyacono correct OCR losses; Miyaco in the Portuguese gloss is an embedded Japanese name.
[c2-l040] Racuji. i, Iiuo caqivotoſu. *Letra que ſe não*
[c2-l040 note] Racuji denotes an omitted written character. The explanatory Iiuo represents ji wo under the dictionary's consonantal-I convention; it is not the OCR's long-s form ſiuo.
[c2-l041 >] *eſcreueo por eſquecimento.*
[c2-l041 note] Completes the definition as a character not written through forgetfulness. The historical verb spelling eſcreueo is preserved.
[c2-l042] Racujit. Votçuru fi. i, Figa catamuqu. *O ir-*
[c2-l042 note] Racujit is sunset, explained by the descending or inclining sun. The scan separates fi. from the explanatory i,; OCR fiai merged these elements and incorrectly split the italic Portuguese ir-.
[c2-l043 >] *ſe pondo o ſol, ou ſol que ſ vai pondo.*
[c2-l043 note] Completes the sun's setting. The second reflexive element is visibly defective or abraded as ſ; the expected e is not supplied without surviving evidence.
[c2-l044] Racujǒ. Votçuru xiro. *Fortaleza rendida.*
[c2-l044 note] Racujǒ denotes a surrendered or fallen fortress. The caron over o is retained; this is distinct from the preceding sunset entry.
[c2-l045] Racuracuto. *Adu. Aſeu prazer, ou deſ-*
[c2-l045 note] The adverb means at one's pleasure or at ease. Aſeu retains the tight printed joining; the tall character is long s rather than the OCR's f.
[c2-l046 >] *canſadamente. ¶* Racuracuto ſuguru. *Pa*
[c2-l046 note] Completes descansadamente and introduces passing one's time at ease. The edge prints Pa without a secure hyphen, so none is inferred before ſar below.
[c2-l047 >] *ſar o tempo folgando, ou leuando boa vida.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes the gloss as spending time in pleasure or living a good life. The initial ſar is the continuation of Pa, not the beginning of a new entry.
