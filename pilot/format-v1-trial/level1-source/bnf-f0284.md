[c1-l001] Ienji. Yoqi coto. *Couſa boa, ou virtudes.*
[c1-l001 note] Ienji (zenji), glossed Yoqi coto, denotes a good thing or virtuous deeds; the Portuguese introduces the moral sense developed in the examples.
[c1-l002 >] *Vt,* Ienjiuo voconǒ. *Fazer boas obras.*
[c1-l002 note] Ienjiuo voconǒ means to perform good deeds. The Japanese example is roman and the introductory Vt and Portuguese translation are italic.
[c1-l003 >] *¶* Ienji jengon. *Idem.*
[c1-l003 note] Restored the visible paragraph sign before Ienji jengon, the pairing of good deeds and good words; Idem refers back to the virtuous conduct just described.
[c1-l004] Ienji. *Meſtre da ley dos Ienxùs.*
[c1-l004 note] The homophonous Ienji (zenji) is a master of the Zen teaching, not the preceding word for good deeds; Ienxùs occurs inside the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l005] Ienjit, l, xenjit. Mayeno fi. *Dia dantes,*
[c1-l005 note] Restored the headword’s capital I. Ienjit and xenjit are alternative readings for the preceding day, explained by Mayeno fi; l introduces the alternative form.
[c1-l006 >] *ou os dias atras.*
[c1-l006 note] The continuation broadens the preceding-day gloss to days in the past. It belongs to Ienjit, rather than opening a new entry.
[c1-l007] Ienjǒ. Mayeno vmare. *Nacimento dantes,*
[c1-l007 note] Ienjǒ (zenjō), explained by Mayeno vmare, means a former birth or existence; the Portuguese description continues on the next row.
[c1-l008 >] *ou idade poſſada. Vt,* Mina core jenjǒno
[c1-l008 note] Corrected cote to core in the example, “all this [is] of a former existence.” Retained poſſada: its first vowel has the round, worn form reflected by the baseline, although passada is the expected Portuguese word.
[c1-l009 >] ingua nari. *Iſto tudo he empago, & retri-*
[c1-l009 note] Ingua nari completes the claim that everything is the karmic consequence of a former existence. The Portuguese has the joined form empago and ends with retri-, continued by the omitted row restored below.
[c1-l009a >] *buição do que fez noutra idade, ou nacimento.*
[c1-l009a note] Restored this entire physical row from the scan. Together with retri- it reads retribution for what one did in another age or birth, completing the karmic explanation of the Japanese example.
[c1-l010] Ienmai. *Erua aßi chamada.*
[c1-l010 note] Ienmai (zenmai) names an edible fern; the Portuguese merely calls it an herb so named, without supplying a more specific botanical description.
[c1-l011] Ienmi. Ienno agiuai. *Goſto da virtude.*
[c1-l011 note] Corrected Ierano to Ienno and reunited virtude. Ienno agiuai is the taste or savor of virtue, explaining Ienmi rather than a separate example.
[c1-l012] Ienmǒ. Ienno nozomi. *Bõs deſejos, ou deſe*
[c1-l012 note] Ienmǒ, explained by Ienno nozomi, denotes good wishes or a desire for virtue. The visible text ends deſe without a secure terminal hyphen; it continues with jo on the next row.
[c1-l013 >] *jo da virtude.*
[c1-l013 note] Jo completes deſejo from the preceding row, without a printed hyphen at that break. The whole Ienmi definition concerns taking pleasure in, or desiring, virtue.
[c1-l014] Ienmon. i, Camiuo ſotta fito. *Homem rapa-*
[c1-l014 note] Restored the capital I of Ienmon. Camiuo ſotta fito describes a person with the hair shaved, matching the Portuguese phrase split across this and the following row.
[c1-l015 >] *do. ¶ Item, Bonzo da ſeita dos Ienxùs.*
[c1-l015 note] Completes rapado and adds the more specific sense of a monk of the Ienxùs sect. The worn internal letters of Bonzo are retained as the recognizable printed word.
[c1-l016] Iennanxi. *Homem virtuoſo.*
[c1-l016 note] Iennanxi (zennan-shi) denotes a virtuous man; this short, complete entry has no continuation on the next row.
[c1-l017] Iennaua. *Vide,* Ieninaua.
[c1-l017 note] Iennaua is referred to Ieninaua, the cord used for threading cash coins on the preceding page; the damaged medial letters do not warrant a different cross-reference.
[c1-l018] Iennen. Yoqi nen. *Bons penſamentos.*
[c1-l018 note] Corrected the final OCR a in penſamentoa to s. Yoqi nen means good thoughts, distinguishing this entry from the chronological Iennen immediately below.
[c1-l019] Iennen, l, xennen. Mayeno toxi. *Annos*
[c1-l019 note] Iennen and xennen, explained by Mayeno toxi, denote previous years. The Portuguese plural Annos continues with its adjective on the following row.
[c1-l020 >] *paſados.*
[c1-l020 note] The short continuation supplies “past” for the preceding Annos. Retained the baseline’s paſados rather than regularizing the historical spelling.
[c1-l021] Iennhonin. Yoqi vonna. *Molher virtuoſa,*
[c1-l021 note] Iennhonin is explained by Yoqi vonna, a good woman; the Portuguese calls her virtuous and continues with an alternative characterization on the next row.
[c1-l022 >] *ou ſancta.*

[c1-l023] Ienni. i, Bicuni. *Rapada, ou freira de*

[c1-l024 >] *Iapão.*

[c1-l025] Iennin. Yoqi fito. *Homem virtuoſo, ou*

[c1-l027] Ienpai. Mayeno tomogara. *Homem antiguo,*

[c1-l028 >] *ou do tempo paßado.*
[c1-l028 note] The continuation describes someone from past times. Joined paſa do: the apparent gap is damaged letterwork within the word, not a meaningful word division; retained the single long ſ of the baseline.
[c1-l029] Ienpen. Mayeno fen. *Hum volume inteiro*
[c1-l029 note] Ienpen is glossed Mayeno fen, the preceding part. The Portuguese first describes a complete volume containing multiple books or parts, with that description continuing below.
[c1-l030 >] *de muitos liuros, ou partes. ¶ Item, Ali*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the description of a volume made up of several books or parts; Item then opens another usage. Ali belongs with quando on the following row and has no secure terminal hyphen.
[c1-l031 >] *quando, Paragrapho, ou capitulo paſſado, ou*
[c1-l031 note] Joined Paragra pho to the printed word Paragrapho. With Ali above, Aliquando introduces the occasional sense of a preceding paragraph or chapter, consistent with Mayeno fen.
[c1-l032 >] *que fica atras.*
[c1-l032 note] “Or which lies behind” completes the preceding-paragraph sense of Ienpen. The row is a continuation, not a new entry.
[c1-l033] Ienpit. Ienno fude. *Certa feição de letras, ou*
[c1-l033 note] Ienpit, glossed Ienno fude, refers to a particular manner of writing; the explanation of its letter forms continues over the next two rows.
[c1-l034 >] *caracteres que os Bonzos Ienxùs folgão de imi-*
[c1-l034 note] Corrected Iemxus to the visible Ienxùs. These are the character forms that Zen monks take pleasure in imitating; imi- continues into tar below.
[c1-l035 >] *tar à ſemelhança das letras da China.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes imitar and specifies imitation of Chinese letter forms. The comparison belongs to the calligraphic definition, not to an additional headword.
[c1-l036] Ienpô. Yoqi nori. *Boa ley.*
[c1-l036 note] Ienpô is explained by Yoqi nori, a good law or teaching; the Portuguese gives the compact equivalent Boa ley.
[c1-l037] Ienqe. Ienno iye. *Familia, ou ordem dos*
[c1-l037 note] Corrected Iamilia to Familia: the worn initial retains the form of F. Ienno iye is a Zen house or lineage, described as a family or order in Portuguese.
[c1-l038 >] *Ienxùs.*
[c1-l038 note] Corrected the OCR initial t to I. The word completes “family or order of the Ienxùs,” so it is an embedded Japanese sect-name, not a separate entry.
[c1-l039] Ienqiô. Yoqi voxiye. *Boa doutrina.*
[c1-l039 note] Ienqiô, explained by Yoqi voxiye, denotes good teaching or doctrine. The circumflex in the supplied headword is retained rather than normalized to a modern reading.
[c1-l040] Ienqi. *Caualeiros que vão diante. Vt,* Ien-
[c1-l040 note] Removed the spurious a in Ienaqi and restored the whole example opening Ien- to roman type. This Ienqi denotes horsemen who go ahead, distinguished from the homophonous Zen term below.
[c1-l041 >] qi imada ſarazaruni, goqiqi ſoiqitaru. *Não*
[c1-l041 note] The example says that before the leading riders have gone, riders behind come close after them. Retained the visibly repeated qi in goqiqi rather than silently replacing it with the expected goqi; the Portuguese translation begins with Não.
[c1-l042 >] *tendo ainda paſſado diante os de caualo, ja lhe*
[c1-l042 note] Corrected ju to the visible ja. The translation describes the rear riders already close behind before those in front have passed; its sentence continues on the next row.
[c1-l043 >] *hião nas coſtas os de de tras.*
[c1-l043 note] The rear riders were following close on the leaders’ backs. Preserved hião and the separately written de tras as historical Portuguese forms.
[c1-l044] Ienqi. *Suſtãcia, ou eſpiritu de Ienxùs. ¶* Iẽ-
[c1-l044 note] Corrected the sect-name’s damaged vowel to Ienxùs and made the complete example fragment Iẽ- roman. This Ienqi is the substance or spirit of Zen, not the preceding word for riders.
[c1-l045 >] qi aru fito. *He homem que tem eſpirito dos*
[c1-l045 note] Restored the dropped p in eſpirito. Iẽ-qi aru fito describes a person possessing the spirit of Zen; the Portuguese translation continues below.
[c1-l046 >] *Ienxùs, ou penetra as ſuas couſas.*
[c1-l046 note] Restored Ienxùs and the continuation indentation. The translation ends by saying that the person penetrates or understands the matters of the Ienxùs; this is not an independent headword.
