[c1-l001 >] *parte de meyo dedo indice. ¶ Item, permet.*
[c1-l001 note] Continues Sunbun from the preceding page: a tenth part of half an index finger. The next sense is figurative; the printed fused permet. is not expanded to per met.
[c1-l002 >] *Hum tamanino. Vt,* Sunbunmo chigauanu.
[c1-l002 note] The figurative meaning is a tiny amount. Sunbunmo chigauanu says that there is not even the slightest difference; the scan reads Sunbunmo, not Sonbunmo.
[c1-l003 >] *Não diſcrepa nẽhum ponto.*
[c1-l003 note] The Portuguese gives 'does not differ in any point', completing the preceding Japanese example. The abbreviated nẽhum is retained.
[c1-l004] Sunca. Sunno itoma. *Vagar, ou breue tẽpo.*
[c1-l004 note] Sunka is a brief interval of free time, paraphrased as Sunno itoma. The nasal abbreviation in tẽpo remains part of the diplomatic text.
[c1-l005 >] *¶* Suncauo yezu. *Não tendo vagar nem*
[c1-l005 note] Sunka wo ezu means having no leisure, not even a short interval. The negative definition is completed on the following row.
[c1-l006 >] *eſpaço breuiſimo.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes the absence of even the briefest interval. The supplied breuiſimo spelling is retained; the compact s-form may include a tightly joined second s, but this remains uncertain and is not silently regularized.
[c1-l007] Suncô. *Certo lugar onde ſe toma o pulſo, que*
[c1-l007 note] Suncô names a particular pulse-taking location. The dictionary's historical explanation identifies it by the examining fingers in the next two lines.
[c1-l008 >] *reſponde ao primeiro dos tres dedos que he c*
[c1-l008 note] The location corresponds to the first of three fingers. The final faint c-shaped glyph is retained as c, although the syntax expects the article o before ĩdice; its right side is too weak to establish a closed o confidently.
[c1-l009 >] *ĩdice cõ que os Chinas, & Iapões tomão o pulſo.*
[c1-l009 note] The finger is the index finger used in the Chinese and Japanese pulse-taking practice described here. The visible tilde over initial i in ĩdice restores an abbreviation lost by OCR.
[c1-l010] Sune. *Perna, ou pees.*
[c1-l010 note] Sune is explained as leg or feet. The source's broader gloss and historical pees spelling are preserved without replacing them with a modern anatomical definition.
[c1-l011] Sune, nuru, eta. *Arrufarſe, ou moſtrarſe*
[c1-l011 note] The verb sune, sunuru means sulking or showing displeasure. Its principal parts distinguish it from the preceding body-part noun.
[c1-l012 >] *de ruim ſembrante com algum mao enſino, ou*
[c1-l012 note] The behavior is described as a bad expression accompanied by ill manners or obstinacy. Mao enſino is retained as the historical phrase rather than modernized.
[c1-l013 >] *teima. ¶* Sunemono. l, ſuneta mono.
[c1-l013 note] Obstinacy completes the gloss; sunemono and suneta mono describe a sulky person. The scan has the alternative marker l, and a space before mono, not the OCR's ul and intrusive i.
[c1-l014 >] *Peßoa que ſe arrufa, & moſtra teimoſo, ou*
[c1-l014 note] The person sulks and behaves obstinately. Arrufa has two r letters with no internal word space, correcting the draft's at rufa.
[c1-l015 >] *mal enſinado.*
[c1-l015 note] The alternative description is ill-mannered or badly brought up. This closes the definition of the sulky person.
[c1-l016] Suneate. *Grebas, ou armas das pernas.*
[c1-l016 note] Suneate are greaves, armor for the legs. Grebas is the printed Portuguese form and is not modernized.
[c1-l017] Sunen. Cazuno toxi. *Muitos annos.*
[c1-l017 note] Sunen means many years, with Cazuno toxi as its Japanese paraphrase. The scan confirms the initial S already present in the supplied draft.
[c1-l018] Sungueqi. Sunno fima. *Vagar, ou breue tẽpo.*
[c1-l018 note] Sungueki is another expression for a brief interval or leisure, paraphrased as Sunno fima. The spelling gue preserves the source's representation of the consonant before e.
[c1-l019 >] *¶* Sũgueqiuoyezu. *Não tẽdo tẽpo nẽ vagar.*
[c1-l019 note] Sungueki wo ezu means having neither time nor leisure. The Japanese example is upright throughout, including its visibly marked ũ; its tight printed spacing and the faint g are retained.
[c1-l020 >] *¶* Sungueqiga nai. *Não ter nem hum*
[c1-l020 note] Sungueki ga nai supplies another negative expression: not having even a tiny amount of time. The phrase continues below.
[c1-l021 >] *tamanino de tempo, ou vagar.*
[c1-l021 note] Completes the absence of the smallest amount of time or leisure. Tamanino is retained as the source's diminutive wording.
[c1-l022] Sunin. i, Amatano fito. *Muitos homẽs.*
[c1-l022 note] Sunin means many people, explicitly equated with Amatano fito. The mark after i is a comma, and the nasal mark in homẽs is retained.
[c1-l023] Sun-in. i, Sunno fima. *Pouco tempo, ou*
[c1-l023 note] Sun-in is a short time or interval, not the draft's Bun-in. The printed internal hyphen distinguishes this form graphically from the preceding Sunin.
[c1-l024 >] *vagar.* S.
[c1-l024 note] Leisure completes the gloss. The final S is upright and labels written usage according to the supplied dictionary key; it does not belong to the italic definition.
[c1-l025] Sunjen. Sunno jen. i, Sucoxino jen. *Hũ*
[c1-l025 note] Sunzen means a little virtue or goodness, explained by both Sunno jen and Sucoxino jen. Hũ begins its Portuguese equivalent and remains divided from the following noun phrase.
[c1-l026 >] *tamanino de virtude. Vt,* Sunjen xacu-
[c1-l026 note] The example contrasts a small measure of goodness with a larger measure of temptation. The scan reads xacu-, with an open c rather than OCR e; the compound continues with ma below.
[c1-l027 >] ma. *Tamanino de virtude, tamanho de*
[c1-l027 note] Ma completes xacuma. The Portuguese deliberately contrasts tamanino, a tiny amount, with tamanho, a larger amount, before naming the opposing force on the next row.
[c1-l028 >] *tentação.*
[c1-l028 note] Temptation completes the contrast with the small amount of virtue. The short continuation is retained as its own physical line.
[c1-l029] Sunoco. *Caniçada que ſe faz nas caſas hum*
[c1-l029 note] Sunoko is explained as reed or bamboo latticework made inside houses. Its raised position is specified on the next row.
[c1-l030 >] *pouco aleuantada do chão. ¶* Sunoco yẽ.
[c1-l030 note] The lattice is raised slightly above the ground. Sunoko en then names a veranda of this construction; the printed nasal mark in yẽ is retained.
[c1-l031 >] *Varanda feita com caniçada de bambus.*
[c1-l031 note] Sunoco yẽ is a veranda made with bamboo latticework. The definition explains the example above rather than introducing another headword.
[c1-l032] Sunpǒ. *Medida. Vt,* Sunpǒuo toru. *To-*
[c1-l032 note] Sunpou denotes measurement, and Sunpǒuo toru means taking a measurement. The entire Portuguese To- is italic; the draft had wrongly left its T in the Japanese roman run.
[c1-l033 >] *mar a medida, ou tamanho da couſa.*
[c1-l033 note] Completes tomar, taking the measure or size of something. The original line division is retained instead of joining the verb.
[c1-l034] Sunxacu. *Medida queſe toma. Vt,* Sunxa-
[c1-l034 note] Sunshaku is a measurement that is taken. The source's tight queſe is retained, and the Japanese example is divided after Sunxa-.
[c1-l035 >] cuuo toru.
[c1-l035 note] Completes Sunxacu wo toru, to take a measurement. This short Japanese continuation has no printed Portuguese addition.
[c1-l036] Sunxi. Sunno cocorozaxi. *Pequeno agaſa-*
[c1-l036 note] Sunshi, paraphrased as Sunno cocorozaxi, expresses a small token of goodwill or affection. The Portuguese divides agaſalhado across the physical line break.
[c1-l037 >] *lhado, ou amor.*
[c1-l037 note] Completes the printed agaſalhado and adds love or affection as the alternative gloss. The unusual noun form is retained, not replaced by modern agasalho.
[c1-l038] Sunzun. *Pedacinhos. Vt,* Sunzunni qiru.
[c1-l038 note] Sunzun means little pieces; Sunzunni qiru illustrates cutting something into those pieces. The repeated-unit form remains exactly as printed.
[c1-l039 >] *Cortar em pedacinhos.*
[c1-l039 note] The Portuguese directly translates the preceding example, to cut into little pieces. It is a continuation rather than a new dictionary entry.
[c1-l040] Suppa. *Engano, ou mentira.*
[c1-l040 note] Suppa denotes deceit or a lie in this entry. The doubled p is retained as part of the Japanese transcription.
[c1-l041] Suppana mono. *Vadio, ou enganador, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] Suppana mono describes an idle person or deceiver. The entry applies the preceding quality to a person without supplying a modernized Japanese equivalent.
[c1-l042] Suqe. *Pontalete. ¶* Suqeuo co. *Por*
[c1-l042 note] Suke is a prop or support; Suqeuo co introduces putting props in place. The short printed co is retained without adding a vowel-length mark on the faint o.
[c1-l043 >] *pontaletes como pera não cair a caſa, &c.*
[c1-l043 note] The props prevent a house from falling, completing the practical example of structural support. Pera remains in its historical spelling.
[c1-l044] Suqe, uru. *Por algũa couſa de baixo doutra de*
[c1-l044 note] Suke, sukuru means putting something beneath another object. The resulting support or elevation is explained below.
[c1-l045 >] *maneira que a ſoſtente, ou aleuante. Vt,* Fã-
[c1-l045 note] The inserted object supports or raises the other. Fã- begins the example's table word, which is completed by daino on the following row.
[c1-l046 >] daino ficui catani itauo ſuqei. *Metei de*
[c1-l046 note] The example directs someone to put a board under the table's low side. The Portuguese line ends with de without a printed hyphen; baixo begins the following physical row.
[c1-l047 >] *baixo daquella parte da meſa mais baixa hũa*
[c1-l047 note] The scan restores the severely garbled instruction: beneath the lower part of the table, put a board. The last word is hũa, not a hyphenated fragment; taboa starts the next column.

[c2-l001 >] *taboa. ¶* Moru tocoroni taraiuo ſuqu-
[c2-l001 note] Taboa completes the board-under-the-table instruction from column 1. A new example places a basin beneath a leak. The second pass confirms tocoroni, not tocoruni, and ſuqu- with u and a terminal hyphen.
[c2-l002 >] ru. *Por de baixo da goteira algũa ſelha. No*
[c2-l002 note] Ru completes the Japanese verb. The Portuguese specifies placing a tub beneath dripping water, then begins a regional qualification with No.
[c2-l003 >] *Cami ſe diz,* Moriuo voqu. *¶ Itẽ, Dar*
[c2-l003 note] In the Kami region the expression is Moriuo voqu. Cami is embedded in Portuguese and is flagged for typeface review; Dar, including its visible final r at the rule, starts the next sense of giving aid.
[c2-l004 >] *ajuda na guerra aos que vão enfraquecendo ou*
[c2-l004 note] The next sense is giving help in war to those who are weakening. Guerra has two r letters in the scan, correcting guera.
[c2-l005 >] *vão no alcance dos inimigos, & hão miſter ſo-*
[c2-l005 note] The aid also concerns those in pursuit of enemies who need reinforcement. Alcance, with l, corrects aſcance; ſo- begins the word for assistance on the next row.
[c2-l006 >] *corro, &c. ¶* Suqe jei. *Gente de ſo-*
[c2-l006 note] Corro completes ſocorro. Suqe jei denotes supporting or reinforcing troops; its Portuguese equivalent is also divided across the line break.
[c2-l007 >] *corro. ¶ Item,* Suqe, uru. *Dizer por*
[c2-l007 note] After completing 'reinforcement troops', the entry returns to the verb suke, sukuru and introduces prompting someone verbally from behind.
[c2-l008 >] *de tras, & ajudar aos que vão dizẽdo de cor*
[c2-l008 note] The helper prompts people who are reciting from memory. De cor has its memorization sense here, not a reference to color.
[c2-l009 >] *algũa couſa. Tambem ſe dirà do que ajuda a*
[c2-l009 note] The recited material is completed, and another use concerns helping someone drink. The scan reads ajuda with j, not OCR aluda.
[c2-l010 >] *beber ao que não pode por ſer pouco amigo de*
[c2-l010 note] The person has difficulty drinking because of little liking for wine. Não and the final de correct OCR mão and da; the explanation finishes on the next row.
[c2-l011 >] *vinho, &c.*
[c2-l011 note] Wine completes the preceding description. This short continuation closes the extended helping or assisting senses of Suqe.
[c2-l012] Suqi, u, uita. *Laurar os campos, & var-*
[c2-l012 note] This suki means plowing fields and low-lying cultivated ground. The scan reads var-, not vur-, at the start of the divided Portuguese varzeas.
[c2-l013 >] *zeas. ¶* Denbacuuo ſuqu. *Idem.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes varzeas. Denbaku wo suku gives the Japanese expression for cultivating fields, assigned the same meaning by Idem.
[c2-l014] Suqi, u, uita. *Vt,* Camiuo ſuqu. *Fazer*
[c2-l014 note] Here kami wo suku means making paper, not plowing. The right-edge Portuguese word is Fazer, correcting the OCR's Fagoir; its object follows below.
[c2-l015 >] *papel.*
[c2-l015 note] Paper completes the preceding making sense. This short line is the Portuguese object, not an isolated Japanese entry.
[c2-l016] Suqi, u. *Vt,* Camiuo ſuqu. *Pentear aos*
[c2-l016 note] The same-looking Japanese expression now concerns combing hair. This Cami is hair, whereas the preceding Cami referred to paper; both remain roman examples, not embedded Portuguese terms.
[c2-l017 >] *mininos pera lhes tirar os pioulhos. ¶* Vma-
[c2-l017 note] The combing removes lice from children. A second example begins Vma-, the word for horse, and continues on the next physical line.
[c2-l018 >] no camiuo ſuqitatçuru. *Troſquiar dambas*
[c2-l018 note] Completes Vmano camiuo ſuqitatçuru, trimming the horse's mane. Troſquiar and dambas correct the draft's j and final r; the Portuguese specifies both sides below.
[c2-l019 >] *as partes a coma do caualo.*
[c2-l019 note] The mane is trimmed on both sides. Coma is the historical Portuguese word for the mane here, not an altered reading of a Japanese word.
[c2-l020] Suqi. *Hũa certa enxada à maneira de pà ferra-*
[c2-l020 note] The noun suki is a particular digging implement, compared to an iron-shod spade. The source divides ferrada across this and the next row.
[c2-l021 >] *da. ¶* Suqide foru, l, foricayeru. *Ca-*
[c2-l021 note] Completes ferrada and gives digging or turning the soil with the implement. Suqide, with u, corrects Saqide; the alternative foricayeru and its marker remain as printed.
[c2-l022 >] *uar, ou virar a terra com esta pà.*
[c2-l022 note] Completes cavar, digging or turning the earth with this spade. The printed accent in pà is retained.
[c2-l023] Suqi. *Inclinação. ¶ Item, Arte, & ex-*
[c2-l023 note] Another suki is an inclination or liking, followed by the special sense of the art and practice of tea. Inclinação corrects the OCR's m.
[c2-l024 >] *ercicio do Chanoyu. ¶* Suqiuo ſuru. *Dar*
[c2-l024 note] Completes the tea-practice sense and introduces Suqiuo ſuru, devoting oneself to it. Chanoyu occurs inside the Portuguese explanation and is flagged as an embedded Japanese term.
[c2-l025 >] *ſe à arte do Chanoyu.*
[c2-l025 note] Completes Darſe à arte do Chanoyu, applying oneself to the art of tea. The line-initial ſe is not joined back across the physical break; Chanoyu is again embedded in Portuguese.
[c2-l026] Suqi, u, uita. *Ser inclinado a algũa couſa.*
[c2-l026 note] The verb suki means being inclined toward something. Its headword has no extra j after q, correcting the OCR while retaining the printed principal parts.
[c2-l027 >] *Vt,* Icuſa, chanoyu nadoni ſuqu. *Ser in-*
[c2-l027 note] The Japanese example names war and tea practice as interests. Chanoyu here belongs to the standalone Japanese example, so it is not a typeface-term candidate merely because the Portuguese translation follows.
[c2-l028 >] *clinado à guerra, Chanoyu, &c.*
[c2-l028 note] Completes the Portuguese translation, being inclined to war, Chanoyu, and similar pursuits. This occurrence of Chanoyu is within Portuguese and is separately flagged.
[c2-l029] Suqi, u, uita. *Ser tranſparente.*
[c2-l029 note] This homonymous suki means being transparent. Its brief definition is independent of the preceding preference or inclination sense.
[c2-l030] Suqi. l, ſuqima. *Greta, ou fenda. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c2-l030 note] Suki, alternatively sukima, is a crack or opening. The scan restores Greta and the complete Itẽ marker; a figurative use follows on the next row.
[c2-l031 >] *per met. Tempo, ou vagar. Vt,* Fimano
[c2-l031 note] Figuratively, the opening is time or leisure. Fimano starts the Japanese expression, whose second component begins the next line without an invented hyphen.
[c2-l032 >] ſuqiuo yezu. *Não ter tempo, ou eſpaçode*
[c2-l032 note] Fima no suki wo ezu means having no time or opportunity. The initial glyph is long ſ, not f; the printed close spacing of eſpaçode is retained.
[c2-l033 >] *fazer algũa couſa. ¶* Suqiuo nerǒ. *Buſ-*
[c2-l033 note] The unavailable opportunity is to do something. Suki wo nerau introduces looking for an opening or suitable moment; the Portuguese verb continues below.
[c2-l034 >] *car conjunção de tempo.*
[c2-l034 note] Completes buscar, seeking a suitable conjunction of circumstances or opportunity in time. The historical conjunção wording is not replaced with a modern paraphrase in the text.
[c2-l035] Suqiconomi, u, ôda. *Ser inclinado, ou ami-*
[c2-l035 note] Sukikonomi means having a liking or inclination for something. The scan reads Suqiconomi without the draft's extra i after Su; the printed ôda ending is retained.
[c2-l036 >] *go de algũa couſa.*
[c2-l036 note] Completes amigo de algũa couſa, being fond of something. This continuation finishes the compound's preference sense.
[c2-l037] Suqifara. *O eſtar em jejum, ou não ter comido*
[c2-l037 note] Sukihara means being on an empty stomach or not having eaten. The alternative Portuguese wording continues with nada below.
[c2-l038 >] *nada. Vt,* Suqifarade gozaru.
[c2-l038 note] The example means being on an empty stomach. Suqifarade repeats the headword before de gozaru; the scan restores fara where the draft had fiua.
[c2-l039] Suqima. *Vide,* Suqi.
[c2-l039 note] Sukima is referred back to Suki, the crack or opening sense above. Vide is italic apparatus, while the Japanese cross-reference remains roman.
[c2-l040] Suqimano caje. *Vento que vem coado por al-*
[c2-l040 note] Sukima no kaze is a draft passing through a gap. The Portuguese describes wind filtering through an opening, with the location continued below.
[c2-l041 >] *gũa porta, ou buraco, &c.*
[c2-l041 note] The opening can be a door or a hole. Gũa completes algũa from the preceding physical line.
[c2-l042] Sǔqiô. i, Cuſa caru vonoco. *Homem baixo*
[c2-l042 note] The headword is paraphrased as a man who cuts grass. The Portuguese calls him a lowly man; baixo corrects OCR beixo, while the two different vowel marks in Sǔqiô are preserved.
[c2-l043 >] *que corta erua.*
[c2-l043 note] The man cuts grass or herbage, completing the Portuguese definition. This matches Cuſa caru in the Japanese paraphrase.
[c2-l044] Suqitouori, u, otta. *Ser tranſparente.*
[c2-l044 note] Sukitoori is being transparent. The r before final i is heavily worn but has residual traces; restoring it yields the compound supported by the gloss and the supplied NINJAL index, without changing the principal-part ending.
[c2-l045] Suqixa. *Peßoa dada ao exercicio, & arte do*
[c2-l045 note] Sukisha is a person devoted to the practice and art of tea. The activity named after do is printed on the following short row.
[c2-l046 >] *chanoyu.*
[c2-l046 note] Chanoyu completes the Portuguese description of the tea devotee. Its n is faint but leaves two stem traces; the word is restored without the OCR's internal gap and flagged as Japanese embedded in Portuguese.
[c2-l047] Suquan. Cazu, maqi. *Muitos liuros, ou par-*
[c2-l047 note] Suquan denotes multiple books or constituent volumes. The scan restores initial S, masculine Muitos, and liuros rather than OCR litros; Cazu, maqi retains the printed comma and spacing.
[c2-l048 >] *tes do meſmo volume.*
[c2-l048 note] Completes partes do meſmo volume, parts of the same volume. The upright Suri. at the far right is a separate catchword, not part of this Portuguese sentence.

## catchword [catchword] Column 2 catchword

[cw-l001 >>] Suri.
