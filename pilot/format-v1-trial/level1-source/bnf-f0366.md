[c1-l001] Nindǒ. Bup. *Hum dos ſeis caminhos, ou*
[c1-l001 note] Nindō is the human realm among the six Buddhist paths. Bup. is the upright Buddhist-domain label; the Portuguese enumeration continues below.
[c1-l002 >] *eſtados de que ſe falla no* Buppô, *que he o homẽ*
[c1-l002 note] The six paths are described as states taught in Buddhist doctrine, with this one identified as the human being. The embedded Buppô has a circumflex and upright type; OCR Buppò misplaced the accent category.
[c1-l003 >] *morador neſte mundo.*
[c1-l003 note] Completes the definition of the human realm as the person inhabiting this world. This is a short continuation of Nindǒ, not an independent entry.
[c1-l004] Ningai. *Corpo humano. ¶ Item, Eſte mũ-*
[c1-l004 note] Ningai is glossed first as the human body and then as this world. The printed mũ- begins mundo across the physical line break.
[c1-l005 >] *do, em que os homens viuem.*
[c1-l005 note] Do completes mũ-do; the second sense is the world in which human beings live. The gloss does not reduce both senses to the modern word for a person.
[c1-l006] Ningiacu. Macaxe tçuqu. *Froxidão, ou pou-*
[c1-l006 note] Ninjaku is explained by Macaxe tçuqu and by Portuguese weakness or lack of resolution. The printed Japanese paraphrase is retained without imposing modern lexical segmentation.
[c1-l007 >] *ca reſolução nas couſas. ¶* Ningiacuna
[c1-l007 note] Ca completes pouca: little decisiveness in matters. Ningiacuna begins the adjectival example describing a person on the following row.
[c1-l008 >] fito. *Homem froxo, & que não ſe reſolue*
[c1-l008 note] Fito completes ninjakuna hito, an irresolute person. The Portuguese specifies someone weak who does not readily make up his mind.
[c1-l009 >] *facilmente.*
[c1-l009 note] Facilmente completes the negative construction above: the person does not resolve matters easily. Its brevity does not make it furniture or a separate entry.
[c1-l010] Ningiǔ. Fitono naca. *Entre os homens.*
[c1-l010 note] Ninjū, paraphrased hito no naka, means among people. The headword's caron on u is retained independently of the different long-vowel mark in Nindǒ.
[c1-l011] Ningu. *Homem de forças.*
[c1-l011 note] Ningu is a strong man, directly matched by homem de forças. The entry is distinct from the following ningue despite their similar opening letters.
[c1-l012] Ningue. Fitoni baquru. *O tomar o demo-*
[c1-l012 note] Ninge is paraphrased hito ni bakuru, taking human form. The Portuguese makes a demon the transforming subject; demo- continues into demonio below.
[c1-l013 >] *nio figura de homem. S.*
[c1-l013 note] Completes the demon's assumption of a human figure. The final S. is an upright written-language label, not part of the italic definition. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l014] Ninguen. *Genero humano.*
[c1-l014 note] Ningen is glossed as humankind or the human race. The collective Portuguese wording is preserved rather than narrowed to one individual.
[c1-l015] Ninguiǒ. Fitono catachi. *Estatua, ou boni-*
[c1-l015 note] Ningyō, literally a human figure in the accompanying paraphrase, is a statue or puppet. Its marked final vowel distinguishes it from the unmarked ningyo entry two rows below.
[c1-l016 >] *frate, &c.*
[c1-l016 note] Frate completes the divided Portuguese boni-frate, a puppet or jointed figure. It belongs to the preceding statue/doll entry.
[c1-l017] Ninguio. *Sereas que com ſeu canto dizem que*
[c1-l017 note] Ningyo is glossed through the European comparison with sirens. The text reports what is said about their singing, rather than asserting a zoological observation.
[c1-l018 >] *fazem dormir aos que as ouuem.*
[c1-l018 note] The reported effect of the sirens' song is to put listeners to sleep. This completes the relative clause begun in the preceding row.
[c1-l019] Ninjen. Fitono maye. *Diante dos homens.*
[c1-l019 note] Ninzen, paraphrased hito no mae, means before or in the presence of people. Maye retains the printed historical spelling.
[c1-l020] Ninji. *Couſa, ou obra humana. S.*
[c1-l020 note] Ninji here denotes a human matter or work. The upright S. marks written usage and distinguishes this noun entry from the verb immediately below. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l021] Ninji, zuru, ita. *Meter a alguem no numero*
[c1-l021 note] The verb ninzuru means admitting someone into a group or appointing him to a rank. The abbreviated principal parts are retained, and the Portuguese sentence continues below.
[c1-l022 >] *dos outros, ou aleuantar em dignidade, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Completes admission among others and adds elevation in dignity or rank. The historical aleuantar spelling is not modernized.
[c1-l023 >] *¶* Fitouo curaini ninzuru. *Aleuantar a*
[c1-l023 note] Hito wo kurai ni ninzuru illustrates appointing a person to a rank. The object and rank expressions are printed as Fitouo and curaini, without editorial spaces inside them.
[c1-l024 >] *alguem a dignidade.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes the translation: raising someone to a dignity or office. This is the continuation of Aleuantar a, not a second example.
[c1-l025] Ninjin. Fitono mi. *Corpo humano.*
[c1-l025 note] This ninjin means the human body, explained by hito no mi. A small but visible i after j restores Ninjin from OCR Ninjn; the three neighboring homographs have different glosses.
[c1-l026] Ninjin. Fitono cocoro. *Coração humano.*
[c1-l026 note] This separate ninjin denotes the human heart or mind, matching hito no kokoro. The worn opening of cocoro is interpreted with the surviving letters and the Portuguese heart gloss.
[c1-l027] Ninjin. *Hũa certa erua medicinal que tem a*
[c1-l027 note] The third ninjin is a medicinal plant, identified by its root in the continuation. The definition should not be conflated with the body and heart entries above.
[c1-l028 >] *raiz como de cinouras, ou rabãos.*
[c1-l028 note] The medicinal plant has a root compared with carrots or radishes. Raiz, not OCR ruiz, is visible; the historical cinouras and rabãos are retained without modernizing their vowels or tilde placement.
[c1-l029] Nininzzure. *O irem dous em companhia.*
[c1-l029 note] Nininzure means two people going together. The printed doubled zz and the Portuguese dous remain part of the diplomatic text.
[c1-l030] Ninjǒ. Fitono naſaqe. *Piedade humana.*
[c1-l030 note] Ninjō here means human compassion, paraphrased hito no nasake. The a of naſaqe is abraded, but the surviving letter spacing does not justify OCR's internal word break; the long ſ is retained.
[c1-l031] Ninjǒ. *O ferir. Vt,* Ximobedomouo nin-
[c1-l031 note] This distinct ninjō means wounding. The example begins shimobe-domo wo, referring to servants; the scan supports Ximobedomouo rather than OCR Ximobodomouo, and nin- continues the verb below.
[c1-l032 >] jǒxi, xetgai xitacotoua chicagoro fun-
[c1-l032 note] Completes ninjōshi and continues with killing the servants, followed by an expression of incomprehension. Xetgai retains the printed checked t; fun- belongs to funbet on the next row.
[c1-l033 >] betni voyobanu. Fei. *L. 2. O ferir, &*
[c1-l033 note] Funbet ni oyobanu says that the action is beyond understanding. Fei. is the upright source label; L. 2. and the Portuguese translation are italic apparatus.
[c1-l034 >] *matar os criados, ou ſeruos não o entendo.*
[c1-l034 note] Completes the translation: the speaker does not understand wounding and killing the servants. The paired criados and ſeruos explains the Japanese shimobe-domo.
[c1-l035] Ninju. Fitocazu. *Numero de gente, ou*
[c1-l035 note] Ninju denotes the number of people, paraphrased hitokazu. Its gloss also allows the collective sense people, continued below.
[c1-l036 >] *gente.*
[c1-l036 note] This short continuation supplies the alternative collective gloss people. It is not a new entry and must remain attached in interpretation to Ninju.
[c1-l037] Ninju. Fitono inochi. *Vida humana. S.*
[c1-l037 note] This homographic ninju means human life, explained by hito no inochi. The scan reads Ninju, not OCR Nanju; S. is an upright written-usage label. Typeface update: S. is provisionally italicized under the bulk convention.
[c1-l038] Ninmin. Fito, tami. *Gente popular.*
[c1-l038 note] Ninmin means the common people, with hito and tami as Japanese explanations. The Portuguese distinguishes the populace from an individual person.
[c1-l039] Ninnicu. *Paciencia com algũa humildade.*
[c1-l039 note] Ninniku here means patient forbearance with some humility. The abraded letter after Pa has the surviving curve of c, supporting Paciencia rather than OCR Pariencia; it is unrelated to the garlic homograph below.
[c1-l040] Ninnicu. *Alhos.*
[c1-l040 note] This separate ninniku is garlic. The simple Portuguese Alhos establishes the botanical sense without merging it with patient endurance above.
[c1-l041] Ninnin. Fitobito. *Cada hum dos homens, ou*
[c1-l041 note] Ninnin, paraphrased hitobito, first has a distributive meaning: each of the people. The alternative gloss continues on the next physical row.
[c1-l042 >] *cada peßoa. ¶ Item, Homens.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes each person and then adds the collective sense men or people. The visible ß-shaped sort in peßoa is retained rather than replaced with modern ss.
[c1-l043] Nino miya. *Filho ſegundo del Rey.*
[c1-l043 note] Ni no miya denotes the sovereign's second son. The Portuguese uses Rey; the note does not replace the printed royal terminology with a modern institutional title.
[c1-l044] Nino vde. *Braço do cotouelo atè o collo da mão.*
[c1-l044 note] Ni no ude is described here as the portion from elbow to wrist. That source definition is retained even though a modern reader may associate ninoude with a different part of the arm.
[c1-l045] Ninpinin. *O que tem figura de homem não o*
[c1-l045 note] Ninpinin begins a new entry for something with human form that is not human. Its line begins at the entry margin, so the erroneous continuation indent is removed while the ID is preserved.
[c1-l046 >] *ſendo, como ſe imagina no* Buppô. *¶ Item,*
[c1-l046 note] Completes the Buddhist nonhuman-being sense before introducing a figurative use. Imagina corrects OCR tmagina; Buppô is upright throughout, with a worn second p but a clear circumflex.
[c1-l047 >] *per met. Peßoa que ſe não conta nem admi-*
[c1-l047 note] The figurative sense is a person not counted or admitted among others; admi- continues as admite at the top of column 2. The damaged ß-shaped sort in Peßoa has visible right-hand strokes absent from OCR Peſoa.

[c2-l006 note] Ninsoku, alternatively ninbu, is a person carrying goods. Latin l introduces the alternative, while fa- begins Portuguese fato on the following row.
[c2-l007 >] *to como mariola, &c.*
[c2-l007 note] Completes fato, goods or baggage, and compares the carrier with a porter. This clarifies that the entry concerns hired carrying work rather than human strength in general.
[c2-l008] Nintai. Fitono tai. *Suſtancia, ou corpo huma-*
[c2-l008 note] Nintai is explained as a person's substance or body. The printed Suſtancia is retained without adding the b of modern substância.
[c2-l009 >] *no. ¶ Item, Peßoa nobre, ou afidalgada.*
[c2-l009 note] No completes humano; a second sense is a noble or genteel person. The ß-shaped sort is visible in Peßoa, and the regional qualification follows below.
[c2-l010 >] *X. No cami ſe diz,* Iintai.
[c2-l010 note] The X. label marks the regional usage just given, and the text says that in cami the form is Iintai. The scan has one X, not OCR Xx; lowercase cami remains italic as printed, while Iintai is upright and represents jintai rather than a sequence beginning with two vocalic i's.
[c2-l011] Ninuǒ. *Rey dos homẽs. ¶ Item, Pri-*
[c2-l011 note] Ninnō is glossed as king of men, with another sense beginning first king. The visible e restores Rey from OCR Ry; Pri- continues below.
[c2-l012 >] *meiro Rey de Iapão.*
[c2-l012 note] Meiro completes Primeiro, giving the dictionary's second gloss as the first king of Japan. The Portuguese designation is reported without adding an identification absent from this entry.
[c2-l013] Ninuxi. *Dono do fato.*
[c2-l013 note] Ninushi means the owner of the goods or baggage. The rounded capital D and following o support Dono, correcting OCR Bano.
[c2-l014] Ninxet. Fitono xet. *Pratica dos homẽs, ou*
[c2-l014 note] Ninset, paraphrased hito no set, denotes human discourse or teaching. The final checked t is retained rather than expanded to a modern tsu syllable.
[c2-l015 >] *opinião, & doutrina que decende, ou vem ſò-*
[c2-l015 note] The definition continues with opinion and doctrine originating from human beings alone. Opinião corrects OCR opimião; the historical decende and divided ſò- remain as printed.
[c2-l016 >] *mente de homẽs.*
[c2-l016 note] Mente completes ſò-mente, restricting the source of the doctrine to human beings. It is the close of the Ninxet definition, not a new example.
[c2-l017] Nippon. Fino moto. *Iapão.*
[c2-l017 note] Nippon is Japan, accompanied by the Japanese paraphrase hi no moto. The visible Fino moto spacing and the Portuguese spelling Iapão are retained.
[c2-l018] Nippongui. i, Nifongui. *Vide ſuprà.*
[c2-l018 note] Nippongi is equated with Nifongui and referred to an earlier entry. The editorial i and the Latin instruction remain distinct from the Japanese names.
[c2-l019] Niqi. *Dous tempos do anno. Como primaue-*
[c2-l019 note] Niki denotes two periods of the year. The definition begins its paired examples with primaue-, continued as primavera below.
[c2-l020 >] *ra, & eſtio, ou Outono, & inuerno.*
[c2-l020 note] Completes primavera and pairs spring with summer, or autumn with winter. The scan reads eſtio, not OCR's transposed eſito; the printed capitalization of Outono is retained.
[c2-l021] Niqibi. *Crauo, ou eſpinha que nace no roſto.*
[c2-l021 note] Nikibi is a facial spot or pimple. The historical nasce-less spelling nace and the provisional roſto s/t classification are retained from the print and aligned baseline.
[c2-l022 >] *No Ximo ſe diz,* Nicumi.
[c2-l022 note] The regional note gives Nicumi as the Ximo form corresponding to nikibi. Ximo is embedded in the Portuguese sentence and remains italic in this impression; Nicumi is the upright cited alternative.
[c2-l023] Nira. *Hũa erua como cebolas que ſe come em*
[c2-l023 note] Nira is an edible onion-like herb. Como is a single printed word, not OCR co mo; the dish in which it is eaten follows on the next row.
[c2-l024 >] *Xiru, & outras couſas.*
[c2-l024 note] Xiru, soup, is a Japanese word embedded in the Portuguese description of how nira is eaten. Its italic type is retained rather than automatically changed merely because the word is Japanese.
[c2-l025] Nirami, u, ǒda. *Olhar com os olhos fitos, &*
[c2-l025 note] The niramu verb is glossed as looking with fixed eyes. Its historical past-form abbreviation ǒda is retained rather than modernized to nda; the manner of looking continues below.
[c2-l026 >] *reſgalados. Vt,* Benqei Togaxiuo chǒ-
[c2-l026 note] The eyes are also described as wide open. The example names Benqei looking at Togaxi and divides chōdo between chǒ- here and do below.
[c2-l027 >] do nirǒde, nanitaru yaxin chǒbonno mo
[c2-l027 note] Completes chōdo nirōde, looking directly or intently, and continues Benqei's challenge about a leader of conspirators. The final mo continues into mono on the next row without a supplied hyphen.
[c2-l028 >] nouo mexicomeraruruzo. Tog. *Olhã-*
[c2-l028 note] The Japanese challenge ends with the form mexicomeraruruzo and the upright citation label Tog. The translation begins Olhã-, with a visible abbreviation tilde and terminal hyphen, not OCR Olhi-.
[c2-l029 >] *do Benquei com os olhos reſgalados aquelle homẽ*
[c2-l029 note] Do completes Olhãdo: Benquei looked wide-eyed at that man. The scan joins aquelle, unlike OCR a quelle; the Portuguese rendering of the Japanese name remains as printed.
[c2-l030 >] *lhe diße que cabeça, ou capitão de trèdos pren-*
[c2-l030 note] Continues the challenge about what chief or captain of traitors is being held. The visible ß-shaped sort gives diße, and a small grave is retained in trèdos; pren- continues the verb below.
[c2-l031 >] *deis?*
[c2-l031 note] Deis completes prendeis, and the visible question mark closes the challenge. OCR deisẽ mistook that punctuation for a letter and diacritic.
[c2-l032] Niramitçume, uru, eta. *Olhar com os olhos*
[c2-l032 note] Niramitsumeru intensifies the fixed stare. The printed principal parts introduce a sustained action, explained across the next row.
[c2-l033 >] *fitos, & reſgalados por algum eſpaço.*
[c2-l033 note] The eyes remain fixed and wide open for some time. This duration distinguishes the entry from the preceding general niramu definition.
[c2-l034] Nire. l, Nireno qi. *Aruore aßi chamada.*
[c2-l034 note] Nire, alternatively nire no ki, is identified simply as a tree of that name. The source does not provide a botanical description here, so none is inserted into the transcription.
[c2-l035] Niſǒ. Futatçuno catachi. *Interior, & ex-*
[c2-l035 note] Nisō is explained as two aspects, interior and exterior. The visible t restores Futatçuno from OCR Futarçuno; ex- continues the Portuguese exterior below.
[c2-l036 >] *terior. Vt,* Niſǒuo ſatoru. i, Vomote-
[c2-l036 note] After completing exterior, the example says to perceive the two aspects. Vomote- begins the explanatory Japanese phrase continued on the next row.
[c2-l037 >] muqiuo mite miyenu tocorouo xiru.
[c2-l037 note] The phrase begins with looking at the exterior; the next row glosses knowing the interior. Miyenu ('unseen') would fit the gloss, but is not supplied without those strokes; miyuru/miyeru are likewise not imposed.
[c2-l038 >] *Pollo exterior adiuinhar, & ſaber o interior.*
[c2-l038 note] The Portuguese explains inferring and knowing the interior from the exterior. This supplies the intended relation between the two aspects without authorizing an emendation of the preceding Japanese wording.
[c2-l039] Niſocu. *Aues. ¶* Xiſocu, niſocuuo xo-
[c2-l039 note] Nisoku is glossed as birds; the example combines quadrupeds and bipeds as food. Xo- begins shokusuru at the line break.
[c2-l040 >] cuſuru. *Comer carne de aues, & animaes.*
[c2-l040 note] Completes shokusuru and translates the example as eating the flesh of birds and animals. Both categories belong to the same example rather than separate definitions.
[c2-l041] Niſsan. i, Fimairi. *Ir cada dia à Tera, ou di-*
[c2-l041 note] The intended word is nissan, daily attendance, equated with himairi. No usable i is visible in the abraded gap after N, so N ſsan is retained rather than supplied from the NINJAL lexical aid; Ir cada is visibly two words. Tera occurs within the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l042 >] *ante do ſenhor.*
[c2-l042 note] Ante completes diante: daily attendance may be at a temple or before one's lord. This continuation completes the two destinations in the previous line.
[c2-l043] Nite. *Propoſição de Ablatiuo. ¶ Itẽ, Par-*
[c2-l043 note] Nite is first described as an ablative preposition, using the printed Propoſição rather than a modernized grammatical term. Par- begins the second account as a particle.
[c2-l044 >] *ticula que com outras ſignifica ſendo. Vt,* Yo-
[c2-l044 note] The particle, combined with other words, means being. Yo- starts yoki in the example on the following row.
[c2-l045 >] qi fitonite. *Sẽdo bom homem.*
[c2-l045 note] Completes yoki hito nite, being a good person. The tilde in Sẽdo is preserved rather than expanded in the canonical transcription.
[c2-l046] Nitomeqi, u, eita. *Modo de pegar algũa cou-*
[c2-l046 note] Nitomeku concerns the sticking or adhesion of something. The compressed principal parts and the Portuguese algũa abbreviation remain printed forms, with cou- continuing below.
[c2-l047 >] *ſa molle, & viſcoſa.*
[c2-l047 note] Completes the description of something soft and sticky. The isolated Nito- beneath this line is the catchword, not a further part of the definition.

## catchword [catchword] Printed catchword

[u2b-l001 >>] Nito-
