[c1-l001] Saimon. Futatabi tô. *O perguntar a ſegunda*
[c1-l001 note] Saimon is explained by futatabi too, asking a second time. This is the repeated-question sense, distinct from the western gate in the next entry; the printed circumflex in tô is retained.
[c1-l002 >] *vez.* S.
[c1-l002 note] Vez completes 'a segunda vez', a second time. The final S labels written usage rather than supplying another part of the Japanese expression. The standalone S. is upright, outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l003] Saimon. Nixino mon. *Porta da parte do*
[c1-l003 note] This Saimon is nishi no mon, a gate on the western side. The Portuguese directional phrase continues into Poente on the following row.
[c1-l004 >] *Poente.*
[c1-l004 note] Poente, the west or sunset side, completes the gate definition. The short continuation is a separate physical line, not a new entry.
[c1-l005] Sainami, u, ǒda. *Affigir, ou atormẽtar, &c.*
[c1-l005 note] Sainami denotes afflicting or tormenting someone. The principal parts and the caron in ǒda are retained rather than regularized to modern verb forms; the Portuguese affliction verb has a compressed impression. Enlargement confirms the anomalous Affigir without an l; it is not silently expanded to Affligir.
[c1-l006] Sainan. Comaca, yauaraca. *Couſa miuda,*
[c1-l006 note] Sainan is explained as komaka and yawaraka, fine and soft. This material-quality sense is distinguished from the following homonymous word for misfortune.
[c1-l007 >] *brãda, & delgada, como peça de ſeda.* S.
[c1-l007 note] The Portuguese continues with softness and thinness, using a piece of silk as the example. Brãda retains its printed abbreviation, and S marks written usage. The final S. is an upright register label.
[c1-l008] Sainan. i, Vazauai, nangui. *Perigos, ma-*
[c1-l008 note] This Sainan is wazawai or nangi, calamity and hardship. Perigos and the divided ma- begin a list of dangers, evils, and troubles completed below.
[c1-l009 >] *les, & trabalhos.*
[c1-l009 note] Les completes ma-les, and trabalhos adds troubles or hardships. The whole list explains the adverse-event sense of Sainan.
[c1-l010] Saino cauara. *Lugar como limbo dos mininos*
[c1-l010 note] Sai no kawara is described through the mission's comparison with the limbo of children. This is the dictionary's explanatory analogy, not an assertion that the two religious concepts are identical.
[c1-l011 >] *na outra vida, ſegundo a opinião dos gentios.*
[c1-l011 note] The place is situated in the next life and attributed to the beliefs of the people called gentios in the source. The Portuguese qualification belongs to the preceding religious description.
[c1-l012] Saiqio. i, Cujiuo tadaſu. *O examinar a de-*
[c1-l012 note] Saikyo is glossed by kuji wo tadasu, investigating a legal dispute. The scan's descending j supports Cujiuo rather than OCR Cuiiuo; de- starts demanda below.
[c1-l013 >] *manda. Vt,* Cujino ſaiqio. *Perguntas,*
[c1-l013 note] Demanda completes the legal-case gloss. Kuji no saikyo is then illustrated as questions or inquiries concerning the dispute; the following line completes the explanation.
[c1-l014 >] *& exame ſobre a demanda.*
[c1-l014 note] Exame sobre a demanda completes the example as examination of the lawsuit. This continuation is not another Japanese headword.
[c1-l015] Saiqin. Moyouoxi tçutomuru. *O por por*
[c1-l015 note] Saiqin is explained by moyoo(sh)i tsutomuru, urging or undertaking an action. The Portuguese O por por begins the construction 'put into practice'; the repeated por is not an accidental duplicated word to delete.
[c1-l016 >] *obra, ou executar algũa couſa.* S.
[c1-l016 note] Obra completes por por obra, putting something into effect, followed by executar. The written-register label S closes the entry. The terminal S. is set upright.
[c1-l017] Saiquai. Futatabi vǒ. *O encõtrarſe, ou ver-*
[c1-l017 note] Saikwai is meeting again, paraphrased as futatabi oo. The initial v in vǒ belongs to the historical spelling; the Portuguese begins meeting or seeing one another again.
[c1-l018 >] *ſe, & falarſe duas vezes, ou a ſegunda vez.*
[c1-l018 note] The continuation completes ver-se and adds speaking together twice or for the second time. It explains the reciprocal reunion sense of Saiquai.
[c1-l019] Saiquat. Futatabi yomigayeru. *Reſuſci-*
[c1-l019 note] Saiquat is coming back to life, explained by futatabi yomigayeru. The final t is the printed checked ending and is not expanded; the Portuguese resurrection verb is divided across rows.
[c1-l020 >] *tar. Vt,* Saiquat ſuru. S.
[c1-l020 note] Tar completes Reſuſci-tar. Saiquat suru supplies the verbal construction for being restored to life, followed by the written-register label S. The S. label after the Japanese example is upright.
[c1-l021] Sairai. Futatabi qitaru. *Vinda ſegunda dal-*
[c1-l021 note] Sairai is a second coming, literally futatabi kitaru. The Portuguese specifies whose return is meant on the next line; dal- is a genuine line-end division.
[c1-l022 >] *gum Cami, ou Fotoque.*
[c1-l022 note] Gum completes dal-gum: the return is of a Cami or Fotoque. Both Japanese religious terms occur inside the Portuguese explanation rather than as independent headwords.
[c1-l023] Sairei. Matçurigoto rei. *Hũas feſtas que ſe*
[c1-l023 note] Sairei denotes a ritual festival, with matsurigoto and rei as the Japanese explanation. The Portuguese begins describing celebrations held for a deity.
[c1-l024 >] *fazem ao Cami.*
[c1-l024 note] Fazem ao Cami completes the festival definition, specifying its recipient. Cami is a Japanese religious term embedded in the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l025] Saireǒ. i, Nimotno qeigo. *Guarda, ou vi-*
[c1-l025 note] Saireoo denotes an escort or guard for goods, explained by nimot no keigo. Nimotno retains the printed checked-t form rather than being expanded to a modern nimotsu spelling; vi- begins vigia below.
[c1-l026 >] *gia que vai com o fato.*
[c1-l026 note] Gia completes vigia, the person who accompanies the goods. Fato here refers to baggage or possessions, not the modern abstract meaning 'fact'.
[c1-l027] Sairon. Futatabi ronzuru. *O diſputar duas*
[c1-l027 note] Sairon is discussing or debating a matter again, as futatabi ronzuru explains. Reinspection of the rounded but abraded vowel supports ron rather than the OCR's ren; the Portuguese likewise specifies repetition.
[c1-l028 >] *vezes a couſa.*
[c1-l028 note] Vezes a cousa completes discussing the matter twice. This is the continuation of Sairon, not a separate frequency headword.
[c1-l029] Saiſai. *Muitas vezes.*
[c1-l029 note] Saisai means many times or repeatedly, exactly the sense of Muitas vezes. The internal long ſ is retained.
[c1-l030] Saiſan. Futatabi, mitabi. *Duas, & tres*
[c1-l030 note] Saisan is explained by futatabi and mitabi, twice and three times. The Portuguese number phrase continues with vezes on the next line.
[c1-l031 >] *vezes. ¶* Saiſan mǒxita. *Ia falei du-*
[c1-l031 note] Saisan mooshita means having said something repeatedly. The Portuguese begins 'I have already spoken two-', with du- completed on the following physical row.
[c1-l032 >] *as, ou tres vezes, ou muitas vezes.*
[c1-l032 note] As completes du-as, followed by 'or three times, or many times'. The OCR's internal space in vezes is removed after checking the damaged but continuous word.
[c1-l033] Saisǒ. i, Yaſai. *Eruas que ſe comem, ou or-*
[c1-l033 note] Saisoo is equated with yasai and glossed as edible greens. Enlargement shows a round s, not OCR g, before the caroned vowel; the damaged a is retained from the surviving word. The spelling is not normalized to the next entry's Saiſo.
[c1-l034 >] *taliça.*
[c1-l034 note] Taliça completes or-taliça, vegetables or garden greens. This brief continuation closes the preceding edible-plants entry.
[c1-l035] Saiſo. Comaxi, araxi. *Couſa miuda, & groſ-*
[c1-l035 note] Saiso contrasts komashi and arashi, fine and coarse. The Portuguese likewise juxtaposes miuda and grossa; these are contrasting qualities, not a malformed single adjective.
[c1-l036 >] *ſa. Vide,* Soſai.
[c1-l036 note] Sa completes groſ-ſa. Vide directs the reader to Sosai, the reversed compound, and the Japanese cross-reference remains distinct from the italic Portuguese instruction.
[c1-l037] Saiſocu. Moyouoxi, ſu. *Perſuaſão, ou cõ-*
[c1-l037 note] Saisoku denotes urging or persuasion, explained by moyoo(sh)i, su. The Portuguese also gives counsel, with cõ- continued below; the nasal abbreviation is preserved.
[c1-l038 >] *ſelho. Item, Aliquando, perſuaſão forte a-*
[c1-l038 note] Selho completes cõ-selho. The entry adds that the word can sometimes mean strong persuasion, with a- continuing into apertando on the next row.
[c1-l039 >] *pertando, & conſtrangendo a alguẽ. ¶* Sai-
[c1-l039 note] The intensified sense involves pressing or constraining someone. The weak abbreviation mark over the final e supports alguẽ, retained as a chosen but faint reading; Sai- starts the example below.
[c1-l040 >] ſocuuo tçuquru. *Meter gente em caſa dal-*
[c1-l040 note] Saisoku wo tsukuru introduces the practice of putting people into someone's house to enforce payment. The gloss begins the description here and continues with its purpose on the next row.
[c1-l041 >] *guem pera o fazer pagar a diuida, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] The people are lodged there to make the debtor pay the debt. This completes the enforcement example, not a general definition of household residence.
[c1-l042] Saiſocunin. *Homens q̃ perſuadem, ou conſtrã-*
[c1-l042 note] Saisokunin names people who urge or constrain others. The final nin, the person-denoting element, is supported by the scan; OCR Saiſocuirin is not retained.
[c1-l043 >] *gem a algũa couſa, &c.*
[c1-l043 note] Gem completes conſtrã-gem, and the object is some action or matter. The line finishes the definition of the people who apply pressure.
[c1-l044] Saiſui. Comacani cudaqu. *Muitomiuda, ou*
[c1-l044 note] Saisui is explained as komaka ni kudaku, breaking something down finely, here applied to detailed explanation. The tightly set Muitomiuda is retained as printed rather than silently respaced.
[c1-l045 >] *encarecidamente. Vt,* Saiſui mǒxi qica-
[c1-l045 note] Encarecidamente adds the sense of emphatic or earnest explanation. Saisui mooshi kika- begins the Japanese example and continues into kikase on the next row.
[c1-l046 >] xe ſoro. *Diße ho muito miuda, & difuſa-*
[c1-l046 note] Xe completes kikase, followed by the polite sooro form printed without a vowel mark. The Portuguese says it was explained minutely and at length; enlargement identifies Diße, not OCR Diſce, while difuſa- continues below.
[c1-l047 >] *mente.*
[c1-l047 note] Mente completes difuſa-mente, describing an expansive or detailed explanation. This last short line closes the left column and is not page furniture.

[c2-l001] Saitai. *Ter molher, ou eſtar caſado. ¶* Sai-
[c2-l001 note] Saitai means having a wife or being married. Sai- starts an example of describing oneself or another as a married person.
[c2-l002 >] tai nomide gozaru. *Ser caſado, ou ter molher,*
[c2-l002 note] Tai completes saitai; no mi de gozaru is the construction for being in the married state. The Portuguese repeats being married or having a wife, and its terminal comma is retained.
[c2-l003 >] *¶* Cano bǒzuua xeiſôdeua nai, ſaitai-
[c2-l003 note] Kano boozu wa seiso(o) de wa nai, saitai- contrasts a celibate monk with a married one. The negative nai is essential to the example; the caron and circumflex are kept occurrence by occurrence.
[c2-l004 >] gia. *Aquelle Bonzo não he dos continentes,*
[c2-l004 note] Gia completes the Japanese assertion. The Portuguese says that this Bonzo is not among the continent or celibate members; the description continues below.
[c2-l005 >] *& regulares, mas tem molher.*
[c2-l005 note] The continuation adds regular religious observance to the contrast and concludes that he has a wife. It belongs to the same example of Saitai.
[c2-l006] Saitan. Futatabi vmaruru. *O naſcer a ſegunda*
[c2-l006 note] Saitan is being born again, explained by futatabi umaruru. The historical v in vmaruru is retained rather than changed to modern u.
[c2-l007 >] *vez neſte mundo como imaginão os gentios.*
[c2-l007 note] The explanation locates this second birth in the present world and attributes the idea to the people termed gentios. That qualification frames the following comparison with a learned religious figure.
[c2-l008 >] *V. G.* Monjuno ſaitan gia. *Diz ſe de*
[c2-l008 note] Monju no saitan ja calls someone a rebirth of Monju. V. G. is the introductory Latin abbreviation, not Japanese G; its type follows the italic gloss. The Portuguese introduces the kind of person to whom the phrase applies.
[c2-l009 >] *hum homem muito ſabio, ou letrado no qual pa-*
[c2-l009 note] The phrase is applied to a very wise or learned man. Pa- begins parece, leading into the claim that the earlier sage seems to have been reborn in him.
[c2-l010 >] *rece que tornou a nacer aquelle diſcipulo muito*
[c2-l010 note] Rece completes pa-rece. The learned man is likened to the rebirth of that exceptionally learned disciple; printed nacer is preserved without adding the consonant of modern nascer.
[c2-l011 >] *docto de Xaca.*
[c2-l011 note] Docto de Xaca completes the description of Monju as a learned disciple of Xaca. Xaca is embedded in the Portuguese explanation, not an independent entry on this row.
[c2-l012] Saiten. Faxira, taruqi. *Colunna, & aguieiro.*
[c2-l012 note] Saiten is explained by hashira and taruki, a column and rafter. The Portuguese architectural terms match those two structural members.
[c2-l013 >] *Vt,* Saiten qezzurazu. *Não laurando as*
[c2-l013 note] Saiten kezurazu gives the negative construction, not hewing or dressing the columns and rafters. The doubled z is retained from the historical romanization.
[c2-l014 >] *colũnas, & aguieiros da caſa. Vide* Bǒxi.
[c2-l014 note] The continuation names the house's columns and rafters and supplies a cross-reference to Booshi. The scan supports colũnas, not OCR colũinas; the nasal abbreviation is retained.
[c2-l015] Saitǒ. Nagatana. *Faca pera cortar nabos,*
[c2-l015 note] Saitoo is equated with Nagatana and described as a knife for cutting turnips. The caron distinguishes this printed headword from the circumflex on the following homonym.
[c2-l016 >] *& outras eruas.*
[c2-l016 note] Other greens are added to the vegetables cut with the knife. This short line ends the utensil definition before the separate vessel entry.
[c2-l017] Saitô. *Hũa maneira de ſelha em que ſe mete o*
[c2-l017 note] Saitô is a kind of tub or receptacle. The gloss continues with the food it contains, and the circumflex is retained rather than made identical to the preceding knife entry.
[c2-l018 >] *comer cortado, ou iguarias q̃ ão de ſair a meſa.*
[c2-l018 note] The vessel holds cut-up food or dishes about to be served. The scan shows cortado, not OCR corſado; the printed q̃ and ão remain unexpanded.
[c2-l019] Sai v. Comacana ame. *Chuua miuda.* S.
[c2-l019 note] Saiu is fine rain, explained by komakana ame. The irregular mark above the first o in Comacana is treated as local paper or ink damage rather than the OCR caron; this remains a nonblocking caveat because the impression is impaired. The conspicuous space in Sai v is retained, and the terminal S. is an upright written-register label.
[c2-l020] Saiuai. *Oportunidade.*
[c2-l020 note] Saiwai is glossed as opportunity or a favorable occasion. This local sense, rather than an imposed modern translation as happiness, governs the following adjectival entry.
[c2-l021] Saiuaina. *Couſa oportuna, ou que vem apro-*
[c2-l021 note] Saiwaina describes something opportune or arriving at a suitable moment. The initial capital is an abraded S, not OCR A; apro- continues on the next line.
[c2-l022 >] *poſito, &c.*
[c2-l022 note] Posito completes apro-poſito, coming appropriately or at the right time. The row closes the adjective's explanation.
[c2-l023] Saiuǒ. i, Futatabi. *Segunda vez. Vt,* Sai-
[c2-l023 note] Saiwoo is explained by futatabi, a second time. The scan shows u, not OCR n, and the following example repeats the same form across the line boundary.
[c2-l024 >] uǒ, ſaifen mǒxita. *Falei hũa vez, &*
[c2-l024 note] Sai-uoo, saihen mooshita expresses having said something time and again. The Portuguese begins 'I spoke once and', continuing on the next row; the historical spelling ſaifen is retained.
[c2-l025 >] *outra, ou muitas vezes.*
[c2-l025 note] Outra completes 'once and another time', followed by the looser sense 'many times'. This closes the repetition example under Saiuǒ.
[c2-l026] Saixacu. Furatabi yerabu. *O comentar, &*
[c2-l026 note] Saishaku is glossed as commenting on or explaining a book. The printed Japanese paraphrase Furatabi yerabu is retained despite its awkwardness beside that meaning; the apparent r is not silently repaired to the expected futatabi spelling.

[c2-l028 >] xacu ſuru.

[c2-l029] Saixen. *Caxas que ſe offerecem, & deitão di-*
[c2-l029 note] Saisen refers here to cash offered before a deity. The Portuguese Caxas is the printed money term. A short double-bar line-end hyphen is visible after di, beginning di-ante across the break; the hyphen is restored rather than dropping the edge mark.
[c2-l030 >] *ante do Fotoque, ou Cami. ¶* Saixenuo
[c2-l030 note] Ante completes diante. The offerings are made before a Fotoque or Cami; the capital C and vowel a are visible. Saixenuo begins the Japanese example, with n rather than the OCR's r and without its spurious internal space.
[c2-l031 >] mairaſuru. *Offerecer eſtas caxas.*
[c2-l031 note] Mairasuru completes saisen wo mairasuru, presenting the offerings. Offerecer estas caxas restates the action in Portuguese.
[c2-l032] Saixi. i, Camiuo matçuru coto. *Feſta, ou*

[c2-l033 >] *ceremonia que ſe faz aos Camis.*

[c2-l034] Saixi. Tçuma, co. *Molher, & filhos.*
[c2-l034 note] This Saishi is tsuma, ko, wife and children. The family sense is separate from the preceding homonymous ritual term.
[c2-l035] Saixin. Futatabi ſuſumu. *O dar arroz,*

[c2-l038 >] *pera prouer os Goquis a ſegunda, ou 3. vez.*
[c2-l038 note] The rice is brought to refill the Goquis for a second or third time. Goquis is the inflected name of the bowls within the Portuguese sentence; the printed numeral 3 is preserved.
[c2-l039] Saixinbachi. *Vaſo, em que trazem eſte ar-*
[c2-l039 note] Saishinbachi is the vessel used to bring this additional rice. Ar- begins arroz across the physical line division.
[c2-l040 >] *roz cozido à meſa.*
[c2-l040 note] Roz completes ar-roz, specified as cooked rice brought to the table. The grave in à is retained from the print.
[c2-l041] Saixiqi. Irodoru. *O pintar de cores, ou*
[c2-l041 note] Saishiki is coloring or painting, explained by irodoru. The Portuguese gives painting in colors and continues with the manuscript-coloring sense below.
[c2-l042 >] *illuminar. Vt,* Saixiqiuo ſuru.
[c2-l042 note] Illuminar completes the coloring gloss. Saishiki wo suru is the corresponding noun-plus-suru example and remains on this physical row.
[c2-l043] Saixiqi, u, xijta. *Illuminar, ou pintar de va-*
[c2-l043 note] The verbal entry Saixiqi gives its principal forms and again means illuminating or painting. The printed past form is preserved rather than remodeled into a modern conjugation.
[c2-l044 >] *rias cores.*
[c2-l044 note] Rias completes va-rias, specifying several colors. This is the continuation of the coloring verb rather than a separate sense of the next headword.
[c2-l045] Saixǒ. Tçucaſadoru catachi. *Certa digni-*
[c2-l045 note] Saishoo denotes a particular office or dignity, with tsukasadoru katachi as its Japanese explanation. The Portuguese noun dignidade is divided across the line boundary.
[c2-l046 >] *dade.*
[c2-l046 note] Dade completes digni-dade, a rank or office. The brief row belongs to Saixǒ and is not omitted as empty-looking material.
[c2-l047] Saixo. i, Fajime. *Principio. ¶* Saixoni
[c2-l047 note] Saisho is hajime, the beginning. The scan restores the missing i in Fajime and c in Principio. Saishoni starts an example that continues at the top of f433; the bottom catchword is recorded separately as furniture.
