[c1-l001 >] romuru. *Diuulgar, & propagar a lei de*
[c1-l001 note] Romuru completes hiromuru from f197; the same fragment there is a catchword. The example means propagating God's law, not a separate verb tomuru.
[c1-l002 >] *Deos.*
[c1-l002 note] Deos completes lei de Deos, the Christian law or teaching being spread. It is Portuguese rather than a Japanese proper name.
[c1-l003] Firomi. i, Firoi tocoro. *Lugar largo, &*
[c1-l003 note] Hiromi is a broad place, paraphrased hiroi tokoro. The paraphrase and scan restore the damaged r in Firomi.
[c1-l004 >] *eſpaçoſo.*
[c1-l004 note] Eſpaçoſo completes the spacious-place definition. No additional Japanese sense starts on this line.
[c1-l005] Firoi niua. *Pateo largo.*
[c1-l005 note] Hiroi niwa is a broad courtyard. Preserve the printed adjective phrase even though the alphabetical position might suggest hironiwa; NINJAL also records the longer form.
[c1-l006] Firoſuguyaqiba. *Aço da Catana que vai con*
[c1-l006 note] Hirosugu-yakiba describes the broad straight tempered edge of a sword. Catana sits within Portuguese syntax, and con begins continuando below.
[c1-l007 >] *tinuando como vea larga, & comprida.*
[c1-l007 note] The steel or hardened edge continues like a broad long vein. This describes its form rather than a separate sword component to add editorially.
[c1-l008] Firoyen. *Varanda larga.*
[c1-l008 note] Hiroen is a broad veranda. The short gloss directly confirms the architectural meaning.
[c1-l009] Firu. *Dedia, ou a meo dia. ¶* Yoru, firu.
[c1-l009 note] Hiru is daytime or midday. Yoru, hiru gives night and day; the draft's extra v before Yoru was not a Japanese syllable.
[c1-l010 >] *Dia, & noite.*
[c1-l010 note] Dia and noite translate the pair in reversed order. Preserve the dictionary's ordering rather than align it artificially word by word.
[c1-l011] Firu. i, Ninnicu. *Alho.* X*.*
[c1-l011 note] The homonym hiru means garlic, equated with ninniku. X limits the recorded usage regionally; it is not part of the Japanese name.
[c1-l012] Firu. *Sambixuga.*
[c1-l012 note] Another hiru denotes a leech. Sambixuga is the printed Portuguese term and is not modernized to sanguessuga.
[c1-l013] Firugayeri, u, etta. *Dobrarẽſe, ou viraremſe*
[c1-l013 note] Hirugaeru means turning or folding back. The following flags-in-wind example establishes the physical sense.
[c1-l014 >] *as bandeiras, &c. com o vento. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l014 note] Flags turn in the wind; bandeiras restores the omitted r. Item signals an additional, figurative reversal.
[c1-l015 >] *Retroceder, ou tornar atras do começado.*
[c1-l015 note] The figurative meaning is going back from something begun. This is a reversal of course rather than a new motion verb.
[c1-l016] Firugayexi, ſu, eita. *Virar o vento as ban*
[c1-l016 note] Hirugaesu is the transitive counterpart: wind turns flags or sleeves. Eita and vento repair OCR errors in the inflection and Portuguese subject.
[c1-l017 >] *deiras, mãgas do veſtido, &c. ¶ Item, Perm.*
[c1-l017 note] Flags and clothing sleeves complete the objects. The editorial Perm. introduces a metaphorical application.
[c1-l018 >] Yacuſocuuo firugayeſu. *Deſfazer o cõcerto.*
[c1-l018 note] Yakusoku wo hirugaesu is undoing an agreement. Cõcerto, not cõterto, names that agreement in the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l019 >] Zombũuo firugayeſu. *Mudar o parecer.*
[c1-l019 note] Zonbun wo hirugaesu is changing one's opinion. Zombũ preserves the abbreviated nasal spelling while the spurious split before ſu is removed.
[c1-l020] Firui. *Comparação: não ſe vſa por ſi, ſe não cõ-*
[c1-l020 note] Hirui means comparison or a comparable counterpart, and is said not to stand alone. The next example supplies the compound construction.
[c1-l021 >] *poſto. Vt,* Firuinai tegaragia. *He faça-*
[c1-l021 note] Hirui nai tegara ja is an unequalled feat. Nai, not naa, is the negation supported by the Japanese and the scan.
[c1-l022 >] *nha que não tem igual.*
[c1-l022 note] The feat has no equal, completing façanha. This confirms the comparative meaning of hirui above.
[c1-l023] Firui. Canaximu namida. *Lagrimas de tri-*
[c1-l023 note] The homonymous hirui means sorrowful tears, explained by kanashimu namida. It is not the comparison noun from the preceding entry.
[c1-l024 >] *ſteza. ¶* Firuini xizzumu. *Banharſe*
[c1-l024 note] Hirui ni shizumu means sinking in tears of sorrow. The Portuguese bathing-in-tears metaphor translates that image.
[c1-l025 >] *em lagrimas de tristeza.*
[c1-l025 note] Em lagrimas de tristeza completes the tearful state. OCR's initial d in dagrimas was a misread l.
[c1-l026] Firuiy. i, Firumexi. *Comer de arroz do meo*
[c1-l026 note] Hiru-ii, written Firuiy, is the midday rice meal, equated with hirumeshi. The final iy belongs to ii, cooked rice, not a stray suffix.
[c1-l027 >] *dia.*
[c1-l027 note] Dia completes meo dia and fixes the meal at midday. The physical line break is kept.
[c1-l028] Firuma. i, Firuno aida. *Eſpaço do meo dia.*
[c1-l028 note] Hiruma is hiru no aida, the daytime interval. The Portuguese narrows it to the midday period in this gloss.
[c1-l029 >] *¶ Item, Comer do meo dia de trabalhado-*
[c1-l029 note] Item gives a workers' midday meal as another sense. Trabalhado continues into trabalhadores.
[c1-l030 >] *res. B.*
[c1-l030 note] Res completes trabalhadores. B marks the register of this meal sense, not an initial of a further word.
[c1-l031] Firumaqi. *Modo de emburilhar com tiras de*
[c1-l031 note] Hirumaki is a wrapping method using strips. Preserve emburilhar, also seen on the previous page, rather than silently modernize it.
[c1-l032 >] *papel, ou outra couſa ao comprido deixando al-*
[c1-l032 note] Paper or other material is wound along the length while leaving spaces. Al begins the abbreviated plural below.
[c1-l033 >] *gũs eſpaços em aberto.*
[c1-l033 note] Gũs completes algũs, with some spaces left open. The description clarifies that the wrapping is not continuous coverage.
[c1-l034] Firumi, u, unda. *Afroxar, ou acouardarſe.*
[c1-l034 note] Hirumu is slackening or losing courage. Afroxar fits both the visible f and the paired acouardarſe; Atroxar was OCR damage.
[c1-l035] Firune. *O dormir polla ſesta.*
[c1-l035 note] Hirune is sleeping during the daytime rest period. Polla ſesta is preserved as the printed Portuguese expression.
[c1-l036] Firunuſubito. *Ladrão dedia. i, deſaforado.*
[c1-l036 note] Hiru-nusubito is a brazen daytime thief. Ladrão and deſaforado restore the coherent Portuguese gloss without changing its historical spelling.
[c1-l037] Fiſabiſato. i, Fiſaxǔ. *Ha muito tempo, ou*
[c1-l037 note] Hisabisa to is equated with hisashū, a long time. The scan supports a caron on the final u; the explanatory i is not kana material.
[c1-l038 >] *auendo muito tempo.*
[c1-l038 note] Auendo muito tempo restates the elapsed-time sense. It continues the preceding adverb rather than defining a separate noun.
[c1-l039] Fiſacata. P. *He palaura que entra em com-*
[c1-l039 note] Hisakata is marked poetic. The explanation treats it as a combining expression, consistent with its use as a conventional poetic epithet.
[c1-l040 >] *poſição com outras, quer dizer ceo, ou couſa*
[c1-l040 note] It combines with other words and means heaven or something celestial. Outras restores the damaged initial vowel without changing the explanation.
[c1-l041 >] *do ceo. Vt,* Fiſacatano tçuqino ficari,
[c1-l041 note] Hisakata no tsuki no hikari is the example, the light of the heavenly moon. The repeated headword resolves the badly read bilacatano.
[c1-l042 >] &c. *Claridade da lũa do ceo.*
[c1-l042 note] The Portuguese translates the moonlight expression explicitly as celestial. Lũa's tilde remains in place as printed.
[c1-l043] Fiſacaqi. *Hũa aruoreſinha de cuja cinza fazẽ*
[c1-l043 note] Hisakaki is a small tree whose ashes are used for lye. The damaged initial Fi is supported by the tree name and NINJAL, not by modernizing its orthography.
[c1-l044 >] *barrela.*
[c1-l044 note] Barrela supplies the lye or washing liquor made from those ashes. It completes the tree's practical-use description.
[c1-l045] Fiſague. *Hum vaſo de pao pera dar agoa co-*
[c1-l045 note] Hisage is a wooden water vessel. The damaged vowel is a, matching the implement name and the alphabetical sequence.
[c1-l046 >] *mo gumil.*
[c1-l046 note] Como gumil compares it with a ewer. The historical gumil form is retained rather than replaced with a modern dictionary form.
[c1-l047] Fiſagui. *Hũa aruore aſſi chamada.*
[c1-l047 note] Hisagi is a tree name. Enlargement supports final i in Fiſagui, rather than the first-pass j; the spelling is not normalized to modern hisagi.

[c2-l001] Fiſaxi. *Acrecentamento pequeno da caſa conti*
[c2-l001 note] Hisashi is a small extension to a house. The initial F was misrecognized as B, but the rest of the name and architectural sense are clear.
[c2-l002 >] *nuado, ou pegado com o meſmo telhado.*
[c2-l002 note] The extension shares or adjoins the same roof. This specifies the architectural relation of the hisashi.
[c2-l003] Fiſaxij. *Couſa antigua, ou de muito tempo.*
[c2-l003 note] Hisashii means old or of long duration. It is unrelated to the preceding eaves noun despite their similar spelling.
[c2-l004 >] Fiſaxiſa.
[c2-l004 note] Hisashisa is the nominal derivative, duration or long standing. Its indentation associates it with the adjective.
[c2-l005 >] Fiſaxǔ.
[c2-l005 note] Hisashū is the contracted adverb. The caron on u is visible and is not replaced by a grave merely from the earlier draft.
[c2-l006] Fisǒ. *Estima. ¶* Monouo fisǒ ſuru. *Eſ-*
[c2-l006 note] Hisō here denotes esteem or treasuring. Mono wo hisō suru is the corresponding verbal expression; retain the short s in the locally aligned OCR.
[c2-l007 >] *timar, ou prezar muito algũa couſa. ¶* Go-
[c2-l007 note] The gloss means valuing something highly. Go begins the honorific expression continued below.
[c2-l008 >] fisǒna mononaredomo, cudaſareyo. *A-*
[c2-l008 note] Go-hisō na mono naredomo kudasareyo asks for something despite its being treasured. The garbled hsôna is restored to fisǒna from the visible types and repeated noun.
[c2-l009 >] *inda que ſeja couſa que eſtimaes muito, daima.*
[c2-l009 note] Although you esteem it greatly, give it to me: seja supplies the concessive construction. Daima is the printed fused pronoun expression.
[c2-l010] Fisǒ. *Roſalgar.*
[c2-l010 note] The separate hisō is rosalgar, an arsenic substance. It must not be merged semantically with esteem despite identical romanization.
[c2-l011] Fiſocana. *Couſa ſecreta.*
[c2-l011 note] Hisoka na means secret. Na marks the adjectival form, with the ni adverb following next.
[c2-l012] Fiſocani. *Adu. Secretamente.*
[c2-l012 note] Hisoka ni means secretly. Adu. is the Portuguese grammatical label, not Japanese reading material.
[c2-l013] Fisǒden. *Nome de hum ceo.*
[c2-l013 note] Hisōden names a heaven or cosmological realm. The compact printed word is retained rather than split around its marked o.
[c2-l014] Fiſofifisǒ. *Nome de outro ceo.*
[c2-l014 note] Fisofifisǒ names another heaven, recognizably related to the Buddhist neither-perception-nor-nonperception expression. Preserve the missing first long-vowel mark rather than silently emend the printed form.
[c2-l015] Fiſome, uru, eta. *A quietar. ¶* Sanco
[c2-l015 note] Hisomeru means quieting or suppressing. Sanco is retained in the example pending the continuation, not replaced with a guessed modern noun.
[c2-l016 >] uo fiſomete qiqu. *Ouuir com grande attẽ-*
[c2-l016 note] Wo hisomete kiku is listening with restrained stillness. The Portuguese explicitly describes great attention or quiet.
[c2-l017 >] *ção, ou quietação. ¶* Mayuuo fiſomuru.
[c2-l017 note] Attention and quiet complete that example. Mayu wo hisomuru introduces knitting the brows, another sense of the same verb.
[c2-l018 >] *Fazer rugas na teſta, como quem lhe não agra-*
[c2-l018 note] The brows create forehead wrinkles as when something displeases a person. The definition explains the expression rather than the literal hiding of eyebrows.
[c2-l019 >] *da algũa couſa.*
[c2-l019 note] Da completes agrada, the thing not being pleasing. This closes the frowning example.
[c2-l020] Fiſomeqi, u, eita. *Fazer rumor, ou burburi*
[c2-l020 note] Hisomeku is making a subdued murmur. Burburi begins the Portuguese word for a low hubbub below.
[c2-l021 >] *nha caladamente.*
[c2-l021 note] Nha completes burburinha, qualified by caladamente. The sound is quiet, not complete silence.
[c2-l022] Fiſǒna. *Couſa muito estimada, ou prezada.*
[c2-l022 note] Hisō na is something highly valued or treasured. It derives from the esteem entry, not the arsenic or heaven homonyms.
[c2-l023] Fisǒni. *Adu.*
[c2-l023 note] Hisō ni is the adverbial counterpart. The short grammatical gloss refers back to the valued or treasured sense.
[c2-l024] Fiſori, u, otta. *Entortarſe a taboa ao ſol,ou*
[c2-l024 note] Hisoru describes a board bending or warping in the sun. The physical deformation precedes a projectile sense.
[c2-l025 >] *empenar. ¶ Item, Reſuelar como aſeta*
[c2-l025 note] Empenar restates warping. Item then describes an arrow glancing or deflecting, preserving the historical Reſuelar form.
[c2-l026 >] *que dà no capecete, ou peito darmas, &c.*
[c2-l026 note] The arrow strikes a helmet or breastplate. Preserve capecete as printed rather than supply modern capacete.
[c2-l027] Fiſſabaqi, u, aita. *Ordenar, ou administrar*
[c2-l027 note] Hissabaku is managing or putting in order. The doubled s is recovered where OCR produced ſt, consistent with the visible types and verb.
[c2-l028 >] *a caſa, &c. ¶ Item, Menear liuremen-*
[c2-l028 note] Managing a household is the first example. The next sense is free, skilled handling of weapons.
[c2-l029 >] *te, & com deſtreza as armas como Catana,*
[c2-l029 note] Katana exemplifies weapons handled dexterously. This Japanese term is embedded in Portuguese syntax.
[c2-l030 >] *Naguinata, &c. ¶* Itouo fiſsabaqu.
[c2-l030 note] Naginata completes the weapons list. Ito wo hissabaku then shifts to sorting thread; the thread gloss resolves OCR's Irouo as Itouo.
[c2-l031 >] *Deſempeçar, & amanhar bem o fio das linhas.*
[c2-l031 note] The action is disentangling and properly arranging thread. That confirms ito rather than iro, colour, in the preceding example.
[c2-l032] Fiſſague, uru, eta. *Leuar algũa couſa depen*
[c2-l032 note] Hissageru means carrying something suspended from the hand. The repeated s-types are not a t, as the OCR draft suggested.
[c2-l033 >] *durada na mão. ¶* Chùni monouo fiſſaguru.
[c2-l033 note] Chū ni mono wo hissaguru describes suspending something in mid-air. Enlargement and the parallel Portuguese gloss support ni in Chùni; the damaged vowel mark is retained as transcribed rather than silently reconstructed.
[c2-l034 >] *Ter dependurada algũa couſa da mão no ar.*
[c2-l034 note] The object hangs from the hand in the air. This gives the spatial relation intended by the previous Japanese example.
[c2-l035] Fiſſaqi, u, aita. i, fiqiſaqi, u. *Raſgar*
[c2-l035 note] Hissaku is explicitly equated with hikisaku, tearing apart. The contracted prefix explains the doubled s rather than requiring a separate lexical hypothesis.
[c2-l036 >] *com força, ou deſpedaçar.*
[c2-l036 note] The tearing is forceful, or a breaking into pieces. Deſpedaçar restores the damaged final vowel in the Portuguese infinitive.
[c2-l037] Fiſſobame, uru, eta. *Puxando virar como*
[c2-l037 note] Hissobameru means turning something by pulling it. The doubled consonant is restored from the scan and headword comparison.
[c2-l038 >] *caualo, &c.*
[c2-l038 note] A horse is the object of the pulling-and-turning action. This is an example rather than an exclusive definition.
[c2-l039] Fiſſocu. *Estar eſcondido, ou encerrado, ou*
[c2-l039 note] Hissoku is remaining hidden or confined. Encerrado repairs the draft's garbled division and confirms the seclusion sense.
[c2-l040 >] *retraido não ſaindo a publico. Vt,* Fiſſocu
[c2-l040 note] The person stays withdrawn and does not appear publicly. ſaindo is sair, going out, not an f-initial verb.
[c2-l041 >] ſuru.
[c2-l041 note] Suru completes hissoku suru. It is a body continuation, not a separate headword.
[c2-l042] Fiſſoroye, uru. eta. *Ajuntar, ou por por or-*
[c2-l042 note] Hissoroyeru means gathering or arranging in order. The repeated por in por por ordem is grammatically possible and retained.
[c2-l043 >] *dem como gente em fieira, &c.*
[c2-l043 note] People arranged in a row illustrate the ordering. Fieira preserves the period Portuguese spelling.
[c2-l044] Fiſſucume, uru, eta. *Forçar, ou conſtrãger,*
[c2-l044 note] Hissukumeru is forcing or constraining. NINJAL and the scan support the headword where OCR produced an impossible btſe cluster.
[c2-l045] Fiſſuguri, u, utta. *Eſcolher o milhor.*
[c2-l045 note] Hissuguru means selecting the best. Eſcolher restores the s-type misread as l; milhor remains the printed historical vowel.
[c2-l046] Fiſsui. Canarazu votoroyuru. *Ir empejorã*
[c2-l046 note] Hissui is inevitable decline, explained as kanarazu otoroyuru. It introduces a compound-only expression continued below.
[c2-l047 >] *do: não ſe vſa ſe não composto. Vt,* Xǒxa
[c2-l047 note] The dictionary says it is not used alone, only in combination. Xǒxa begins the prosperity-and-decline expression on the next page.
