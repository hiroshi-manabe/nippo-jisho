[c1-l001] Gocuguet. Qiuamatta tçuqi. i, Xiuaſu.
[c1-l001 note] Gokugetsu is the final lunar month, identified as shiwasu. The printed final t in Gocuguet is retained, while Qiuamatta tçuqi supplies the Japanese explanation.
[c1-l002 >] *Duodecima lũa.*
[c1-l002 note] Duodecima lũa specifies the twelfth moon or lunar month. The nasal abbreviation remains unexpanded.
[c1-l003] Gocu-i. Qiuamatta curai. *Suprema digni*
[c1-l003 note] This goku-i means the highest rank or dignity, explained by kiwamatta kurai. The internal hyphen separates the vowels and is not a physical line-break mark.
[c1-l004 >] *dade.*
[c1-l004 note] Dade completes dignidade. The divided Portuguese word has no added hyphen on the preceding row.
[c1-l005] Gocu-i. Qiuamatta cocoro. *Subſtancia,*
[c1-l005 note] This separate goku-i is the innermost meaning or essence. The scan supports cocoro, correcting OCR cotoro; the Portuguese explanation continues across the next row.
[c1-l006 >] *ou amago dalgũa ley, ſciencia, arte, &c.*
[c1-l006 note] The essence may be that of a law, science, or art. Amago supplies the idea of the core, and the historical spellings and tilde in dalgũa are retained.
[c1-l007] Gocujǒ. Qiuamaru vye. i, Iǒjǒno coto.
[c1-l007 note] Gokujo means the very highest, explained by kiwamaru ue and jojo. The initial I in Iǒjǒno has consonantal value here and is not changed to modern J.
[c1-l008 >] *Couſa ſuprema, ou excelentißima. ¶ Item,*
[c1-l008 note] The Portuguese gives something supreme or excellent. Item introduces a second application of the same word.
[c1-l009 >] *Hũa laya de Cha a milhor que ha.*
[c1-l009 note] The second application is the finest available kind of tea. Cha occurs inside a Portuguese explanation and is marked separately for possible whole-word typeface review.
[c1-l010] Gocumi. Qiuamatta agiuai. *Excelentißimo*
[c1-l010 note] Gokumi denotes excellent flavour, with kiwamatta ajiwai providing the Japanese analysis. The Portuguese adjective modifies the nouns on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] *goſto, ou ſabor.*
[c1-l011 note] Goſto and ſabor complete the taste definition. Their pairing confirms that the entry concerns flavour rather than a general preference.
[c1-l012] Gocumon. *Lugar onde ſe prègão, ou poem as*
[c1-l012 note] Gokumon is the place where the heads of executed people are nailed up or displayed. The historical prègão is retained in the Portuguese relative clause.
[c1-l013 >] *cabeças dos juſtiçados. ¶* Cubiuo gocu-
[c1-l013 note] The displayed objects are the executed people's heads. The example kubi wo goku- begins a phrase about exposing a head at that place.
[c1-l014 >] monni caquru. *Por a cabeça dalguem que*
[c1-l014 note] Monni completes gokumon ni, and kakuru supplies the act of placing or hanging. The Portuguese translation describes putting up the head of someone executed.
[c1-l015 >] *juſtiçarão em publico.*
[c1-l015 note] The execution and exposure are public. This row completes the example's translation rather than starting another judicial entry.
[c1-l016] Gocunet. Qiuamatta atçuſa. *Grandißima*
[c1-l016 note] Gokunetsu is extreme heat, explained by kiwamatta atsusa. The headword's final checked t is preserved rather than expanded.
[c1-l017 >] *quentura, ou calma.*
[c1-l017 note] Quentura and calma both describe heat in this context. Calma should not be read as the modern English notion of tranquillity.
[c1-l018] Gocuracu. Tanoximiuo qiuamuru. *Parai-*
[c1-l018 note] Gokuraku is a paradise of delights. The scan supports G and Tanoximiuo, 'bringing enjoyment to its utmost', rather than OCR C and Tanoxiniuo.
[c1-l019 >] *ſo de deleites.*
[c1-l019 note] So completes Paraiſo. The phrase identifies a paradise of delights, finishing the gokuraku definition.
[c1-l020] Gocuriǒ. i, Qimiro vonchiguiǒ. *Renda,*
[c1-l020 note] Gokuryo denotes the lord's own revenues or domain. The gloss makes kimi no onchigyo the likely intended explanation, but the scan's worn Qimiro is retained as the chosen literal reading rather than silently changing r to n.
[c1-l021 >] *ou terras que o ſenhor tem aplicadas aſi ſem*
[c1-l021 note] The lord keeps these revenues or lands assigned to himself. The tightly printed aſi is preserved, and the negative qualification continues below.
[c1-l022 >] *as diſtribuir com os criados.*
[c1-l022 note] The lands are not distributed among the lord's retainers. Criados, not OCR criadoa, completes the contrast with the lord's personal domain.
[c1-l023] Gocurǒ. Qiuamatta. voi. *Velhice conſu-*
[c1-l023 note] This gokuro means extreme old age, explained by kiwamatta oi. The small stop after Qiuamatta is retained despite separating the adjective from its noun.
[c1-l024 >] *mada. ¶* Gocurǒ tçucamatçutta. *Eſtou*
[c1-l024 note] The example uses the humble verb tsukamatsutta to say that the speaker has reached great old age. The translation begins 'I am' and continues below.
[c1-l025 >] *muito velho.*
[c1-l025 note] Muito velho completes the statement 'I am very old'. It belongs to the age entry, not the following respectful word for trouble.
[c1-l026] Gocurǒ. *Trabalho falando com reſpeito, &*
[c1-l026 note] This second gokuro denotes trouble or labour spoken of respectfully. Its honorific qualification continues on the next row.
[c1-l027 >] *honra. Vt,* Gocurǒ atte cudaſarei. *Pe-*
[c1-l027 note] Vt introduces a respectful request to undertake the trouble. The damaged second t in atte is read from its faint stem and the Japanese construction; the OCR paragraph-sign-plus-t is an erroneous rendering of italic Vt.
[c1-l028 >] *çouos q̃ ainda q̃ ſeja trabalho façais iſto, &c.*
[c1-l028 note] Pe- plus çouos forms the request 'I ask you': please do this even though it involves trouble. Both q̃ abbreviations remain unexpanded.
[c1-l029] Gocuſocu. Bup. *Ponto principal das medi-*
[c1-l029 note] Gokusoku is marked as Buddhist terminology and described as the chief point of meditation. Bup. is a roman domain label, distinct from the italic Portuguese definition.
[c1-l030 >] *taçoẽs dos Ienxus.*
[c1-l030 note] The meditation is that of the Ienxus, referring to the Zen school. The Japanese school-name is embedded in the Portuguese sentence; the printed nasal abbreviation in taçoẽs is retained.
[c1-l031] Gocuſot. Gigocuro acqi. *Algozes do in-*
[c1-l031 note] Gokusotsu denotes hell's executioners or demons. Acqi is supported by its visible q-descender, correcting OCR acei; the damaged Gigocuro is retained with r, although the intended explanation is evidently jigoku no akki.
[c1-l032 >] *ferno, ou diabos.*
[c1-l032 note] Ferno completes inferno, followed by 'or devils'. This confirms the infernal-agent sense of the preceding Japanese entry.
[c1-l033] Gocuſui. Qiuamatta nemuri. *Profundo*
[c1-l033 note] Gokusui means profound sleep, explained by kiwamatta nemuri. The Portuguese adjective continues into the noun below.
[c1-l034 >] *ſono.* S.
[c1-l034 note] Sono completes 'deep sleep'. The closing S. is a roman written-usage label and is separated from the italic gloss.
[c1-l035] Gocutçubuxi. *Homem que não preſta pera*
[c1-l035 note] Gokutsubushi is someone useful for nothing beyond eating. The scan and supplied headword index both support initial G, rather than modernizing the word to a form beginning K or retaining OCR C.
[c1-l036 >] *nada mais que pera comer.*
[c1-l036 note] The person is fit for nothing except eating. This completes the dismissive definition without adding a register label absent from the print.
[c1-l037] Gocuxin. *Vt,* Gocuxinna fito. *Homem*
[c1-l037 note] The example gokushinna hito describes a person whose heart is peaceful. Its Portuguese noun phrase continues on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] *de grande paz, & ſoſego do coração. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l038 note] The person's heart has great peace and repose. Item then signals another sense of the same headword.
[c1-l039 >] *Muito aprimorado.*
[c1-l039 note] Muito aprimorado adds the sense 'highly refined' or 'perfected'. The short row remains a distinct physical continuation.
[c1-l040] Gocuxo. Fitoyano tocoro. *Tronco, ou*
[c1-l040 note] Gokusho is the place of confinement, explained by hitoya no tokoro. Tronco is used here in the prison or detention sense, not merely as a tree trunk.
[c1-l041 >] *lugar da priſão.*
[c1-l041 note] Lugar da priſão completes the definition as the place of imprisonment. This confirms the confinement sense of Tronco above.
[c1-l042] Gocuxô, l, gocuxôna. *Couſa muito peque-*
[c1-l042 note] Gokusho, alternatively gokushona, describes something extremely small or scanty. The circumflexes distinguish this entry's printed form from the unmarked prison word.
[c1-l043 >] *na, & pouca.*
[c1-l043 note] Na completes pequena, followed by pouca. The definition combines small size with small amount.
[c1-l044] Gocuyacu. *Seruiço obrigatorio que hum faz*
[c1-l044 note] Gokuyaku is obligatory service performed by someone. The person to whom it is owed is specified on the next row.
[c1-l045 >] *ao ſenhor que o manda.*
[c1-l045 note] The service is rendered to the lord who commands it. The relative clause completes the preceding social-duty definition.
[c1-l046] Gocuzai. Qiuamatta tçumi. *Peccado con-*
[c1-l046 note] Gokuzai is a consummate or extreme offence, explained by kiwamatta tsumi. The scan supports Qiuamatta and con-, not OCR Qiuanatta and cen-.
[c1-l047 >] *ſumado.*
[c1-l047 note] Sumado completes conſumado. The next column's Gocuzainin develops the related word for a person steeped in such wrongdoing.

[c2-l001] Gocuzainin. Tçumini qiuamatta fito. *Ho*
[c2-l001 note] Goku-zainin is a person extreme in wrongdoing, explained by tsumi ni kiwamatta hito. Ho continues as Homem below without an added line-end hyphen.
[c2-l002 >] *mem conſumado em peccados, & maldades.*
[c2-l002 note] Mem completes Homem: a person steeped in sins and wickedness. The scan supports peccados, correcting OCR peceados.
[c2-l003] Godai. i, Côtai. *Tempo vindouro, ou idade*
[c2-l003 note] Godai is the future age, also named Côtai. Enlarged second-pass inspection shows faint upward-pointing accent strokes over o, so Côtai is chosen instead of OCR Cǒtai; the worn apex remains a nonblocking graphic uncertainty. The Portuguese continues with futura below.
[c2-l004 >] *futura. ¶* Godaino tamexini ſuru. *Dei*
[c2-l004 note] The example godai no tameshi ni suru concerns making an example for future generations. Dei continues into the Portuguese verb on the next row, with no supplied hyphen.
[c2-l005 >] *xar exemplo, ou eſcarmento pera o futuro.*
[c2-l005 note] Xar completes deixar: leaving an example or warning for the future. Futuro, not OCR fuluro, agrees with the time-reference throughout this entry.
[c2-l006] Gǒdǒ. Tçuyoi nuſubito. *Cruel ladrão, ou*
[c2-l006 note] Godo is a violent robber, explained as tsuyoi nusubito. Both printed carons are retained, and the Portuguese adds a forceful characterization below.
[c2-l007 >] *esfola caras.*
[c2-l007 note] Esfola caras is an expressive description of the violent robber. The spaced words are preserved rather than replaced with a modern compound.
[c2-l008] Godǒ. Bup. Satoru michi. *Modo de me-*
[c2-l008 note] This godo is Buddhist and concerns the way of attaining understanding. The d in Godǒ corrects OCR c; me- begins the Portuguese meditar below.
[c2-l009 >] *ditar, & entender as couſas do Buppô.*
[c2-l009 note] The definition is meditation and understanding matters of Buddhism. The scan shows Buppô with two p letters and a circumflex, not OCR Bupò; it is embedded in the Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l010] Godoiri. *Hũa laya de Sacazzuqui de barro*
[c2-l010 note] Godoiri names a kind of earthenware drinking cup. Sacazzuqui is Japanese vocabulary within the Portuguese explanation, and its size is specified below.
[c2-l011 >] *grande.*
[c2-l011 note] Grande makes this a large earthenware cup. The short row belongs to the preceding vessel entry.
[c2-l012] Gǒfat. Camigueno ſaqi. *Pontinhas dos ca-*
[c2-l012 note] Gohatsu refers to the tips of hairs. The initial Portuguese P is visible in Pontinhas, correcting OCR Fontinhas, while ca- continues below.
[c2-l013 >] *bellos da cabeça.*
[c2-l013 note] Bellos completes cabellos: the hairs of the head. This confirms the meaning of camigue no saki in the Japanese explanation.
[c2-l014] Gofei. *Papeis cortados, & dependurados de*
[c2-l014 note] Gohei consists of cut papers suspended from a small stick. The initial G corrects OCR C, and the Portuguese sentence continues across the next two rows.
[c2-l015 >] *hum paoſinho de que vſão os gentios nas cere-*
[c2-l015 note] The small stick is used by the people the compilers call gentios in ceremonies. The visible i restores gentios; paoſinho is retained without modernizing its consonant spelling.
[c2-l016 >] *monias diante do Cami.*
[c2-l016 note] The ceremonies take place before the kami. Cami is a Japanese religious term embedded in the Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l017] Gofen. i, Qixo. *Voßa mercè.*
[c2-l017 note] Gohen, explained by kisho, is a respectful form of address. Voßa mercè gives the corresponding Portuguese courtesy expression, with its historical accent retained.
[c2-l018] Gofǒ. Voncata. *Idem.*
[c2-l018 note] Goho, explained by onkata, has the same respectful-address sense. The scan supports Gofǒ rather than OCR Cufǒ; Idem refers to the preceding courtesy gloss.
[c2-l019] Gofun. i, Yenogu. *Tintas de cores pera*
[c2-l019 note] Gofun is here explained broadly as enogu, coloured pigments or paints. The gloss does not justify narrowing it to one modern pigment composition.
[c2-l020 >] *pintar.*
[c2-l020 note] Pintar completes 'coloured paints for painting'. The damaged first vowel and following n support pintar rather than OCR pentar.
[c2-l021] Gǒga. *Enſeada do mar, ourio.*
[c2-l021 note] Goga is a sea or river inlet. Enſeada corrects the OCR's extra c; the tight ourio is retained as printed spacing rather than automatically separated.
[c2-l022] Gogi. *Vt,* Buppô gogi. *Guarda, ou pro*
[c2-l022 note] Goji concerns protection of the Buddhist teaching. The headword is Gogi, not OCR Ciogi, and pro continues into the next row's Portuguese noun without an added hyphen.
[c2-l023 >] *teição do Cami ſobre as couſas do* Buppô. Bup.
[c2-l023 note] The gloss attributes guardianship over Buddhist matters to the kami. Buppô is visibly roman here, unlike the surrounding italic Portuguese; the separate Bup. is a domain label, not part of the name.
[c2-l024] Gogin. Vxirogin. *Retaguarda do exercito.*
[c2-l024 note] Gojin is the army's rear guard. Enlargement resolves the Japanese explanation as Vxirogin, ushirojin, rather than OCR Vxuogin, and confirms initial G in the headword.
[c2-l025] Gogin. Itçutçuno qegare. Bup. *Cinco*
[c2-l025 note] This second gojin is Buddhist and is explained as five defilements. The Portuguese begins a definition in terms of sensory objects.
[c2-l026 >] *obiectos dos ſentidos que custumão macular o*
[c2-l026 note] The five objects of the senses are said to stain or defile a person. This is the dictionary's moral-religious explanation, not a modern scientific classification.
[c2-l027 >] *homem.*
[c2-l027 note] Homem completes the object of macular: the person is defiled. The one-word continuation is retained as a physical body row.
[c2-l028] Gogiǒ. *Mandado de peſſoa nobre.*
[c2-l028 note] Gojo is an order from a noble person. The concise gloss distinguishes it from the neighbouring Buddhist entries.
[c2-l029] Gogiocu. Itçutçuno nigori. *Cinco nodoas,*
[c2-l029 note] Gojoku is explained by five forms of turbidity or impurity. The Portuguese describes five stains, with an alternative explanation below.
[c2-l030 >] *ou fealdades de peccados.*
[c2-l030 note] The alternative gloss is the ugliness or deformity of sins. Fealdades is one printed word, correcting the OCR's fealda des.
[c2-l031] Gǒgo. i, Vgoqi fataraqu. *Mouerſe.*
[c2-l031 note] Gogo here means moving or acting, explained by ugoki hataraku. The scan supports Gǒgo rather than the OCR fragment Crôgo.
[c2-l032 >] *¶* Gǒgo itaſu, l, itaſanu. *Mouerſe, ou*
[c2-l032 note] The positive and negative itaſu and itaſanu give movement and inability to move. Latin l separates alternatives, and the Portuguese continues below.
[c2-l033 >] *não ſe poder mouer, nem andar.*
[c2-l033 note] The negative gloss is being unable to move or walk. It completes the paired examples rather than defining a new word.
[c2-l034] Gogo. i, Nochi. *Depois, ou ao diante. Vt,*
[c2-l034 note] Unmarked Gogo means afterwards or in the future, with nochi as its Japanese explanation. The example is on the next row.
[c2-l035 >] Gogo matdai made. *Ate os vltimos tem-*
[c2-l035 note] The example extends the time-reference to the final ages. The damaged t of matdai is compatible with the checked-coda spelling, and vltimos retains the historical initial v; the OCR paragraph sign is absent from the scan.
[c2-l036 >] *pos, ou idade derradeira.*
[c2-l036 note] Pos completes tempos, followed by 'the last age'. This finishes the example about the remotest future.
[c2-l037] Gogo. *Depois de meo dia.* S.
[c2-l037 note] This gogo means after midday. The closing S. is a roman written-usage label, separate from the italic Portuguese gloss.
[c2-l038] Gogo. Miuxiro. *Nas coſtas do Zaxiquion-*
[c2-l038 note] Gogo, explained by miushiro, denotes the rear of the room in which the ruler sits. Zaxiqui is the Japanese room-name embedded in the gloss; the adjoining on- begins Portuguese onde.
[c2-l039 >] *de està o Rey.*
[c2-l039 note] De completes onde: the room is where the king is. The short continuation belongs to the spatial, not the afternoon, entry.
[c2-l040] Gǒgǒ. Tçuyoqu, tçuyoxi. *Vt,* Gǒgǒna
[c2-l040 note] Gogo here is strength or rough forcefulness. The headword's faint second caron is retained; tçuyoxi, with a damaged rounded o, corrects OCR tçuyexi and supplies the adjective paired with tçuyoqu.
[c2-l041 >] fito. *Homem forte, & brauo, ou aſpero.*
[c2-l041 note] Hito completes the example of a strong, fierce, or harsh person. The Portuguese alternatives explain its forceful rather than merely energetic sense.
[c2-l042] Goguen. Itçutçuno manaco. i, Guen, ni,
[c2-l042 note] The entry labels five eyes through manaco but then lists guen and ni, continuing with other faculties below. That tension in the dictionary's explanation is preserved rather than harmonized.
[c2-l043 >] bi, jet, xin. *Cinco ſentidos.*
[c2-l043 note] Bi, jet, and xin complete the list, rendered collectively as five senses. Jet retains the checked final t; the list is not silently rewritten to match the preceding 'five eyes' wording.
[c2-l044] Gǒgui. Tçuyoi coto. *Força, ou violencia.*
[c2-l044 note] Gogi means force or violence, explained by tsuyoi koto. The gloss is the abstract notion illustrated on the next row.
[c2-l045 >] *¶* Gǒguini voyobu. *Chegar a fazer força.*
[c2-l045 note] Gogi ni oyobu means going so far as to employ force. The Portuguese 'come to use force' confirms the construction without changing the printed voyobu spelling.
[c2-l046] Goguiacu. Itçutçuno ſacay ima. *Cinco pec-*
[c2-l046 note] Gogyaku introduces five grave offences. The damaged o in the headword is read with the surviving outline and the continuation on f245; the visible gap in ſacay ima is retained rather than normalized.
[c2-l047 >] *cados grauiſsimos de matar Fotoque. Vt,* Fo-
[c2-l047 note] The list begins with killing a Buddha, using Fotoque within the Portuguese gloss. The scan supports grauiſsimos with a long and a short s, not OCR grauiſimnos. Fo- starts the Japanese example continued on f245; the bottom toqeuo repeats that continuation as a catchword.
