[c1-l001] Iixei. Yoqi cotoba. *Verſo, ou ſentença que*
[c1-l001 note] Jisei is a deathbed verse or saying; yoqi cotoba means good words. The scan supports cotoba, not OCR coroba; consonantal initial I makes Iixei jisei, not iisei.
[c1-l002 >] *hum diz estando pera morrer.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes the definition: words spoken when one is about to die. Preserve the printed pera and the short s in estando rather than modernizing the wording.
[c1-l003] Iixet. Toqi toqi. *Hora. Vt,* Iixet tǒrai
[c1-l003 note] Jisetsu denotes the time or occasion. The example begins jisetsu toorai, the arrival of the time; final t in Iixet is the historical checked-coda notation.
[c1-l004 >] ſuru. *Chegarſe, ou vir a hora, ou tempo.*
[c1-l004 note] Suru completes the example about the time arriving. The Portuguese explicitly gives both hora and tempo, confirming the temporal meaning.
[c1-l005] Iixi. i, Vaga fun. *Propria ſuidade, ou eſter*
[c1-l005 note] Jishi is one's own excrement, identified by waga fun. The scan reads lowercase fun rather than OCR Fuu; the odd-looking Portuguese ſuidade is retained as printed, and eſter continues below without a securely visible terminal hyphen.
[c1-l006 >] *co. Vt,* Iixi cuſaqi cotouo xirazu. Xix.
[c1-l006 note] Co completes eſterco. Jishi kusaki koto wo shirazu says that one does not perceive one's own excrement as foul; Xix. is the source label, not part of the Japanese sentence.
[c1-l007 >] *Ninguem ſe conhece aſi meſmo. Aßi como lhe*
[c1-l007 note] The Portuguese interprets the saying as failure to recognize one's own faults, then compares this with not smelling one's own excrement. The visible separation in aſi meſmo is retained.
[c1-l008 >] *não cheira mal ſeu esterco.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes the comparison: one's own excrement does not smell bad to oneself. The source's esterco and historical typography are preserved.
[c1-l009] Iixi. Futarino co. *Dous filhos.*
[c1-l009 note] This homophonous jishi entry means two children, explicitly explained by futari no ko and dous filhos; it is distinct from the preceding excrement entry.
[c1-l010] Iixi. Vaga co. *Filho proprio.*
[c1-l010 note] Here jishi means one's own child, as waga ko and filho proprio show. The separate headword line must not be merged with the preceding homophone.
[c1-l011] Iixi. Chigo co. i, *Minino.*
[c1-l011 note] This jishi denotes a young child, explained by chigo ko and Portuguese minino. The printed i, is an explanatory marker, not an additional Japanese syllable.
[c1-l012] Iixin. Mizzucarano mi. *Eu em peßoa. Vt,*
[c1-l012 note] Jishin is oneself in person, explained by mizukara no mi. Eu, not OCR Du, supplies the Portuguese first-person pronoun; the scan's doubled z in Mizzucarano is retained.
[c1-l013 >] Iixin teuo cudaqu. *Trabalhar muito peſ-*
[c1-l013 note] Jishin te wo kudaku describes personally applying considerable effort. The Portuguese peſ- begins peſſoalmente on the next row; the visible terminal hyphen is retained.
[c1-l014 >] *ſoalmente.*
[c1-l014 note] Completes peſſoalmente, specifying that the effort is made in person. This short continuation is its own physical line.
[c1-l015] Iixin. i, Iifixin. *Coração piadoſo, ou miſe-*
[c1-l015 note] A different jishin means a compassionate heart and is equated with jihishin. Iifixin uses f for the historical h-row sound; neither the headword nor the synonym begins with OCR F or ſ.
[c1-l016 >] *ricordioſo.*
[c1-l016 note] Completes miſericordioſo, merciful, the second Portuguese description of the compassionate heart.
[c1-l017] Iixin. Mizzucaraga cocoro. *Proprio cora-*
[c1-l017 note] This jishin denotes one's own heart, explained by mizukara ga kokoro. Cora- begins coração; it is a new homophonous entry, not a continuation of the compassionate-heart gloss.
[c1-l018 >] *ção. Vt,* Iixinuo coraſu. *Mortificarſe*
[c1-l018 note] Completes coração, then gives jishin wo korasu. The Portuguese interprets it as self-mortification, with the inward qualification on the next line.
[c1-l019 >] *interiormente.*
[c1-l019 note] Interiormente specifies that the preceding mortification is inward or spiritual. Its connection is to the example, not the next entry.
[c1-l020] Iixǒ. Mizzucarano xǒ. *Minha natureza,*
[c1-l020 note] Jishoo is one's own nature, with mizukara no shoo as the Japanese explanation. Both long-o signs are visibly carons.
[c1-l021 >] *ou natural.*
[c1-l021 note] Ou natural supplies a second Portuguese expression for one's natural disposition. It completes the preceding definition.
[c1-l022] Iixǒ. *Louuarſe aßi meſmo. Vt,* Iixǒuo
[c1-l022 note] A separate jishoo means praising oneself. The example begins jishoo wo; the source's aßi meſmo is preserved.
[c1-l023 >] yǔ. *Idem.*
[c1-l023 note] Yuu completes jishoo wo yuu, speaking in self-praise. Idem indicates that this example has the already stated meaning.
[c1-l024] Iixǒ. Tadaxij ji. i, Iino xǒ. *Modo, ou for-*
[c1-l024 note] This jishoo concerns the form of written characters, explained by tadashii ji and ji no shoo. A small raised terminal mark after for is visible; it begins forma on the next row.
[c1-l025 >] *ma da letra. Vt,* Iixǒga yoi, l, varui. *Ser*
[c1-l025 note] Ma completes forma. Jishoo ga yoi or warui says the character-form is good or bad; l is the printed alternative marker, not Japanese.
[c1-l026 >] *a forma da letra boa, ou ruim.*
[c1-l026 note] Completes ser with the two evaluations of the written letter's shape. This is about letter-form, not the truth of its content.
[c1-l027] Iixu. Toqi mamoru. *Relogeiro, ou o que*
[c1-l027 note] Jishu is the person who keeps time, literally one who watches the hours. Relogeiro is retained as the printed Portuguese word.
[c1-l028 >] *tem cuidado das horas.*
[c1-l028 note] Completes the occupational gloss as one responsible for the hours. It clarifies the timekeeper sense rather than requiring the narrower modern sense of clockmaker.
[c1-l029] Iixǔ. *Hũa certa ſeita de Iapão, que deſcen-*
[c1-l029 note] Jishuu names a Japanese religious school. The Portuguese says it descends from the group named on the next line; the visible terminal hyphen divides deſcende.
[c1-l030 >] *de dos Iǔdǒxùs.*
[c1-l030 note] Completes the descent of the Jishuu sect from the Joodoshuu tradition. The local print is transcribed Iǔdǒxùs: first ǔ, then ǒ, and final ù before s. The vowel forms/marks are irregular and partly weak; this selected reading is not normalized to the expected sect name. The term is embedded in Portuguese.
[c1-l031] Iixxei. Fi, foxi. *Sol, & eſtrellas.* S.
[c1-l031 note] Jissei here denotes the sun and stars, explicitly hi and hoshi. S. is the upright written-register label, outside the italic gloss.
[c1-l032] Iixxei. Fino iqiuoi. *Influencia do ſol.* S.
[c1-l032 note] This separate jissei is the sun's influence or force, hi no ikioi. The final S. again marks written usage and is not part of the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l033] Iixxet. *Nouas verdadeiras. Vt,* Iixxetuo
[c1-l033 note] Jissetsu denotes true news or a reliable report. The object-marked Iixxetuo begins an example about establishing its truth by hearing.
[c1-l034 >] qiqi qiuamuru. *Ouuir bem as nouas ver-*
[c1-l034 note] Kiki kiwamuru means hearing thoroughly or ascertaining by inquiry. The scan shows Ouuir and a space before bem; ver- begins verdadeiras.
[c1-l035 >] *dadeiras.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes verdadeiras, qualifying the news as true. This row is part of the preceding example, not an independent entry.
[c1-l036] Iixxi ixxǒ. *De dez morrerem osnoue, &*
[c1-l036 note] Jisshi isshoo describes extreme danger, explained as nine of ten dying. The scan reads osnoue rather than OCR esnoue; the tightly printed article and numeral remain joined.
[c1-l037 >] *viuer hum. Vt,* Iixxi ixxǒno tatacaiuo
[c1-l037 note] Completes the contrast with one surviving. The example begins a battle of jisshi isshoo, a fight attended by extreme risk.
[c1-l038 >] naſu. *Batalhar com grande perigo, & riſ-*
[c1-l038 note] Nasu completes the Japanese battle expression. The Portuguese says to fight in great danger; riſ- visibly continues as riſco.
[c1-l039 >] *co da vida onde os noue morrem, & hum vi-*
[c1-l039 note] Completes risco da vida and describes nine dying while one lives. The terminal vi- visibly divides viue at the physical line boundary.
[c1-l040 >] *ue. ¶* Iixxiixxǒno vazzuraiuo ſuru.
[c1-l040 note] Ue completes viue. The new example applies the same extreme-danger expression to illness, with wadzurai wo suru; the draft's tightly joined Iixxiixxǒno is retained.
[c1-l041 >] *Estar doente de doença perigoſa, de que polla*
[c1-l041 note] Begins the illness example's translation: to suffer a dangerous disease. Polla is the printed historical form, not silently changed to modern pela.
[c1-l042 >] *mòr parte morrem os que a tem.*
[c1-l042 note] Completes the relative clause: most people who have the disease die. The grave in mòr and the unaccented printed tem are retained.
[c1-l043] Iixxi. Macotono co. *Verdadeiro filho, & le-*
[c1-l043 note] Jisshi is a true or legitimate child, explained by makoto no ko. Filho is clearly supported by the scan, repairing OCR flho; le- starts legitimo.
[c1-l044 >] *gitimo.*
[c1-l044 note] Completes legitimo, legitimate, the second qualification of the child. The physical continuation remains separate.
[c1-l045] Iixxin. Fibini arata nari. *Ir cada dia crecendo*
[c1-l045 note] Jisshin means daily renewal or progress, with hibi ni arata nari as the explanation. The gloss's crecendo is the visible historical spelling and is not supplied with an extra s.
[c1-l046 >] *como na virtude, letras, &c. Vt,* Iixxin-
[c1-l046 note] The growth applies to virtue, learning, and similar accomplishments. Iixxin- begins the illustrative construction across the line break.
[c1-l047 >] no cǒuo faguemaſu. *Ir cada dia fazendo*
[c1-l047 note] Completes jisshin no koo wo hagemasu, applying oneself to daily progress. The scan supports dia rather than OCR di; the Portuguese sentence continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l007] Iixxocu. Fino iro. *Coraparente do ſol.*

[c2-l008] Iiyacu. Terano yacu. *Officios do moſteiro,*

[c2-l010] Iiyacu. Mizzucarano cuſuri. i, Vaga nomu

[c2-l011 >] cuſuri. *Mezinha que eu tomo por mim.*

[c2-l012] Iiyo. Vare, fito. *Eu, & outros: comunmen-*

[c2-l014 >] conjenu fito. *Homem aſinalado, ou di-*

[c2-l015 >] *ferente dos outros.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes diferente dos outros, different from others. It belongs to the illustration of jiyo, not a new headword.
[c2-l016] Iiyô. Mizzucarano yô. *Propria neceßida-*
[c2-l016 note] Jiyoo denotes one's private need or business, explained by mizukara no yoo. Both long-o marks are circumflexes; the Portuguese noun continues on the next row.
[c2-l017 >] *de. ¶* Iiyôga gozaru. *Ter algũa nece-*
[c2-l017 note] De completes neceßidade. Jiyoo ga gozaru means having private business or a particular need; the whole Portuguese Ter belongs to italic type, not the OCR's split T/er.
[c2-l018 >] *ßidade particular. ¶* Iiyôni maitta. *Vim*
[c2-l018 note] Completes neceßidade particular, then gives jiyoo ni maitta, coming on private business. The scan reads maitta, not OCR moitta.
[c2-l019 >] *a hum negocio, ou neceßidade particular.*
[c2-l019 note] Completes vim: I came for a particular matter or need. The printed historical forms hum and neceßidade are retained.
[c2-l020] Iiyǔ. *Liberdade. Vt,* Iiyǔ jizaini furu-
[c2-l020 note] Jiyuu is freedom. The example begins jiyuu jizai ni furumoo, behaving freely and at will; the headword's u carries a caron, not the OCR grave.
[c2-l021 >] mǒ. *Andar liure, & à vontade.*
[c2-l021 note] Moo completes the historically contracted furumoo across the line break. The Portuguese explains free, unconstrained conduct.
[c2-l022] Iiyǔna. *Couſa liure. ¶* Iiyǔna cotouo
[c2-l022 note] Jiyuuna is the adjectival form, free or unconstrained. The initial is I rather than OCR F; the example starts jiyuuna koto wo.
[c2-l023 >] ſuru. *Fazer as couſas à ſua vontade, &*

[c2-l026 >] monouo yǔ. *Falar algũa couſa liure, &*

[c2-l028] Iizai. Mizzucara ari. *Liberdade.*
