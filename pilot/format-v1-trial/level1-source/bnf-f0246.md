[c1-l001] Gongo. *Palauras. Vt,* Gongoni voyoba-
[c1-l001 note] Gongo denotes words or speech. The example begins gongoni oyobanu, something beyond verbal expression; the scan's initial G replaces OCR C. The printed hyphen belongs to voyoba-.
[c1-l002 >] nu, l, noberarenu. *Não ſe pode explicar*
[c1-l002 note] Nu completes oyobanu; noberarenu offers the alternative “cannot be expressed.” The Portuguese begins the corresponding explanation, “cannot be explained.”
[c1-l003 >] *cõ palauras. ¶* Gõgouo jeſsuru. *Faltarẽ pa*
[c1-l003 note] The first translation ends “with words.” Gongo wo zessuru then describes words failing to convey something. The short mark in Gõgo is retained as a nasal abbreviation, rather than the draft's long-o caron; its impression is weak. Pa continues as lauras without a supplied hyphen.
[c1-l004 >] *lauras pera ẽcarecer algũa couſa. ¶* Gõgodǒ-
[c1-l004 note] Lauras completes palauras: lacking words to praise or emphasize something. The next expression is gongododan, continued below; its nasal abbreviation and the distinct caron over the later o are kept separately.
[c1-l005 >] dã. i, gõgouo jeſſuru. Id*ẽ. Palaura cõ*
[c1-l005 note] Dã completes gongododan. The repeated Japanese gloss explains it as being beyond words, followed by italic Idẽ, “the same.” The abbreviated nasal marks are faint but compatible with this reading; the OCR's Ioẽ is not supported by the surviving d.
[c1-l006 >] *que ſe ẽcarece algũa couſa em bem, ou mal.*
[c1-l006 note] The phrase can emphasize something either favorably or unfavorably. This qualification belongs to gongododan and should not be narrowed to its modern predominantly condemnatory use.
[c1-l007] Gongo xindai. *Palauras, & obras.*
[c1-l007 note] Gongo shindai pairs speech with bodily action; the Portuguese explicitly gives “words and deeds.” It is a separate entry, not a continuation of the preceding evaluation.
[c1-l008] Gonguiǒ. Tçutome voconǒ. *Exercicios bõs.*
[c1-l008 note] Gongyo is explained by tsutome and okonau, performing religious exercises or observances. The Portuguese calls these good exercises; the printed Japanese spellings and the abbreviated bõs remain unchanged.
[c1-l009] Gonichi. Nochino fi. *Tempo vindouro, ou*
[c1-l009 note] Gonichi is a later day or future occasion, paraphrased nochi no hi. The Portuguese definition starts with “time to come.”
[c1-l010 >] *tempo ao diante. ¶* Gonichino tameni mǒſu.
[c1-l010 note] The first gloss continues “time ahead.” Gonichi no tame ni mosu means saying something for future reference, which the next line translates.
[c1-l011 >] *Digo, ou falo pera odiante, ou tempo futuro.*
[c1-l011 note] This translates the preceding example as speaking for the future. The historical spacing odiante and spelling pera are preserved rather than normalized.
[c1-l012] Gônin. i, Acugôuo naſu fito. *Homem per*
[c1-l012 note] Gonin here is a person who commits evil deeds, as akugo wo nasu hito makes explicit. Its circumflex distinguishes it graphically from the subsequent Gǒnin entry; per continues into the next row without an added hyphen.
[c1-l013 >] *uerſo. Vt,* Giǔzaino gônin. *Homem*
[c1-l013 note] Uerso completes perverso. The example refers to an evildoer burdened with grave offenses; Giǔzai retains the printed historical spelling and its caron.
[c1-l014 >] *peruerſo, & que tem muitas maldades.*
[c1-l014 note] The example's translation is a wicked person with many evil deeds. It completes the criminal or karmic sense, before the separate territorial entry below.
[c1-l015] Gǒnin. *Homem, ou gente dalgũa comarca,*
[c1-l015 note] Gǒnin denotes a person or people of a district. The definition continues with their rising in revolt; the visible caron is not the preceding entry's circumflex.
[c1-l016 >] *ou territorio que ſe aleuanta, ou amutina.*
[c1-l016 note] The district's inhabitants rise or mutiny. This relative clause completes the definition and anticipates the explicit example on the following row.
[c1-l017 >] *¶* Gǒninga vocoru. *Aleuantarſe, ou amuti*
[c1-l017 note] Gonin ga okoru illustrates the local people's uprising. The damaged initial group reads Goninga, not Groninga; no additional long-vowel mark is supplied where the impression does not establish one.
[c1-l018 >] *narſe a gẽte de hũa parte do reino, ou comarca.*
[c1-l018 note] Narſe completes amutinarſe, describing rebellion by people in one part of a kingdom or district. The two nasal abbreviations remain unexpanded.
[c1-l019] Goninbari. i, Gonin faru fodono yumi.
[c1-l019 note] Goninbari is a bow requiring the strength of five people to bend or string. The Japanese explanation is gonin haru hodo no yumi.
[c1-l020 >] *Arco que armão cinco homẽs. ¶* Gonin-
[c1-l020 note] The Portuguese confirms a bow strung by five men. The example begins Gonin- and continues on the next physical row.
[c1-l021 >] barino yumi. *Idem.*
[c1-l021 note] Bari no yumi completes the compound example for the same five-man bow. Idem refers to the preceding definition.
[c1-l022] Gonja. *Homem inſigne em letras, & vir-*
[c1-l022 note] Gonja is characterized as outstanding in learning and virtue. The definition's religious dimension becomes explicit in the continuation.
[c1-l023 >] *tude, & zeloſo da ſaluação dos outros.*
[c1-l023 note] Tude completes virtude; the person is also zealous for others' salvation. This is the dictionary's own religious explanation, not a new entry.
[c1-l024] Gonjet. Cotoba xita. *Palauras. Vt,* Gõ-
[c1-l024 note] Gonjet, with a retained final t, denotes verbal expression and is paraphrased by kotoba and shita. The example begins the abbreviated Gõ-, whose small nasal mark differs from a clear long-o caron.
[c1-l025 >] jetni voyobanu. *Não ſe pode explicar*
[c1-l025 note] Jetni completes gonjet ni oyobanu: not expressible in words. The Portuguese translation begins “cannot be explained.”
[c1-l026 >] *com palauras.*
[c1-l026 note] “With words” completes the preceding example's translation. This short line remains independently represented.
[c1-l027] Gonjet. Cotobauo motte toqu. *Preegar,*
[c1-l027 note] The second Gonjet entry means explaining through words: kotoba wo motte toku. The Portuguese gives preaching, with speaking added below.
[c1-l028 >] *ou falar.*
[c1-l028 note] “Or speaking” completes the second Gonjet definition, distinguishing an activity from the preceding entry's words or expression.
[c1-l029] Gonjiqi. Cazari cazaru. *Ornato, ou luſtre.*
[c1-l029 note] Gonjiqi concerns ornament or splendor; kazari and kazaru supply Japanese equivalents. The Portuguese luſtre is retained with its draft allograph rather than globally regularized.
[c1-l030] Gonjǒ. Mǒxi aguru. *Falar a peſſoa nobre.*
[c1-l030 note] Gonjo is deferentially addressing a noble person, paraphrased moshi aguru. The scan establishes Gonjǒ rather than OCR Cionjǒ.
[c1-l031 >] *Vt,* Gonjǒ tçucamatçuru, l, toguru.
[c1-l031 note] Gonjo tsukamatsuru and gonjo toguru are alternative expressions for making such a respectful address. Vt is italic; the Japanese forms and alternative marker remain roman.
[c1-l032] Gonjǒ. *Ornato, & fermoſura. Vt,* Qi-
[c1-l032 note] This separate Gonjo entry concerns adornment and beauty, not addressing a superior. Qi- starts the illustrative qirei gonjo phrase below.
[c1-l033 >] rei gonjǒ. *Idem.*
[c1-l033 note] Rei completes qirei, yielding qirei gonjo for beautiful adornment. Idem points to the preceding ornament-and-beauty definition.
[c1-l034] Gonǒ. Von nayami. *Doença do Rey.*
[c1-l034 note] Gono is an honored person's illness, explained here specifically as the king's illness. Von nayami retains its printed honorific spelling.
[c1-l035] Goppô. Xiuazano mucui. *Retribuição das*
[c1-l035 note] Goppo is recompense for deeds, paraphrased shiwaza no mukui. The Portuguese begins “retribution for the works,” continued below.
[c1-l036 >] *obras que hum faz.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*q̃ fez.*
[c1-l036 note] The main text completes recompense for the deeds one does. At the far right the scan also prints a parenthesis-like mark and q̃ fez, omitted by OCR. This displaced phrase completes the next entry's “evils that he did”; it remains at its actual printed height rather than being moved into that sentence.
[c1-l037] Goppônin. *Homem caſtigado pollos males*
[c1-l037 note] Gopponin is a person punished for evil deeds. Its completion q̃ fez is printed above at the far right of c1-l036, where it has been restored; no new physical row is invented.
[c1-l038] Goqe. i, Vottoni vocuretaru võna. *Viuua.*
[c1-l038 note] Goke is a widow, literally a woman surviving her husband. The short, sloping mark over võna is read as a damaged nasal abbreviation for onna rather than a long-o caron; its exact shape is not fully secure. The gloss ends with a period.
[c1-l039] Goqeiri. *Caſamento da viuua com algum*
[c1-l039 note] Gokeiri denotes a widow's marriage to a new husband. The damaged capital is G, and the Portuguese reads com, not OCR cem.
[c1-l040 >] *homem, q̃ ſoccede na caſa do primeiro marido.*
[c1-l040 note] The new husband succeeds to the first husband's household. This qualification is essential to the entry's meaning, beyond merely a widow remarrying.
[c1-l041] Goqi. *Eſcudela de pao ẽ que ſe come o arroz.*
[c1-l041 note] Goki is a wooden bowl used for eating rice. The Portuguese nasal abbreviation ẽ is kept without expansion.
[c1-l042] Goqi. Itçutçuno voni. *Cinco demonios aos*
[c1-l042 note] This separate Goki means five demons, explicitly itsutsu no oni. The following rows recount their subjugation and a related mountain-pilgrimage practice.
[c1-l043 >] *quais hum Yamabuxi por nome Yenno guiǒja*
[c1-l043 note] The relative clause names the yamabushi En no Gyoja as the person who subdued them. Yamabuxi begins with Y, not OCR V; the mark in guiǒja is a caron. These embedded Japanese designations are retained in the locally italic explanation.
[c1-l044 >] *venceo, & ſujeitou, & agora em lugar des-*
[c1-l044 note] The named ascetic conquered and subjected the demons. The account then turns to people appearing in their place; des- continues as tes below.
[c1-l045 >] *tes aparecem là na ſerra que ſe diz Vonmine,*
[c1-l045 note] Tes completes destes. The account says that in their place others appear on the mountain called Vonmine. The first e of aparecem is almost lost: tiny ink remnants occupy its position, and the surrounding Portuguese supports this supplied reading. It remains a nonblocking damaged-letter uncertainty, not a clearly intact glyph. Vonmine is an embedded Japanese mountain name.
[c1-l046 >] *cinco homẽs veſtidos de cinco cores que ſaem*
[c1-l046 note] Five men wearing five colors emerge in place of the demons. The line continues the historical account, rather than defining another sense of Goqi.
[c1-l047 >] *ao encontro aos Yamabuxis que vão á ſerra em*
[c1-l047 note] These men meet the yamabushi traveling to the mountain. The embedded plural Yamabuxis belongs within the Portuguese sentence and is a typeface-review candidate.
[c1-l048 >] *romaria.*
[c1-l048 note] Romaria completes the description of a pilgrimage. The short line is body text above the lower rule, not a catchword.

[c2-l001] Goqiguanjo. Negaiuo inoru tocoro. *Te-*
[c2-l001 note] Gokiganjo is a place for prayers or petitions. The Portuguese identifies it as a temple built by royal initiative; Te- continues into ra.
[c2-l002 >] *ra que o Rei fez, ou mandou aleuantar.*
[c2-l002 note] Ra completes Tera, a temple made or ordered built by the king. The initial syllable is kept on the preceding physical row.
[c2-l003] Goqiǒ. Itçutçuno fon. *Cinco liuros da Chi-*
[c2-l003 note] Gokyo is defined as five books, itsutsu no hon, of China. The capital is visibly G, correcting OCR C; Chi- continues into na.
[c2-l004 >] *na que tratão das couſas moraes. V. G.*
[c2-l004 note] The five Chinese books concern moral matters. V. G. introduces the following list and is printed in the same italic apparatus, rather than with an isolated roman G.
[c2-l005 >] Yeqi, raiqi, môxi, jǒjo, xunju.
[c2-l005 note] This row lists the titles as Yeqi, raiqi, môxi, jǒjo, and xunju. The scan's circumflex in môxi replaces the draft's caron; the list is retained literally rather than adjusted to an expected canon.
[c2-l006] Goracu. Tanoximi, u. i, Racu. *Praze-*
[c2-l006 note] Goraku means pleasure or enjoyment, with tanoshimi and raku as equivalents. The second equivalent begins with the clear R of Racu, not OCR Bacu.
[c2-l007 >] *res. Vt,* Tenjǒno goracu. *Contenta-*
[c2-l007 note] Res completes prazeres. Tenjo no goraku illustrates heavenly delights, translated by contentamentos across this line and the next.
[c2-l008 >] *mentos do ceo.*
[c2-l008 note] This completes “the delights of heaven.” Ceo is the printed historical Portuguese spelling, not an error to modernize.
[c2-l009] Goran. *Ver peßoa nobre. Vt,* Goran a-
[c2-l009 note] Goran is honorific seeing, used when the person doing the seeing is noble. The example begins goran aru, not a command to look at a noble person.
[c2-l010 >] ru, l, naſaruru.
[c2-l010 note] Ru completes aru; nasaruru supplies the alternative honorific predicate. The whole row belongs to the example introduced above.
[c2-l011] Goranji, zuru, ita. *Ver peßoa nobre.*
[c2-l011 note] Goranji gives principal forms of the honorific verb for seeing. The Portuguese again specifies a noble subject, and the historical endings are preserved.
[c2-l012] Goranji vocurare, ruru, eta. *Ir algũa peßoa*
[c2-l012 note] Goranji okurare describes respectfully watching someone depart. The scan supports vocurare rather than OCR vocurate; the noble observer is identified in the continuation.
[c2-l013 >] *nobre a companhando com a viſta ao q̃ ſe vai.*
[c2-l013 note] A noble person follows the departing person with their gaze. The printed separation a companhando is retained rather than silently normalized to acompanhando.
[c2-l014] Gôrei. Tçuneno tamexi. *Cuſtume ordina-*
[c2-l014 note] Gorei here is customary precedent, tsune no tameshi, not an order or command. The Portuguese begins “ordinary custom.”
[c2-l015 >] *rio, como foro.*
[c2-l015 note] Rio completes ordinario; como foro likens the custom to an established practice or customary obligation.
[c2-l016] Gorenchǔ. *Senhora, ou molher de Yacata,*
[c2-l016 note] Gorenchu denotes a noble lady or the wife of a yakata. Yacata is a Japanese designation embedded in the Portuguese gloss, whose typography is kept italic here.
[c2-l017 >] *ou Tono grande.*
[c2-l017 note] The wife may be that of a great tono, or lord. Tono is another Japanese designation embedded in the explanatory Portuguese.
[c2-l018] Goreônin. i, Qininno muſume. *Filha de*
[c2-l018 note] Goreonin respectfully denotes a daughter, initially specified as a noble person's daughter by kinin no musume. The scope is broadened below.
[c2-l019 >] *peßoa nobre, ou de outra ſomenos falando*
[c2-l019 note] The expression can also refer to the daughter of someone of lower rank, provided that one speaks respectfully. Somenos carries that comparative social qualification.
[c2-l020 >] *com honra.*
[c2-l020 note] “With honor” completes the usage qualification. It explains why the designation is not restricted absolutely to noble birth.
[c2-l021] Goreôxo. *Renda que eſta encabeçada na co-*
[c2-l021 note] Goreosho concerns revenue assigned to the crown. The headword begins with G, not OCR C; the visibly unaccented eſta is retained.
[c2-l022 >] *roa, ou renda, & terras particulares do Rey.*
[c2-l022 note] Roa completes coroa; the alternative explanation is the king's own revenue and lands. Both descriptions belong to the same entry.
[c2-l023] Gǒri. Famaguri, famaguri. *Certas ameijo-*
[c2-l023 note] This Gori is a shellfish term, glossed by repeated hamaguri and Portuguese ameijoas. The repetition is printed and is not removed as redundant.
[c2-l024 >] *as da praya.*
[c2-l024 note] As completes ameijoas, specifying clams of the shore. Praya retains its historical y spelling.
[c2-l025] Gǒri. *No Cami ſe diz* Caraſuuri. *Hũa*
[c2-l025 note] This distinct plant term is called karasuuri in the Cami region. Caraſuuri is visibly roman inside the italic explanation; Cami is an embedded regional designation.
[c2-l026 >] *fruita como melonzinhos, que nacem de*
[c2-l026 note] The fruit resembles little melons growing from a plant. The source's fruita and nacem are retained, rather than modernized.
[c2-l027 >] *certa era.*
[c2-l027 note] The printed era completes the plant description where erva would be expected semantically. No intervening v is securely visible, so the apparent source anomaly is retained.
[c2-l028] Gorimecaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer ſoar com os*
[c2-l028 note] Gorimekashi gives forms for making a crunching sound with the teeth. The Portuguese specifies the circumstances in the next two rows.
[c2-l029 >] *dentes quando ſe come couſa verde, ou dura*
[c2-l029 note] The sound arises while eating something green or hard. This is a description of chewing or crunching, not speech.
[c2-l030 >] *como castanhas, &c.*
[c2-l030 note] Chestnuts provide the example of hard food. The draft's st representation is retained under the supplied policy for ambiguous italic st ligatures.
[c2-l031] Gorimeqi, u, *e*ita. *Trincar a couſa verde*
[c2-l031 note] Gorimeki gives the related crunching action. The Portuguese trincar describes biting the green or hard item between the teeth.
[c2-l032 >] *&c. nos dentes.*
[c2-l032 note] “And so on, in the teeth” completes the chewing definition. The abbreviated continuation remains a separate physical line.
[c2-l033] Gorin. Facano vyeni tatçuru xiruxino ixi.
[c2-l033 note] Gorin is explained as a marker stone erected above a grave: haka no ue ni tatsuru shirushi no ishi. The spelling vyeni is retained.
[c2-l034 >] *Pedras que ſe poem por ſinal em cima das co-*
[c2-l034 note] The Portuguese describes stones placed as markers over graves. Co- begins covas at the physical line ending.
[c2-l035 >] *uas dos defuntos, como campas, &c.*
[c2-l035 note] Uas completes covas dos defuntos, the graves of the dead, compared with tombstones or slabs. No additional monument terminology is inserted.
[c2-l036] Gǒriqi. Yamabuxino comono. *Moço, ou*
[c2-l036 note] Goriki here is a yamabushi's attendant or servant, as yamabushi no komono explains. Portuguese Moço corrects OCR Meço.
[c2-l037 >] *criado de Yamabuxi.*
[c2-l037 note] “Servant of a yamabushi” completes the definition. Yamabuxi is embedded in the Portuguese explanation and appears in its italic type.
[c2-l038] Gǒriqi. Tçuyoi chicara. *Grandes forças.*
[c2-l038 note] This second Goriki means great physical strength, tsuyoi chikara. It is distinct from the preceding attendant sense.
[c2-l039 >] *¶* Gǒriqina monogia. *He homẽ de grã-*
[c2-l039 note] Gorikina mono ja says that someone is a strong person. The scan contains a pilcrow before the example, absent from the draft; grã- continues the Portuguese adjective below.
[c2-l040 >] *des forças.*
[c2-l040 note] Des completes grandes forças, “great strength.” The visible o in forças corrects OCR ferças.
[c2-l041] Gǒriqixa. *Forçoſo, ou homẽ de grandes forças.*
[c2-l041 note] Gorikisha denotes a powerful or strong person. Both Portuguese formulations explicitly describe strength, not a different occupation.
[c2-l042] Gorǒ. Itçutçuno tçucare. *Cinco maneiras*
[c2-l042 note] Goro is five kinds of fatigue, paraphrased itsutsu no tsukare. The weak accent is retained as the draft's caron.
[c2-l043 >] *de canſaço, ou fraqueza. S.*
[c2-l043 note] The five kinds are of tiredness or weakness. Final S. is an upright usage label for written language, not part of the italic definition.
[c2-l044] Gorocu. *Pedras grandes. Vt,* Gorocuuo
[c2-l044 note] Goroku means large stones. The example begins with the stones as the object, goroku wo, and continues with verbs of dropping or throwing.
[c2-l045 >] votoxicaquru, l, naguru. *Lançar estas*
[c2-l045 note] Otoshikakuru and naguru are alternatives for casting these stones. The Portuguese translation begins here and specifies their military use below.
[c2-l046 >] *pedras da fortaleza ſobre os inimigos.*
[c2-l046 note] The stones are thrown down from the fortress onto enemies. This completes the example, not an independent definition.
[c2-l047] Gorocu. *Liuro de autoridades dos Ienxus.*
[c2-l047 note] This separate Goroku is a book of authoritative sayings associated with the Zen sect, represented by printed Ienxus. That embedded designation is not modernized in the transcription.
