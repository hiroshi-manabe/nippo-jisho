[c1-l001] Imi. *Agouro. Vt,* Connichi imiga aita.
[c1-l001 note] Imi denotes a taboo or ominous restriction. Connichi imiga aita says that the restriction ended today; the scan supports g rather than OCR q and a full stop after aita, not a continuation hyphen.
[c1-l002 >] *Oje ſe acabou o agouro, ou prohibição de ir a*
[c1-l002 note] The Portuguese explains that the taboo or prohibition against going somewhere ended today. Oje is the historical spelling, not a reason to insert modern initial h.
[c1-l003 >] *algũa parte ou fazer algũa couſa.*
[c1-l003 note] The continuation adds going to a place or doing something. Both occurrences of algũa retain their abbreviated nasal rather than being expanded.
[c1-l004] Imigoto. *Obra, ou ceremonia q̃ ſe faz por agouro.*
[c1-l004 note] Imigoto is a deed or ceremony performed because of a taboo or omen. The scan restores the missing i in the headword and reads Obra, ou, not the OCR's cou.
[c1-l005] Imijij. *Couſa ditoſa. Vt,* Quafô imijij fi-
[c1-l005 note] Imijij here means fortunate or blessed. Quafô imijij fi-to describes a fortunate person; the Japanese spelling and the circumflex on Quafô remain distinct from modern forms.
[c1-l006 >] to. *Homem ditoſo, & bemauenturado.*
[c1-l006 note] To completes fito, followed by "a fortunate and blessed man." The physical division fi-to is preserved.
[c1-l007 >] Imijiſa. Imijǔ. || {mark} *(* || {word}*agouro.*
[c1-l007 note] Imijiſa and Imijǔ are derivatives of the preceding adjective. The far-right agouro belongs logically to Imimono on the next row; its faint preceding parenthesis is retained from the surviving stroke and the clearer parallel in c1-l022.
[c1-l008] Imimono. *Couſa de q̃ ſe tem aſco ou ſetoma por*
[c1-l008 note] Imimono is something regarded with aversion or as ominous. The definition ends with por here and is completed by the displaced agouro physically above, not by a missing new line.
[c1-l009] Imizo. *Rego por onde corre agoa pera as*
[c1-l009 note] Imizo denotes an irrigation channel carrying water toward cultivated lowlands. The historical agoa and pera are retained instead of modernized spellings.
[c1-l010 >] *varzeas.*
[c1-l010 note] Varzeas completes the destination of the irrigation water. This is a short continuation of Imizo, not an independent geographical entry.
[c1-l011] Imo. *Inhame.*
[c1-l011 note] Imo is glossed Inhame, a yam-like tuber. The initial I is visible and corrects OCR lowercase l; the gloss does not justify a more precise modern species identification.
[c1-l012] Imo. *P. i, Molher.*
[c1-l012 note] This homonymous Imo is a poetic term for a woman or wife. P. marks poetic usage, and the historical Portuguese Molher is preserved.
[c1-l013] Imoji. l, imonoxe. *Fundidor de vaſos de*
[c1-l013 note] Imoji, alternatively imonoxe, is a founder of vessels. The alternative is part of the headword apparatus, while the material specification continues below.
[c1-l014 >] *ferro, ou bronzo.*
[c1-l014 note] The vessels are made of iron or bronze. Bronzo is the printed historical form and is not silently replaced by modern bronze.
[c1-l015] Imomaqi. *Hum comer que ſe faz de inhames*
[c1-l015 note] Imomaqi is a prepared food made from yams. The Portuguese verb is faz, with f, not the OCR's ſaz; the description continues with how the yams are prepared.
[c1-l016 >] *ralados feitos em Xiru como polme.*
[c1-l016 note] The yams are grated and made into Xiru with a batter-like consistency. Xiru is Japanese embedded in the Portuguese explanation; the scan prints the whole word in the surrounding italic type.
[c1-l017] Imomuxi. *Certo bicho aßi chamado que ſe cria*
[c1-l017 note] Imomuxi is described as a creature that develops in yams. The gloss supports the caterpillar/grub sense without requiring assignment to a particular species.
[c1-l018 >] *nos inhames.*
[c1-l018 note] Nos inhames completes the habitat or food-plant description. Its relation to Imomuxi above is retained as a separate physical row.
[c1-l019] Imonoxe. i, Imoji. *Vide ſuprà.*
[c1-l019 note] Imonoxe is equated with Imoji and directs the reader upward. Imoji is a Japanese synonym after i, not an embedded Portuguese term requiring a typeface toggle.
[c1-l020] Imori. *Hum bicho como oſga, ou lagartixa*
[c1-l020 note] Imori denotes the creature compared with a gecko or small lizard. The Portuguese is a historical descriptive comparison, not a modern taxonomic classification.
[c1-l021 >] *peçonhenta.*
[c1-l021 note] Peçonhenta completes the description as poisonous. This qualification belongs to the preceding animal entry and is preserved as the source's claim.
[c1-l022] Imoto. *Irmaã mais moça.* || {mark} *(* || {word}*moça.*
[c1-l022 note] Imoto means a younger sister. Irmaã retains the clear tilde on the second a; the additional far-right parenthesis and moça complete the following Imotomuco gloss and are preserved as displaced material.
[c1-l023] Imotomuco. *Cunhado marido da jrmaã mais*
[c1-l023 note] Imotomuco is the brother-in-law married to one's younger sister. Its final moça is physically above at c1-l022; the printed jrmaã is retained rather than modernizing its initial letter.
[c1-l024] Imoxe. *P.* Fùfu. *Marido, & molher.*
[c1-l024 note] Imoxe is a poetic expression for husband and wife, equated with Fùfu. The grave on the first u belongs to the Japanese form, not to the Portuguese apparatus.

[c1-l025] In. Cague. *Tera, ou moſteiro: ordinaria mente*
[c1-l025 note] The scan reads In. Cague., not OCR ſu. Cague means "shade," whereas the Portuguese here explains a temple or monastery; this juxtaposition is retained without repairing the source's mismatch.
[c1-l026 >] *ſe vſa com algũa compoſiçaõ. Vt,* Innai. *Dẽ*
[c1-l026 note] The entry says that In is usually compounded and gives Innai. The two n letters are visible; the stale Ianai hint would obscure the intended "inside the temple" example.
[c1-l027 >] *tro da Tera, ou do circuito da Tera. Itẽ he pa-*
[c1-l027 note] Tro completes Dẽtro. The explanation means inside a Tera or its precinct, then starts another use in names; the embedded Tera occurs twice on this row.
[c1-l028 >] *laura de nomes de caſas, ou familias, & tam*
[c1-l028 note] The explanation concerns names of houses or families. Tam continues below as bem, without an inferred terminal hyphen.
[c1-l029 >] *bem de gente dalta familia*: *ordinaria mente ſe*
[c1-l029 note] Bem completes tambem, adding persons of high family. Ordinaria mente is visibly separated and remains so in the diplomatic text.
[c1-l030 >] *uſa no cabo da compoſiçaõ. Vt,* Qenrei
[c1-l030 note] The element is generally used at the end of a compound. Qenrei begins the example name Qenrei mon in across the physical break.
[c1-l031 >] mon in.
[c1-l031 note] Mon in completes the example name, illustrating final in. The printed separation remains intact rather than being joined according to modern spelling.
[c1-l032] In. *Palaura de liuros que ſe toma, ou por ſom-*
[c1-l032 note] This In is a bookish term with several senses, beginning with shade. The capital I is restored from the scan, and ſom- continues as bra below.
[c1-l033 >] *bra, humidade, Outono, &c.*
[c1-l033 note] Bra completes sombra; humidity and autumn are added as further senses or associations. This is the shade/yin entry, distinct from the temple-name discussion above.
[c1-l034] In. Voxite. *Sinete, l, ſello. Vt* Inuo ata-
[c1-l034 note] In is equated with Voxite and defined as a seal or signet. The first example divides atayuru as ata-yuru; Vt has no added punctuation beyond what the scan supplies.
[c1-l035 >] yuru. *Dar oſeu ſello, ou ſinal. ¶* In
[c1-l035 note] Yuru completes the verb "give," translating as giving one's seal or sign. The paragraph sign introduces a second expression involving setting or stamping a seal.
[c1-l036 >] uo ſuyuru. l, voſu. *Por o ſello, ou ſinal.*
[c1-l036 note] In uo ſuyuru, alternatively voſu, means affixing the seal or mark. The historical suyuru is not regularized to a modern verb form.
[c1-l037 >] *¶ Item, Piſada, ou raſto de aues. Vt,* Xatǒ
[c1-l037 note] A further sense is a bird's footprint or track. Xatǒ begins an example about gulls leaving marks in the sand, not a new headword.
[c1-l038 >] ni inuo qizamu camome. *Gaiuotas*
[c1-l038 note] The Japanese describes gulls engraving footprints on sandy shore. Qizamu is the verb, and camome is the bird; the Portuguese translation begins with Gaiuotas.
[c1-l039 >] *que deixaõ raſto dos pès na area da praya.*
[c1-l039 note] The Portuguese completes the description of footprints left in beach sand. The tilde remains on the final o of deixaõ, and pès retains its visible grave.
[c1-l040] In. Vru uǒ. *Semente humana. Vt* In*u*o
[c1-l040 note] This In is glossed as human semen. The Japanese paraphrase is printed Vru uǒ with separation; it is retained without forcing the generated kana hint into a new canonical spelling.
[c1-l041 >] moraſu. *Derramar aſemente. ¶* Ij in-
[c1-l041 note] Moraſu completes the first expression for emitting semen. Ij in- starts a second example about self-induced emission; its printed spacing and letters are retained rather than interpreted as ordinary modern ii, "good."
[c1-l042 >] uo moraſu. *Fazer polluçaõ porſi.*
[c1-l042 note] The second example is explained as causing an emission oneself. The source's historical polluçaõ and joined porſi remain unmodernized.
[c1-l043] Ina. *Hum peixe de rio.*
[c1-l043 note] Ina is a river-fish name. The brief gloss supplies no secure basis for a narrower species identification in the transcription note.
[c1-l044] Ina. *Particula negatiua. i, Não.*
[c1-l044 note] Another Ina is the negative particle, glossed "no." The scan has i, rather than the OCR's i. before Não.
[c1-l045] Inaba. *P. Arroz que eſtà inda nas varze*
[c1-l045 note] Inaba is marked poetic and denotes rice still standing in the lowland fields. The Portuguese reads Arroz que eſtà inda, correcting several OCR errors; varze continues at the top of column 2.

[c2-l011] Inacazzuto. *Preſente do campo, ou daldea.*

[c2-l012] Inacǒdo. *Aldeão.*

[c2-l013] Inacoto. *Couſa de que ſe hũ eſpanta.*

[c2-l016] Inago. *Gafanhotos.*

[c2-l017] Inaguqi. *Rostolho que fica depois do arroz*
[c2-l017 note] Inaguqi is the stubble remaining after rice is cut. Rostolho is retained as printed, including the provisional st classification.
[c2-l018 >] *ſegado.*

[c2-l019] Inagura. *Cileiro de guardar arroz cõ eſpiga.*

[c2-l020] Inamaqi. *Vide* Inabaqi.
