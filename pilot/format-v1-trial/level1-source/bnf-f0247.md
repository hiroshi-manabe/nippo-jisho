[c1-l001] Gorǒjerare, ruru, eta. *Meliùs,* Goranjera-
[c1-l001 note] This honorific seeing verb is referred to the preferred form beginning Goranjera-. Meliùs is italic editorial Latin, while both Japanese forms remain roman.
[c1-l002 >] ruru. *Ver peßoa nobre.*
[c1-l002 note] Ruru completes the preferred form goranjeraruru. The Portuguese indicates seeing by a noble person, not seeing a noble person as the object.
[c1-l003] Gorǒji, zuru, ita. *Ver peſſoa nobre.*
[c1-l003 note] Goroji supplies another honorific verb for seeing and its principal forms. The scan supports the same noble-subject interpretation as the preceding entry.
[c1-l004] Goromecaxi, ſu. *Fazer traquinada, ou fa-*
[c1-l004 note] Goromekashi concerns making a rattling noise. Fa- begins the repeated Portuguese fazer, whose description continues with small objects in a box.
[c1-l005 >] *zer ſoar à maneira de grãos, ou bolotas que e* || {word}*ſ*
[c1-l005 note] The noise is compared with grains or acorns rattling. Enlargement supports bolotas: the worn t retains part of its upper stroke and is not an i. A long ſ omitted by OCR is set lower at the extreme right after e; with tão below it forms eſtão. Its displaced placement is preserved, and the expanded crop contains its full descender.
[c1-l006 >] *tão dentro dalgũa caixinha baſcolejandoa.*
[c1-l006 note] Tão completes eſtão: the objects are inside a little box that is shaken. The scan supports caixinha, not caicinha, and the historical baſcolejandoa is retained.
[c1-l007] Goromeqi, u. *Modo de ſoarem estas couſas*
[c1-l007 note] Goromeki names the manner in which those objects rattle. Its sentence-ending point is displaced to the far right of the next physical row, where it is represented.
[c1-l008] Gôſa. *Obra.* || {punct} *.*
[c1-l008 note] Gosa is glossed simply as a deed or work. The isolated far-right point is also visible on this row; it appears to close the preceding Goromeqi gloss and is not suppressed or moved upward.
[c1-l009] Goſan. Itçutçuno yama. *Cinco varelas do*
[c1-l009 note] Gozan is literally five mountains; the Portuguese identifies five temples or religious establishments, rather than five ordinary hills.
[c1-l010 >] *Miyaco affamadas. V. G.* Qenninji,
[c1-l010 note] These are renowned establishments of Miyako. A clear descending y in Miyaco was omitted by OCR; this embedded place-name remains italic. The list begins Qenninji, with no extra i between the final n and j, and V. G. is wholly italic apparatus.
[c1-l011 >] Nanjenji. Tǒfucuji, Tenriǔji, Xôcocuji.
[c1-l011 note] The list continues with Nanzenji, Tofukuji, Tenryuji, and Shokokuji. The weak impression in Nanjenji is consistent with the restored a; punctuation and the printed long-vowel marks are retained.
[c1-l012] Goſan. *Cinco, & tres nos dados.*
[c1-l012 note] This separate Gosan is a five and a three on dice. It is not a continuation of the five-temple list.
[c1-l013] Gǒſo. Qibixǔ vttayuru. *Acuſação forte,*
[c1-l013 note] Goso denotes a forceful complaint or accusation, paraphrased kibishu uttayuru. The scan shows no extra i after Qibixǔ; the headword's weak mark is read as a caron rather than the draft's circumflex.
[c1-l014 >] *ou aſpera.*
[c1-l014 note] “Or harsh” completes the characterization of the accusation. The short row belongs to Goso, not the following sound-symbolic entry.
[c1-l015] Gôſogoſoto. *Modo, ou ſom de quando torcẽ*
[c1-l015 note] Gosogosoto describes the manner or sound of twisting ropes. The first vowel mark is weak; the aligned Gôſogoſoto reading is retained as a qualified graphic choice rather than normalized from the unmarked repetition in the example or the lexical index.
[c1-l016 >] *as cordas, &c. Vt,* Nauauo xigoxacu
[c1-l016 note] The example begins nawa wo shigo-shaku, a length of rope. The next rows give the action and the Portuguese rendering in palmos.
[c1-l017 >] goſogoſoto nǒte, &c. Mon. *Torcen-*
[c1-l017 note] The rope is twisted noisily or briskly; nǒte is the printed historical form. The scan reads &c., not OCR &e. Mon. is a roman source label, separate from italic Torcen-.
[c1-l018 >] *do ſete, ou oito palmos de corda com preßa,*
[c1-l018 note] Do completes torcendo. The translation specifies seven or eight palmos of rope, twisted with haste; the source's measure is not recalculated or harmonized.
[c1-l019 >] *& eſtrondo.*
[c1-l019 note] “And noise” completes the illustration of gosogosoto. Its sound component is as important as the haste mentioned above.
[c1-l020] Gôſomecaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer ſoar os vesti*
[c1-l020 note] Gosomekashi concerns making new paper garments rustle. The small mark above the headword vowel is damaged, so the aligned circumflex reading is retained with uncertainty rather than replaced from lexical expectation. Vesti continues below without an inferred hyphen.
[c1-l021 >] *dos nouos de papel, ou couſas ſemelhantes.*
[c1-l021 note] Dos completes vestidos, specifying new paper clothing or similar things. The explanation concerns the material's sound, not making the clothing itself.
[c1-l022] Gǒſomeqi, u, eita. *Fazerſe eſte ſom, ou*
[c1-l022 note] Gosomeki is the corresponding occurrence of the rustling sound. The mark above the first o is poorly preserved; the aligned caron reading is retained as a nonblocking graphic uncertainty, not treated as a secure phonological distinction.
[c1-l023 >] *estrondo como quem bole com papel, &c.*
[c1-l023 note] The sound is compared with handling or moving paper. Bole is a form of Portuguese bulir, not an unexplained Japanese word.
[c1-l024] Goſui. Itçutçuno votoroye. *Cinco defeitos,*
[c1-l024 note] Gosui is five forms of decline, itsutsu no otoroe. The Portuguese begins with five defects, adding torments in the next row.
[c1-l025 >] *ou tormentos. Vt,* Goſuino curuximi.
[c1-l025 note] The alternative gloss is torments; gosui no kurushimi illustrates the suffering associated with the five declines.
[c1-l026] Gotai. *Sentidos com todo o corpo.*
[c1-l026 note] Gotai is explained as the senses together with the whole body. The concise wording is retained without replacing it with a later anatomical interpretation.
[c1-l027] Gotai roccon. *Todo o corpo com ſentidos,*
[c1-l027 note] Gotai rokkon combines the whole body with its senses. The next row adds its powers or faculties.
[c1-l028 >] *& potencias.*
[c1-l028 note] Potencias completes the definition with the body's faculties or capacities. It is not a separate entry or a usage label.
[c1-l029] Gotai xinbun. *Todo o corpo, & membros,*
[c1-l029 note] Gotai shinbun concerns the body and its individual parts. The damaged ending of Gotai still supports ai, not the OCR's isolated italic à.
[c1-l030 >] *& junturas em particular.*
[c1-l030 note] The definition includes the joints individually. This clarifies the more articulated, part-by-part description of the body.
[c1-l031] Gotan. *Palaura.* S.
[c1-l031 note] Gotan means a word or utterance. The final upright S. marks written usage and is not italic Portuguese text.
[c1-l032] Gotei, l, *potiùs,* Goteixu. *Dono da caſa.*
[c1-l032 note] Gotei is referred more particularly to goteishu, the house's master. Potiùs is wholly italic Latin; Dono, supported by the surviving n, replaces OCR Doro.
[c1-l033 >] i, Iyeno nuxi, l, teixu.
[c1-l033 note] Ie no nushi and teishu provide Japanese equivalents for the master of the house. They are standalone equivalents, not embedded terms to flag for typeface toggling.
[c1-l034] Gotei. *Cinco Reis principaes, & nomeados*
[c1-l034 note] This Gotei denotes five principal, renowned rulers. It is a distinct entry from the preceding household-master term.
[c1-l035 >] *da China, que ouue antiguamente.*
[c1-l035 note] The rulers are of ancient China. The historical Portuguese ouue and antiguamente are preserved as printed.
[c1-l036] Goten. *Paços do Rey.*
[c1-l036 note] Goten is a royal palace or royal apartments. The source gives a concise plural Portuguese expression, paços do Rey.
[c1-l037] Gǒteqi. Tçuyoi teqi. *Forte inimigo. Vt,*
[c1-l037 note] Goteki is a strong enemy, explicitly tsuyoi teki. Its example continues on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] Gǒteqi fuxegui gataxi. *He difficultoſo*
[c1-l038 note] Goteki fusegi gatashi says that a strong enemy is hard to resist. The enlarged word supports doubled ff in difficultoſo; the faint e in He remains compatible with the draft.
[c1-l039 >] *de reſistir ao inimigo forte.*
[c1-l039 note] The translation finishes “to resist the strong enemy.” The existing mixed ſist sequence is retained rather than changed by a general ligature rule.
[c1-l040] Gotô. Qirino qi. *Hũa aruore boa pera fa-*
[c1-l040 note] Goto is identified as the kiri tree. The Portuguese starts an explanation of its wood being suitable for making boxes.
[c1-l041 >] *zer caixas, &c.*
[c1-l041 note] Zer completes fazer: the tree's wood is good for making boxes and similar objects. No species name is added to the diplomatic text.
[c1-l042] Gǒtô. Qeno ſaqi. i, Fudeno ſaqi. *Ponta*
[c1-l042 note] Goto here is the tip of a hair or brush, ke no saki and fude no saki. Portuguese Ponta, not Panta, names the point or tip.
[c1-l043 >] *do pincel, ou pena de Iapão.*
[c1-l043 note] The tip belongs to the brush, described as Japan's equivalent of a pen. Iapão remains Portuguese text, not a Japanese typeface term.
[c1-l044] Gotocu. i, Canaua. *Trempe de ferro.*
[c1-l044 note] Gotoku, also kanawa, is an iron trivet. This object sense is separate from the following adverbial expression.
[c1-l045] Gotocu, l, gotocuni. *Adu. Como, ou aßi*
[c1-l045 note] Gotoku and gotokuni mean “like” or “as.” Adu. identifies the adverbial usage and is italic with the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l046 >] *como. Vt,* Mayeno gotocu. *Aßi co-*
[c1-l046 note] Mae no gotoku means “as before.” Both the first gloss and the illustrative translation continue across physical line boundaries.
[c1-l047 >] *mo dantes.*
[c1-l047 note] Mo completes como, yielding “as formerly.” The row ends the body of column one, not a catchword.

[c2-l001] Gotomecaxi, ſu, aita. *Fazer eſtrondo como*
[c2-l001 note] Gotomekashi is making a noise like gnawing. The specific comparison with a rat follows below.
[c2-l002 >] *quando o rato, &c. roe algua couſa.*
[c2-l002 note] The noise is that of a rat or similar creature gnawing something. The unmarked algua is retained because no secure tilde is established here.
[c2-l003] Gotomeqi, u, eita. *Fazerſe aquelle ſom, ou*
[c2-l003 note] Gotomeki is the corresponding occurrence of that gnawing sound. The Portuguese construction is intransitive, fazerſe.
[c2-l004 >] *eſtrondo.*
[c2-l004 note] Estrondo completes the sound definition. This single-word continuation is genuine body text.
[c2-l005] Gotoqino. *Tal, ou qual.*
[c2-l005 note] Gotokino is an attributive expression meaning “such” or “of that kind,” matched by Portuguese tal ou qual.
[c2-l006] Gôuacu. Xiuaza madô. i, Acugô. *Malda-*
[c2-l006 note] Gowaku concerns evil deeds, with akugo as an equivalent. The gloss begins maldade; all three printed circumflexes are retained.
[c2-l007 >] *de, ou màs obras.*
[c2-l007 note] De completes maldade, followed by “evil works.” The source's grave-accent spelling màs is not modernized.
[c2-l008] Gouǒ. *Certa mezinha.*
[c2-l008 note] Goo is identified only as a certain medicine. The concise source does not warrant adding a composition or medical claim.
[c2-l009] Gouǒ. *Figura de aue, ou animal eſcrita, ou*
[c2-l009 note] This separate Goo is an image of a bird or animal, drawn or printed. The following lines describe its use as a religious protective paper.
[c2-l010 >] *impreßa em papel cõ outras letras queſe dà nas*
[c2-l010 note] The image is printed on paper with other writing and distributed at temples. The source's joined queſe and nasal abbreviation cõ are preserved.
[c2-l011 >] *Teras à gente como nomina, &c.*
[c2-l011 note] People receive it at temples as a nomina, a written charm. Teras is a Japanese-derived temple designation embedded in the Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l012] Gouǒyen. *Mezinha muito vſada em Iapão.*
[c2-l012 note] Gooen is described as a medicine widely used in Japan. This records the dictionary's statement without adding ingredients or a claim of efficacy.
[c2-l013] Goxe. Nochino yo. *Mundo futuro. ¶* Go-
[c2-l013 note] Gose is the future world or next life, paraphrased nochi no yo. Go- starts the example continued on the following physical row.
[c2-l014 >] xe bodaino tameni jengonuo naſu. *Fa-*
[c2-l014 note] Gose bodai no tame ni zengon wo nasu describes doing good works for enlightenment in the next life. The scan clearly reads tameni, not OCR rameni; Fa- starts fazer.
[c2-l015 >] *zer obras que aprouetem pera a ſaluação.*
[c2-l015 note] Zer completes fazer: doing works that profit salvation. The printed aprouetem is retained, while ſaluação corrects the f/ſ confusion. The text has ordinary continuation indentation; the dark blot to its left is not a letter or a reason to make the row flush left.
[c2-l016] Goxeccu. *Cinco tempos em que de obrigação*
[c2-l016 note] Gosekku introduces five occasions requiring a formal observance. The scan supports the cedilla in obrigação, absent from the OCR draft.
[c2-l017 >] *ſe faz reuerencia, & viſita ao ſenhor da ter*
[c2-l017 note] The required observance is paying respects and visiting the territorial lord. Ter continues into terra without a supplied line-end hyphen.
[c2-l018 >] *ra em cinco meſes do anno que he ao prin*
[c2-l018 note] These visits fall in five months of the year. Prin begins principio; neither this split nor the preceding terra split requires an unprinted hyphen.
[c2-l019 >] *cipio do anno no primeiro mes, ao terceiro dia*
[c2-l019 note] The enumeration begins with the start of the year in the first month, then the third day of the third month. The source's wording is retained rather than harmonized with a modern festival list.
[c2-l020 >] *da terceira lũa, ao quinto dia da quinta lũa, ao*
[c2-l020 note] The third-day reference is completed, followed by the fifth day of the fifth lunar month. Lua here counts months in the calendar being described.
[c2-l021 >] *ſeptimo dia da ſeptima lũa, aos noue dias da*
[c2-l021 note] The next occasions are the seventh day of the seventh month and the ninth day of the ninth month. The latter month is supplied below.
[c2-l022 >] *nona lũa.*
[c2-l022 note] “Ninth month” completes the five-occasion enumeration. The short row is part of the same long Goxeccu entry.
[c2-l023] Gǒxei. Tçuyoi xei, l, tçuyoi iqiuoi. *Grã-*
[c2-l023 note] Gosei denotes great strength or impetus, explained by tsuyoi sei and tsuyoi ikioi. The printed caron is clear in the headword.
[c2-l024 >] *de força, & impeto.*
[c2-l024 note] De completes grande, yielding “great force and impetus.” The nasal abbreviation above grã on the preceding row is preserved.
[c2-l025] Goxen. i, Vtano sǒxi. *Hũ liuro de verſos.*
[c2-l025 note] Gosen is a book of poems, uta no soshi. The Japanese equivalent uses the printed short s in sǒxi; it is not regularized to long ſ.
[c2-l026] Goxi. *Liuro de arte militar.*
[c2-l026 note] Goshi is identified as a book on military art. The short definition does not itself give enough information to add an expanded title.
[c2-l027] Gǒxi. *Eſcudelas de pao, ou goquis. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l027 note] This Goshi denotes wooden bowls, also called goquis in the Portuguese explanation. A second vessel sense begins after Item.
[c2-l028 >] *Hum vaſo de cobre como Goqui, em que en-*
[c2-l028 note] The second sense is a copper vessel resembling a Goqui. Goqui is an embedded Japanese comparison term; en- continues into entornão below.
[c2-l029 >] *tornão agoa no Chanoyu.*
[c2-l029 note] The copper vessel receives water poured out during chanoyu. The embedded Chanoyu is retained in the printed italic explanation.
[c2-l030] Goxi, ſuru, ita. *Eſperar. Vt, Sanquaino*
[c2-l030 note] Goshi gives principal forms of a verb meaning to wait or expect. Sanquaino starts a Japanese example and is locally printed italic, unlike its roman continuation; it is not an embedded Portuguese term for the separate font-toggle inventory.
[c2-l031 >] tçuideuo goſu. *Eſpero por conjunção em*
[c2-l031 note] Sankwai no tsuide wo gosu expresses awaiting an occasion to meet. The Portuguese begins “I await a conjunction or opportunity in which...”
[c2-l032 >] *que nos vejamos. As vezes prometer, ou fa-*
[c2-l032 note] The opportunity is one in which the speakers see each other. The entry then adds the senses of promising or making an arrangement.
[c2-l033 >] *zer concerto.* S.
[c2-l033 note] Zer completes fazer concerto, making an agreement or arrangement. Final S. is an upright written-usage label.
[c2-l034] Goxin. i, Firune. *Sono de meo dia.* S.
[c2-l034 note] Goshin is a midday nap, hirune. The short Portuguese definition is followed by the roman S. usage label.
[c2-l035] Goxin. Itçutçu caraxi. *Cinco eruas, ou ra-*
[c2-l035 note] This Goshin means five pungent plants, itsutsu karashi. The Portuguese includes herbs or roots, with ra- continued below.
[c2-l036 >] *izes que requeimão. V. G.* Nira, ninnicu,
[c2-l036 note] The roots or herbs have a burning, pungent taste. Nira and ninniku begin the Japanese list; V. G. is wholly italic explanatory apparatus.
[c2-l037 >] fitomoji, &c.
[c2-l037 note] Hitomoji completes the examples of pungent plants. It is part of a standalone Japanese list, not a Japanese word embedded in a Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l038] Goxiqi. Itçutçuno iro. *Cinco cores. ſ.*
[c2-l038 note] Goshiki is five colors, itsutsu no iro. The small italic ſ. introduces the enumeration; it is not an uppercase written-usage S. label.
[c2-l039 >] Xǒ, uǒ, xacu, biacu, cocu. *Azul, A-*
[c2-l039 note] The Japanese color names are followed by Portuguese equivalents beginning blue and yellow. The printed romanization and long-vowel marks are retained without changing them to modern readings.
[c2-l040 >] *marelo, vermelho, branco, preto. ¶* Go-
[c2-l040 note] Marelo completes Amarelo; the other colors are red, white, and black. The point after vermelho has a descending tail and is read as a comma; Go- starts the following example.
[c2-l041 >] xiqino ito. *Retros de cores.*
[c2-l041 note] Goshiki no ito denotes colored silk thread. The Portuguese retros is a thread term here, not a directional adjective.
[c2-l042] Goxo. *Carta do Cubǒ, de Yacata, ou ſenhor*
[c2-l042 note] Gosho is a letter from the kubo, a yakata, or another noble lord. The scan supports Yacata, not Yaceta, and a caron in Cubǒ; the titles remain inside the italic Portuguese explanation.
[c2-l043 >] *nobre. ¶* Goxo chǒdai tçucamatçuri
[c2-l043 note] The example begins a respectful expression for receiving such a letter. Goxo corrects OCR Gioxo; the mark in chǒdai is read as a caron from the surviving strokes.
[c2-l044 >] ſoro. *Receber carta do ſenhor da Tenca,*
[c2-l044 note] Soro completes the deferential Japanese sentence. The translation describes receiving a letter from the ruler of the realm, expressed through embedded Tenca.
[c2-l045 >] *ou de outra peßoa nobre.* S.
[c2-l045 note] The letter may alternatively be from another noble person. The final S. is an upright usage label.
[c2-l046] Goxǒ. Nochino vmare. *Vida fu tura, ou*
[c2-l046 note] Gosho is future birth or life, nochi no umare. The damaged Portuguese word begins with f, not long ſ; the conspicuous internal gap in fu tura is retained after enlargement. It is understood as futura, but its separation remains a qualified diplomatic choice.
[c2-l047 >] *outro mundo. ¶ Item, Saluaçao. Vt,*
[c2-l047 note] The definition adds the other world and salvation, then introduces an example continued on the next page. No secure nasal mark appears over the ending of Saluaçao, so the unmarked form is retained. The catchword below is separate furniture.
