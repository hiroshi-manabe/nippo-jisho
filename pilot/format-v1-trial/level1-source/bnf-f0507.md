[c1-l001] Tôchincǒ. *Hũs grãosſinhos pretos feios de va-*
[c1-l001 note] Tōchinkō names small black medicinal pellets. The scan supports grãosſinhos, correcting the corrupt diminutive; feios is retained as printed rather than supplying the tempting t of feitos. The last syllable begins varias on the next row.
[c1-l002 >] *rias confeições medicinaes.*
[c1-l002 note] Completes varias confeições medicinaes: the pellets consist of various medicinal preparations. The tilde belongs to confeições, and the physical division after va- is retained.
[c1-l003] Tochitochito. *Aduerb. Vide,* Tochime-
[c1-l003 note] Tochitochito is labelled an adverb and cross-referenced to tochimeki. The Japanese cross-reference begins with a roman T; the OCR incorrectly included that letter inside the italic apparatus.
[c1-l004 >] qi, qu.
[c1-l004 note] Qi completes Tochimeqi, followed by its principal form qu. This short physical row finishes the cross-reference and does not constitute a new entry.
[c1-l005] Tochǒ. *Cortinas que ſe poem diante do altar, ou*
[c1-l005 note] Tochō denotes curtains placed before an altar. The caron is visible, and the Portuguese description continues with other places where such curtains are hung.
[c1-l006 >] *da capella, ou de outro lugar ẽ caſas de nobres.*
[c1-l006 note] The curtain may also stand before a chapel or another place in noble houses. The abbreviated ẽ is retained without expanding it to em.
[c1-l007] Tochǔ. l, tochǔni. Michino naca. *Meo*
[c1-l007 note] Tochū and tochūni are explained by michi no naka, the middle of the road or journey. Latin l introduces the alternative form; neither Japanese phrase is embedded in a Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l008 >] *do caminho, ou caminho.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes meo do caminho, then allows caminho itself as a gloss. This is a continuation of tochū rather than a separate road-name.
[c1-l009] Tǒchǔ. Ataraxiqi chǔxet. *Seruiços feitos*
[c1-l009 note] Tōchū is paraphrased as atarashiki chūsetsu, newly rendered loyal service. The visible r in Ataraxiqi corrects the OCR repetition Atataxiqi; the Portuguese feitos is clear here.
[c1-l010 >] *de nouo, ou dagora. S.*
[c1-l010 note] The services are newly performed or of the present time. The final upright S. marks written usage and is separated typographically from the italic gloss.
[c1-l011] Tocǒ. *Adu.* l, tocacu. *Aßi, ou de qual-*
[c1-l011 note] Tokō, with tokaku as the alternative, is adverbial: thus, or in any manner. The divided qual- continues to qualquer below.
[c1-l012 >] *quer maneira. Vt,* Tocǒ mǒſuni voyoba-
[c1-l012 note] Tokō mōsu ni oyobanu means there is no need to speak further in this or that fashion. The negative ending nu is carried over to the next physical row.
[c1-l013 >] nu. *Não ha pera que falar mais de qualquer*
[c1-l013 note] Nu finishes oyobanu, and the Portuguese begins the explanation that there is no reason to say more. Its final qualquer continues into maneira below.
[c1-l014 >] *maneira que ſeja. ¶* Tocǒ ſuru mani fi-
[c1-l014 note] After the first translation closes, tokō suru ma ni hi- begins another example, about time passing while one is occupied in miscellaneous activity. The terminal fi- is the first part of the daylight expression continued below.
[c1-l015 >] ga cureta. *Em quanto homem deſcuidada-*
[c1-l015 note] Ga cureta completes hi ga kureta, the day having grown dark. The Portuguese starts the circumstantial clause about being carelessly occupied, with deſcuidada- divided across the row.
[c1-l016 >] *mente ſe ocupa aßi, ou aßi, ou em não ſei que*
[c1-l016 note] Completes descuidadamente and describes being occupied in this way or that, or in something unspecified. The extra s in OCR aßsi is unsupported; both visible expressions are retained as aßi.
[c1-l017 >] *o ſol ſe pos, ou ſe fez noite.*
[c1-l017 note] The sun set, or night came: this completes the Portuguese rendering of hi ga kureta. A visible word gap separates ſol and ſe.
[c1-l018] Tôcô. Forano cuchi. *Boca da cauerna, ou lapa.*
[c1-l018 note] Tōkō is explained by hora no kuchi, the mouth of a cave or grotto. Both long-o marks in the headword point upward and are retained as circumflexes.
[c1-l019] Toco. *Baileu, ou catre de dormir.*
[c1-l019 note] Toko is a sleeping platform or bedstead, rendered by baileu and catre de dormir. This meaning is distinct from the ornamental alcove platform in the next entry.
[c1-l020] Toco. *Hũa maneira de baileu, ou degrao que*
[c1-l020 note] The second toko is a kind of raised platform or step. The scan has Hũa, not the spurious internal i of OCR Hũia; the relative clause continues below.
[c1-l021 >] *està na cabeceira do Zaxiqu, onde poem rama-*
[c1-l021 note] The platform stands at the head of a zashiki, where bouquets are placed. Zaxiqu is a Japanese room-name embedded within the Portuguese gloss; the OCR final a is actually the following comma.
[c1-l022 >] *lhetes, &c.*
[c1-l022 note] Lhetes completes ramalhetes, bouquets. The brief row closes the alcove-platform description and retains its separate physical line.
[c1-l023] Tocogamachi. *Faſquias, ou molduras que eſ-*
[c1-l023 note] Tokogamachi denotes the slats or mouldings of this raised platform. The Portuguese eſ- continues as estão on the next row.
[c1-l024 >] *tãc nas bordas deſte baileu.*
[c1-l024 note] Completes eſ- with the printed-looking tãc, describing mouldings at the platform’s edges. The last letter is open and c-shaped in this impression; although tão is expected linguistically, the aligned OCR tãc is retained with this nonblocking glyph uncertainty.
[c1-l025] Tocomuxi. *Perſouejo.*
[c1-l025 note] Tokomushi is a bedbug. The abraded Portuguese gloss is read Perſouejo: a faint inner stroke favours e over the OCR o, but the vowel after P remains uncertain. The historical remaining letters are preserved without substituting modern percevejo.
[c1-l026] Toconatçu. *Nome de hũa flor que ſempre hà.*
[c1-l026 note] Tokonatsu is a flower-name explained through its continual presence. The final mark in hà is a grave rather than the OCR tilde; the statement does not justify imposing a modern species-name.
[c1-l027 >] *¶ Item, per met, ſe diz de hum homem*
[c1-l027 note] The entry shifts to a figurative application to a person. Per met is the printed indication of this transferred sense, and the following relative clause supplies its content.
[c1-l028 >] *que quaſi ſempre està, ou parece da meſma*
[c1-l028 note] The person is always, or almost always seems, of the same age. The paired verbs being and appearing account for the figurative connection to the ever-present flower.
[c1-l029 >] *idade.*
[c1-l029 note] Idade completes da mesma idade, the apparently unchanging age of the person. It is a genuine short continuation of the preceding figurative definition.
[c1-l030] Toconatçuna. l, toconatçuno. *Couſa que*
[c1-l030 note] Tokonatsuna and tokonatsuno are alternative adjectival forms meaning constant or unchanging. The next lines explain the property and give trees as an example.
[c1-l031 >] *ſempre està da meſma maneira ſem ſe mudar,*
[c1-l031 note] Explains the adjectival forms as always remaining in the same state without changing. The repeated same-manner wording belongs to the definition, not an additional Japanese example.
[c1-l032 >] *como aruores que lhe não cae a folha, &c.*
[c1-l032 note] Evergreen trees whose leaves do not fall illustrate this constancy. The historical aruores and singular folha are retained without grammatical modernization.
[c1-l033] Tocono vmi. P. i, Namida. *Lagrimas.*
[c1-l033 note] Toko no umi is a poetic expression equated with namida, tears. P. is the upright poetic-use label; namida is a Japanese explanatory synonym rather than a term inside Portuguese prose.
[c1-l034] Tocoro. *Hũas raizes de erua amargoſas que*
[c1-l034 note] This tokoro names bitter roots of an herb, not the ordinary noun for a place. The visible u restores erua from OCR eraa; the definition continues with their use as food.
[c1-l035 >] *ſe comem.*
[c1-l035 note] The bitter roots are eaten. This short relative clause completes the botanical entry and must remain distinct from the following homographic headword.
[c1-l036] Tocoro. *Lugar.*
[c1-l036 note] The second tokoro means a place, directly matched by Portuguese lugar. The two adjacent tokoro entries have different senses and are both retained.
[c1-l037] Tocorode. *Por tanto, ou por quanto: por iſ-*
[c1-l037 note] Tokorode is explained as a consequential or causal connective: therefore, because, or for that reason. The Portuguese iſ- continues as iſſo on the next row.
[c1-l038 >] *ſo, ou ſendo aßi. Vt,* Cǒ aru tocorode.
[c1-l038 note] The gloss also permits sendo aßi, being so; kō aru tokorode supplies the Japanese example. Its caron is retained rather than regularized from modern vowel expectations.
[c1-l039 >] *Sendo aßi desta maneira, ou por quanto aßi*
[c1-l039 note] The Portuguese renders the example as things being this way, or because they are so. The latter clause continues to its verb on the next line.
[c1-l040 >] *he, &c.*
[c1-l040 note] He completes the preceding causal clause, because it is so. The brief ending belongs to the tokorode example rather than to the plural tokorodocoro entry below.
[c1-l041] Tocorodocoro. *Lugares, ou cada lugar.*
[c1-l041 note] Tokorodokoro means places or each place, preserving both plural and distributive interpretations given by the Portuguese.
[c1-l042] Tocorojichi. *Penhor que ſe toma noutra terra*
[c1-l042 note] Tokorojichi is a pledge or distraint taken in another territory. The worn initial has the surviving form of T, and toma corrects OCR Ioma; the next rows specify what is seized and why.
[c1-l043 >] *de fazenda, ou algũa peßoa que ahi foy ter*
[c1-l043 note] The pledge can consist of property or a person who has arrived in the other territory. The Portuguese person-word retains the printed ß-type spelling.
[c1-l044 >] *por algũ agrauo, ou diuida.*
[c1-l044 note] The taking of the pledge is occasioned by an injury or a debt. This closes the description of tokorojichi without importing a modern legal definition.
[c1-l045] Tocoroni. *He particula como de Datiuo junta*
[c1-l045 note] Tokoroni is described in the dictionary’s grammatical terminology as a particle resembling a dative. The following line specifies that it combines with verbs.
[c1-l046 >] *com verbos. Vt,* Mairǒzuru tocoroni. *A-*
[c1-l046 note] Mairōzuru tokoroni means when about to go; the prospective form mairōzuru is historical Japanese morphology. Portuguese A- begins Avendo across the physical division.
[c1-l047 >] *uendo deir, ou estando pera ir me veo ao encõ-*
[c1-l047 note] Completes Avendo: when obliged or about to go, someone came to meet me. The scan restores uendo, deir and veo ao encõ- from the corrupt OCR sequence; the compressed deir is retained as printed, and encontro continues below.
[c1-l048 >] *tro, ou me derão, &c.*
[c1-l048 note] Tro finishes encontro, and ou me derão adds the alternative that they gave something to the speaker. The line is indented like the preceding continuation, not flush with the headwords.
