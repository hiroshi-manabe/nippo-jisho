[c1-l001] Rin-a. Fayaxino caraſu. *Coruo, que anda*
[c1-l001 note] Hayashi no karasu identifies a crow of the woods. The internal hyphen in Rin-a separates the syllables and agrees with the preceding page's catchword.
[c1-l002 >] *no mato, ou boſque.*

[c1-l003] Rinbiǒ. *Doença como de engurria.*

[c1-l004] Rinbô. *Hum certo circulo de metal, de que di-*
[c1-l004 note] Rinbō first denotes a particular metal circle. Di- begins the verb introducing what books say about it.
[c1-l005 >] *zẽ varias couſas fabuloſas nos liuros. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l005 note] Completes dizem and reports that books tell various fabulous things about the object. Itẽ introduces another sense without expanding the abbreviation.
[c1-l006 >] *Hũa certa marca, ou diuiſa, que ſe pinta nos*
[c1-l006 note] The second sense is an emblem or device painted on cloth. Pinta has t, not the OCR's pinia.
[c1-l007 >] *vestidos, ou em outros panos como de armar,*
[c1-l007 note] Specifies garments and other fabrics, such as hangings, as surfaces carrying the emblem. The physical line continues into &c. below.

[c1-l008 note] Rinbō no maru is given as another expression for the same circular emblem. The circumflex is retained in the repeated form.
[c1-l009] Rinbocu. Fayaxino qi. *Aruoredo, ou boſq̃.*
[c1-l009 note] Hayashi no ki explains woodland trees or a grove. The Portuguese boſq̃ is an abbreviation and is not expanded in the transcription.
[c1-l010] Rinca. Fayaxino xita. *Ao pè, ou de baixo*
[c1-l010 note] Hayashi no shita means at the foot of or beneath a stand of trees. The noun governed by de baixo follows on the next row.
[c1-l011 >] *do aruoredo, ou boſque.*
[c1-l011 note] Completes the location beneath trees or woodland. It belongs to the first Rinca entry.
[c1-l012] Rinca. Tonarino iye. *Caſa vizinha.*
[c1-l012 note] This separate Rinca means a neighbouring house, as the Japanese tonari no ie makes explicit. The homographic forest entry above is not merged with it.
[c1-l013] Rincacu. Qirinno tçuno. *Corno de hum a-*
[c1-l013 note] Kirin no tsuno denotes the horn of the kirin. The Portuguese begins a comparison with another horned animal; a- continues animal below.
[c1-l014 >] *nimal como vnicorne, ou bada. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l014 note] Completes animal and compares it with a unicorn or bada. The source's comparisons are retained without asserting a zoological identity for the kirin.
[c1-l015 >] *per met. Couſa rara. Vt,* Tauonazaru co-
[c1-l015 note] Introduces the metaphorical sense 'a rare thing' and starts an example about a heart that does not bend. The Japanese co- continues kokoro below.
[c1-l016 >] coroua rincacu yorimo mare nari. *Cora-*
[c1-l016 note] Says that such a heart is rarer than a kirin's horn. Mare, not the OCR's mate, provides the comparative meaning; Cora- begins the Portuguese translation.
[c1-l017 >] *ção que ſe não dobra nem vira, he mais raroq̃*
[c1-l017 note] Completes coração and translates the unwavering heart as rarer than the animal's horn. The abbreviated q̃ remains attached to raro as in the aligned draft; it is not expanded to que.
[c1-l018 >] *o corno deſte animal.*
[c1-l018 note] Finishes the comparison with the horn of the animal discussed in Rincacu. This continuation does not introduce another headword.
[c1-l019] Rincan. Fayaxino aida. *Dentro do boſque,*
[c1-l019 note] Hayashi no aida locates something within the woods. The synonym aruoredo continues the Portuguese description on the following row.
[c1-l020 >] *ou aruoredo.*
[c1-l020 note] Completes the explanation of Rincan as being within a wood or stand of trees. The short row belongs to the preceding entry.
[c1-l021] Rinchô. Fayaxino tori. *Paßaro do boſque.*
[c1-l021 note] Hayashi no tori identifies a woodland bird. The circumflex in Rinchô is preserved, distinct from the caron in the following botanical headword.
[c1-l022] Rinchǒ. *Hũs crauinhos, ou fulas que vem no*
[c1-l022 note] The headword begins Rin-, not the OCR's Rui-. The Portuguese compares the plants to small carnations and describes when they appear; fulas is retained as printed rather than replaced with a modern botanical name.
[c1-l023 >] *prĩcipio da primauera. No Cami ſe diz,* Rin-
[c1-l023 note] Says that they appear at the beginning of spring and introduces the Cami regional name. The damaged opening word is read prĩcipio: the abbreviated nasal vowel and traces of the medial letters fit principio, but the middle is poorly inked. The name begins Rin- and continues below.
[c1-l024 >] chǒqe.

[c1-l026 >] *ou canaueal.*

[c1-l027] Rincǒ. *Ida do Rey a algũa parte.*

[c1-l028] Rindǒ. *Hũa certa erua.*

[c1-l029] Ringuẽ. Teivǒno cotoba. *Palaura de Rey.*
[c1-l029 note] Teiō no kotoba explains a king's word or utterance. Cotoba has t, not the OCR's r; the shortened nasal ending of Ringuẽ is retained.
[c1-l030] Ringǒ. Tonarino ſato. *Lugar, ou pouoação*
[c1-l030 note] Tonari no sato identifies a neighbouring settlement. The synonym is ſato, not ſaro, and the caron distinguishes this headword from the fruit Ringo below.
[c1-l031 >] *vizinha.*
[c1-l031 note] Supplies 'neighbouring' to complete the preceding description of a settlement. The word is fully within the crop, with the right margin visible.
[c1-l032] Ringo. *Hũa fruita como maçaãs pequenas.*
[c1-l032 note] Ringo is compared to small apples. The scan has initial R rather than B; the historical Portuguese maçaãs is preserved without modernizing its vowel sequence.
[c1-l033] Ringocu. Tonarino cuni. *Reino vizinho.*
[c1-l033 note] Tonari no kuni explains a neighbouring realm. The initial R was dropped by OCR but is fully visible at the left of the native crop.
[c1-l034] Ringono qi. *Aruore que dà hũa fruita como*
[c1-l034 note] Ringo no ki is the tree that bears the fruit compared to small apples above. The Portuguese comparison continues on the next row.
[c1-l035 >] *maçãozinhas.*
[c1-l035 note] Completes the fruit comparison with the historical diminutive maçãozinhas. The unusual vowel spelling is legible and is not normalized to a modern form.
[c1-l036] Ringua. *Hora de dormir.*
[c1-l036 note] Ringua is glossed as the time for sleeping. It is a distinct entry between the apple tree and the reluctance-to-give entry.
[c1-l037] Rinjacu. Voximi voximu. *O ter difficulda-*
[c1-l037 note] Oshimi oshimu describes reluctance or begrudging. The Portuguese begins 'having difficulty' and continues difficulda-de below; the printed Japanese form is retained rather than regularized.
[c1-l038 >] *de de dar, ou emprestar algũa couſa. ¶* Go-
[c1-l038 note] Completes the definition as reluctance to give or lend something. Go- is the honorific opening of the following Japanese request, not part of the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l039 >] rinjacu naqu tamauaru bequ ſoro. *Peço*
[c1-l039 note] Completes Go-rinjacu naqu, asking for something to be granted without reluctance. Peço begins the Portuguese translation on this same physical row.
[c1-l040 >] *vos que me cõcedais iſto ſem repugnãcia, & dò.*
[c1-l040 note] Finishes the request that the addressee grant the thing without reluctance or regret. The nasal abbreviations and the grave accent in dò are preserved.
[c1-l041] Rinjet. *Recado, ou nouas que contão acrecẽ-*
[c1-l041 note] Rinjet denotes a report or news embellished by the teller. The nasal abbreviation and line-final division in acrecẽ- are retained.
[c1-l042 >] *tãdo de ſi varias couſas. Vt,* Rinjetuo yǔ.
[c1-l042 note] Completes the description of adding details of one's own and gives rinjet o iu as the example. The object marker is printed uo, and yǔ retains its caron.
[c1-l043] Rinji. *Obra, ou ſeruiço, que ſe impoem, ou*
[c1-l043 note] Rinji is additional work or service imposed on someone. The Portuguese definition continues across the next row.
[c1-l044 >] *acrecenta a alguem alem do custumado.*
[c1-l044 note] Specifies that the work is added beyond what is customary. The source's acrecenta and custumado are retained rather than modernized.
[c1-l045 >] *¶* Rinjino quayacu. *Officio, ou obra obri-*
[c1-l045 note] Rinji no quayacu is an example of an extra obligatory duty. The damaged n in Rinjino was mistaken for ù; comparison with the headword and its visible strokes supports n.
[c1-l046 >] *gatoria, que ſe acrecenta deſta maneira alem do*
[c1-l046 note] Completes obrigatoria and says that this obligation is added beyond the normal work. The row continues into the next one, with no missing text at its right edge.
[c1-l047 >] *que hum custuma fazer.*
[c1-l047 note] Finishes the description of work beyond what a person ordinarily does. Custuma remains in its historical spelling.
[c1-l048] Rinjina coto. *Idem.*
[c1-l048 note] Rinjina coto is referred back to the preceding sense. Several poorly inked letters were lost in the OCR's Fnjna, but the initial R and the full sequence Rinjina can be recovered from the native line.

[c2-l004 note] Completes xǒnen and its explanation as ending or dying well. The Japanese fragment is roman, while the Portuguese gloss is italic.

[c2-l006] Rinqi. Voximu cocoro. *Ciumes que hum tẽ*

[c2-l007 >] *de outro ſer amado, ou fauorecido. ¶* Rin-
[c2-l007 note] Completes the reason for the jealousy and begins the verbal expression Rin-qi suru. The final hyphen belongs to the Japanese example.
[c2-l008 >] qi ſuru. *Ter ciumes.*
[c2-l008 note] Completes rinki suru, 'to be jealous'. The long s in ſuru is retained as a graphic form, not treated as f.
[c2-l009] Rinri. Tonarino ſato. *Lugar vizinho, ou*
[c2-l009 note] Rinri, explained by tonari no sato, means a neighbouring place or settlement. The very weak penultimate letter is chosen as r from its low rightward trace and the lexical context, also supported by the NINJAL entry; damage leaves r versus the draft's i a nonblocking uncertainty.
[c2-l010 >] *pouoação que eſtà perto.*
[c2-l010 note] Completes the gloss as a settlement that is nearby. The grave accent in eſtà is retained.
[c2-l011] Rinrin. *Vt,* Rinrinto xite. *Modo de eſtar*
[c2-l011 note] The example repeats rinrin in rinrin to shite, not the OCR's Ronrinto. The first sense describes a fresh or cool condition, whose object follows below.

[c2-l012 note] Completes the freshness sense and introduces a second sense describing the sound of a voice or instrument. The terminal al- belongs to alguma across the line break.
[c2-l013 >] *gũa voz, ou inſtrumento muſico.*
[c2-l013 note] Completes the sounding voice or musical instrument. Inſtrumento has t, not the OCR's i in inſirumento; the long s is preserved.
[c2-l014] Rintan. i, Tonari. *Caſa, ou lugar vizi*
[c2-l014 note] Tonari explains a neighbouring house or place. The word vizinho divides after vizi without a visible terminal hyphen, so none is supplied.
[c2-l015 >] *nho. S.*
[c2-l015 note] Completes vizinho and retains the source's S. label. This abbreviated label is not expanded or made a separate entry.
[c2-l016] Rin v. Nagaame. *Chuua que dura muito.*
[c2-l016 note] Nagaame explains long-lasting rain. The visible separation in the headword Rin v is retained rather than joined to match the following example's Rinv.
[c2-l017 >] *Vt,* Rinv imada fare yarazu. *Ainda não*
[c2-l017 note] Says that the long rain has not yet cleared. The Portuguese negative is não, not the OCR's mão; Japanese fare represents hare in the supplied romanization.
[c2-l018 >] *eſteou agrande, & comprida chuua.*
[c2-l018 note] Completes the statement that the heavy, prolonged rain has not stopped. The historical eſteou and the draft's joined agrande are retained; no lexical modernization is introduced.
[c2-l019] Rinuocu. Tonarino iye. *Caſa vizinha.*
[c2-l019 note] Tonari no ie explains a neighbouring house. The headword begins R, not the OCR's B, and its historical vowel sequence is left intact.
[c2-l020] Rinxen. Teiuǒno vonvôxe. *Mandado del*
[c2-l020 note] Teiō no on-ōse denotes a royal command. Rinxen begins with R rather than B; the honorific von- in the synonym is retained as printed.
[c2-l021 >] *Rey.*

[c2-l022] Rinxi. *Prouiſão, ou patente del Rey.*

[c2-l023] Rinxô. *Sexta lũa, ou mes de Iapão. S.*
[c2-l023 note] Rinxô is identified as the sixth Japanese lunar month. The initial R and circumflex are visible; the S. label is retained without expansion.
[c2-l024] Rinye. Vauo meguru. i, Mayô. *Andar*
[c2-l024 note] Wa o meguru and mayou introduce wandering in a circle or going astray. The Portuguese develops the religious sense before contrasting an ordinary conversational use below.
[c2-l025 >] *errado no caminho da ſaluação, andando em*
[c2-l025 note] Continues the religious definition as straying from the path of salvation. This is the dictionary's explanatory framing, not an added doctrinal assertion.
[c2-l026 >] *continua roda de nacimentos, & transforma.*
[c2-l026 note] Describes a continuous cycle of births and transformations. The dot-like terminal mark after transforma is retained from the aligned draft rather than silently made a hyphen; the word nevertheless continues below.
[c2-l027 >] *çoens varias. Mas o ſentido ordinario he re-*

[c2-l028 >] *petição das meſmas couſas que hum ja deuia de*
[c2-l028 note] Completes repetição, the repetition of things one ought already to have forgotten. This damaged letter remains a nonblocking uncertainty, rather than being silently treated as fully legible.
[c2-l029 >] *ter eſquecido, ou não deuia de falar. Vt,*
[c2-l029 note] Adds the alternative of things one ought not to mention at all. Vt introduces the Japanese example on the next line.

[c2-l031 >] *& repetir o que hum deuia de calar para não*

[c2-l033] Rinyẽ. Sonono fayaxi. *Pomar, ou horta. S.*
[c2-l033 note] Sono no hayashi describes an orchard or garden. The tilde in Rinyẽ is retained and distinguishes its printed form from Rinyen on the next line.
[c2-l034] Rinyen. Fuchini nozomu. *O chegar, ou*

[c2-l036] Rinyocu. Vrocuzu, tçubaſa. *Peixes, &*

[c2-l037 note] Completes the pair 'fish and birds' and retains its S. label. It belongs to Rinyocu, not to the following seating entry.
[c2-l038] Rinza. Tonarino za. *Aſſento perto, ou*

[c2-l040] Rinzǒ. *Hũa maneira de tabernaculo, que fa-*
[c2-l040 note] Rinzō begins an explanation of a revolving repository or cabinet. The Portuguese fa- continues fazem on the next line.
[c2-l041 >] *zem andar à roda como eſtante, que ſerue de*
[c2-l041 note] Completes fazem and describes making the repository rotate like a bookstand. Its function as a library or book repository is specified below.

[c2-l042 note] Says that the revolving repository serves as a library in the temples described by the source as gentile. Liuraria has a dotted i, not nasal ũ. Teras is the embedded temple term. The upright T and less decisive sloping lowercase forms lead to retention of the draft's T/eras font boundary, with a nonblocking typography caveat.
[c2-l043] Riǒ. l, reǒ. *Cubiculo.*
[c2-l043 note] Riǒ and reǒ are alternative forms for a small room or chamber. The lowercase l is the source's vel marker, not a numeral or a bar.
[c2-l044] Riǒ. l, reǒ. Futatçu. *Vt,* Riǒno mimi.
[c2-l044 note] This separate entry means two or both, clarified by futatsu. Riǒ no mimi gives the example of both ears; it is not merged with the homographic room entry.
[c2-l045 >] *Ambas as orelhas. ¶* Riǒno te. *Am-*
[c2-l045 note] Translates both ears and adds riǒ no te, both hands. The whole Japanese example is roman; the draft had mistakenly included its initial R within the Portuguese italic span.
[c2-l046 >] *bas as mãos.*
[c2-l046 note] Completes Ambas as mãos, the translation of both hands. The abbreviation-free nasal vowel and the physical division Am-bas are retained.
[c2-l047] Riǒan. *Eſcuridades, ou treuas. ¶ Item,*
[c2-l047 note] Riǒan first means darkness or gloom. Item introduces a further sense beyond this page; the catchword is separate page furniture, not a continuation merged into this body row.
