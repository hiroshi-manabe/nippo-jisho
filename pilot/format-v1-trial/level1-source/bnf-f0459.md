[c1-l001] Suri. *Ladrão, ou corta bolſas.*
[c1-l001 note] Suri is a thief or pickpocket; corta bolſas literally describes a purse-cutter. This noun is distinct from the rubbing verb immediately below.
[c1-l002] Suri, u, utta. *Esfregar. ¶* Teuo ſuru.
[c1-l002 note] The verb means rubbing, with the printed principal part utta containing two t types. Te wo suru, rubbing the hands together, introduces the gesture glossed as asking forgiveness on the next row.
[c1-l003 >] *Pedir perdão. ¶ Item, Moer como tinta,*
[c1-l003 note] Rubbing or clasping the hands is explained as asking forgiveness. The next sense is grinding, with ink as an example; the second pass resolves the mark after Item as a comma.
[c1-l004 >] *mostarda, &c. ¶* Fanni ſuru, l, fan-
[c1-l004 note] Mustard continues the list of things ground. Han ni suru introduces printing, with the alternative hangi wo suru divided after fan-; the terminal hyphen is visible.
[c1-l005 >] guiuo ſuru. *Imprimir.*
[c1-l005 note] Guiuo completes fan-guiuo, the printing-block expression. Imprimir translates both Japanese alternatives as printing; the physical division is retained.
[c1-l006] Suriagari, u, atta. *Dormindo reuoluerſe pera*
[c1-l006 note] Suriagari describes shifting upward while asleep. The abbreviated principal parts remain as printed, rather than being expanded to full Japanese forms.
[c1-l007 >] *cima, ou eſtando deitado chegarſe pera cima.*
[c1-l007 note] Completes the movement upward: turning in sleep or moving higher while lying down. The Portuguese construction continues the same entry, not a separate sense of standing up.
[c1-l008] Suriague, uru. *Fazer mai curta a Catana*
[c1-l008 note] Suriague concerns shortening a sword. The final letter of expected mais is severely abraded: the provisional mai is retained rather than silently supplying a clear s. Catana is the Japanese weapon name embedded in the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l009 >] *limando o ferro polla parte dos cabos. Vt,*
[c1-l009 note] The sword is shortened by filing its iron at the hilt end. Vt introduces the following Japanese example; polla is retained as the historical contraction.
[c1-l010 >] Catanauo ſuriaguru. *¶ Item, Permet.*
[c1-l010 note] Katana wo suriaguru illustrates the preceding sword operation. The scan also has Item between the paragraph sign and Permet.; the draft omitted this transition to an extended sense.
[c1-l011 >] *Aleuantar pera cima hũa couſa que ſe abaixou*
[c1-l011 note] The extended sense is raising something that has slipped downward. Aleuantar and the printed abbreviation hũa remain unmodernized.
[c1-l012 >] *do lugar donde eſtaua. ¶* Vô a couo ſu-
[c1-l012 note] Completes the reference to the object's former position. The damaged example Vô a couo is interpretable as the child being carried, probably intended Vôta couo; the missing internal letter is not securely legible and the visible spacing is retained. Su- continues the lifting verb below.
[c1-l013 >] riaguru. *Aleuantar pera cima a criança q̃*
[c1-l013 note] Riaguru completes suriaguru. The Portuguese says to lift the child higher; the abbreviated q̃ introduces the relative clause on the next row.
[c1-l014 >] *ſe traz às coſtas. ¶* Suriagatte. l, ſuri
[c1-l014 note] The child is carried on the back. Suriagatte, not the draft Suciagatte, then contrasts moving upward with surisagatte below; the damaged medial letters are supported by the earlier verb and the visible r/g types. No terminal hyphen is supplied after ſuri.
[c1-l015 >] ſagatte ne o. *Do mi chegando vos pera ci-*
[c1-l015 note] Sagatte completes surisagatte. The Japanese command concerns sleeping higher or lower; expected neyo and Portuguese Dormi are abraded, leaving the provisional ne o and Do mi. Their meaning is clear from the paired directions, but the gaps are not silently filled.
[c1-l016 >] *ma, ou chegando pera baixo.*
[c1-l016 note] Ma completes ci-ma, upward; the alternative is moving lower. This closes the sleeping-position example and the long suriague entry.
[c1-l017] Suriai, ǒ, ota. *Roçarſe hũa couſa com outra.*
[c1-l017 note] Suriai is mutual rubbing or chafing, not the malformed Surini of the draft. The gloss says one thing rubs against another. The first o has a caron; the damaged following ota is retained without importing the second accent from NINJAL.
[c1-l018] Suribachi. *Gral de barro, ou de pedra.*
[c1-l018 note] Suribachi is a mortar of earthenware or stone. Gral is the historical Portuguese noun, not a malformed modern spelling.
[c1-l019] Suricha. *Cha moido.*
[c1-l019 note] Suricha is ground tea. The initial matches the surrounding capital S types rather than B; Cha is italic here as part of the Portuguese gloss.
[c1-l020] Surichatçubo. *Boyẽzinho onde metem Cha*
[c1-l020 note] Surichatsubo is a small vessel for ground tea. The damaged middle of the headword belongs to cha-tçubo, confirmed by the surviving types and NINJAL. Boyẽzinho denotes the little jar; the medial vowel is weak. No hyphen is visible after Cha.
[c1-l021 >] *moido.*
[c1-l021 note] Moido completes Cha moido, the ground tea put in the jar. It is a separate short physical continuation, not another headword.
[c1-l022] Suricobachi. *Gral de barro, ou de pedra.*
[c1-l022 note] Surikobachi again denotes an earthenware or stone mortar. Barro is abraded but its remaining types and the repeated gloss above support it, rather than the OCR fragments la ro.
[c1-l023] Suricocuri, ru, cutta. *Alinpar eſfregando*
[c1-l023 note] Surikokuru means cleaning by rubbing vigorously. The weak doubled t of cutta is supported by the visible principal-part traces and the following te-form; NINJAL supplies independent lexical confirmation, not the typography.
[c1-l024 >] *rijo. ¶* Miuo ſuricocutte arǒ. *Lauar-*
[c1-l024 note] Rijo supplies the vigorous manner. Mi wo surikokutte arau means washing oneself by scrubbing; the scan supports the doubled t and arǒ, correcting ſuricocurte atǒ. The final Lauar- continues below.
[c1-l025 >] *ſe eſfregando rijo.*
[c1-l025 note] Completes lavar-se: washing oneself while rubbing hard. The small missing-looking letter in the draft rjo is the visible i of rijo.
[c1-l026] Suricogui. *Meliùs,* fachinomi. *Mão dogral.*
[c1-l026 note] Suricogui is a pestle, literally the hand of the mortar, and fachinomi is offered as the preferred name. Enlarged second-pass inspection shows a grave accent on the u of italic Meliùs; dogral retains the printed joining.
[c1-l027] Suricudaqi, qu, aita. *Moer como moſtarda,*
[c1-l027 note] Surikudaki means grinding or crushing, with mustard as the example. The headword starts with S, not B, and its abbreviated aita ending is retained as printed.
[c1-l028 >] *&c. ¶* Caneuo ſuricudaite maqiyeni
[c1-l028 note] Kane wo surikudaite makie ni begins making powdered metal into decorative lacquer work. The final particle is ni, with n rather than the draft m; the phrase continues with suru below.
[c1-l029 >] ſuru. *Fazendo ouro, ou prata, ou latão ẽ.*
[c1-l029 note] Suru completes the Japanese example. The Portuguese lists gold, silver, and brass reduced to powder; the abbreviated ẽ leads into poo below. The small terminal mark remains a provisional dot as in the aligned draft, not a hyphen inferred from the continuation.
[c1-l030 >] *poo, pintar, ou fazer lauores, & folhagẽs.*
[c1-l030 note] Poo completes the powder expression; the resulting metal is used for painting or ornamental and foliage designs. Folhagẽs has initial f, not long s, supported by its cross-stroke and the decorative context.
[c1-l031] Suricuzzu. *Limaduras.*
[c1-l031 note] Surikuzu is filings or the particles produced by abrasion. The abraded Portuguese middle belongs to Limaduras, not Limadaras.
[c1-l032] Surifon. *Liuros impreßos.*
[c1-l032 note] Surifon denotes printed books, directly matched by Liuros impreßos. The f represents the historical Japanese h-row sound; it is not a long-s error.
[c1-l033] Surimigaqi, qu, gaita. *Eſfregando alimpar,*
[c1-l033 note] Surimigaki means cleaning by rubbing or polishing. The visible mi syllable and capital S correct the malformed Burimgaqi; the abbreviated gaita is preserved.
[c1-l034 >] *ou polir principalmente couſas de metal.*
[c1-l034 note] The gloss continues with polishing, especially metal objects. This qualifies the preceding cleaning action rather than introducing a new headword.
[c1-l035] Suriǒ. *Muitos peſos como onças, &c.*
[c1-l035 note] Suriǒ denotes multiple units of weight, compared with ounces. The caron is visible; the gloss concerns quantities of weight, not the rubbing verbs surrounding it.
[c1-l036] Suriqiri, ru, qitta. *Cortar roçando, ou limã-*
[c1-l036 note] Surikiri means cutting through by rubbing or filing. Both t types in qitta are visible. The final limã- has a tilde and a printed continuation mark.
[c1-l037 >] *do. ¶ Permet.* Suriqitta fito. l, ſuri-
[c1-l037 note] Do completes limando. Permet introduces the extended description surikitta hito, alternatively surikiri, for a person whose resources are used up.
[c1-l038 >] qiri. *Homẽ que não tẽ ja que gaſtar de pobre.*
[c1-l038 note] Completes surikiri and explains a person so poor that he has nothing left to spend. Homẽ and tẽ carry abbreviation tildes; the damaged short word is ja, not the draft ta.
[c1-l039] Suritade, zzuru, eta. l, ſuritadeuo ſuru.
[c1-l039 note] Suritade is given both as a verb and as a noun with suru. The following ship-cleaning gloss identifies the operation; the printed zzuru is retained without morphological normalization.
[c1-l040 >] *Eſfregar, & lauar a fune de pois de chamus-*
[c1-l040 note] The operation is scrubbing and washing a boat after scorching it. The Japanese fune is embedded in the Portuguese explanation. Lauar replaces the garbled latar; de pois remains two printed words, and chamus- continues below.
[c1-l041 >] *cada, & queimado o bicho.*
[c1-l041 note] Cada completes chamuscada. The scorching has burned the wood-boring creature; the singular bicho is retained rather than expanded into a zoological identification.
[c1-l042] Suritçuzzumi. *Certo tabaquinho que ſe tange*
[c1-l042 note] Suritsuzumi is a small percussion instrument. The Portuguese tabaquinho and tange describe an instrument being played; its means of sounding is specified below.
[c1-l043 >] *com hum inſtrumento de pao.*
[c1-l043 note] The instrument is played using a wooden implement. Inſtrumento corrects the draft inſirumento. The local draft st reading is retained: the merged italic shapes do not justify a global short-s/long-s reclassification.
[c1-l044] Suriye. *Comer de paßaros que ſe da moido.*
[c1-l044 note] Suriye is ground bird food. Comer is used as a noun for food, and the abraded initial is read as capital S in keeping with its surviving curve, not the draft b.
[c1-l045] Suro. *Hũa laya de veſtido ſingelo de ſeda brã-*
[c1-l045 note] Suro is described as a single-layer or unlined garment of white silk. The visible headword is Suro; the Portuguese ſingelo is damaged but distinguishable from ſorgelo. Brã- continues branca.
[c1-l046 >] *ca adamaſcada.*
[c1-l046 note] Ca completes branca, and adamaſcada describes the silk as damasked. The weak c inside the word replaces the spurious OCR gap.
[c1-l047] Surudona. *Couſa aguda na ponta. ¶ Per*
[c1-l047 note] Surudona means sharp at the point. The paragraph sign begins Permet, which is physically divided across the columns as Per / met.; no hyphen is supplied. The headword is not Furona.

[c2-l001 >] *met.* Surudona fito. *Homem agudo, &*

[c2-l003] Surume. *Siba, ou chocos ſecos.*
[c2-l003 note] Surume is glossed with Portuguese names for dried cephalopods. Chocos has o, not the draft a; the historical identification is preserved without imposing a modern species distinction.
[c2-l004] Sururito. *Adu. Modo de fazer a couſa li-*
[c2-l004 note] Sururito is an adverb describing doing something lightly or readily. Its Portuguese ligeira is divided after li- at the edge.
[c2-l005 >] *geira, & expeditamente. Vt,* Catanauo
[c2-l005 note] Geira completes ligeira, paired with expeditamente. The example begins with katana wo, which is the object of drawing the sword on the following row.
[c2-l006 >] ſururito nuqu. *Arrancar a Catana ligei-*
[c2-l006 note] Sururito nuku means drawing the sword smoothly or swiftly. Catana occurs inside the Portuguese translation as well as the preceding Japanese example; ligei- continues below.
[c2-l007 >] *ramente ſem impedimento.*
[c2-l007 note] Ramente completes ligeiramente, and sem impedimento emphasizes lack of obstruction. This concludes the smooth-drawing example.
[c2-l008] Suruſuruto. *Adu. Idem. Vt,* Suruſu-
[c2-l008 note] Surusuruto is given the same adverbial sense as sururito. The damaged headword has the repeated suru sequence, not the broken Su uſuruto; the example is divided after Suruſu-.
[c2-l009 >] ruto faxiru. *Correr depreſſa, & ſem em-*
[c2-l009 note] Ruto completes surusuruto; hashiru supplies running quickly and without hindrance. The Portuguese is depreſſa, not depreſia; em- begins embaraço.
[c2-l010 >] *baraço.*
[c2-l010 note] Baraço completes embaraço, obstruction. The short physical line belongs to the running example above.
[c2-l011] Suſa. *Palha cortada miudamente pera miſturar*
[c2-l011 note] Susa is finely chopped straw prepared for mixing with clay. The gloss explains a building material, not a personal name.
[c2-l012 >] *com o barro.* X. *No Cami ſe diz,* Ca-
[c2-l012 note] The straw is mixed with clay. X is the upright regional label; the Portuguese then contrasts Cami usage and introduces kabe-wara across the line break. Cami is a Japanese regional term inside the explanation.
[c2-l013 >] beuara, l, Qiriuara.
[c2-l013 note] Beuara completes Cabewara; Qiriuara is the alternative term, introduced by Latin l. Both are the regional equivalents promised by se diz above.
[c2-l014] Suſamajij. *Couſa fria, & medonha. Vt,*
[c2-l014 note] Susamajii is described as cold and fearsome. The visible j before ij restores the adjective rather than the malformed Suſamaij; its example follows.
[c2-l015 >] Suſamajij nari. *Modo de eſtar com frio,*
[c2-l015 note] Susamajii nari describes an appearance or condition of coldness. The Portuguese continues after a comma, not the draft period, into the altered-complexion explanation.
[c2-l016 >] *ou com as cores mudadas, & alterado.*
[c2-l016 note] The complexion has changed and the person is disturbed or altered. This expands the preceding appearance gloss rather than defining a new Japanese expression.
[c2-l017 >] Suſamajiſa.
[c2-l017 note] Susamajisa is the nominal derivative, the corresponding coldness or fearsome quality. It is a separately printed form belonging to the preceding adjective.
[c2-l018 >] Suſamajǔ.
[c2-l018 note] Susamajǔ is the corresponding adverbial form. Its u carries a downward caron; the short row is intentional and not missing Portuguese text.
[c2-l019] Suſamaxij. *Idem.*
[c2-l019 note] Susamashii is referred to the preceding adjective by Idem. The x is the Japanese sh-series spelling, not an error to be normalized to j.
[c2-l020] Suſami, u. *Alienarſe, ou botar de ſi. Vt,*
[c2-l020 note] Susami is first explained as withdrawing or casting something away. The following examples distinguish the objects and emotional contexts of that withdrawal.
[c2-l021 >] Youo ſuſamu. *Dar de mão ao mundo.*
[c2-l021 note] Yo wo susamu means renouncing the world, rendered dar de mão ao mundo. Youo contains the noun yo and object particle, not an English-looking pronoun.
[c2-l022 >] *¶* Miuo ſuſamu. *Ter pouca conta conſigo*
[c2-l022 note] Mi wo susamu concerns taking little care of oneself. The Portuguese continues with self-mortification on the next physical row.
[c2-l023 >] *mortificandoſe, & abatendoſe, &c. ¶* Fi-
[c2-l023 note] Self-mortification and abasement complete the preceding gloss. The visible t restores mortificandoſe; Fi- begins hito in the next example.
[c2-l024 >] touo ſuſamu. *Alienarſe dalguem no amor,*
[c2-l024 note] Touo completes hito wo: withdrawing from another person in affection. The Portuguese extends this to dealings or intercourse on the following row.
[c2-l025 >] *trato, &c. Itẽ, Cõ* Furi, u. *Vt,* Ame
[c2-l025 note] Trato completes the interpersonal gloss. The apparatus then says that susamu is used with Furi, u; this cited Japanese verb is inside the explanatory apparatus, and Ame begins the rain example.
[c2-l026 >] furi ſuſamu. *Ceſſar a chuua. ¶* Ame
[c2-l026 note] Ame furi susamu is expressly glossed as rain ceasing. The u in furi, also supplied by the cited verb above, corrects fari. The next Ame begins a contrasting construction.
[c2-l027 >] ſuſamu. *Chouer rijo. ¶* Caje ſuſamu.
[c2-l027 note] Ame susamu is glossed as raining hard, whereas the preceding compound was rain ceasing. Caje susamu then introduces a wind example; the dictionary's contrast is preserved rather than reconciled by emendation.
[c2-l028 >] *Ventar rijo. ¶* Fanani ſuſamu. i, Fana-
[c2-l028 note] Ventar rijo translates the strong wind. Hana ni susamu is then paraphrased with hana wo aisuru across the line break, introducing enjoyment of flowers.
[c2-l029 >] uo aiſuru. *Folgar, ou gozarſe de ver as*
[c2-l029 note] Uo aiſuru completes the Japanese paraphrase. The Portuguese describes taking pleasure in seeing the flowers, not the harsh-weather sense above.
[c2-l030 >] *flores.* P.
[c2-l030 note] Flores completes the preceding explanation. P. is the upright poetry label closing the gloss, not part of the italic Portuguese sentence.
[c2-l031] Suſamiſute, tçuru, eta. *Deixar, ou lançar*
[c2-l031 note] Susamisute combines the preceding verb with throwing away or abandoning. The printed principal forms tçuru and eta are preserved.
[c2-l032 >] *de ſi com enfadamento algũa couſa.*
[c2-l032 note] Completes the definition: casting something away with displeasure or weariness. The tilde is over u in algũa.
[c2-l033] Suſaqi. *Ponta que ſae ao mar como coroa, ou*

[c2-l034 >] *baixo de area deſcuberto.*

[c2-l035] Suſo. *Partes inferiores como fralda, ou borda*
[c2-l035 note] Suso denotes lower parts or edges. The Portuguese adjective is inferiores, not the OCR infertores; garment hems begin the examples.

[c2-l036 note] The examples include a garment edge, the foot of a mountain, and a horse's feet. These illustrate the shared lower-part sense.
[c2-l037 >] *Vt,* Yamano ſuſo. *Raiz, ou pee do mõ-*
[c2-l037 note] Yama no suso is the base or foot of a mountain. Raiz is a figurative root or base here; mõ- is the printed abbreviated beginning of monte.
[c2-l038 >] *te. ¶* Qirumonono ſuſo. *Fralda, ou*

[c2-l039 >] *borda do veſtido de baixo. ¶* Vmano ſu-

[c2-l040 >] ſo. *Pees do caualo.*
[c2-l040 note] So completes uma no suso, expressly glossed as the horse's feet. Preserve this stated sense without narrowing it to a modern anatomical term.
[c2-l041] Suſotçugui. *Remẽdo das fraldas do veſtido.*
[c2-l041 note] Susotsugi is a patch or repair at the garment's hem. Remẽdo retains its printed abbreviation tilde.
[c2-l042] Suſouaqe. *Hum pouco, como reliquias de cou-*
[c2-l042 note] Susowake is a small share, like a remainder of something obtained. The Portuguese cou- continues cousas on the next row.
[c2-l043 >] *ſas que hum alcançou, ou lhe derão. ¶* Su-
[c2-l043 note] Sas completes cousas: things acquired or received as gifts. Su- starts the repeated Japanese headword in its verbal example.
[c2-l044 >] ſouaqeuo ſuru. l, itaſu. *Partir com ou-*
[c2-l044 note] Susowake wo suru, alternatively itasu, means sharing part with others. The polite alternative remains a separate roman Japanese form; ou- continues outros.
[c2-l045 >] *tros algũa couſa do q̃ lhe derão.*
[c2-l045 note] Tros completes outros. The person shares some of what was given to him; q̃ and algũa retain their printed tildes.
[c2-l046] Suſu. *Fulugem da chamine, ou da caſa que não*
[c2-l046 note] Susu is soot from a chimney, or from a house lacking one. The worn word chamine is supported by its repetition on the next row.
[c2-l047 >] *tem chamine.* Suſuuo faqu. *Tirar eſta fu*
[c2-l047 note] Tem completes the negative relative clause about a house without a chimney. Susu wo haku means sweeping away soot; Suſuuo and Tirar replace the garbled draft. The Portuguese fu continues as lugem on the next page; no line-end hyphen is visibly established.
