[c1-l001] Fucurobi, uru, ita. *Melius,* Focorobij.
[c1-l001 note] Fukurobi is referred to the preferred form fokorobi, 'come unstitched'. The italic word is Latin Melius, 'better', not the OCR's Melidu; it introduces a preferred lexical form, not a Portuguese definition.
[c1-l002 >] *Vide ſuprà.*
[c1-l002 note] Vide supra directs the reader back to the earlier fokorobi entry; the short continuation is editorial Latin.
[c1-l003] Fucurocu. Saiuai tamamono. *Riquezas,*
[c1-l003 note] Fukuroku (fortune and emolument) is glossed saiwai tamamono, blessings or gifts. Riquezas begins the Portuguese account of wealth and possessions.
[c1-l004 >] *& bens. Vt,* Fucurocu mini amaru. *Ter*
[c1-l004 note] Fukuroku mi ni amaru describes wealth exceeding one's needs or station. Ter continues into the next physical line; Vt introduces the Japanese example.
[c1-l005 >] *abundancia de riquezas, & bens temporaes.*
[c1-l005 note] The gloss specifies an abundance of riches and temporal goods, confirming material prosperity rather than a purely religious blessing.
[c1-l006] Fucurogu. *Criança que nace enuolta com as*
[c1-l006 note] Fukurogu denotes a child born enclosed in the birth membranes. The definition continues with parias on the next row; preserve nace rather than modernizing its spelling.
[c1-l007 >] *parias.*
[c1-l007 note] Parias completes the reference to the birth membranes or afterbirth. This is the end of the preceding definition, not an independent entry.
[c1-l008] Fucurogumo. *Aranhas que estão metidas em*
[c1-l008 note] Fukurogumo, literally bag-spiders, are spiders enclosed in a bag-like web. The Portuguese description establishes the compound's concrete sense.
[c1-l009 >] *algum bolſo, ou tea que ellas tem feito. No*
[c1-l009 note] The enclosure is a pocket or web made by the spiders themselves. No starts a regional qualification completed by Cami below.
[c1-l010 >] *Cami ſe diz,* Firagumo.
[c1-l010 note] In the Kami region the corresponding term is hiragumo. The scan separates Cami and se; Camr was an OCR error, not a regional name.
[c1-l011] Fucurozǒxi. *Liuro, ou eſcritura em papel do*
[c1-l011 note] Fukurozōshi is a book or writing on folded paper. The word dobrado is divided after do without an inferred hyphen.
[c1-l012 >] *brado que não tem eſcrito nada nas coſtas, ou*
[c1-l012 note] The paper is not written on its reverse. Coſtas and the following interior explain the blank inner surfaces created by folding.
[c1-l013 >] *no interior das folhas.*
[c1-l013 note] The blank interior of the leaves completes the explanation of the folded-paper book construction.
[c1-l014] Fucuſa. *Couſa branda, ou macia. Mas não*
[c1-l014 note] Fukusa describes something soft or gentle. The qualification beginning Mas não restricts its use to compounds rather than denying the definition.
[c1-l015 >] *ſe vſa ſem algũa compoſição. Vt,* Fucuſa-
[c1-l015 note] The dictionary says this form is not used without composition, and supplies fukusa-mono. Keep the printed line-end hyphen and the OCR-aligned tilde.
[c1-l016 >] mono, &c.
[c1-l016 note] Mono completes fukusa-mono, a soft object or cloth, illustrating the stated compound usage.
[c1-l017] Fucuſaguinu. *Sendal, ou pedaço de peça del*
[c1-l017 note] Fukusaginu is a soft fine cloth. Sendal names a light fabric; the scan supports pedaço, 'piece', rather than podaço. Del continues into delgada below.
[c1-l018 >] *gada, & branda pera a limpar algũas peças*
[c1-l018 note] The cloth is thin and soft, used to wipe certain objects. The final s in algũas was misrecognized as punctuation; it agrees with plural peças and is visible before the gap.
[c1-l019 >] *delicadas, & bem vruxadas como o Natçume*
[c1-l019 note] The objects are delicate and well lacquered: vruxadas derives from Japanese urushi. Natçume identifies the tea container; both it and the surrounding explanation are italic here.
[c1-l020 >] *do Chanoyu, &c.*
[c1-l020 note] Chanoyu supplies the tea-ceremony setting for the natsume and wiping cloth. The Japanese cultural term is italic within this Portuguese phrase.
[c1-l021] Fucuſana fito. *Permet. Homem brãdo, &facil.*
[c1-l021 note] Fukusa na hito extends softness metaphorically to a mild, easygoing person. Permet. is the printer's compact 'by metaphor' label, not part of the Japanese expression.
[c1-l022] Fucuſamiſo. *Miſo delido.*
[c1-l022 note] Fukusamiso is softened or dissolved miso, as Miſo delido explains. The italic miso is a Japanese word used inside the Portuguese definition.
[c1-l023] Fucuſamono. *Pano brãdo, & macio pera a-*
[c1-l023 note] Fukusamono is a soft cloth for cleaning. The word a-limpar crosses the row boundary; its hyphen is visible, unlike several unhyphenated divisions elsewhere on this page.
[c1-l024 >] *limpar os vaſes do Cha.*
[c1-l024 note] The cloth cleans the vessels used for tea. Retain vaſes and italic Cha as printed rather than changing the vessel noun or imposing roman type on a loanword.
[c1-l025] Fucutacu. *O florecer muito em riquezas.*
[c1-l025 note] Fukutaku means flourishing greatly in wealth. Florecer is the historical Portuguese form visible here and does not need an added s.
[c1-l026] Fucutçǔ. Farano itami. *Dor de barriga, ou*
[c1-l026 note] Fukutsū is glossed hara no itami, abdominal pain. Both the Japanese expansion and Portuguese dor de barriga agree; the caron over u is retained.
[c1-l027 >] *estumago.*
[c1-l027 note] The scan reads estumago, completing 'belly or stomach'. Preserve this vowel rather than normalizing to modern estômago or keeping OCR estamago.
[c1-l028] Fucutǒ. *Peixe aſsi chamado. Vide,* Fucu.
[c1-l028 note] Fukutō names the fish referred to under fuku. The Portuguese adverb has two s forms; the first-pass OCR omitted one. The cross-reference is Japanese and remains roman.
[c1-l029] Fucutocu. Saiuai ſaiuai. *Boa dita, & for-*
[c1-l029 note] Fukutoku denotes good fortune. The first expansion Saiuai is damaged at a; the remaining lower ink, following i, repeated saiwai and Portuguese good-fortune gloss support this qualified reading. The repetition is preserved, not silently repaired.
[c1-l030 >] *tuna proſpera. ¶* Fucutocuni vmareta fi-
[c1-l030 note] Fukutoku ni umareta hito means a person born fortunate. Fortuna proſpera completes the definition before the example begins.
[c1-l031 >] to. *Homẽ q̃ naceo bẽ afortunado, & ditoſo.*
[c1-l031 note] The Portuguese explicitly describes someone born fortunate and happy. The abbreviated Homẽ, q̃ and bẽ remain unexpanded in Level 1.
[c1-l032] Fucuxi, ſuru, ita. *Paßar, ou tornar a ver*
[c1-l032 note] Fukushi, suru, ita is review or repetition of learning. Paßar here means going over something, clarified by tornar a ver rather than physical passage.
[c1-l033 >] *o que hum aprendeo, ou ouuio. Vt,* Narai
[c1-l033 note] The material reviewed is what one learned or heard. Narai begins a Japanese maxim about learning without subsequent review.
[c1-l034 >] yomuto iyedomo, fucuxezareba, tonari
[c1-l034 note] Yomu to iyedomo, fukusezareba means 'although one reads, if one does not review'. Tonari belongs with no takara on the following line, the neighbor's treasure.
[c1-l035 >] no tacarauo cazôruga gotoxi. *Ainda q̃*
[c1-l035 note] Counting a neighbor's treasure is the comparison: knowledge not internalized brings no benefit. The contracted historical cazôru form is retained rather than expanded to a modern verb spelling.
[c1-l036 >] *aprenda, & lea ſe ſe não repete, & eſtuda*
[c1-l036 note] The gloss states that learning and reading are unprofitable without repetition and study. The repeated ſe has two grammatical functions and is not a duplicate to delete.
[c1-l037 >] *não a proueita nada: he como o que conta as ri*
[c1-l037 note] Learning without review is compared to counting another person's wealth. Retain the visible separation a proueita and the unhyphenated ri/quezas division rather than modernizing the typography.
[c1-l038 >] *quezas de ſeu vizinho. ¶* Sorani mono
[c1-l038 note] After the neighbor's riches, sorani mono begins an example of recalling something by heart. The worn vowel in Sorani is supplied by the remaining type and the memory gloss, not treated as an empty space.
[c1-l039 >] uo fucuſuru. *Paßar algũa couſa polla memo*
[c1-l039 note] Sora ni mono wo fuku suru means going over something from memory. The printed fucuſuru joins the nominal element to suru; do not insert an analytical word boundary.
[c1-l040 >] *ria ſem ver liuro.*
[c1-l040 note] Sem ver liuro explicitly excludes looking at a book, confirming the 'by heart' sense of sorani rather than the literal sky.
[c1-l041] Fucuxi, ſuru, ita. *Paſſarſe a outro, ou dei-*
[c1-l041 note] This separate fukushi means going over to someone's side or submitting. Its meaning differs from the preceding review-of-learning entry despite identical principal forms.
[c1-l042 >] *tarſe de ſua parte: comummente ſe lhe ajunta,*
[c1-l042 note] The explanation says another element is commonly prefixed. Deitar-se de sua parte continues the sense of adherence or submission.
[c1-l043 >] Qi, curu. *Vt,* Qibucu ſuru.
[c1-l043 note] Qi, glossed curu ('come'), combines with fuku in kibuku suru, submitting or coming over in allegiance. The visible comma after Qi is important: curu is not the continuation of a single name.
[c1-l044] Fucuxin. Cocorono vchi. *Dẽtro do coração. S.*
[c1-l044 note] Fukushin is the inside of the heart, glossed kokoro no uchi. Enlargement confirms the cedilla in coração. S. is an italic written-language label; loose setting inside the word does not establish a separate word.
[c1-l045] Fucuxǒgun. i, Soyedaixǒ. *Sotocapitão.*
[c1-l045 note] Fukushōgun is a deputy commander, paraphrased soye-daishō. Sotocapitão is the corresponding subordinate-captain term; retain both printed long-o marks.
[c1-l046] Fucuxǔ. *Porro hua erua como alho.*
[c1-l046 note] Fukushū names a leek-like plant. The last letter is marked u, not i; its caron and the herb definition distinguish this noun from the preceding fukushi verbs.
[c1-l047] Fucuye. *Vestido.*
[c1-l047 note] Fukuye denotes clothing or dress, matched by Vestido. It is the final body entry of column 1, with no omitted continuation.

[c2-l001] Fucuyô. *Eſtima. ¶* Fucuyô ſuru. *Estimar.*
[c2-l001 note] Fukuyō is esteem; adding suru gives 'to esteem'. The two instances of the same word both retain their circumflex, and the Portuguese noun/verb pair makes the construction explicit.
[c2-l002] Fucuyǔ. *Proſperidades, & riquezas.*
[c2-l002 note] Fukuyū signifies prosperity and riches. The marked u is retained rather than confused with the preceding long-o word.
[c2-l003] Fucuyǔna. *Homem rico, & abaſtado.*
[c2-l003 note] Fukuyū na gives the adjectival expression for a rich, well-provided person. The attached na is part of the printed Japanese form.
[c2-l004] Fucuyǔni. *Adu. Proſperamente, ou com*
[c2-l004 note] Fukuyū ni is the adverbial counterpart, 'prosperously'. Adu. is italic grammatical apparatus, not a Japanese citation label.
[c2-l005 >] *abundancia, & fortuna.*
[c2-l005 note] Abundance and fortune complete the description of prosperous circumstances. This remains a continuation of the adverbial entry.
[c2-l006] Fucuzǒ. *Cobrir, & eſconder. ¶* Fucu-
[c2-l006 note] Fukuzō denotes concealment or reservation. The following example uses its absence to express speaking without holding anything back.
[c2-l007 >] zǒnonǒ monouo yǔ. *Falar tudo ſem dei*
[c2-l007 note] Fukuzō no nō mono wo yū expresses speaking without concealment; nō is the historical adverbial negative form. The scan has n after zǒ, not OCR m, and the Portuguese explains frank disclosure.
[c2-l008 >] *xar, nem encobrir nada.*
[c2-l008 note] The gloss finishes 'without leaving out or concealing anything'. The preceding dei and this xar form deixar across the physical line break.
[c2-l009] Fuda. *Taboinhas em que ſe eſcreuem prohibiçõ-*
[c2-l009 note] Fuda are small boards carrying written notices. The Portuguese specifies prohibitions, with the final plural ending continuing below.
[c2-l010 >] *es, ou nomes de peßoas, &c.*
[c2-l010 note] Personal names may also be written on the boards. This broadens fuda beyond prohibition notices to name tablets or labels.
[c2-l011] Fudai. i, Daidai tçutauaru. *Couſa de mui-*
[c2-l011 note] Fudai is explained as daidai tsutawaru, transmitted through generations. This establishes the hereditary sense used in the following master-and-servant examples.
[c2-l012 >] *tas idades. ¶* Fudaino xujin, l, fudai-
[c2-l012 note] Fudai no shujin and fudai no genin are alternative examples of an inherited lordship or service relation. The single l is 'or', not a Japanese syllable.
[c2-l013 >] no guenin. *Amo, ou criado de muitas ida*
[c2-l013 note] Genin means a subordinate or servant here; Amo and criado explicitly distinguish master and servant. The word idades continues without an added hyphen.
[c2-l014 >] *des, & antiguo.*
[c2-l014 note] Antiguo qualifies the longstanding, ancestral relationship. Its older spelling is retained rather than changed to modern antigo.
[c2-l015] Fǔdai. *Elemento do ar. Vt,* Fǔdaini jǒ-
[c2-l015 note] Fūdai is the element of air, distinct from hereditary fudai by the marked vowel. The example repeats Fǔdai with ni; OCR Fidaini lost both the correct vowel and its mark.
[c2-l016 >] chǔgueno ſanbu ari. *No ar ha tres regiõ-*
[c2-l016 note] Jō-chū-ge no sanbu ari identifies upper, middle and lower divisions. The Japanese compounds and the Portuguese 'three regions' agree.
[c2-l017 >] *es. ſ. ſuprema, media, infima.*
[c2-l017 note] The three regions are highest, middle and lowest. The italic ſ. is an explanatory abbreviation, not an additional Japanese term.
[c2-l018] Fudaino mono. *Catiuo, ou criado por deſcẽ-*
[c2-l018 note] Fudai no mono is a captive or servant by hereditary descent. The definition resumes the unmarked-u fudai family after the intervening air-element entry.
[c2-l019 >] *dencia de muitas idades.*
[c2-l019 note] Descent over many generations completes the hereditary-servant definition. No new Japanese headword begins on this row.
[c2-l020] Fudai sǒden. *Couſa que vem por ſuceſſão de*
[c2-l020 note] Fudai sōden is transmission or inheritance through generations. The noun succession in Portuguese matches sōden; the printed short initial s is retained.
[c2-l021 >] *muitas idades. ¶* Fudai sǒdenno mono.
[c2-l021 note] The example applies ancestral succession to a person in service. Keep the attached no in sǒdenno while interpreting it as a genitive particle.
[c2-l022 >] *Criado antiguo que vem por deſcẽdencia de ſeu*
[c2-l022 note] The servant's status descends through the family. The scan begins with capital C and reads deſcẽdencia, not the OCR's linguistically malformed deſcẽdencta.
[c2-l023 >] *pay, auòs, &c.*
[c2-l023 note] The family list reads father, grandparents, and so on. Enlargement shows the rounded remnant of o with a separate grave above it, not the OCR's dotted i. The left side is worn, so auòs remains a qualified scan reading.
[c2-l024] Fudan. Tayezu. i. Itçumo. *Sempre.*
[c2-l024 note] Fudan is 'continually', explained by tayezu ('without ceasing') and itsumo ('always'). These are Japanese alternatives, not Portuguese loanwords embedded in the gloss.
[c2-l025] Fudancǒ. *Certa caſca de aruore, ou folhas de*
[c2-l025 note] Fudankō is continually burning incense, here described as tree bark or leaves. The gloss goes on to explain both fragrance and timekeeping, not simply the name of one plant.
[c2-l026 >] *loureiro, &c. que ſe queimão diante do Foto-*
[c2-l026 note] Laurel leaves and similar material are burned before the Buddha. Foto- begins Fotoque, italic within the Portuguese explanation, and must not be mistaken for a fresh Japanese headword.
[c2-l027 >] *que como cheiro, & jũtamente ſeruem de relo-*
[c2-l027 note] The incense supplies fragrance and simultaneously serves as a clock. The physical relo- division anticipates the following jio.
[c2-l028 >] *jio, por queſe vão queimando, & gastando pou-*
[c2-l028 note] Relojio is a historical clock spelling. Its j has a descending stroke in the scan; OCR ſio obscured the word. The explanation measures time by gradual burning and consumption.
[c2-l029 >] *co a pouco.*
[c2-l029 note] Pouco a pouco completes the account of slow burning. It belongs to the clock mechanism, not a separate definition of fudan.
[c2-l030] Fudan, jǒgiǔ. *Sempre, & ſem ceßar.*
[c2-l030 note] Fudan and jōjū express continuity, 'always and without ceasing'. The final vowel of jǒgiǔ is u with a caron, not o; this distinguishes the printed form from an inferred jōgyō.
[c2-l031] Fudaracu. *Paraiſo de Quãuon, o qual dizem*
[c2-l031 note] Fudaraku is Kannon's paradise, the Buddhist Potalaka. Quãuon is the Japanese deity's name embedded in italic Portuguese; its abbreviated nasal spelling is retained.
[c2-l032 >] *os gentios eſtar pera parte do Sul. ¶* Fu-
[c2-l032 note] The compilers report that Buddhists locate this paradise to the south. Their term gentios reflects the missionary voice; the note describes that wording without adopting it as a neutral religious classification.
[c2-l033 >] daracuni vataru. *Embarcarſe pera eſte pa*
[c2-l033 note] Fudaraku ni wataru means crossing to Fudaraku. The dictionary interprets it as embarking for the paradise; pa continues into paraiſo without an added hyphen.
[c2-l034 >] *raiſo o que fazem algũs Bonzos metendoſe em*
[c2-l034 note] Some Buddhist clerics are described as undertaking this voyage. Bonzos is italic here, and the sentence continues with their entering a vessel.
[c2-l035 >] *hũa embarcação bem paramentada, & orna-*
[c2-l035 note] The vessel is equipped and ornamented. Preserve the physical orna-/da division rather than moving material between rows.
[c2-l036 >] *da, & carregada de pedras donde ſe botão ao*
[c2-l036 note] The account says the vessel is loaded with stones, and its occupants throw themselves into the sea. This is the source's particular description of the voyage, not a general account supplied by the reviewer.
[c2-l037 >] *mar, & ſe afogão.*
[c2-l037 note] The sentence ends with drowning in the sea. This completes the explanation attached to Fudaracu ni vataru.
[c2-l038] Fudaracu xecai, l, fudaracu. *Eſte paraiſo*
[c2-l038 note] Fudaraku sekai, or simply Fudaraku, names the same paradise. Xe is the dictionary's regular spelling for historical se, so xecai is not an OCR anomaly.
[c2-l039 >] *de Quanuon.*
[c2-l039 note] Kannon's name appears here as Quanuon, with an explicit n rather than the earlier nasal abbreviation. Retain the occurrence-level spelling difference.
[c2-l040] Fude. *Pena. ¶* Fudeuo yǔ. *Fazer pe-*
[c2-l040 note] Fude is a writing brush, rendered by Portuguese pena. Fude wo yū describes making or tying a brush, confirmed by Fazer penas de Iapão below.
[c2-l041 >] *nas de Iapão. ¶* Fudeuo ſaxiuoqu. *A-*
[c2-l041 note] Fude wo sashiwoku is putting the brush aside, hence finishing writing. The scan has roman F throughout the Japanese expression; OCR assigned a false italic P to the preceding run.
[c2-l042 >] *cabar de eſcreuer. S. ¶* Fudeuo ſomu.
[c2-l042 note] Fude wo somuru, 'to dip or apply the brush', introduces a written-language expression for writing. Restore the worn F from its visible form and the repeated brush construction; Tudeuo does not fit the Japanese.
[c2-l043 >] ru. *Eſcreuer. S.*
[c2-l043 note] Ru completes somuru. Eſcreuer supplies the meaning and S. identifies the literary register; the printed period at the preceding row's end is retained rather than silently treated as a hyphen.
[c2-l044] Fudeqi. *Couſa mal feita, ou que não ſahio*
[c2-l044 note] Fudeki means badly made or unsuccessful. The Portuguese reads ſahio, the historical past of sair, 'turned out', rather than ſabio; the following bem acabada completes the predicate.
[c2-l045 >] *bem acabada.*
[c2-l045 note] 'Well finished' completes the negated description: something that did not turn out well. This is not a positive definition contradicting fudeki.
[c2-l046] Fudeqina. *Couſa imperfeita, ou mal acabada.*
[c2-l046 note] Fudeki na is the adjectival counterpart, imperfect or badly finished. The attached na is preserved as printed.
[c2-l047] Fudetori. *Eſcriuão.*
[c2-l047 note] Fudetori is a scribe or clerk, literally a brush-holder. The lower Fude- repeats the start of the following page and remains catchword furniture, not another body row.
