[c1-l001] Tandon. *Huns pilouros que fazem de pò de cer-*
[c1-l001 note] Tandon names small pellets made from the powder of a particular charcoal. The printed cer- continues as to on the following physical line; the headword remains roman.
[c1-l002 >] *to caruão que poem nos perfumadores os quaes*
[c1-l002 note] The charcoal pellets are placed in perfume burners. This line completes certo from the preceding line and introduces the relative clause describing how long the pellets burn.
[c1-l003 >] *durão muito aceſos.*
[c1-l003 note] The pellets remain alight for a long time, completing the Tandon definition. The vowel read as u in durão is abraded; that incorporated reading fits both its visible remnant and the sense.
[c1-l004] Tane. *Semente. ¶* Tanega cauaru. *Se-*
[c1-l004 note] Tane means seed. The pilcrow introduces Tanega cauaru, whose explanation concerns children of differing paternity; Se- continues as rem on the next line.
[c1-l005 >] *rem dous, ou mais filhos da meſma mãy, &*
[c1-l005 note] The example describes two or more children of the same mother. The final conjunction links this line to the different-father qualification below; mãy retains its printed nasal mark.
[c1-l006 >] *de differente pay. ¶* Taneuo tatçu. *Dei-*
[c1-l006 note] The parentage example ends with different fathers. A new pilcrow introduces Taneuo tatçu, destroying the seed; the Portuguese Dei- continues as tar on the next line.
[c1-l007 >] *tar a perder a ſemẽte. Itẽ, permet deſtruir de*
[c1-l007 note] Deitar a perder means to ruin the seed; the entry also extends this metaphorically to complete destruction. The printed abbreviation and nasal marks are retained without expanding the canonical text.
[c1-l008 >] *todo. ¶* Taneuo maqu. *Semear eſpa-*
[c1-l008 note] The first word completes the preceding total-destruction sense. Taneuo maqu begins a separate example of sowing by scattering, with espa- continuing on the following line.
[c1-l009 >] *lhãdo a ſemente. ¶* Taneuo voroſu. *Dar*
[c1-l009 note] The scattering example ends here. Taneuo voroſu introduces giving seed to cultivators; the explanation continues with the landowner as the giver.
[c1-l010 >] *o ſenhor, ou dono das terras ſemẽte empreſtada*
[c1-l010 note] The landowner supplies seed as a loan. This continues the example introduced above, rather than defining a new Japanese item; the nasal abbreviation in ſemẽte is retained.
[c1-l011 >] *aos lauradores pera ſemear, ou repartila o q̃*
[c1-l011 note] The borrowed seed is for farmers to sow, or is distributed by their headman. The printed q̃ abbreviates que and is retained rather than silently expanded.
[c1-l012 >] *he cabeça dos lauradores pellos que a não tem.*
[c1-l012 note] This completes the alternative distribution arrangement: the head of the cultivators shares seed among those who lack it. Cabeça is figurative leadership, not an anatomical reference.
[c1-l013 >] *¶* Taneuo caſu. *Meter a ſemente nagoa pe-*
[c1-l013 note] Taneuo caſu introduces soaking seed in water. The medial letter is long s, not f; nagoa remains joined as printed, and pe- continues below.
[c1-l014 >] *ra amolecer, & ficar deſposta pera ſemear*
[c1-l014 note] The soaking softens the seed and prepares it for sowing. This is uninterrupted Portuguese explanatory text, with the historical spelling deſposta retained.
[c1-l015 >] *na terra.*
[c1-l015 note] The final phrase specifies sowing in the ground and closes the long Tane entry. It remains an indented continuation, not a separate entry.
[c1-l016] Tanen. Vouoi toxi. *Muitos annos.*
[c1-l016 note] This Tanen means many years, also expressed by the roman Japanese gloss Vouoi toxi. It is a separate homograph from the following entry about other thoughts.
[c1-l017] Tanen. *Outros penſamentos, ou cuidar outras*
[c1-l017 note] The second Tanen denotes other thoughts or thinking about other matters. The Portuguese sentence continues on the next physical line; its headword is not merged with the preceding homograph.
[c1-l018 >] *co iſas. ¶* Tanen naquxite Oraciouo mǒ-
[c1-l018 note] The visible spacing in co iſas is retained. The Japanese example means to say one's prayer without distracting thoughts; Oraciouo is wholly roman, and mǒ- continues below.
[c1-l019 >] xe. *Orai ſem cuidar outras couſas, ou ſem*
[c1-l019 note] The fragment xe completes mǒxe. The Portuguese imperative asks the reader to pray without thinking about other things, with an alternative phrasing continuing below.
[c1-l020 >] *vos deſtrahir com o penſamento.*
[c1-l020 note] The closing phrase restates the instruction not to be distracted in thought. It completes the prayer example and the second Tanen entry.
[c1-l021] Tango. i, Goguat itçuca. *Aos cinco dias*
[c1-l021 note] Tango is explained by the Japanese expression Goguat itçuca. The Portuguese dates it to the fifth day, with the relevant lunar month specified on the following line.
[c1-l022 >] *da quinta lua, ou mes.*
[c1-l022 note] The date is the fifth day of the fifth lunar month. The source's lua, ou mes wording is preserved rather than converted into a Gregorian date.
[c1-l023] Tangua. l, Tanguaya. *Caſa como hoſpital*
[c1-l023 note] Tangua, alternatively Tanguaya, denotes a lodging establishment compared to a hospital. The following line specifies its guests; the historical comparison need not imply a modern medical institution.
[c1-l024 >] *onde agaſalhão Bonzos peregrinos. ¶* Tan-
[c1-l024 note] The lodging receives travelling Buddhist monks. A pilcrow then introduces a Japanese example beginning Tan-, whose continuation belongs to the same word on the following line.
[c1-l025 >] guayano yǒna tocoro. *Caſa, ou lugar co-*
[c1-l025 note] The initial fragment completes Tanguayano. The example compares a house or place to such a lodging; the Portuguese co- continues as mo below.
[c1-l026 >] *mo hoſpital onde concorre muita gente de vari-*
[c1-l026 note] The comparison is to a lodging where many people gather from various places. The source's vari- continues on the next line, without creating a separate lexical unit.
[c1-l027 >] *as partes.*
[c1-l027 note] This completes varias partes, referring to the different places from which visitors come. It closes the extended example under Tangua.
[c1-l028] Tanguaya. *Vide* Tangua.
[c1-l028 note] Tanguaya is a cross-reference to Tangua immediately above. Vide is italic Portuguese editorial text, while both Japanese headword forms remain roman.
[c1-l029] Tani. *Valle.*
[c1-l029 note] This Tani means valley. It is a complete short entry and must remain separate from the identically written headword for a tick on the next line.
[c1-l030] Tani. *Carrapato.*
[c1-l030 note] This homograph is defined as a tick. The source writes Tani; it is not silently modernized to a different contemporary romanization.
[c1-l031] Tanigaua. *Ribeiro, ou ribeira dagoa, que vai*
[c1-l031 note] Tanigaua denotes a stream running between valleys. The printed dagoa remains joined, and the relative clause continues on the next physical line.
[c1-l032 >] *por antre valles.*
[c1-l032 note] This continuation specifies that the stream runs among valleys. It closes Tanigaua before the separate, previously omitted Taniguchi entry.
[c1-l032a] Taniguchi. *Boca, ou entrada do valle.*
[c1-l032a note] The scan contains a complete omitted entry here: Taniguchi, the mouth or entrance of a valley. A new stable suffix ID restores this physical row without renumbering later lines.
[c1-l033] Tanin. Betno fito. *Outro homem que não he*
[c1-l033 note] Tanin is glossed by Betno fito and described as another person who is not a relative. The final qualification continues below; the Japanese gloss stays roman.
[c1-l034 >] *parente.*
[c1-l034 note] Parente completes the definition of Tanin as someone outside one's kin. This single-word physical line is an indented continuation, not an independent entry.
[c1-l035] Tan-in. *Doença de freimas.*
[c1-l035 note] Tan-in is a distinct hyphenated headword for an illness associated with phlegm. The historical medical wording is transcribed, not offered as a modern diagnosis.
[c1-l036] Taninju. *Muita gente.*
[c1-l036 note] Taninju means many people or a large number of people. The scan presents a self-contained headword and short Portuguese definition on this line.
[c1-l037] Taninoto. i, Tani. *valle.* P.
[c1-l037 note] Taninoto is equated with Tani, valley. The terminal roman P. is the source's poetic-use label; it does not belong to the Portuguese definition or the Japanese headword.
[c1-l038] Tanixi. *Caramujos das varzeas.*
[c1-l038 note] Tanixi denotes snails associated with flooded fields. The historical spelling varzeas is preserved, without adding a species identification absent from the source.
[c1-l039] Tanja. l, Tanxa. *Hũs poos vermelhos, que*
[c1-l039 note] Tanja, also Tanxa, names a red powdered substance. Its uses as colouring or medicine continue on the following line; both Japanese variants remain roman.
[c1-l040 >] *ſeruẽ de tinta vermelha clara, ou de mezinha.*
[c1-l040 note] The red powder serves as a light red pigment or a medicinal preparation. The source's historical use is reported without endorsing the substance as a present-day treatment.
[c1-l041] Tanjacu. *Verſos eſcritos em tira de papel.*
[c1-l041 note] Tanjacu denotes verses written on a strip of paper. The entire Portuguese definition fits on this physical line and closes the entry.
[c1-l042] Tanjei. l, qixei. *Deprecações. Vt,* Tan-
[c1-l042 note] Tanjei, alternatively qixei, means supplications. Vt introduces an example whose headword is divided Tan- / jeiuo across the line boundary.
[c1-l043 >] jeiuo tçucuſu. *Rogar, ou fazer depreca-*
[c1-l043 note] The fragment jeiuo completes Tanjeiuo in an expression for earnest supplication. The Portuguese depreca- continues as ções on the next line.
[c1-l044 >] *ções intenſamente.*
[c1-l044 note] The final words describe making supplications intensely or earnestly. They complete both the divided Portuguese word and the example under Tanjei.
[c1-l045] Tanji, zuru, ita. *Tanger. ¶* Biuauo tan-
[c1-l045 note] Tanji gives verbal forms for playing a stringed instrument. The example Biuauo tan- continues across the column boundary as zuru; the printed division is preserved.
