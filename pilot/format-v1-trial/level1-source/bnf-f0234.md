[c1-l001] Guebon. *Couſa baixa. Vt,* Guebon gue-
[c1-l001 note] Gebon denotes a low grade or inferior thing; the example continues into gexō on the next row. The scan supports Guebon and the divided gue-, rather than OCR Guebem and guc-.
[c1-l002 >] xǒno fito. *Homẽ de baixa laya. ¶ Itẽ,*
[c1-l002 note] Completes gebon gexō no hito, a person of low standing or character. The Portuguese calls him a man of low rank; the nasal abbreviation in Homẽ is retained without expansion.
[c1-l003 >] *Grao inferior em comparação de Chǔbon, &*
[c1-l003 note] The second sense is an inferior grade relative to chūbon and jōbon. The marked u in Chǔbon has a caron, not a tilde; this Japanese comparison occurs inside the Portuguese explanation.
[c1-l004 >] *jǒbon.*
[c1-l004 note] Jōbon completes the comparison of lower, middle and upper grades. This is Japanese vocabulary embedded in the preceding Portuguese sentence, not a separate headword.
[c1-l005] Guebu. *Terceira parte, ou vltima de tres li-*
[c1-l005 note] Gebu is the third or last division in a three-volume work. The printed li- begins livros on the next physical row.
[c1-l006 >] *uros, ou volumes.*
[c1-l006 note] Completes livros, ou volumes: books or volumes. This short continuation belongs to Gebu and remains a separate line.
[c1-l007] Guecacu. Qiba, tçuno. *Dente como de por*
[c1-l007 note] Gekaku is explained by kiba and tsuno, tusk and horn. The Portuguese begins the example of a wild boar; por continues into co without adding an unprinted hyphen.
[c1-l008 >] *co montes, ou elefante, & corno.*
[c1-l008 note] Completes porco montes and compares the tusk with that of a boar or elephant, then adds horn. The historical spelling montes is retained without supplying an accent.
[c1-l009] Guecai. *Este mundo inferior.*
[c1-l009 note] Gekai is this lower world; the gloss inferior describes its position, without adding a modern cosmological identification.
[c1-l010] Guecan. *Os derradeiros dez dias do mes em cõ*
[c1-l010 note] Gekan denotes the final ten days of the month. The abbreviated cõ begins comparação across the physical break.
[c1-l011 >] *paração de Chǔcan. jǒcan. Que ſão os dez do*
[c1-l011 note] The comparison names chūkan and jōkan, the middle and first ten-day periods. The scan supports marked vowels and the capital Ch; the separators are retained as printed rather than normalized into a list.
[c1-l012 >] *meo, & os do principio.*
[c1-l012 note] Completes the explanation: the ten days in the middle and those at the beginning. This belongs to the comparison under Guecan.
[c1-l013] Guechacu. Cudaritçuqu. *Chegar vindo do*
[c1-l013 note] Gechaku is paraphrased by kudari-tsuku, arriving after coming down. The following Portuguese identifies the point of departure.
[c1-l014 >] *Miyaco, ou de outra parte ſuperior.*
[c1-l014 note] The arrival is from Miyako or another superior place. Miyaco is a Japanese place designation within the Portuguese sentence and remains in the locally printed italic type.
[c1-l015] Guechi. Xitano chiye. *Pouco ſaber. Vt,*
[c1-l015 note] Gechi, glossed shita no chie, means limited knowledge or understanding. The Portuguese is Pouco saber, not OCR Fouco; the example follows.
[c1-l016 >] Guecon guechina fito. *Homem de pouco ſa*
[c1-l016 note] Gekon gechi na hito describes a person of weak ability and little understanding. The Portuguese sa continues into ber below without a supplied hyphen.
[c1-l017 >] *ber, & fraca habilidade.*
[c1-l017 note] Completes saber and adds weak ability. The two Portuguese characteristics explain gechi and gekon together, rather than introducing another entry.
[c1-l018] Gueco. *Peßoa que não bebe vinho. ¶* Gue-
[c1-l018 note] Geko denotes someone who does not drink wine or alcoholic drink. The scan supports Peßoa rather than OCR Peßca; Gue- begins the following example.
[c1-l019 >] co xǒna fito. *Homem que não bebe vinho,*
[c1-l019 note] Completes geko shō na hito, a non-drinking person. The Portuguese repeats the abstention and continues with additional qualities below.
[c1-l020 >] *& no de mais he temperado, modesto, & apri*
[c1-l020 note] The person is also temperate, modest and refined in other respects. The original modest-type ligature is retained as modest, without a global st/ſt substitution.
[c1-l021 >] *morado.*
[c1-l021 note] Morado completes aprimorado, refined or well-mannered. This single-word continuation finishes the description of the non-drinker.
[c1-l022] Guecǒ. Cudarimucǒ. *Decer de lugar alto,*
[c1-l022 note] Gekō is descending or going down, paraphrased kudari mukō. The initial is G, not OCR C, and both long-o marks are carons. Reinspection does not securely establish a word space inside the close printed Cudarimucǒ, so the baseline joining is retained.
[c1-l023 >] *& principalmente do Miyaco.*
[c1-l023 note] The descent is especially from Miyako. The place name is embedded within the Portuguese explanation, rather than a Japanese synonym line.
[c1-l024] Guecocu. Cuniye cudaru. *Tornar do Miya*
[c1-l024 note] Gekoku, kuni e kudaru, means returning from Miyako to one's own province or domain. The scan reads Guecocu, not Guccocu; Miya continues into co on the next row without an added hyphen.
[c1-l025 >] *co pera ſeu reino.*
[c1-l025 note] Co completes Miyaco, and pera seu reino specifies return to one's own domain. The divided Japanese place name remains within the Portuguese sentence.
[c1-l026] Guecocujǒ. Ximo, vyeni catçu. *Sendo bai*
[c1-l026 note] Gekokujō is paraphrased as the lower overcoming the upper. The Portuguese begins its social example with Sendo bai, continued as baixo below.
[c1-l027 >] *xo, & criado, por ſuas boas partes, & induſ-*
[c1-l027 note] Completes baixo: someone low-ranking and a servant rises through good qualities and industry. Xo is Portuguese, not Japanese, and is therefore included in the printed italic gloss.
[c1-l028 >] *tria, ou fortuna vir a valer, & ſer ſenhor,*
[c1-l028 note] Tria completes industria; fortune is given as another way the servant may gain standing and become a lord. The explanation continues the reversal of status.
[c1-l029 >] *& o ſenhor vir a ſer criado, ou baixo.*
[c1-l029 note] The former lord in turn becomes a servant or a low-ranking person. This completes the dictionary's explicit illustration of gekokujō.
[c1-l030] Guecǒdǒ. Cudari mucǒ michi. *Caminho,*
[c1-l030 note] Gekōdō, kudari mukō michi, is the path used in descending. Both marked o types in the headword remain carons; the Portuguese explanation follows.
[c1-l031 >] *polo qual ſe dece de lugar alto, como quando*
[c1-l031 note] The path leads down from a high place. The ensuing example makes clear that this can be the return route from worship or a visit.
[c1-l032 >] *vem de viſitar o Cami, ou Fotoque.*
[c1-l032 note] Completes the example of returning from visiting a kami or hotoke. The worn opening supports vem, not OCR vuem; Cami and Fotoque are Japanese religious terms embedded in Portuguese.
[c1-l033] Guecon. i, Qiconno naimono. *Peßoa de*
[c1-l033 note] Gekon is explained as a person without kikon, stamina or perseverance. The printed Portuguese noun is Peßoa, not OCR Teßoa; its qualifying phrase continues below.
[c1-l034 >] *fraca compreiſão, & q̃ não chega ao cabo nem*
[c1-l034 note] Describes a weak constitution or capacity and failure to carry things through. The historical compreiſão and abbreviated q̃ are preserved, rather than modernized or expanded.
[c1-l035 >] *perſeuera, &c. ¶* Iǒcon. *O que tem*
[c1-l035 note] Completes the lack of perseverance, then contrasts jōkon, a person who possesses the opposite strength. The initial I in Iǒcon is the source's consonantal spelling.
[c1-l036 >] *vigor, & perſeuera.*
[c1-l036 note] Vigor and perseverance complete the description of jōkon. This is the contrasting positive term under Guecon, not an unrelated gloss.
[c1-l037] Guecqei. *Nome de hũa dignidade, ou nobres*
[c1-l037 note] Gekkei is described as a dignity or a class of nobles. The Portuguese number shift from a dignity to nobles is printed and has not been regularized.
[c1-l038 >] *que ſeruem ao Dairi.*
[c1-l038 note] The nobles serve the Dairi, the imperial court or sovereign referred to here. Dairi occurs within the Portuguese relative clause and is a typeface-review term.
[c1-l039] Guecqiǔ. Tçuqino miya. *Caſa que imagi-*
[c1-l039 note] Gekkyū is tsuki no miya, the moon palace. The u carries a caron in this impression; imagi- begins the Portuguese verb imaginão, not OCR irmagi-.
[c1-l040 >] *não os gẽtios eſtar dentro da lũa onde hum como*
[c1-l040 note] Completes imaginão: the dictionary reports a house imagined by non-Christians inside the moon. The worn initial n and the abbreviation in gẽtios support this reading; the analogy hum como continues below.
[c1-l041 >] *Anjo a faz crecer, & minguar.*
[c1-l041 note] The dictionary likens the lunar inhabitant to an angel and says it makes the moon wax and wane. This reports the source's analogy, not an independent identification of the deity.
[c1-l042] Guecqiǔden. *Idem.*
[c1-l042 note] Gekkyūden is given the same moon-palace meaning as Guecqiǔ. The scan supports G and the ascender of d rather than OCR Cuecqiǔcen; the small u-mark is worn and is retained as a caron provisionally.
[c1-l043] Guecqua. Tçuqi, fana. *Lũa, & roſas.*
[c1-l043 note] Gekka combines tsuki and hana, moon and flowers; the Portuguese uses lua and rosas. The printed nasal mark belongs to u in Lũa, not to an a.
[c1-l044] Guedai. *Titulo dalgum liuro. ¶* Guedai-
[c1-l044 note] Gedai is the title of a book. The example repeats Guedai- across the break; the initial G is supported by the visible type rather than OCR C.
[c1-l045 >] uo caqitçuquru, l, voſu. *Por titulo nal-*
[c1-l045 note] Completes gedai wo kakitsukuru or osu, putting a title on a book. The object particle reads uo, not no, and the Portuguese verb Por replaces OCR Tor; nal- continues below.
[c1-l046 >] *gum liuro, ou nas coſtas delle com outro papel*
[c1-l046 note] The title may be placed on the book or on its back using another piece of paper. No sentence-final punctuation is supplied where the physical line continues.
[c1-l047 >] *pegado ao modo de Iapão, ou da China.*
[c1-l047 note] The paper is attached in the Japanese or Chinese manner. The scan resolves the garbled OCR as pegado ao modo de Iapão, ou da China; these geographical names are Portuguese forms, not Japanese typeface terms.

[c2-l010] Gueden. i, Zocuxo. *Liuros de couſas hu-*

[c2-l011 note] Completes human and secular matters, then contrasts naiden, a book treating Buddhist subjects. The rightmost Liuro is worn but intelligible.
[c2-l012 >] *que trata de couſas do Buppô.*

[c2-l014 >] dǒuo naſu. *¶ Item, Permet. Cerimoni-*

[c2-l015 note] These ceremonies or supplications are performed by sorcerers or the group named on the following row. The unusual tilde placement in deprecaçoẽs is retained.
[c2-l016 >] *Yamabuxis pera botar o diabo fora, ou pera ou*
[c2-l016 note] Yamabushi are named as performers of rites to drive out the devil or for another purpose. The entire printed Portuguese-context line, including Yamabuxis, is italic; the isolated roman Y was an OCR styling error.
[c2-l017 >] *tro effeito.*
[c2-l017 note] Tro completes outro, followed by effect or purpose. This closes the description of the rites under Guedǒ.
[c2-l018] Guedocu. *Hũa certa mezinha.*
[c2-l018 note] Gedoku is identified only as a particular medicine. The brief gloss does not establish an exact recipe or substance.
[c2-l019] Guefai. Xitano tomogara. *Gente baixa, ou*
[c2-l019 note] Gehai, shita no tomogara, denotes people of low or inferior rank. The Portuguese description continues on the next line.
[c2-l020 >] *de inferior grao. ¶* Guefaino fito. *Idẽ.*
[c2-l020 note] The example gehai no hito repeats the same meaning. The scan supports Guefaino rather than OCR Cuefamno; Idẽ is a printed abbreviation of idem.
[c2-l021] Guefit. *Meliùs,* Cafit. Xitano fude. *Ru*
[c2-l021 note] Gehit is redirected to the preferred kahit, shita no fude, inferior writing or workmanship. Meliùs is italic apparatus; Ru begins ruim without a supplied hyphen.
[c2-l022 >] *im letra, ou ruim eſcriuão. Vt,* Iǒfit,
[c2-l022 note] Completes ruim letra and gives a poor writer as an alternative sense. The contrast begins jōhit, the superior counterpart; the checked final t remains unexpanded.
[c2-l023 >] cafit. *Bom eſcriuão, ou pintor, & ruim eſ-*
[c2-l023 note] Kahit completes the contrast with jōhit. The Portuguese explains a good writer or painter versus a poor one, and divides escriuão at the line ending.
[c2-l024 >] *criuão, ou pintor.*
[c2-l024 note] Completes escriuão and repeats painter, finishing the comparison of good and poor workmanship. The line belongs to Guefit, not a new headword.
[c2-l025] Guefô. *Ma ley, ou ley do diabo, ſuperſtições,*
[c2-l025 note] Gehō is described polemically as bad law, the devil's law, or superstitions. The scan reads ſuperſtições, not OCR ſuperſtiçães; the characterization belongs to the historical dictionary.

[c2-l028] Guefon. *Meliùs,* Guebon. Xitano xi-
[c2-l028 note] Gehon is redirected to the preferred gebon, an inferior grade. Xitano xi- begins shita no shina and continues on the next row.

[c2-l029 note] Na completes shina, grade or class; the Latin instruction means see above. The scan has na rather than OCR ria, agreeing with the Japanese paraphrase.
[c2-l030] Guegu. Xitajitano mono. *Gente baixa ou in*
[c2-l030 note] Gegu is explained as shita-jita no mono, people of inferior status. The G is visible despite wear; in continues into inferior below.
[c2-l031 >] *ferior.*
[c2-l031 note] Ferior completes inferior, finishing the low-status-person definition. No new entry begins on this short continuation.
[c2-l032] Guegue. i, Iǒri. *Calçado como alparcas de*

[c2-l040] Guegi. *Mandado. ¶* Guegiuo ſuru, l,

[c2-l042] Guegio. Ximo, vonna. *Eſcraua ou molher*

[c2-l043 >] *de ſeruiço baixa.*

[c2-l044] Guegiqi. Yaſui atai. *Valor, ou preço baixo.*
[c2-l044 note] Gejiki, yasui atai, is low value or a low price. The Portuguese and Japanese paraphrase agree on cheapness rather than moral baseness.
[c2-l045] Gue-i. *Meliùs,* Ca-i. *Dignidade, ou cargo*
[c2-l045 note] Ge-i is redirected to the preferred ka-i and means a low dignity or office. The internal hyphens are lexical and are not line-end divisions.
[c2-l046 >] *baixo.*
[c2-l046 note] Baixo completes the description of a low-ranking dignity or post. This short continuation ends Gue-i.
[c2-l047] Gueifa. i, Toqino coye. *Grita que ſe dà no*
[c2-l047 note] Geiha is equated with toki no koe, a battle cry. The worn vowel in Toqino supports o in context; the Portuguese reads Grita, not OCR Gria.
[c2-l048 >] *principio da batalha.*
[c2-l048 note] Completes the cry given at the start of battle. The separate Guei at the far right is the catchword, not part of this definition; its duplicate has been removed from the body text.
