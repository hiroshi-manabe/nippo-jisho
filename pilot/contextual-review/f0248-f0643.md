# Contextual Review of f0248 and f0643

## Purpose

This experiment tests whether explicit Japanese/romanization and Portuguese-context passes find errors left by image-focused review, and whether final scan confirmation prevents linguistic expectation from altering genuine printed forms.

`f248` was chosen because its typography and earlier corrections are familiar. `f643` was chosen as the stronger comparison because it had not received the same focused contextual discussion. The canonical compact Level 1 files at commit `9b4141b` formed the baseline. Wikisource, OCR, translations, and headword data were not consulted.

## Method

For each complete page:

1. Read the Level 1 Japanese forms as historical Japanese and test their morphology, syntax, and known Jesuit romanization patterns.
2. Read every Portuguese gloss for lexical and syntactic coherence and test its relationship to the Japanese.
3. Treat every implausible form only as a flag, not as a correction.
4. Inspect the relevant native-resolution Gallica tile and surrounding lines.
5. Classify the result as a confirmed diplomatic correction, a printed anomaly to preserve, an unresolved reading, or later-stage analysis.

All physical lines on both pages were included. The scan-confirmation pass used the twelve overlapping sixth-column tiles for each page.

## Comparison

| Page | New confirmed corrections | Rejected contextual suspicions | Unresolved | Principal yield |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `f248` | 0 | 3 | 0 | The already corrected `tçutomeuo` remained stable. |
| `f643` | 2 | 2 | 0 | Portuguese lexical and historical-spelling review. |
| **Total** | **2** | **5** | **0** | Context found residual errors, while scan confirmation prevented five normalizations. |

The earlier image-focused passes were effective at line coverage, typeface, diacritics, and conspicuous letter shapes, but they did not expose all linguistically implausible sequences. The new passes found two residual errors on the less familiar page. Conversely, five plausible linguistic emendations were contradicted by visible print. Context is therefore useful as an error detector but unsafe as the final authority.

The two contextual perspectives should remain separately named for now. The motivating `tçutomeuo` correction arose from Japanese morphology, whereas both new `f643` corrections arose from Portuguese. Combining them into an undifferentiated rereading would make it harder to ensure that both perspectives were actually applied.

## Timing limitation

The review was performed interactively while the protocol and comparison table were being developed. End-to-end editorial time was therefore not isolated reliably. A separately timestamped `f643` scan-confirmation sweep of all twelve sixth-column tiles ran from `2026-07-29T10:04:24Z` to `2026-07-29T10:05:50Z` (1 minute 26 seconds), but this excludes the preceding contextual flagging and must not be reported as total review time.

A future production-rate trial should time initial transcription, Japanese/romanization review, Portuguese review, and final scan confirmation separately without interleaved documentation work.

## Conclusion

The experiment supports retaining all four review perspectives within Level 1 quality control. They do not collapse Level 1 into later analysis because only scan-confirmed printed readings enter the transcription. The comparison also validates the explicit final scan pass: without it, at least five genuine source forms on these two pages would have been vulnerable to silent normalization.
