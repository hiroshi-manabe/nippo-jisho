The `c1-l019` final mark is a circumflex (`û`), contrasting with the visibly caroned `ǔ` in the preceding form. Tighter enlargement shows three rounded letter bodies before `uriû`: a `d` with its ascender and right side nearly lost, a badly faded `o`, and the clear `c`. This supports `docuriû`, not the contextually tempting normalization `acuriû`. The NINJAL headword inventory independently records `Docuriù.` later in the dictionary (`072c14`, Gallica `f156`), establishing that a `docuri-` form occurs in the source even though the inventory does not record this non-headword occurrence after `i.`. The `c1-l019` reading therefore appears to be a new textual observation from the image-first transcription; this is a provisional novelty claim pending a comprehensive literature review.

At `c2a-l020`, a thin faded stem between `e` and `r`, with an upper trace, confirms `cheiro`. At `c2b-l011`, the descender is unambiguously `q`; comparison with `naſaqe` on the same page identifies the preceding damaged character as long `ſ`, producing `Suſuqino` and agreeing with Japanese *susuqi no*. These are scan readings supported by context, not context-driven normalizations.
