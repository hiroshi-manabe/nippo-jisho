# Production Re-review: bnf-f0014

## Purpose and state

`bnf-f0014` is the first page processed end to end with the strengthened pre-human-review procedure adopted after the high correction yield on `f13`. It is also the first direct test of that procedure against independent human comparison. The human pass found thirteen further corrections, so the procedure did not meet its working target of no more than two or three. Both columns are recorded as `needs_correction` until the regenerated text is rechecked; page furniture remains `pending`.

The canonical evidence is the Gallica master `f0014.jpg` identified by the checksum in the Level 1 source. Wikisource was not consulted during this re-review. NINJAL version 202510 was used only after the scan-derived checkpoint for entry coverage and suspicious-form comparison.

## Passes performed

1. Compared all 94 dictionary-text lines, both running headers, and the catchword with the native master through all twelve overlapping sixth-column tiles.
2. Used larger targeted crops wherever the default tile left a letter, mark, or spacing judgment uncomfortable.
3. Parsed the Japanese forms and examples for plausible words, particles, and inflectional endings, then returned every suspicious sequence to the scan.
4. Read every Portuguese gloss for syntactic and lexical coherence, historical spelling, abbreviations, and line-end continuation.
5. Compared the Japanese and Portuguese meanings to expose readings plausible in only one language.
6. Rechecked confusable glyph families, including long `ſ`/`f`, `u`/`v`, `g`/`q`, `n`/`u`, `m`/`u`, `i`/`l`, consonant counts, tildes, and accents.
7. Compared the 31 expected NINJAL source-order entries (`001c01`–`001d18`) after the independent text existed. No entry candidate was missing.
8. Adjudicated every candidate against enlargement, then repeated a complete scan sweep from the beginning. The final sweep produced no new candidate.

## Corrections found before human review

| Line | Earlier reading | Confirmed print | Reason it was found |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `c1-l012` | `mino xita laqi.` | `mino xita ſaqi.` | Japanese *shita saki* made the apparent `l` suspicious; enlargement confirms long `ſ`. |
| `c1-l016` | `Gordura, unto` | `Gordura, vnto` | Dedicated `u`/`v` audit confirms printed initial `v`. |
| `c1-l028` | `q̃ uſão` | `q̃ vſão` | Dedicated `u`/`v` audit confirms printed initial `v`. |

Several plausible suspicions were rejected after enlargement. The print supports `aburaqitta fito`, anomalous `Aburaguitra`, and `Aburicauarague`. Context was used to decide where to reinspect, not to regularize these forms.

## Corrections found by human review

The user compared both generated columns with the scan and reported the following readings. Each was independently reinspected at larger scale before amendment.

| Line | Pre-review reading | Confirmed print | Principal error mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `c1-l007` | `ate a fiuela` | `ate afiuela` | Expected Portuguese grammar supplied a source space. |
| `c1-l008` | `chicaraqane` | `chicaragane` | Japanese *chikara-gane* and enlargement distinguish `g` from `q`. |
| `c1-l010` | `nalba` | `naiba` | `i`/`l` confusion. |

| `c1-l023` | `funuuru` | `ſumuru` | Long `ſ`/`f` and `m`/`n`/`u` confusion; historical Japanese context resolves *sumuru*. |
| `c1-l027` | `pastas` | `paſtas` | Short `s` substituted for printed long `ſ`. |
| `c1-l028` | `vſão` | `vzão` | Expected Portuguese form displaced the printed `z`. |
| `c2-l011` | `Aburauotçugu` | `Aburauo tçugu` | Visible Japanese word boundary omitted. |
| `c2-l013` | `l,` | `i,` | `i`/`l` confusion. |

| `c2-l037` | `Aquentarſe` | `Aquẽtarſe` | The printed abbreviation was silently expanded instead of preserved. |

The difficult `c1-l023` was not decided from semantic expectation alone. Enlargement establishes initial long `ſ`; the remaining types support `umuru`, and [a published web note from Komazawa University](https://www.komazawa-u.ac.jp/~hagi/ko_tame53.html) independently reports *Aburauo sumuru* for this exact dictionary passage.

## Result

The Level 1 page retains process status `scan_confirmed`, but the human result demonstrates that this status cannot stand as an accuracy claim. Thirteen further corrections are far above the working target. The failed result changes the procedure: future pre-review must align each source token explicitly, perform an additional glyph sweep in reverse line order, and check base letters separately from diacritics and abbreviation marks.

The canonical corrections have been applied and the interface regenerated. Both column units remain open until the user checks the revised rendering; only then can reviewer provenance be recorded. This preserves the distinction between applying feedback and completing the correction-and-recheck loop.
