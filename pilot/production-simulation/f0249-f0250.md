# Timed Level 1 Production Simulation: f0249–f0250

## Purpose and selection

This simulation tests the complete Level 1 workflow on two consecutive pages that had not previously been transcribed. `f249` follows the reviewed `f248` page and therefore also tests the printed `Gugo-` catchword continuation. `f250` continues the same ordinary dictionary sequence.

The two pages contain 196 recorded physical lines: 189 dictionary-text lines and seven lines of running headers, page number, signature, and catchwords. Together they raise the validated Level 1 corpus from 330 to 526 physical lines.

The initial visual checkpoint was made directly from the cached native-resolution Gallica masters and overlapping sixth-column tiles. Wikisource, OCR, translations, and NINJAL headword data were not consulted until that checkpoint existed. NINJAL version 202510 was then used during Japanese/romanization review as a source-order and suspicious-form prompt, never as authority over the scan.

These timings measure this interface and operator on only two pages; they are a pass-level operational baseline, not yet a corpus-wide rate forecast.

## Rejected suspicions and preserved anomalies

The final scan pass rejected several attractive emendations:

- `Couoriro` was preserved although Japanese context suggests `Couorino`.
- `Guxer` was preserved although the NINJAL headword row gives `Guxet`.
- Portuguese `Mucayſe`, `caſtiças`, and `enrrejeitados` were preserved as visibly printed rather than silently replaced by more familiar forms.
- Joined `deſi`, `perſi`, and `dagoa` were retained as documentary spacing.

No flagged span remained materially unresolved. The production simulation therefore did not supply the real uncertainty case still needed to design optional uncertainty notation.

## Continuity and format coverage

- The `f248` catchword `Gugo-` continues as the first `f249` entry, `Gugo.`
- The `f249` catchword `Gun-` continues as `f250` first entry `Gunpô.`
- The `f250` catchword `Gǔyô.` was confirmed against the first entry on `f251`.
- A second displaced-text case, `(o homem.`, was represented with the already existing far-right named-span syntax. It completes the following `Gunjǒ` definition but remains in physical position at Level 1.
- No new line, zone, typeface, indentation, placement, or furniture syntax was required.

## Format and workflow decision

The compact Markdown format is adopted as **Level 1 format version 1**.

The evidence supporting adoption is:

1. Six complete pages and 526 physical lines compile and round-trip deterministically.
2. Two unfamiliar consecutive pages were entered and reviewed without changing the syntax.
3. Cross-page catchwords and a new displaced-text example remained auditable.
4. Separate contextual passes found errors that a visual pass missed, while final scan confirmation rejected unsupported normalization.
5. The format remained directly readable and editable throughout fresh transcription.

The production review-status vocabulary is:

- `visual_draft`: initial scan-derived transcription and physical lineation exist;
- `context_reviewed`: both Japanese/romanization and Portuguese-context passes are complete;
- `scan_confirmed`: all contextual flags have been adjudicated and the final complete scan sweep is complete.

The legacy `trial_reviewed` status remains valid only for pilot pages whose earlier review history does not map cleanly onto the new production states. It must not be assigned to new work.

Adoption does not freeze the format forever. Evidence from production may require a compatible revision, especially when the first genuinely unresolved reading or literal Markdown delimiter appears. Such cases should produce a documented versioned change rather than speculative syntax now.
